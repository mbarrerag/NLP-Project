# Influence of the hydrogeological and chemical properties of the aquifer that underlies the sanitary filling Navarro of the city of Santiago de Cali, on the mobility of heavy metals.

The leachates generated as a byproduct of the processes of decomposition and stabilization of the solid urban waste confined to the weather function as a means of transport of pollutants to the soil and groundwater, impacting them significantly. The heavy metals, are part of the four main groups of pollutants containing the leachate, present as a higher characteristic that generate a high risk of affectation to human health, the ecosystems to which they are naturally transported and the final receptors, due to their high toxicity. Generally the concentrations of heavy metals in leachate systems of final disposal of urban solid waste are relatively low, ranging from micrograms per litre (μg/L) to some milligrams per litre (mg/L), considering that the natural environmental conditions of these systems do not favour the solubilitization of larger quantities, however, are highly impacted pollutants. The present research is based on the hypothesis that, in aqueous media, such as a lixividate pen in an aquifer, these pollutants are of different forms, each with different patterns of transport, bioavailability and toxicity, in addition subject to experience different mechanisms of The hypothesis is corroborated by the results obtained in the experimental phase and the analytical solution of the transport equation. The conceptual model of the contaminated system and the description and analysis of the development of the site as a system of final disposal of urban solid waste and source of pollution were developed. It was found that the physical and mineralogical characteristics of the granular material of the aquifer and the layer that superysates, favor the phenomenon of immobilization of the pollutants under study
