# Characterization of secondary metabolites produced by the IBUN-2755 strain, involved in antimicrobial and antifungal activity against rice pathogens.

The use of biocontrolling organisms as a strategy to reduce the use of agrochemicals in crops, is a practice that is increasing. Particularly relevant is the development of products of biological origin that allow control of rice pathogens in a specific and effective way reducing the rates of contamination and the recurrence of diseases by the emergence of resistant pathogens. However, to reach the development of a product of biological origin that has the characteristics necessary to be effective in the control of phytosanitary diseases, it is necessary to have a comprehensive knowledge of the organism producing the active substances that will be part of that product. In this context, the study of the Bacillus velazensis strain IBUN 2755 becomes relevant since it has shown to have a strong antibacterial and antifungal activity in vitro, against three pathogens that have been reported as causing large losses in rice crops and therefore this strain is profiled as a potential micro-organism biocontrollerant. The extensive study of the strain includes the characterization of the possible bioactive secondary metabolites produced by the strain, which are directly involved in the anti-bacterical activity and anti-aconform and anti-feverial factors. the one that presented less active supernatants against the three pathogens and the medium MOLP P.U the one that surrendered the most active supernatants. Likewise these extraction processes were carried out on two different volumes of each medium: 25 and 200 ml.
