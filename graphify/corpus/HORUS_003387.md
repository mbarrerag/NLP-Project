# Interprofessional education in perinatal maternal health: perception in medicine and nursing

to understand the discourses of students and teachers of medicine and nursing in relation to interprofessional education (IPA) in perinatal maternal health. qualitative study with textual, contextual and sociological analysis of discourse from the socio-hermeneutic perspective, using focus groups of students and professors of medicine and nursing in the area of maternal and perinatal health. Two emerging categories were identified: “institutional and academic discourse” and “what is necessary for IPA”. traditional institutionality, based on a uniprofessional academic discourse, generates barriers and resistance to the demands of an increasingly interdependent health care environment. The IPA requires skills such as empathy, effective communication and teamwork, as well as the development of spaces for encounter and dialogue, overcoming hierarchies and power and competence relationships for its proper implementation. the discourse of students and teachers of medicine and nursing is permeated by the experience of institutional and dynamic barriers of entrenched power that reproduce in the spaces of formation.
