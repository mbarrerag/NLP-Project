# Edaphic factors associated with the development of avocado wilt complex and implementation of a GIS tool for risk visualization

V.Avocado wilt complex (AWC) is an important disease and disorder of the avocado crop. It is associated with different causal agents that induce similar disease symptoms of expression, with Phytophthora cinnamomi, Verticillium sp., and abiotic hypoxia-anoxia being the most important ones under tropical conditions. The objectives of this work were to identify the edaphic variables associated with AWC foci and develop a GIS tool for the prevention of this disease. Microbiological, topographic, physical and chemical soil properties were performed, and AWC incidence and severity were estimated in plots planted with avocado. The effect of each of the soil properties on AWC was determined using a generalized additive model. Additionally, three categories of crop management were proposed and a GIS tool was designed to determine the risk of AWC presence. Diseased plants with AWC were more frequently found (P<0.001) in flatter areas. Sulfur content in the soil (P<0.001, -0.09), infiltration (P<0.001, -1.7), hydraulic conductivity (P<0.001, -0.7), microbial diversity (P<0.001, -0.85), fungi (P<0.001, -9.58) and bacteria (P<0.001, -3.51) populations in the soil were negatively correlated with AWC development. On the other hand, copper (P<0.001, +0.65), high clay content in soil (P<0.001, +9.91), and moisture retention capacity (P<0.001, +3.29) were positively correlated with AWC development. The obtained results may be used as technical parameters for the selection of the most suitable areas for the establishment of avocado crops based on the elimination of areas that may favor AWC and that can be easily visualized under a GIS tool
