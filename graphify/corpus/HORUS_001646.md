# Evaluation of nitrogen deficiency in maize leaves’ diffuse reflectance spectra by means of a light–vegetation interaction model and support vector machine

Crops are immersed in constantly changing environments that are sometimes unfavourable for their growth, development, and productivity. Environmental conditions include both biotic and abiotic factors that limit agricultural productivity and are considered a threat to food security. Establishing signs of stress in plants and mechanisms to adapt to them are fields of study from different areas, such as agronomy and genomics. In this sense, there is a need for tools and systems that allow for the monitoring of crop health. Here, the use of intelligent systems plays an important role in the field of precision agriculture. Passive multispectral remote sensing sensors are among the intelligent systems that enable the measurement of optical diffuse reflectance across the electromagnetic spectrum. The diffuse reflectance of vegetation provides valuable information on the expected behaviour of a healthy plant. This information is obtained from its biological components which are expressed in terms of optical parameters. With this in mind, this article presents the inversion of a direct diffuse reflectance model, enabling the selection of the vegetation’s optical parameters that allowed the detection of abiotic stress in maize crops. This selection was achieved using measured spectral data and machine learning techniques. 400 diffuse reflectance signatures of maize crops, measured at AGROSAVIA in Rionegro-Antioquia, were processed. Optimization techniques were implemented for each signature to invert a direct model, estimating their optical parameters. Subsequently, the parameters for detecting abiotic stress in the transient crops were obtained using a T-test and evaluated with support vector machines. As a result, the method was found to discriminate maize leaves under nitrogen stress, identifying those with 25%, 50%, and 75% of the nitrogen requirement
