# THE IMPORTANCE OF OBJECTIVE CONFIRMATION OF AIRWAY OBSTRUCTION IN PRESCHOOL WHEEZING

Recurrent wheezing is a very common and heterogeneous condition in preschool children, affecting up to 40% of children by the age of three. The high prevalence of this entity and the high clinical and economic burden associated with it underscore the need for accurate diagnosis to ensure proper management and thus improve clinically important outcomes in affected patients. With respect to this, the presence of wheezing is usually determined retrospectively from a parental report, and it is well known that frequently parents do not adequately distinguish wheezing from other respiratory sounds such as stridor or snoring. Because of this, it is considered very important that an objective confirmation of wheezing be carried out either through a consultation that takes place during the occurrence of acute symptoms or through the use of a recording (i.e. by means of electronic wheeze detection using digital stethoscopes that use artificial intelligence). The assessment of the presence of wheezing during a consultation should be performed by an experienced professional, since ambiguous identification and interpretation of lung sounds during auscultation has been described when this assessment is performed by healthcare professionals with varying levels of knowledge and training
