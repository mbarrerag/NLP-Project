# MALARIA AAVIAR: OPPORTUNITY OF COLLABORATION AND STRENGTHENING NETWORKS FOR ANDIN COUNTRIES

Avian malaria caused by Plasmodium and others hamosporidae such as Haemopoteus and Leucocytozoon has negative impacts on the survival of organisms, their reproductive capacity, parental care and even causing extinction of species. Classical studies of malaria using birds, quite successful, went into disuse to adopt models closer to humans such as mice and monkeys. The model that still persists Serinus canaria has shown diversity in response to strains of hamosporidae. When analyzing the database MalAvi, of the 16383 registered molecular lineages, only 4079 come from SouthAmerica, where 42.3% come from Brazil. So Andean countries, with our great diversity in birds and ecosystems we have an opportunity to share niches. Leucocytozoon for example, exotic for the neotropic, with the discoveries of distribution in pámos and mountain chains, open an opportunity to characterize their life cycle, completely unknown. Even thinking that the vectors are not insects of the genus Simulium but Gigantodax. Likewise, we have the opportunity to use the disease and distribute avian models widely
