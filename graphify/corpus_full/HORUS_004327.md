# Menstrual poverty and social and economic development: recommendations of public policy for the care of children and adolescents in Colombia

Most girls, adolescents and women must allocate economic and emotional resources to manage their menstruation, in Colombia the menstruating people who are in the extreme poverty line must devote between 6.2% and 14.8% of their monthly income, to managing this in a dignified way. In this sense, menstrual poverty, related to the lack of access to menstrual management products and the few conditions for dignified menstruation, is a phenomenon that affects the length of life and is consolidated as one of the factors influencing the social and economic development of girls and adolescents, which would increase the barriers and inequalities faced by reason of their gender, can be evidenced especially given the negative impacts that can be had in the education sector and in relation to their school performance. it can have in the educational field and in relation to their school performance. Therefore, it is important that national and local government addresses this issue and implement gender-based policies for the protection and guarantee of girls and adolescents rights, this contribution to the reduction of gender inequality in the long term
