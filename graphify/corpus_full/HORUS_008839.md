# SEARCHING THE SUN AND TOOLING THE FRY IN THE MONTHS OF ANDES: THE TERM BIOLOGY OF THE ENDEMY ANADIA BOGOTENSIS (SQUAMATA, GYMNOPHTHHALMIDAE).

The ectoterm organisms have different physiological mechanisms, behavioral or thermoregulation strategies, which vary in space and time. The lizards are useful models to study how the elevation affects thermal critics and thermoregulation strategies. This work focused on identifying the variation of thermal traits of three populations of Anadia bogotensis in an altitude gradient (2700, 3000 and 3450 m.n.m.) and in different climatic times in the eastern Andes mountain range (Colombia). Body temperatures were recorded in field, air and substrate, as well as operational temperatures. In laboratory, preference temperatures were measured, and maximum and minimal criticisms. We obtained an association of body temperature with the temperatures of the substrate for the low elevation population, the air and substrate for the populations of 3000 m and 3450 m, and an effect of size on the body temperature for the population at 3000 m. We found no differences between Tpref or Ctmax between populations or climatic periods recorded.
