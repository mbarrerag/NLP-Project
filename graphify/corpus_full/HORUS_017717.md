# Analysis of physicochemical and mechanical properties of intravenous saline containers subjected to microwave heating processes.

For the health area, in the distribution, storage and transport of medicines, there are several polymer packagings marketed in Colombia. For the specific case of the packaging of intravenous saline solutions, the polymeric material most used is polyvinyl chloride (PVC). Unfortunately, there is no clear information about the exact composition of these packagings, nor the effects on the material and saline solution when exposing these to microwave heating, a process normally used to condition the liquid before supplying it to a patient. For this reason, saline solutions packages marketed in Colombia were characterized by infrared spectroscopy (IR), thermogravimetry (TGA), differential heatmetry of scanning (DSC), stress-formation test and gas chromatography coupled to mass spectrometry (GC-MS). Characterizations made before and after submitting the packaging together with its saline solution to microwave heating. The results indicate that the polymeric material in the packaging is PVC with a high DEP content as a plasticate.
