# Current context of indigenous peoples and covid-19: diseased housing in COLOMBIA, MEXICO, BRAZIL

Abstract The document aims to present the analysis of the realities that indigenous communities experience in the face of the pandemic, as well as to analyze the strategies, policies and actions of the different Latin American governments in the face of the intercultural approach. The methodology used was semi-structured interview with five actors and nursing leaders working with the indigenous communities of the Latin American countries: Colombia, Mexico, Brazil and Peru. The interviews were recorded, transcribed and analyzed according to some thematic axes. At the Latin American level, indigenous peoples have taken measures for the prevention and mitigation of COVID-19, such as the closure of their territories and strengthening of their traditional practices; on the other hand, as a result of social inequalities and inequalities, health systems do not have the capacity to meet emergency care needs, among other difficulties is geographical dispersion. Similarly, universities and other health institutions must begin to impart knowledge to health professionals about interculturality
