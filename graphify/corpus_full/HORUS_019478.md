# USING DATA ANALYTICS FOR ENHANCED SMART LOADING ZONES DESIGN

Urban space is often provided for freight operations in the form of (un)loading zones (LZ). City authorities have the challenge of gaining knowledge about LZ utilization by using technology implementations and enforcement practices to capture parking behaviours. Since Smart LZ evoke the involvement of technology and data in the planning and management of this type of transport infrastructure, there is a promising research field for designing analytical frameworks that shape dynamic and user-oriented decision-making processes. This paper studies the potential of data analytics for enhancing the design of LZ in urban contexts. The focus is on the strategic level of LZ decisions that include the definition of the number of LZ, and the identification of optimal location-allocation of LZ based on establishments locations, walking distances and parking demand. The methods included the machine learning techniques k-means++ , DBSCAN, and integer linear programming in a case study. This paper moves forward greenfield analysis for LZ location based on data science tools and dynamic location-allocation of stalls for LZ based on parking demand during the day, occupancy levels, and infrastructure availability according to technological data collection. This research provides foundations for the development of methods and tools for designing Smart LZ and the framework for strategic decisions on freight parking
