# Violence and servitude: The imposition of Hispanic rule in Santafé de Bogotá, 1550-1600

The foundation of Santafé de Bogotá, in 1539, extended the Hispanic domain over the muiscas, inhabitants of the highlands of the Cordillera Oriental in the Northern Andes. The violence allowed to impose the notions of "service and obedience" that supported the monarchical sovereignty, although transformed in the process. After the conquest, the "lord" was sustained in legal apparatuses; systems of circulation and exchange of goods, forms of production and appropriation of the works; and suitable concepts to represent the social relations. This work analyzes each of these aspects, from the establishment of the Audiencia until the end of the sixteenth century, when the domain of the king was strengthened through its officials, as visitors, correctors and protectors, fiscal and economic policies, and the congregation of Indians in villages.
