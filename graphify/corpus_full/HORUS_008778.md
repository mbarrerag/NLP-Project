# Extending boundaries between architecture and city: Formal variations and perceptual alterations of the edge to a structuring interstitial

This research focuses on the reconsideration of the boundary between architecture and city, exploring the notion of "extended limit". The conventional conception of this limit is questioned as a line, proposing instead the idea of a deep and habitable space. It highlights the lack of response to the unpredictability of the sequences of living in urban Latin American contexts, and seeks inspiration in architectures of extended limits that offer innovative answers to the changing needs of contemporary life. The study analyzes the conceptual transgression of the limit and proposes its extension as an interstitial, private in property, but public in relation to the city. It is suggested that this extension can address the lack of spaces that satisfy the various possibilities of living. The methodological approach focuses on establishing an inconceptual method to extend the limit in densified urban contexts, considering the implementation of buildings with extended limits characteristics. The work addresses the definition of the extended limit and the various possible extensions, including activities, interstities, perceptions and sequences. It examines the relevance of these extensions in a specific context by means of an architectural project of intervention in a district of Bogotá.
