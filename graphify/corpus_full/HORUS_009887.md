# GEOGRAPHICAL ACCESSIBILITY OF ECONOMIC POINTS AS A PLANNING TOWARDS THE CITY OF 15 MINUTES

The 15-minute city is a strategy to consolidate polycentric cities, where urban activity is not centralized in economic poles, but rather has optimal geospatial coverage that promotes short trips and, with this, more likely to be carried out in active transport modes. The objective of this research is to propose a concrete methodology for the determination of urban economic poles, places that present a high concentration and diversity of services and great attraction of travel. The methodology consists of the analysis of two main variables: attraction/generation of travel and use of surrounding land; this was applied in the city of Manizales, where six economic poles were identified and a territorial accessibility model was applied to evaluate the pedestrian coverage provided by these economic poles to the population according to their socioeconomic level. We conclude that the proposed methodology allows identifying in a preliminary way those urban areas that, due to their activity and dynamics, can be considered economic poles and, therefore, could be subject to a more detailed analysis in terms of urban management. Furthermore, we note that there is an inequality for the low socioeconomic levels in the access to areas of greater urban activity in Manizales
