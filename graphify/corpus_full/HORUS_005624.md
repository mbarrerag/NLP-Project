# Harmonic compensation from battery storage systems

Unconventional renewable energy sources (FNCER) each day increase their share in the country's energy matrix, mainly because of the need to reduce CO2 emissions. Due to the intermittent nature of FNCER, it is necessary to integrate energy storage systems to ensure the availability of the resource at any time and place. Batteries are the most frequently used devices in these applications, however, substantially increase the cost of the system. The inclusion of FNCER, as well as the connection of various linear and non-linear charges in the electrical system, generate power quality problems such as harmonics. Considering the above and as a strategy to justify the cost of the energy storage system, it is possible to look for alternatives to mitigate harmonics from the charging and unloading process of batteries. This article analyses the use of battery-based energy storage systems as a preliminary solution to mitigate these phenomena. The study is based on evidence of modifications in the charging requirement and nominal capacity of the battery.
