# We have the right to violence

A war anecdote tells us that a Roman merchant was pressured by German soldiers to give them the gold he owned. Faced with the refusal of the merchant, the soldiers decided to occupy his house while that merchant was giving in to the request made. With a merchant strategy, he planned to free himself from the soldiers: he told them that the gold intended had been hidden on an island of the Tiber; that his son would take them by boat and reveal the exact place there. Having to cross the river, and arriving at the point, the merchant’s son threw out the oars, jumped into the water and swam to the shore, starting the escape. The soldiers, for their part, went to the bottom of the river. Of this fact, can it be said that the merchant and his son committed murder? And therefore, did they have to pay a penalty that would deprive them of freedom, even of life, for the act committed? Or perhaps, does the soldiers’ death count as a natural consequence of the acts perpetrated against the population and the merchant family?
