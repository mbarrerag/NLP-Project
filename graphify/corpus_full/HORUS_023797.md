# INTRODUCTION TO THE SOLID STATE

This text is based on my experience as a teacher of the Introduction course to the solid state of the Department of Physics of the Universidad Nacional de Colombia, Bogotá. It consists of nine chapters that seek to cover most of the topics that should be addressed in the course and can help the student guide and to develop in them capacities of self-study.The book can also serve for people who initiate their line of research in solid state or who want to have an introduction in this field, in order to later be able to deepen in one of the different areas of condensed matter.To follow the book they must have knowledge of quantum mechanics, and although it is not necessary, it is useful to have seen the course of statistical mechanics.In the first chapter we introduce the problem of solid and identical particles, which will lead us to the concepts of fermion and boson. Then we study a gas of electrons at zero temperature and at finite temperature, and we calculate the purpose of the punctual and spatial capacity.The second chapter is dedicated to the description of a crystalline system as a more a base, and to the concepts of punctual and spatial group. We also make a short introduction to the representation of occupancy numbers and calculate the calorific capacity due to the ion network. In the seventh chapter we calculate the transport properties in the Drude model for a classic electron gas and make an introduction to the plasma. In the eighth chapter we study a quantum electron gas under the effect of electric and magnetic fields, in particular we analyze the quantumization of magnetic flow, Landau levels and quantum Hall effect. Finally, in the ninth chapter we explore the effects of electrical and magnetic fields for electrons in band theory, define the cyclotronic mass and find the Hass van-Alphen effect. At the end of this chapter we make an introduction to the physics of semiconductors. (Extracted from some parts of the preface and introduction)
