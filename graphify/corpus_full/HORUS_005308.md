# Characterization of injuries caused by less lethal weapons in Colombia from 2017 to 2021

The use of the least lethal weapons in Colombia has been a cause of controversy and debate in recent years as a result of the social impact of the consequences that result from their use, this phenomenon has not been analyzed widely within the territory reason why it was decided through this study to characterize the lesions and sociodemographic variables associated with these events for the period from 2017 to 2021. A retrospective descriptive study was carried out based on the information provided by the National Institute of Legal Medicine and Forensic Sciences (INMLCF) through the National Reference Centre on Violence (CNRV). For the area of forensic clinic, a statistical analysis of 740 related cases was carried out, finding predominance of events related to the use of police sticks, affecting mostly young adult men and where the most common lesions were of soft tissues. For the area of forensic pathology, 5 cases were documented where the majority were related to kinetic impact projectors with primarily incomproming of the skull. The characteristics of the lesions described in the expert reports were determined for these cases, as well as the sociodemographic and contextual variables of interest for the future events., a statistical analysis was performed on 740 related cases, finding a majority of events related to police battles, committing in most of the cases young adult men and with most common injuries in soft tissue. For the foreign pathology area, 5 cases were found most related to kinetic impact projects compromising the head. For those cases, the characteristics of the injuries registered on the foreign expert's reports were determined, as well as the sociodemographic and contextual variables of interest for the events. This study is the foundation of future research related to the use and injury derived from non-lethal weapons in our country
