# Pharmacological and behavioral effects of chronic nicotine administration on a self-modeling task

Current research has highlighted the importance of associative learning in addiction. It has been shown that by means of pavlovian procedures the keys associated with an appetitative stimulus such as a drug can acquire incentive salience, becoming more attractive and facilitating behaviors of search for drugs and relapse. The allocation of incentive salience can vary according to the phenotype of the subjects (goal trackers or sign trackers). Although in both phenotypes the conditioned stimulus (EC) acquires predictive value on the undisturbed stimulus (EI), only in the signtrackers the EC acquires incentive salience. In this regard, it has been reported that nicotine has the ability to amplify the value of keys associated with incentive stimuli such as food or water. The present study had as an evaluation the effects of chronic nicotine administration in the acquisition of automolation task and expression of FosB/DFB in areas related to the salience of incentive. 61 male rats Wider classified as signtrackers were given in a presentation of the chronic nicotine response to the task of automolation and expression and expression in the expression. Although for both phenotypes the conditioned stimulus (CS) can address predictive value over the unconditioned stimulus, only for the sign trackings the CS agreement incentive health.
