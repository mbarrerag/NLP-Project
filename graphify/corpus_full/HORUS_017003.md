# CHAPTER 1: DEVELOPMENT, GROWTH AND GENETIC FACTORS ASSOCIATED IN FOOD WIN

Within the muscle, genetics plays a relevant role by way of the expression of genes both specific of the muscle tissue and those ubiquitous, which together intervene to regulate physiological processes associated with the synthesis of meat with positive quality attributes. Thus these genes, by regulating the biochemical and cellular composition of the muscle, are also involved in the control of the quality and quantity of meat (te Pas et al., 2004). Genetic diversity results in alternative mRNA, which result in variations in the levels of expression of proteins. If the variation within the genes could be related to differences in the production capacity of meat in meat animals (e.g. growth), this could be used as a genetic marker with the ability to be applied in a selection program assisted by markers (MAS) (te Pas and Visscher, 1994; te Pas et al., 2004). Therefore, it is of great importance to identify such genetic markers within the functional gene, because these cause a direct effect on the characteristic associated with production (te Pass and Visscher, 1994).
