# Search and design of antimicrobial peptides in silicon by analyzing the proteomas of viruses, bacteria and fungi

Due to the increasing resistance of some pathogenic organisms to different antimicrobials, the need to find new antimicrobial compounds as treatment options has been increased. In response, new alternative approaches have been adopted, including the use of antimicrobial peptides (AMPs). MPAs are a natural part of the immune system of all organisms, several studies have shown that MPAs have a great advantage compared to usual antibiotics based on their broad spectrum activity, action mechanisms, host cell selectivity and less likely to generate resistance. For these reasons, this research focused on the identification, selection, modification and evaluation of in-silico MPAs found in the protein of viruses, bacteria and fungi by using specific bioinformatics and artificial intelligence tools that assessed parameters such as structure, hemolytic capacity, toxicity, capacity to bind membranes, their potential as antimicrobial and their potential effect against cancer and cell penetration. Consequently, the new peptides found in this study are expected to be candidates for future in vitro and in vivo trials. modification, and evaluation about in silica AMPs found in the protection of viruses, bacteria, and fungi through the use of specific bioinformation and artificial intelligence tools that assessed parameters such as structure, hemolytic capacity, toxicity, membrane-binding capability, their potential as antimicrobials, and their possible anticancer and cell-penetration effects.
