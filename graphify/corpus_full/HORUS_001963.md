# Development of a methodology for the valuation of the financial performance of multinational companies engaged in outgoing foreign investment, originating in emerging economies

This research project presents a detailed methodology for assessing the financial performance of multinational companies that make outward foreign direct investment, originating from emerging markets. The methodology involves a mixed econometric model that incorporates fixed and random effects to examine the impact of financial indicators such as EBIT, net margin and total assets, together with macroeconomic factors such as purchasing power parity (PPP) and macroeconomic competitiveness, on asset performance (ROA) of these companies. The analysis includes a sample of 259 multinational entities from Colombia, Mexico and Chile, analyzed with financial data of 10 years, operating in several sectors, including manufacturing, energy, mining and financial services. The findings suggest that effective asset management and a solid capital structure play an important role in maintaining an optimal financial return in the world markets. In addition, the study demonstrates the importance of the macroeconomic environment of the country of origin in the strategies of expansion of multinational companies. They operate in different areas such as manufacturing, energy, mining, and financial services. The results indicate that efficient management of assets and a strong capital structure are crucial for maintaining optimal financial performance in international markets. Futuremore, the study highlights the significance of the domestic macroeconomic conditions in shaping the expansion strategies of multinational corporations. This comprehensive approach seeks to enhance understanding of the choices that impact the financial performance of multinational firms in emerging nations. As a result, it contributes to the progress of knowledge in the realm of international business and finance
