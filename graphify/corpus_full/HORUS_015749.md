# Resisting the Milipatriarchy: Experiences of Young Women in Joint Antimilitarist Organizations in Bogotá and their Contributions

This thesis, which emerges from my personal experience as an activist, analyzes the participation of young women in five mixed antimilitarist organizations in Bogotá and examines their contributions to these collective processes and to the context of militarisation in the country. The proposal is guided by a feminist theoretical framework to understand antimilitarism and the commitment of the viewpoint as a methodological axis. This is a qualitative research that approached the experience of the activists through semi-structured interviews and participating observation, which allowed to identify the understandings that these young women have built on militarism, militarization and antimilitarism, as well as the main bets and actions that drive from their organizations. This thesis also reflects on the challenges that women face in order to materialize effective participation within the antimilitarist processes. It is concluded that among the main contributions of women are the support of their collectives, strengthening of networks, the construction of direct actions, the opposition and the politication of care; and that, given the reproduction of patriarch inside the organizations, it is urgent to implement actions to detect and transform gender inequalities. . s processes. It is concluded that the main roles of active women are to support their collectives, to strengthenthen networks, to create non-violent direct action, and to politicalize care; furthermore, given that homeland reproduces within organizations, urgent actions must be taken to detect and transform gender inequalities
