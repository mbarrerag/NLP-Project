# Variation of plasma volume as a confounding factor in the hematological biological passport.

Acute exposure of resident cyclists trained at altitude at higher temperature and barometric pressure decreases blood markers due to expansion of plasma volume (VP). The response of blood markers such as haemoglobin concentration, hematocrit, plasma volume and stimulation index (Off-score) was monitored to ten men road cyclists residing and training at moderate altitude (2500 m, 456 mmHg, 22°C) during a seven-day training camp at lower altitude (456 m, 566 mmHg, 26°C). The haemoglobin concentration decreased to 1.4 g/dl, haematocrit to 3.3%, plasma volume increased to 644 ml and the Off-score marker varied to 17.1 points. Monitoring the variation of such blood markers measured in the athlete's biological passport (PBA) against variations in environmental conditions, established ranges of variation of Off-Score, allowing for improved control of prohibited substances in sport
