# ADDRESSING THE OPPORTUNITY GAP IN THE LATIN AMERICAN NEUROSCIENCE COMMUNITY

Strategic initiatives to foster equity, diversity and inclusion (EDI) in (neuro)science have increased substantially in recent years around the world, aided by a massive coordinated international commitment and effort to expand science globally (500 Women Scientists, BiasWatchIndia, BiasWatchNeuro, Black in Neuro, Declaration on Research Assessment, EDIS by Wellcome, Queer in Neuro, Women in Neuroscience Repository, Women's NeuroNetwork, World Women in Neuroscience and EDI committees at various universities). Yet, imbalances persist, particularly in resource-limited countries in the Global South that have additional challenges related to funding, political instability and sociocultural inequities1. Taken together, these imbalances create a playing field with unequal starting points. In Latin America, economic factors, cultural norms and political environments greatly influence the degree of research investment and minority rights protection, two major elements that contribute to low levels of efficiency, productivity and diversity in scientific research activities. By outlining these and other challenges, we call for more attention and customized solutions to address the specific disadvantages faced by Latin American neuroscientists (LAN)
