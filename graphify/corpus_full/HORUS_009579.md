# BIRD DIVERSITY ACROSS AN ANDEAN CITY: THE LIMITATION OF SPECIES RICHNESS VALUES AND WATERSHED SCALES

The evaluation of several biodiversity indicators and considering several spatial scales could evidence conservation problems and improve biodiversity monitoring in urban environments. We estimated species richness (order q = 0) and Shannon (order q = 1) and Simpson (order q = 2) diversities using Hill numbers, and performed clustering and multidimensional non-metric scaling (NMDS) analyses to compare seven urban micro-basins and a peri-urban site in a city in the northern Andes (Medellín, Colombia). We found 113 resident bird species: 50 (44%) exclusively within the urban area, 21 (19%) exclusively in the peri-urban site and 42 (37%) shared. Some urban basins had a species richness similar to the peri-urban site, but Shannon and Simpson diversities were always lower, showing clearer patterns of bird diversity reduction when considering the abundances. The composition of bird species differed between urban areas and the peri-urban site, with the urban micro-basins clustering with each other in the clustering and NMDS analyses. The periurban site formed its own group, suggesting homogenization in the composition of bird species due to the reduction of species turnover within the urban area, with endemic, almost endemic and rare species being restricted to periurban areas where remnants of native forest persist. Indicators that evaluate several biodiversity scales and analyses that consider more local spatial scales are necessary to better understand biodiversity patterns in Andean cities and to design urban planning strategies that prevent biodiversity loss
