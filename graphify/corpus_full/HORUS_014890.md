# Epistemology and legal informatics in the application of the principle of solidarity in the face of the reinforced job stability of persons with disabilities from the management of legal risk.

The variety of case law criteria in the allocation of risks and burden sharing that is evident in cases of increased labour stability due to the decrease in the worker's working capacity constitutes a problem related to the scope of the principle of solidarity that directly affects the employment inclusion of persons with disabilities.In the basis of these differences, it has been pointed out that acting in solidarity implies taking full or partial responsibility for the human costs involved for the person's health situation, distributing the burdens of the person concerned reasonably among other persons, for which the case law has taken into account the family (care), social (prestational) and for its employers and contractors, the duty to preserve it in employment unless it is just cause, without prejudice to the obligation to relocate it, train it and adjust the conditions of its work to the change in its existing conditions.The general objective of the research is to identify the epistemological foundations that underlie the different criteria used by the High Courts to determine the extent of the solidarity in the allocation of risks and distribution of burdens, if the situation of reinforced labour is affected by the situation of the general employment and the situation of disability
