# MULTIOMIC-BASED DYNAMIC ANALYSIS TO IMPROVE PHA PRODUCTION PROCESS

The development of biodegradable materials obtained from renewable sources contributes in two ways to reduce the impact of human activities. Polyhydroxyalkanoates (PHAs) are a group of biopolymers that have been shown to be candidates to replace petrochemical plastics. Some strategies to reach commercial production of PHAs are: i) replacement of first-generation substrates, such as sugars and oils, with second- and third-generation substrates; ii) prospecting of new strains and improvement of the available strains; and iii) process improvement. Recently, the formulation of metabolically structured models has been consolidated as a strategy to improve processes, reducing experimental costs and time for development. Unlike other microorganisms, Burkholderia cepacia presents non-exclusive growth and production phases, as it accumulates PHAs in early phases of fermentation. In a previous work (Torres-Ospina and Riascos, 2020. in computer aided chemical engineering 48, pp. 673-678), the metabolic flux quantification, at different times in a batch fermentation with oleic acid, allowed to observe that in the growth phase 61% of the carbon is used for biomass and energy generation and 39% for PHA generation, while in the production phase the carbon distribution is 43% and 57%, respectively, reaching a PHA/biomass weight ratio of 77%. From the results of that work, it can also be concluded that the switch between the growth and production phases is not instantaneous and implies some adjustments in the functioning of the metabolism. Therefore, to extend the growth phase, it is necessary to develop metabolic regulation strategies. The present work focuses on the study of B. cepacia metabolism in a dynamic state, to increase knowledge of intracellular behavior and propose strategies to improve process productivity. To reach this objective, a combination of metabolomics and fluxomics is applied in the transient periods between fermentation phases. Based on the relationships between omics data, a dynamic regulation strategy that allows rebalancing the flows and considers the changing conditions into the cell and the fermentation broth is developed. This regulation strategy will allow us to propose alternatives to improve the production process. Initial results suggest that metabolites in the intersection of the major pathways (acetyl-coA, oxaloacetate, succinate, and others) undergo concentration changes related to metabolic variations; likewise, changes in cofactors concentration generate relevant information for this analysis. Currently, fluxomics, by linear metabolic flux analysis (MFA), and metabolomics, by gas chromatography coupled to mass spectroscopy, have been developed and, employing the time-series data, a dynamic model and the regulation proposal are being elaborated. Key words: Multi-omic analysis, dynamic model, time-series data, polyhydroxyalkanoates production, metabolic regulation
