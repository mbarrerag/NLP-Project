# Prediction of infiltration into the soil by integrating remote sensors and spatial inference into an Andean microbasin

The infiltration is the process of entry of water into the soil, its estimation is important both to make an efficient use of the water resource in crops, and for the effects of hydrological models. Due to its methodological and spatial complexity, various models have been developed for its estimation; however, the suitability and adjustment of these to the particular conditions of each soil is generally not simple, leading to the need to reduce its uncertainty to obtain more consistent results. This research developed a spatial model to estimate the basic infiltration rate using humidity and vegetation indices from Sentinel-1, attributes of the terrain obtained from the digital elevation model ALOS PALSAR and some soil properties. The results showed that the spatial self-regressive model with self-regressive errors allowed to model the baseline infiltration spatially, where the adjustment of the observed and estimated values presented a correlation coefficient of 0.98.
