# PHYSIOLOGICAL RESPONSE OF PEA (PISUM SATIVUM L) TO THE APPLICATION OF NANOPARTICLES UNDER WATERLOGGING CONDITIONS

Global agriculture faces constant challenges due to the increase in the frequency of adverse weather events as a result of climate change (Kopecká et al., 2023). Legume crops such as peas have been classified as highly sensitive crops to waterlogging, since their photosynthetic activity drastically decreases due to non-stomatal limitations and with low or no recovery after the waterlogging condition (Ploschuk et al., 2018; Zeng et al., 2020). Emerging technologies such as nanoparticles have shown biostimulant effects in different plants under abiotic stress conditions. In order to investigate the impact of silver, silicon nanoparticles (NPs) and compound fertilizers (NPK) on their physiology and performance under waterlogging (7 days). Experiments were carried out in greenhouses, divided into evaluations in vegetative stages and until harvest, implementing waterlogging in different phenological phases. The results indicate that the application of NPs could be an effective strategy in the response of pea plants to waterlogging stress, promoting better growth and photosynthesis, and increasing stress tolerance. Ag and Si NPs demonstrated to be effective in improving physiological parameters such as photosynthetic rate, stomatal conductance, carboxylation efficiency and transpiration rate, leveling with control plants. Performance components were highly sensitive to stress; However, applications of Si, Ag and NPK NPs showed improvements in variables such as grains per pod, green 100 grain weight and seed yield (t ha-1) compared to waterlogged plants. Finally, through correlation with tolerance indices (STI and WTI) to stress, it was determined that variables such as stomatal conductance, the SPAD index and water use efficiency are indicators of tolerance. Finally, the interaction of NPs with pea plants under waterlogging suggests mitigation of adverse effects, providing tolerance, subsequent recovery and less impact on yield
