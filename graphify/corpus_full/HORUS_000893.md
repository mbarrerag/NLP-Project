# Architecture in tourism: the reunion of human and nature in the province of Ricaurte

The global environmental crisis encourages a reflection on the ways in which humans inhabit nature. Nature tourism emerges as a practice that promotes environmental care, cultural respect and education. In this context, architecture plays a key role in promoting practices that seek a reunion between humans and nature. The province of Ricaurte, in the department of Boyacá, is characterized by its ecosystem, geological and historical diversity, which has favored the development of tourist dynamics. This research explores, through a qualitative methodology, the ways of inhabiting hosts linked to nature tourism in municipalities such as Villa de Leyva, Arcabuco and Gachantivá. Vernacular architecture and new constructive forms allow to interpret how this territory is inhabited through the use of local and traditional materials, such as the adobe and the Guadua. However, the proliferation of modern tourism models, such as the “glamping”, evidence how a separation between humans and nature is perpetuated by prioritizing comfort over the deep interaction with the environment.
