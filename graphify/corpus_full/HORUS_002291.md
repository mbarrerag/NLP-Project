# Use of computational models for the determination of attention bias and its relationship with cerebral electrical activity during learning of predictive relationships between stimuli.

Learning is a complex phenomenon that allows establishing predictive relationships between environmental stimuli and relevant consequences for survival.A line of research that has gained great relevance is that it establishes differences between two learning profiles: the one based on the approximation to signals associated with reward (sign-tracking) and that guided by the objective of the task (goal-tracking).These differences have been related to attentionalities and to greater vulnerability to the development of dysfunctional behaviors.However, the characterization of learning styles in human beings is still limited, in part due to the lack of tasks comparable to those used in animal studies.In this study, the behavioral, attention and neurophysiological changes produced by the predictor stimuli of rewards with different degree of uncertainty about the magnitude of the reward during a task of visual attention. To this, the analysis of the response times, direction of the look through eye tracing and the oscillative brain activity in the alpha band recorded with electroencephalography will be used.
