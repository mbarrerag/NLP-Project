# Anatomical variations in thoracic sympathetic nerves, histological characteristics and associated clinical implications: extended literature review.

It is considered of the utmost importance that the variations of the sympathetic thoracic nervous system reported so far be described and that health scientists have full knowledge of histological characterization and the possible associated clinical implications. In this document, publications on the topic of the last decade were compiled internationally, and an analysis was made of the repercussions in some surgical techniques, such as sympathyectomy, sympathy and ramicotomy. Likewise, from a clinical point of view, the characterization of these anatomical variations allowed the formulation of hypotheses that could explain surgical failures, such as persistent hyperhidrosis, or inadequate responses to it, as they occur in compensatory hyperhidrosis. On the other hand, it allowed a closer approach to a series of clinical situations, in which the common denominator was pain, in such a way that it characterized the nerve pathway by which this nociceptive information is transmitted to the central nervous system, with possible variations, allows a more secure resection of it and thus provides a better quality of life to patients with pain disorders of the upper abdomen.
