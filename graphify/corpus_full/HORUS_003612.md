# ADVANCING CONCRETE PERFORMANCE THROUGH HIGHLY REACTIVE RICE HUSK ASH

The use of active nanopowders, particularly pozzolanic materials, has been considered as a viable solution for addressing durability issues in concrete formulations. These mineral additives can be obtained through various methods, including mining, industrial processes, and the utilization of by-products from natural or agroindustrial activities, such as rice husk ash (RHA). These additions can improve the rheological properties of concrete, enhancing its fluidity and resistance to segregation. They can also boost mechanical strength and extend service life. Furthermore, their incorporation can lead to reduced energy consumption and environmental impact, particularly by diminishing the reliance on cement. This can help mitigate the emission of harmful gases like NOx, CO2, dioxins, and others that contribute to ozone layer depletion. This study examines the synthesis and evaluation of highly reactive rice husk ash (TRHA) as a potential substitute for Portland cement in high-performance concretes and cement pastes. Two types of RHA were utilized as partial replacements for Portland cement (PC) in this investigation: one derived from rice husk incineration (TRHA) and the other obtained through rice husk immersion in acid followed by thermal treatment (ARHA). The results indicate that the latter ash, which is composed mostly of silica, has an amorphous structure, a white color, and exhibits reactivity similar to that of silica fume (SF), a well-known commercial nanopowder used to improve the mechanical and durability properties of concrete mixtures
