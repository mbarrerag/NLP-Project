# FOOD MEMORIA IN INDIGENOUS COMMUNITIES EMBERA CHAMI FOR FOOD SAFETY AND SOVERANIA

Contact with the western culture of the Embera Chamí communities has led to a process of loss of food culture that acts against their security and food sovereignty. Recovering ancestral food, as well as beliefs and practices related to these is a way to contribute to rescuing their identity and building a route to achieve the right to healthy, sufficient and culturally acceptable food. This research identifies the ancestral food and agro-food practices of the Embera Chamí community of the municipalities of Bolívar, El Dovio and Versailles settled in the department of Valle del Cauca (Colombia), in order to contribute to the achievement of their Food Security and Sovereignty. The methodology used is qualitative, with data collection through the development of focal groups, in-depth interviews and observation participating in the indigenous territory of the three municipalities during the months of August to October of the second semester of 2017. These communities share ancestral food such as banana mouth, banana, maize and pira food, with knowledge of the chenke mountain, cider, fernze, irac, irre, jeseraka, harvest of sticks, gran, gran, crab, fish and the animals of the guava mountain
