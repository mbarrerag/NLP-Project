# URBAN SPACE AND THE RIGHT TO URBAN LIFE

Abstract In the city of Manizales, Colombia, the production of urban space is given by dominant patterns that relate to capital, which leads to the commodification and control of space by hegemonic groups. This article reviews the way in which this space is conceived from the knowledge-power relationship, through the analysis of urban policies that regulate the organization of the city and, at the same time, generate inequalities, which leads to a population lacking economic and political power being excluded and marginalized from the city. However, this population develops practices, knowledge and forms of organization that allow them to survive by creating ways of being, being and making different from the normed and programmed visions, which suggests different spaces where excluded inhabitants give daily struggles for existence; struggles that extend to the claim for the right to urban life
