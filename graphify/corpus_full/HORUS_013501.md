# Effects of social isolation on induction of plasticity processes and learning of contextual stimuli associated with nicotine

Social interaction is fundamental to the proper development of the nervous system and the acquisition of survival skills. It has been shown that preventing social interactions during adolescence increases the effects of psychoactive drugs; however, there are few reports on the impact of social isolation on the effects of nicotine. This study assessed the effects of social isolation during adolescence on (1) the accumulation of transcription factor ΔFosB in the prelimbic cortex, the accumbens nucleus and the paraventricular nucleus of the thalamus, (2) the awareness of the locomotor effects of nicotine after repeated administration and (3) the conditioned preference of place induced by different nicotine doses (0.1 or 0.3 mg/kg). The results indicate that isolated animals accumulate less than ΔFosB in the structures of the reward circuit evaluated; the isolation did not affect the processes of the moturous awareness induced by low nicotine dosage, but did observe an effect on the induction of a greater preference by places associated with nicotine. of the transcription factor ΔFosB in the prelimbic cortex, nulus accumbens, and paraventricular nulus of the thalamus, (2) sensitization to the locomotor effects of nicotine after reviewed administration, and (3) conditioned place preference induced by different doses of nicotine (0.1 or 0.3 mg/kg).
