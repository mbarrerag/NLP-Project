# PROMISORIAL PLANTS FOR THE TROPIC SECO IN THE NEAR-TERM NEAR-SWEET

This letter was made in the framework of the project Strengthening of the socio-economic and cultural network of production, harvest, transformation and commercialization of traditional fruits and handicrafts of the near West Antioqueño, led by the research group Conservation and Management of Agroecosystems - CyMA, inter-institutional group of the Colombian Polytechnic Jaime Isaza Cadavid and the National University of Colombia. The near West Antioqueño includes the municipalities of Santafé de Antioquia, San Jerónimo and Sopetrán. This region has been promoted as a tourist power by different departmental governments, with different denominations such as the tourist ring of the west and the route of the sun and the fruit. The holidays that are celebrated in the municipalities of the near West Antioqueño account of the importance that have had the products of the traditional orchards: in Sopetrán the fruit festival, in San Jerónimo is celebrated the party of the sun and the agricultural sector and in Santa Fe de Antioquia the tamarind. The tourist potential is due to the confluence of factors such as the proximity to the city of Medellín, the historical, religious and architectural wealth of Santafé, the architectural landscapes, expressed in different trades characteristic of the region. Tourists, consumers and local people have a fundamental role in the conservation of plant species, culture and traditions, through a solidarity relationship that values the particularities of the territory and makes sustainable consumption of local resources. The booklet initially presents a section to highlight the importance of traditional orchards in the dry forest, and of the community that has developed trades from the cultivation of plants, their processing and marketing, all directly or indirectly linked to tourism. Below, the species found in the traditional orchards are listed in a study carried out by Álvarez et al. in 2013 [1] and supplemented in later works by the authors. Finally, the information on 15 species, prioritized for their importance for the communities and their potential for insertion in the tourism activity of the region. The harvest schedule of the main species is also added. Information on the selected species constitutes an integration of the knowledge obtained from the literature on them, and the knowledge of the communities with which they have worked in this project, especially in the topics related to the use and management of local species, period of harvest and recipes
