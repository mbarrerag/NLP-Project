# MEGAFAUNA: GENERAL CHARACTERIZATION OF INVERTEBRATES ASSOCIATED WITH THE SEABED IN THE DEEP REGION OF THE COLOMBIAN CARIBBEAN

Despite the fact that much of the Colombian territory is submerged, knowledge of the seabed beyond a depth of 1,000 m is limited. Deepwater biodiversity is currently poorly understood in the Caribbean region, as no systematic exploration has been carried out. The greatest limitation for this exploration and characterization is the lack of technological resources capable of reaching depths below 1,000 m. Despite this, the offshore hydrocarbon industry has managed to contribute to deep water exploration (beyond 1,500 m depth). During the exploration phase, these industries carried out several cruises with fishing traps, ROVs, drift camera systems and core samplers that allowed the first observations of benthopelagic ecosystems, soft bottoms and structuring communities associated with cold seeps, at depths between 1,274 m and 3,455 m, in the Colombian Caribbean. Approximately 5,500 photographs, more than nine hours of video, and biological samples reveal a wide diversity of megafauna, with 149 invertebrate species, including 37 of 43 new records for the country's marine biodiversity, eight new records for the great Caribbean, and two new records for the Atlantic Ocean. Additionally, structuring communities associated with cold seeps were recorded. These results represent a fundamental knowledge of the deep-sea ecosystems of Colombia and the starting point for understanding the wide diversity in the deep waters of the Caribbean basin
