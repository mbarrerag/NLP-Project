# Synthesis and characterization of co3O4 nanostructures doped with Ni for application in gas detection.

In this research, the synthesis of Co3O4, Co3-xNixO4 x = 1 to 16% and Co3-xNixO4 + rGO x = 0 and 4% was performed by the techniques sol-gel and hydrothermal analyzing the influence of the temperature from 400 °C to 1000 °C, as well as the effect of the nickel concentration on the morphology and chemical composition of the compound. In turn, nanostructured electrodes of cobalt oxide and cobalt oxide with nickel were manufactured by means of the electrochemical anodisation method. The samples that presented the highest specific surface area were selected as sensors of acetone and ethanol, bactericidal efficiency of E. Coli / S. Aureus, and photocatalytic absorption of methylene blue spectrocopate, ultraviolet-transparent function (V-Vyopt) and evalutive technique of X (FT) were determined by means of the optical properties of the functional Four. angles and a displacement in the Raman bands where peaks decrease intensity and increase their widening with the increase of Ni concentration generated by the difference of ionic radii. The synthesized oxides showed changes in Raman vibratory modes related to defects and distortions in the crystalline net of the material resulting from the increase in temperature and addition of nickel. At 1000 °C the formation of heterostructures composed by Co3O4, NiCo2O4 and CoO possibly result from substitution and temperature. Sortometry analysis indicate that the samples with greater surface area are Co3-xNixO4 x = 4% and Co3-xNixO4 x = 4% + rGO obtained by both techniques. Sensors were prepared from two methods from these samples and were exposed to methanol vapour, ethanol and acetone where a response to these vapors was evidenced by the higher degree of resistance.
