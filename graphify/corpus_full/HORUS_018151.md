# THE PECULIARITIES OF GRAMMATICAL GENDER REPRESENTATION AND PROCESSING IN BILINGUALS: A META-ANALYSIS ON THE CROSS-LINGUISTIC GENDER CONGRUENCY EFFECT

There are two main hypotheses regarding the representation and processing of grammatical gender in bilingual populations: the integrated and the autonomous view (1). Cross-linguistic interactions during the selection of gender values are only expected in the former. Through the study of the so-called gender congruency effect (GCE, a facilitation in the naming or translation of second-language [L2] nouns when their first-language translations have the same gender value in comparison to when they differ), authors have tested both perspectives and obtained controversial results. In this study, we conducted a meta-analysis exploring the GCE of 25 experiments from 11 studies. The role of four variables in the effect was examined, namely: the syntactic structure produced to translate or name the target (¿casa¿ [house] vs. ¿la casa¿ [the house]), the phonological gender transparency of the languages at issue (i.e., the presence of phonological gender cues related to the ending letter of nouns), the degree of L2 proficiency, and task requirements (naming vs. translation). The results show a small but significant highly variable GCE regardless of such variables giving support to a gender-integrated view
