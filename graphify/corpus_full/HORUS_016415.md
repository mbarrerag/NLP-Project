# Comprehensive assessment of soil health in pastoral and silvopastoral systems in the dry tropics

The tropical dry forest (Bs-T), threatened and little known, has had drastic changes in its structure and diversity due to its degradation.This has resulted mainly from deforestation and the establishment of intensive crops and conventional livestock activity.The silvopastoral systems with active density and stability of the soil provide benefits, such as the contribution of litter and nutrients among others.However, little is known about the improvement of the indicators of soil health when establishing the SSPs. The objective of this research is to evaluate the effect of the use and management of the soil on the health of the soil through chemical, physical and biological indicators of soil health under five different arrangements: (i) intensive cultivation of maize (Zea mays) (M), (ii) mixed pasture (Megathyrsus maximum, S+Bentium aristatum) (P), (iii) SSP composed of (M maximum + D. aristatum) + shrubs (Crescentia cute + Leucaena leucocephala) (P+ ag) and (iv) DSP multi-strate (P) composed by pass., presented higher values in the organic matter content of soil, oxidable C-active, Ca, Mg, P, B, CICE in dry season and N-NO3 in rainy season. Thus, it can be concluded that the establishment of SSP improved the value of many of the indicators of soil health compared to what occurs in the past and the monoculture of maize.
