# USES OF THE LAWS OF FREE VIENTES OF 1814 AND 1821 BETWEEN ANTI-IQUE CLEARANCES. EXAMPLES AND INDICATIONS FOR A WORKING HIPOTESS

In the first half of the 19th century, the slave-manumission laws, issued in New Granada in 1814 and 1821, had important effects among the enslaved population. Among them, the immediate use of the regulations by the enslaved in their judicial strategies to claim their freedom or that of their children was included. This article recounts the context in which these manumission laws were issued, and presents a set of particular cases in which the enslaved people made use of these legal discourses to claim their freedom or that of their children. These cases serve not only as an example, but are used as an indication to propose a working hypothesis according to which, despite legal mediation, the enslaved people could appropriate legal discourse and interpret it to redefine the meanings of their freedom in relation to the possibilities of manumission
