# TECHNICAL AND ECONOMIC ASSESSMENT OF ELECTROCHEMICAL PEROXIDATION AS ALTERNATIVE TREATMENT OF A TEXTILE INDUSTRIAL RESIDUAL WATER POLLUTED WITH BLACK COLORANT ACID 194 - PRELIMINARY STUDY

The World Health Organization reported that between 17% and 20% of industrial water pollution is caused by the textile industry. It has been estimated that, in this industry, only 10% and 30% of colourants are discharged through their effluents to the environment because they do not adhere to the fiber [1]. The coloured effluents contain toxic substances that cause water pollution and loss of environmental balance, by increasing the Chemical Demand for Oxygen (DQO), the Biological Demand for Oxygen (DBO5), altering the pH and changing the organic-inorganic chemical content of the body of water. These coloured wastes also cause an acute toxic effect on the aquatic ecosystem due to low penetration of light and oxygen consumption. Since all ecosystems are but interconnected, the alterations to the aquatic environments affect the overall integrity of the entire biosphere having an unpredictable effect on health. In the coffee-growing axis, different textile companies use the black acid colour 194 (NA194) in their dye processes. This optimal allowed to achieve color and DQO removals of 63% and 95%, respectively.
