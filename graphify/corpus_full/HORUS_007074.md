# Use of mitochandrial markers as a tool in the management of trade wildlife in Colombia

The extraction and illegal trade of wildlife constitutes an important driver of biodiversity loss at global and regional level. This represents considerable threats to natural populations and exerts immense pressure on environmental entities charged with effectively managing hundreds of confiscated individuals each year. Although the optimal result is the adequate rehabilitation and release of confiscated individuals into their natural habitat, the lack of reliable information about the origin of the animal and uninformed management decisions could have undesirable effects on natural populations, such as exogamic depression and homogenization of diversity. Consequently, the use of genetic information to infer the most likely origin of confiscated individuals has been highly recommended as a tool in decision-making for management strategies. This study aimed to explore the use of mitocondrial markers as a tool to infer the geographical origin of some of the species of parrots, turtles and snakes most commercialized in Colombia. To achieve this, we initially explored the phylogeographic structure in ten highly commercialized species by analyzing 647 sequences of individuals of known natural populations.
