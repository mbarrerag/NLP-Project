# Zoran Mušič and the visual experience of the body in the Dachau concentration camp

In the concentration camps of World War II, man experienced the radical loss of his body. The strenuous working days, the cold or heat, and chronic hunger, tore apart the material body, while destroying the social body, understood as the embodiment of the customs and daily life proper to the respective places of origin of the prisoners. In such conditions, of the men who were deported, only corpses remained that if they could move... In this scenario, in the Dachau camp, he wanders and looks forward to the Slovenian painter Anton Zoran Mušič, one more deportee, who is determined to draw the state in ruins of those bodies, seeing in them something that could not lose any more, something indestructible that he himself calls essential. From the hand of authors such as Colette Soler, Primo Levi and Maurice Blanchot, this work seeks to approach that essential statement by the painter, analyzing some aspects of his emergence in the concentration camp, and its effect on the drawings he made there
