# DUTY-SEWS AND ARCHIVITES: SIPHILIS IN THE BOGOTA OF PRINCIPLES OF THE 20th Century OVER THE BIOCULTURAL PERSPECTIVE.

Syphilis is one of the public health problems that has affected Bogotá for more than 100 years. This can be studied through the biocultural perspective, which analyzes the impact on the population through biological as social factors that can increase the risks of contagion and death. Although in historical sources syphilis was frequently reported, in the bioarcheological register of the osteological collection of the Central Cemetery of Bogotá its presence was scarce. The above motivated an exercise of historical bioarchaeology that contrasted the data of that collection with the records of syphilis as cause of death in the necrological books from 1900 to 1922. Thus, a sample of 59 individuals with possible lesions associated with syphilis was taken to make a differential diagnosis and a sample of 37,931 records of deaths recorded in the books. It was obtained that only 5 individuals presented probable lesions of treponematosis, while 711 necrological records presented syphilis as a cause of death (1.9%), highlighting that 84% of deaths were of non-adults, that 59.6% of young women, adults and siphilitis patients had a possible cause of death
