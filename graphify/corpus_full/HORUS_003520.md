# FLOWER AND INFLOURSCENCE STRUCTURE AND MORPHOLOGICALITY

The structure and the biological function are strongly related to each other. Likewise, the structure and morphology of plants with flowers is central for the functioning of sexual reproduction, pollination and, in general, for the antechology of angiosperms. The morphological and functional characteristics of the flower (his anteis, floral longevity, type of rewards and supply patterns) are the phenotypic space that is related to the type of pollination (biotic or abiotic) processes, and the type of pollinator. Likewise, the location of flowers in different places of the phenotypic space has allowed them to be classified into floral syndromes; a very useful and discussed concept in the framework of the understanding of the ecological structure and evolution of the plant-pollinizing interaction. In 1994, the study of flowers was central in the advancement of botany and the ecology of plant-polinating and plant-dispersor interactions (thesis, the evolution of the polypolis and the polypolis). The plant-pollinating interaction, as a result of coadaptation, co-evolution, and chance processes. In the functioning of plant-pollinating interaction, the floral structure plays a decisive role in establishing the mechanisms of association between pollinators and plants, affecting the functionality and biological fitness of plants with flowers, and the biotic vectors of their pollen. This chapter focuses on the study of the floral morphology of various species of angiosperms, so that the functional value of the different floral structures in pollinization will be assessed, taking into account the concept of functional floral morphology proposed by Dafni in 1992 (practice 1.1). Since most plants with flowers present flowers in inflorescences, the effect of these structures on pollination is addressed, posing hypotheses beyond flowercentrism.
