# Relationship between empathy and conservationist attitudes in young adults university students.

The current environmental state has generated a wide interest in elucidating the complex relationships that humans have established with ecosystems. This anthropic mediation has had devastating consequences as environmental catastrophes. Likewise, the study of empathicity allows the analysis of relations in the intersubjective and inter-organistic order, among them, the link of human beings with nature. This research establishes the existence of a positive average correlation between empathic levels (specificity) and conservationist attitudes (heterospecigenicity) in young adults, in which 312 students, between 18 and 30 years of age, were involved, to whom two psychometric tests of self-perceptive surveys were applied: the Basic Empathetic Scale and the Conservational Attitude Scale. Subsequent to this, physiological records of behavior were performed based on visual monitoring patterns in Eye-tracker. The results suggest that there is a correlation between both categories and the correlation is determined with a greater degree of affective empathy to pain with emotional conservative attitudes
