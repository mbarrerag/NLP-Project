# Optimizing wind farm portfolios: A risk-mitigation approach to strengthen renewable integration and market resilience

The increasing penetration of renewable sources in the energy market enhances sustainability but also introduces variability, driven by climate dynamics, that can negatively affect stakeholders (private investors, consumers, regulators, etcetera). For firms considering new renewable energy facilities or capacity expansions, a decision tool that optimally selects locations and evaluates them as a portfolio, by jointly assessing expected returns and variability, can generate both financial value and broader societal benefits. This work formulates the location of k prospective wind farms as a portfolio optimization problem, using the coefficient of variation of predicted power outputs as the key criterion. Results show that the two objectives of maximizing average return and minimizing variability are conflicting, leading to a Pareto front of solutions rather than a single dominant choice. When selecting portfolios by minimizing the coefficient of variation, results indicate that: (i) portfolios with at least two farms ( k ≥ 2 ) are preferable, since single farms exhibit the highest risk; (ii) diversification is achieved by choosing geographically distant sites, thereby reducing correlations among outputs; and (iii) (3) complementary analyses are needed to ensure economic and technical feasibility. Overall, even small reductions in variability can have meaningful implications for system stability, reserve requirements, and renewable integration. These findings suggest the need for policy instruments that (i) reward predictability alongside output, (ii) incentivize geographical diversification, and (iii) align investor decisions with system-wide goals for sustainable market resilience
