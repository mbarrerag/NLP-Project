# WAY OF ORIGIN AND TRANSCULTURE: ENGRMINE OR THE SYDNEY OF JOSÉ NIETO (1844)

The following article makes a reading of the first novel by Juan José Nieto, Ingermina or the daughter of Calamar (1845) from a sociocritical perspective. The review seeks to show how the author is inscribed in the genre of the historical novel to evaluate the cultural exchange between the natives of Calamar and the Spanish conquerors and, thus, propose ethical models, from which it can register an identity for its region, Cartagena. It is expected to answer questions about the reasons that lead to Nieto to review the past and the promotion of the region over the country. Based on the concepts of transculturation of Ortiz and Rama, as of historical intention of Ricoeur, it is established that the novel shows a process of mestizoje in which the conquest is shown as a positive process that brought civilizing elements like the Catholic religion. For this, the article is displayed in three parts. In the first part, the regional work, it is established the relationship between the historical and social context and the author's writing. From this, it can be seen that the novel appears motivated by the unstable political situation brought by the Guatemalans of the Supremes
