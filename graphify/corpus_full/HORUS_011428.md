# STRUCTURAL AND FUNCTIONAL AMENDMENTS IN HIPOCAMPAL CELLS ASSOCIATED TO THE INJECTION OF THE FACIAL NERVUM

Different models of peripheral nerve injury in rats have been used to study structural changes that occur in the central nervous system related to sensoriomotor processing. Our research group has recently described a decrease in long-term plasticity in the commissural synapsis CA3-CA1 of the hippocampus, associated with this type of lesion. Added to this, an increase in plasma corticosterone levels, a decrease in the consolidation of spatial memory and an increase in microglial activation in the hippocampus of animals with facial nerve axotomy has been found. In this work, the neuronal morphology of the pyramidal cells of the CA3 and CA1 regions of the hippocampus was analyzed in rats with reversible or irreversible lesion of the facial nerve. To this, a neuronal staining was performed with the Golgi-cox method in injured animals and slaughtered animals at different post-lesion times.
