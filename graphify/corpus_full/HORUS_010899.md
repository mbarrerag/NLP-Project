In porous structure food, the technique used by matrix engineering is vacuum impregnation, which is a non-destructive technology that allows the incorporation of external liquids into the food structures, applying pressure changes during the process (Yanyun Zhao & Xie, 2004). The procedure used in vacuum impregnation consists of subjecting the food initially to sub-mosphere pressures, where the gas (degasification) and even part of the native liquids are removed from the inside of the structure towards the impregnation liquid, generating a possible volumetric deformation in the food matrix, until reaching the equilibrium during the time of this stage. Subsequently, the system is restored to the local barometric pressure, where the greater amount of the liquid is incorporated into the matrix at this stage, due to the pressure changes imposed in the process.
