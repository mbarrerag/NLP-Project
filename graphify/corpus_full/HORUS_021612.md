# Evaluation of the influence of compaction temperature on the physical, chemical and mechanical properties of an Asphaltite of the Capricho del Guaviare.

Asphaltite is a natural mixture that occurs between coarse grains of sand from sandbitisque, impregnated with bitumen distilled naturally and carried to surface by metamorphism. Today in the department of Guaviare (Colombia) it is used by the local community as a construction material for low traffic volumes pathways, so it is studied as an economic and efficient alternative to traditional asphaltic mixtures and adapted to the context of the region. As this material has traditionally been used empirically, this research focuses on the evaluation of the physical, chemical and mechanical properties of a Asphaltite from the department of Guaviare. The use of these materials in other countries has demonstrated its benefit, and therefore it is necessary to study them. In order to evaluate separately the materiality, the granular and bituminous of the asfaltites, this research was determined by a quantitative extraction test with centrifugal properties (with which the granular fraction was determined by a material inspection, two chemical forms, granulometry, and difaction).. Likewise, the influence of temperature on the rheology of asphalt was studied from penetration and dynamic viscosity tests. The rheological curve was constructed by determining the dynamic viscosity with the Brookfield rotational viscosimeter, at temperatures between 60°C and 170°C, where it was possible to conclude that the adequate compaction temperature of this material is 143±2°C. To evaluate the mechanical and volumetric behavior of compacted asphaltite, briquettes were manufactured at 100°C, 143°C and 155°C in rotating compactor, where it is evident that at a temperature of 143°C the material reaches the lowest percentage of air voids (14%). Mechanical tests performed on compacted asphaltite allowed to determine acceptable behavior in terms of stability, flow and susceptibility to water at low transit volumes
