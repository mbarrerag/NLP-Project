# STUDY OF THE ESTERIFICATION REACTION TO PRODUCE PROPYL PROPANOATE USING ION EXCHANGE RESINS AS CATALYST

Solvents are essential ingredients for the manufacture of multiple basic consumer products in homes, such as paints, varnishes, waxes, cleaning and disinfection products, cosmetics, degreasers, among others [1]. Some solvents are highly toxic and contribute to environmental pollution by generating smog when they react with nitrogen oxides. Due to the negative impacts of some solvents on the human health and environment, in recent years, a set of regulations was generated to restrict the usage of highly polluting chemical compounds. In this regard, a variety of chemicals such as esters and alcohols have been identified as alternatives and sustainable substitutes for most of the problematic solvents. For instance, an alternative for the substitution of toluene and xylene is the propyl propanoate since it has similar physicochemical properties and less toxicity. Indeed, propyl propanoate is a non-hazardous solvent, biodegradable in weeks, has good activity as a solvent and low solubility in water [2]. Propyl propanoate is produced by the direct esterification of propionic acid and propanol in the presence of an acid catalyst. Currently, there is limited availability of kinetic information on the system. In fact, only two commercial catalysts have been studied (Amberlyst 46 y Dowex 50W). Accordingly, in this work five commercial catalysts were evaluated to identify one with highest conversion and selectivity. Therefore, in this work a kinetic study of the esterification reaction to produce propyl propanoate was carried out in two main steps. Initially, different catalysts were assessed, among them two homogeneous (Sulfuric acid and Methanesulfonic acid) and three heterogeneous (Amberlyst 15, Amberlyst 46 and Amberlyst 131). Secondly, the catalyst with the best performance was selected to proceed with the kinetic tests. These experiments were performed at different temperatures (343.15 - 393.15 K), catalyst loadings (0.4 - 6.5%), and acid to alcohol molar ratios. According to results, catalyst Amberlyst 131 offers the highest performance, and it was selected to perform the kinetic study. Side products did not appear during the experiments. Experimental results were fitted using a pseudo-homogeneous model based on mole fractions. The obtained parameters adequately represent the experimental data so that they can be used for the design and analysis of propyl propanoate production process
