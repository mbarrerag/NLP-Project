# ARTIFICIAL MOLECULATION FORMED FOR A CIRCULAR CHANGE OF SEMI-CONDUCTORS: STUDY OF ELECTRONIC AND TECHNICAL PROPERTIES

The growth of semiconductor systems by dripping technique allows the manufacture of artificial multi-atomic circular complexes of quantum points. This experimental fact has motivated us to calculate the electronic and thermal properties for an electron confined in an artificial molecule of a two-dimensional nature. This molecule is composed of several discs whose centers are located on a circle. Modeling the system by means of an epicycloid and a hypocycloid, the potential of containment is calculated. Schrödinger’s equation for an electron in the presence of electric and magnetic orthogonal fields, is solved using the technique of finite elements. This methodology allows to consider any number of quantum disks so that, for a very large number of points, the system approaches a uniform section ring. Once the energy spectrum is determined and assuming that the system is in contact with a thermal focus, the corresponding partition function is calculated using Boltzmann statistics. Subsequently, the entropy, heat capacity and internal energy of the system are obtained according to the different geometric parameters that characterize the artificial molecule, as well as to the intensity of the electrical and magnetic fields previously reported results
