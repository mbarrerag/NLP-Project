# Development of construction biomaterials using white rotting fungi Ganoderma lucidum and Pleurotus ostreatus with lignocellulosic residues by solid fermentation

The need to achieve an environmental impact of zero and sustainability has served as a boost for the development of new or improved materials that seek this objective. Hence, different terms arise such as biomaterials, biocompounds, ecocompounds or green compounds that can be defined as compounds that have at least one component that is derived from a biological or natural source. Biologically based building materials are sustainable not only by reducing carbon emissions and the amount of energy contained, but because it provides comfort with less energy use. The use of this type of materials can lead to sustainable development, reducing manufacturing costs and environmental pollution. This proposal seeks to contribute to knowledge in the use of waste and lignocellulosic materials little known and untapproached from the department of Caldas as the grovewood (montanoa quadrangularis Schultz Bipontianus in K. Koch) for the development of building materials, obtained with the help of the cultivation of white rotting fungi Ganoderma lucidum and Pleurotus ostreatus
