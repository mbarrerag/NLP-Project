# REASON: HISTORICAL ELEMENTS TO UNDERSTAND THE CONCEPT AND ITS APPLICATION IN HUMAN SPECIES

The concept of race is commonly assumed as biological origin; however, throughout history both natural sciences and social sciences have determined its definition and use until it is transformed into a concept of a cross-sectional scope to human thought. We then sketch four crucial historical moments in its development.In the Aristotelian metaphysics, there appears a first concept of race used as a differentiating term of human groups associated with a preliminary idea of ancestrality and, possibly, to a classification and hierarchicalization by states of the soul among human beings. In a second moment, the classification concept of Linneo opposes the sense of unity and continuity of nature to mark a differentiating and discontinuous principle as that of species. Linneo uses the theory of humors, of Galenical origin and extended by Arabs, for the classification of variability within our species, which will later be used by the brakeology that introduces a quantifying element in human nature. Darwin in the Origin of Man frames human variability in an evolutionary context. In the twentieth century the concept of gene transforms the classification of species and by man, and lets us see that human differences are not so much more than human differences
