# Expression of biomarkers for effect and exposure to mercury in wild birds in areas affected by alluvial gold mining in Colombia.

The anthropogenic emissions of mercury (Hg) have drastically affected ecosystems, having harmful effects on the health and well-being of wildlife. Birds are a particularly vulnerable group to environmental pollutants, due to their position in the trophic chain, longevity and high bioaccumulation capacity. Hg concentration is usually determined in blood, feathers and eggs, with harmful effects identified from cell biomarkers and tissue. However, few studies have been developed at more specific hierarchical levels such as biochemical and molecular, and it has become necessary to develop diagnostic methodologies that include them and that are non-lethal and easy to implement. Recent findings on molecular mechanisms underlying Hg-mediated toxicity in humans have shown that selenoproteins, especially the oxidative glutathion (GPX) and the ethylity of the multrates (GST) and the multorality of the multrates (TrxR) are important targets in the inhibition of the multrates, thus they are profile as potentials of the multage and multrates.
