# Study of electro-optic response of few-particle systems at non-uniform semiconductor quantum points

In contemporary society, electro-optical devices play a crucial role in the technological development of different areas such as telecommunications, computing, sensory, medicine, and in general any field implements optical/psychic and lighting reading/writing devices [1]. New devices are being implemented thanks to the dizzying advance of the science of semiconductor materials, where it is currently possible to nanostructure monticles or three-dimensional islets embedded in a matrix whose morphology and size can be predetermined and adjusted with nanometric accuracy study [2,3]. Given the nanometric dimensions of these systems, called quantum points (PCs), which are comparable with the wavelength of De Broglie of an electron, they provide the ideal conditions for the emergence of mechanical-quantity phenomena within the scope of their work. The presence of this type of phenomena, all of which are considered to be functional, and all of which are considered to be highly experimental. The importance of self-state calculation is based on the fact that these physical quantities form the basis for describing in detail a different range of phenomena that can be linked with opto-electronic applications. Similar theoretically cut-off studies have been carried out for systems such as rings [16,17], cylinders [14-19], lenses [20], pyramids [7] and other quantum structures [6-11]. However, according to the state of art survey carried out, the number of contributions that devote their study to systems of few particles heavily confined to non-uniform PCs [21] is very small. This type of calculations is extremely complex from a physical-mathematic point of view and for this purpose it is essential to implement some approximations of physics [23-26] supported by experimental reports [27,37,38] and in association with computer tools and the Finite Elements Method [22]. Reports of this project may contribute to understanding the correlation effects between particles for potential applications oriented to the development of optoelectronic devices
