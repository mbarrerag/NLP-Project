# NICLEED RED RED GARIGULES ININFECTED BY THE DENAL RANGE OF PARASITS

The wild fauna is an important source of microorganisms among them blood parasites such as Plasmodium, Haemoproteus, trypanosomes and filarias among others. Its genetic characterization is of great importance since many of them have made hospitable jumps infecting the human and species of economic interest. However, its genetic characterization has been decimated, since birds, amphibians, reptiles and fish have nucleated red blood cells. On the other hand, the cultures for these parasites are limited to Plasmodium or do not exist. This dilutes the amount of DNA of the parasite obtained in processes of extraction of conventional nucleic acids, being a phenomenal challenge at the time of obtaining its genome. Using the animal model of common dove (Columba livia), we standardize a methodology that allows the enrichment of 10,000 times the DNA of the parasite in relation to that of its host, obtaining adequate amounts of permeal separation, with the expected depth to obtain a genome of novo.
