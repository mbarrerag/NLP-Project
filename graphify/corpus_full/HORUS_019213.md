# Inclusion of environmental and mine closure costs in strategic mining planning of a coal deposit.

At present, extractive projects face major sustainability challenges in the territories where they are carried out, traditionally environmental and social variables have not been taken into account in the optimization and sequencing processes of mining plans, away from holistic and low-risk results. Efforts to reduce geological, economic and operational uncertainty have been considerable in recent years, however, few researches are addressing the problems of environmental and social uncertainty that projects may present. This research raises the internalization of costs associated with mine closure, environmental costs and conflict resolution costs, calculated on the basis of equations suggested by several authors in the state of art. It addresses the case base of an open-pit coal mine located in Colombia with pre-established initial conditions and associated costs, calculated after the optimization process. Subsequently, a new case is generated in which these costs are charged in the economic valuation of the mineral by punishing its calorific power. At the end of the study, a sensitivity analysis is made with future projections of the coal price. The results obtained indicate that, internalizing these costs, it is possible to reduce the risk of the company and the economic performance could be better
