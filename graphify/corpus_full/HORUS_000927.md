# Revealing oxygen's influence on ethanol steam reforming: A transient-state operando DRIFT-MS study with Ni-Co catalysts

The influence of oxygen on ethanol steam reforming was investigated using a Ni-Co mixed oxide catalyst with a 1:1 molar ratio, synthesized through the thermal decomposition of a hydrotalcite-like precursor. The surface evolution of the species at 400 °C, both in the absence (SRE) and presence of oxygen (OSRE), was elucidated using transient operando DRIFT-MS. When oxygen was added, the results showed an increase in the selectivity for CO2 with respect to CO and a decrease in CH4 due to the oxidation of the CHx species. These findings were consistent with GC results in the catalytic test. Three distinct carbonate species were identified on the surface at 400 °C: i) bridge (BC), ii) bidentate (C2), and iii) polydentate (PC) with their formation being strongly influenced by the catalyst surface. The presence of O2 notably affected the quantity and stability of these carbonates. Post-reaction characterization, including TGA, revealed the formation of carbon structures, while Raman, TEM, and XRD analyses identified the predominant formation of well-organized multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) due to both tip and base growth mechanisms. Overall, the results suggest that oxygen primarily facilitates the oxidation of CHx species and amorphous carbon, leading to a reduced quantity of disordered carbon and a higher proportion of ordered carbon structures in OSRE compared to SRE
