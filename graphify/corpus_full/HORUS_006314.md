# “Furious, aggressive and manic.” psi practices in the face of violence in Colombia, 1937-1961

This thesis is interested in the form of the practices psi —psychiatry, psychology and psychoanalysis—carried out the political and non-political violence that was raging the country in the mid-20th century. From a closer approach to the intramural and extramural life of the Manicomio de Varones de Sibaté, the study revealed that, outside the asylum, psychiatrists made violence a symptom of different anomalies of the social body and, starting in the 1960s, the inclusion of a multidisciplinary perspective allowed them to consider more carefully the incidence of social factors such as poverty, racism and state repression. On the other hand, within the Manicomio, violence could or could not be considered in the elaboration of individual diagnoses, since for psychiatrists it was one more in the set of symptoms of patients, regardless of the severity of the violent events related to the case. in individual diagnoses, as psychoiatrists saw it as just one group many symptoms of the patients, repercussions of the violent acts related to the case. This leads us to conclude that the Colombian experience differences from foreign experiences, where during turbulent periods, asylums were used to pathologize, isolate, and torture political options
