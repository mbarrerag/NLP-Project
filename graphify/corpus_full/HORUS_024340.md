# CLINICAL CASE: ADENOMA OF PARATIROIDS AND RADIOGUIED CIRUGY

the diagnosis of primary hyperparathyroidism is established when high circulating concentrations of calcium and parathyroid hormone are found. However, there are several presentations of primary hyperparathyroidism, even with inappropriately normal levels of parathyroid hormone. The diagnostic challenge arises when clinical manifestations are little flowery, as described by Albright in 1934 ''are so characteristic of this condition the vague symptoms that are only evident after they have disappeared''.Sifting systems implemented in some high-income countries allow to diagnose primary hyperparathyroidism even in asymptomatic and without signs of disease. The aim: is to emphasize the importance of considering this diagnosis even in the absence of classical metabolic alterations and to present a minimally invasive surgical technique that allows during surgery the functional location of parathyroid adenomas and their treatment
