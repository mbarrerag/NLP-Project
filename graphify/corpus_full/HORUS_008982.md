# STUDY OF CATALYTIC OZONATION AS AN ADVANCED TREATMENT FOR REMOVAL OF PHARMACEUTICALS IN WASTEWATER

The presence of pharmaceuticals in the aqueous environment is a largely studied issue, due to the usual occurrence of these compounds in environmental waters and the risk associated. The main source of pharmaceutical contamination are wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs) discharges since the conventional treatments, such as secondary biological systems, applied by WWTPS are not designed to entirely degrade these compounds [1]. Hence, it is necessary to implement advanced treatments to enhance the elimination of these contaminants and restrict their continuous release into the environment. Catalytic ozonation treatments have demonstrated to be highly effective and in the last few years, have received wide attention since the addition of a catalyst rapidly induces ozone decomposition to generate more active free radicals, which significantly increase the degradation of pollutants [2]. Among heterogeneous catalysts, zeolites have shown good application potential in catalytic ozonation for organic micropollutant removal from water. In this work, the suitability of catalytic ozonation for the elimination of pharmaceutical residues in WWTP has been evaluated, special attention has been paid to the degradation of ibuprofen. The degradation efficiency of the treatments has been studied, but also the possible transformation products generated by these processes. Finally, an LC-MS/MS method has been applied to real and treated EWW to evaluate the presence and degradation of 40 pharmaceuticals
