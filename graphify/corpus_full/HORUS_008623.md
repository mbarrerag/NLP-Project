# Design of Fault Tolerance Embedded Systems using Aproximate Computing Techniques.

Due to the technological scale, the susceptibility of modern systems to radiation effects has increased steadily. Consequently, it has become essential to protect systems against such failures. While these failures, called \textit{soft errors}, can be transient rather than permanent mitigation outcomes, they can alter the performance of the system and cause malfunctions or failures in electronic systems. Researchers have proposed techniques of tolerance to failures that cover various approaches to address this problem. These techniques range from modification of chip materials and manufacturing processes to alternative solutions at design level. These alternatives at design level have called attention to mitigation approaches based on hardware, software or a combination of both, commonly known as hybrid methods. However, many of these techniques are based on redundancy, which imposes a major computational overload. To address this challenge, the Approximate Computing techniques (CA) have called for the particular outcomes as an alternative to reduce the overcosts associated with the mitigation of transient failures. These proposals have shown that the CA can improve efficiency by balancing the system's coverage, the overcosts and the precision of the outcomes.
