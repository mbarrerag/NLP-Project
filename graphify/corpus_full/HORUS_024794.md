# MODEL FOR INCLUSION OF ENVIRONMENTAL COSTS AND MINE CLOSURE IN MINING PLANNING.

In recent decades, numerous projects around the world have been confronted with complex and high-cost socio-environmental problems, increasing the need to create sustainable projects and operations (Bebbington et al., 2008), (Alexandrescu and Baldus, 2016). Additionally, the mining planning process has addressed some of these problems in an erroneous or inappropriate way, particularly the socio-environmental aspects surrounding the design and execution of an operation. Proof of this are the various cases presented in recent years in our country where there are difficulties in the interrelation of project-communities, resulting in stagnation in the start of operations and sometimes the temporary and/or final suspension of projects
