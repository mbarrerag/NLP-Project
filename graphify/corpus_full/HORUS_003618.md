# TACKLING CHALLENGES IN ENERGY CONVERSION, SAVING, AND STORAGE TECHNOLOGIES: A FOCUS ON EQUITY IN ENERGY ACCESS AND TRADITIONAL HOUSING-INSIGHTS FROM MEXICO

This analysis examines the relationship between energy poverty and traditional housing, emphasizing the interconnection that can exacerbate the vulnerability of marginalized communities. The investigation focuses on the challenges of accessing reliable energy services and secure housing, with the aim of identifying the underlying factors that perpetuate these disparities. The proposal of solutions and policies aimed at mitigating inequities is guided by a lens of social justice. Inclusive strategies that promote community engagement and collaborative efforts across sectors are advocated to foster substantive transformation. The research conducted in Oaxaca, Mexico City, and Chiapas adopts a mixed-methods approach to comprehensively examine disparities in energy and housing access across diverse regions and demographic groups within Mexico. The study involves qualitative analysis through semi-structured interviews with representative participants, complemented by quantitative data obtained through surveys and secondary sources. The study emphasizes the significance of inclusive policies that address energy poverty, particularly within marginalized communities. Integrated solutions that incorporate renewable technologies and community participation are identified as effective strategies. Through case studies, distinct challenges and opportunities in each region are highlighted, emphasizing the necessity of tailored approaches. Oaxaca, for instance, necessitates policies that respect cultural practices, while Mexico City grapples with urban inequalities requiring governmental interventions. Chiapas exemplifies successful community-driven initiatives, underscoring the pivotal role of local participation. This research underscores the intricate nature of energy poverty and stresses the significance of sustainable, culturally appropriate solutions that are customized to specific regional contexts in Mexico
