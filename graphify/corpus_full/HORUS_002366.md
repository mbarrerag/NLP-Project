# CHARACTERIZATION TIME CORD CORDING IN TWO NEONATAL UNITS

The time in the clamping of the umbilical cord has been the objective of multiple studies, where advantages have been identified in the late clamping, such as better hematocrit, higher iron levels and lower hospital mortality. To characterize newborns hospitalized in two neonatal units of the city of Bogotá, according to the time of clamping of umbilical cord. A descriptive, cross-sectional, prospective observational study. The information recorded in the maternal and newborn clinical histories attended during the second half of the year 2021 was reviewed. 283 patients were collected, with prevalence of immediate cord clamping of 13.4%, early 24.7%, usual 60.4% and late 1.41%, and a percentage of adherence to the 70.3% rule. More prevalence of mechanical ventilation, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, anaemia, transfusion, intravencular hemorrhage and mortality in the earlier pintinations and a higher prevalence of polycythemia in late pintizations were documented. Greater benefits were found with the greatest cord pincling at 60 seconds of birth
