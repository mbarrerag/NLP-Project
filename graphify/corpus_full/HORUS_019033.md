# Echocardiographic characteristics, relevance and clinical impact of transthoracic echocardiogram in patients with suspected/confirmed coronavirus-19 infection (SARS-CoV2) in the national university hospital in Colombia.

In December 2019 the beginning of the Coronavirus epidemic (SARS-COV2) was documented, with origin in Wuhan, China. In the time of pandemic it is vital to define how appropriate the indication and urgency of an echocardiogram, as well as to determine if there is a clinical impact on the patient. The echocardiographic records of suspected and confirmed cases of SARS-Cov2 were reviewed. Demographic and clinical variables were obtained. The confirmed variables were biochemistry and echocardiographical variables associated with mortality. The most frequent indications of the echocardiogram were obtained according to the 2011 ACS 2020 guidelines and other urgent indications constructed from other scientific societies. The impact was measured if there was change in the behavior. The indications of the echocardiogram and the clinical impact were evaluated by two cardiologists, in case of non-concordar, the third group evaluated these cases. The results were: Of the 149 patients confirmed, the median age was 66 years (RIQ 56-73). The median hospital stay was 6–5%. 79.7% of these patients had a change in behavior. Moreover, when the indications were grouped according to the ASE 2020, 205 (99%) were of high priority, and of these 80% had a change in behavior. The agreement between cardiologists of these high indications (kappa=0.96). For clinical impact there was an important agreement (kappa=0.887) Discussion and conclusions: The clinical characteristics of patients with SARS-Cov2 were similar to those of other published studies. The elevation of different biomarkers is similar to other reported series. The overall mortality and ICU of these patients is considerable, indicating the complexity of the pathology and the management of this entity. The dilatation and/or overload of the right ventricle was the only echocardiographic finding associated with mortality. The clinical impact was greater with the appropriate indications, however, there were also changes in the behavior with inappropriate indications, which may correspond to gaps in the information that are sometimes not fully recorded in the clinical records, as well as specific scenarios not described in the management guides, which shows that the patients's indication may be limited in this group
