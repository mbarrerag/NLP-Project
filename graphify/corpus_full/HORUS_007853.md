# Estimation of poverty indicators for small areas, assuming measurement error in the covariates

The poverty maps play an important role in making public policy and allocating resources to the neediest areas of the country. In many situations, reliable information about poverty in certain geographic regions is often limited due to the lack of coverage of national surveys. For this reason, special methods of estimation must be considered for obtaining estimators with good statistical properties associated with certain geographic regions. An example of these methods is the small area estimation, which in the case of indicators of poverty allows obtaining more precise estimators in areas that have few information. In this study, we propose an estimation methodology for small areas to predict poverty indicators when the independent variables have measurement error, combining the SIMEX procedure to estimate model parameters with the Maximum Likelihood Method for estimating true values of variables with measurement error. Additionally, we used the large integrated household survey conducted by DANE to estimate poverty indicators in each of the localities of the city of Bogot ́a, considering an explanatory variable at level area with measurement error. The proposed methodology proved to be effective in reducing bias and variance in the prediction of poverty indicators when a method for correcting the error is applied and compared with the situation where measurement errors are ignored. These results are important because they have impact on making decisions based on these indicators, which are obtained from reliable information
