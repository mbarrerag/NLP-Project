# PROPOLIS SUPPLEMENTATION EFFECT ON THE EXPRESSION OF GENES ASSOCIATED WITH INTESTINAL HEALTH OF BROILERS

The Problem Intestinal health is a major component in the general health status of birds because the integrity of the gut cells and their adequate function allow the optimal use of nutrients and the increasing resistance to disease. Antibiotics have been used as growth promoters to maintain intestinal health; however, their regulation and banning in different countries led to search alternatives, such as supplementation with products like propolis. Despite the emergence of these alternatives, few is known about the interaction that exists with the intestinal mucosa and the possible effect on the functions of epithelial cells. How we investigated or researched the problem The aim was to assess intestinal integrity in broilers fed with propolis diets by measuring the expression of genes encoding tight junction proteins (TJs) in the intestinal barrier Four hundred fifty broilers were allocated in five groups: group 1 - without antibiotic growth promoter (AGP) nor propolis, group 2 - with AGP, and three groups (3-5) with propolis inclusion from 150 ppm until 900 ppm. The relative quantification of the expression of the zonula occludens (ZO), claudin, and occludin genes was performed by RT-qPCR in jejunum and ileum of broilers of each group at 22 and 42 d. Results Upregulation in the expression of ZO, claudin, and occludin was evidenced in the treatments versus the control group. In the jejunum of the 22 d chicks, the groups with 300 and 450 ppm of inclusion showed an increase in the expression of ZO. In the ileum, the inclusion of 300 ppm of propolis increased the expression of this gene in chickens at the same age. Chicken ileum of 42 d showed an increase in the expression of these genes with a propolis inclusion of 600 ppm. No differences in expression were observed between claudin and occludin in the ileum and jejunum of birds compared to the group supplemented with AGP. Implications / Conclusions This study constitutes a basis for the generation of knowledge related to the response of genes of epithelial integrity to the inclusion of alternative diets for poultry, which may have an effect similar to AGP. The expression of TJs can be used as a marker of intestinal health, and therefore it can be considered a complementary method to monitor certain physiological and pathological changes in the intestinal barrier
