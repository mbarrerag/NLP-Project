# I feel it's a lie: Intuitive origin of credibility judgments about news

Understand how people determine whether the information is true or not, is key to facing the phenomenon of disinformation. In this process, two types of processing, one intuitive and one deliberate, intervene. The theory feelings-as-information proposes that feelings, understood as phenomenological experiences, are used as signals in the formation of judgments. Likewise, numerous researches have proposed interventions based on promoting deliberate processing to improve the detection of false news. The present research sought to determine whether promoting a deliberate processing eliminates the influences of cognitive feelings on credibility judgments. To do this, a pre-registered experiment with factorial design 2 (high and low feelings of accessibility) x 2 (write reasons indicating that the headlines were true and false) between subjects where a total of 128 people participated. The results indicate that promoting a deliberate processing eliminates the influences of the feelings of accessibility but not so determine the effects of primaty and familiarity. It is concluded that even during a deliberate processing there are intuitive influences.
