# Maritimity in the Western Caribbean: practices of territorial appropriation of Creole peoples around the Seaflower Biosphere Reserves and the Nicaraguan Caribbean

In the last ruling of the Hague (21 April 20202) on the border dispute between Nicaragua and Colombia, the International Court "...invites the parties to reach points of agreement through bilateral treaties" (Lastra, 2022). Experts of universities of both countries and others, gathered at the International Seminar _x0093_The Colombo-Nicaragense_x0094_Convened by the Institute of Caribbean Studies of the National University of Colombia Caribbean Headquarters last October, coincide in this bilateral spirit of resolution of the dispute, noting also that in these treaties or bilateral agreements the participation of the Creole people of both nations is fundamental. Appealing to the mental geography of the Creole peoples of the western Caribbean, that is to say, to the memories of the relations built during about two centuries between the Caribbean populations of the Central American coast and the Colombian island Caribbean, through continuous migrations and cultural exchanges that continue today in force (Márquez, 2013), we propose documenting the practices of territorial appropriation of the Creole peoples of the western Caribbean around the reserves of the Seaflower and the Caribbean of Nicaragua (object/s) and the local economies of Colombia.
