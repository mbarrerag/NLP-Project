# LAND: TRANSIT BY IDEAS IN THE OCCIDENTAL CULTURE. (XITE-XIXth TO XIXth MEETING)

The aim of the article was to study the transformations of the concept earth as an object of modern science in the West. Methodologically, the text resorted to the history of ideas with the aim of identifying the transit of its singular representations between the seventeenth and nineteenth centuries. For such a purpose, the analysis developed by some philosophers and historians of science such as Giorgio Agamben, Michel Foucault, François Dagognet, Arthur Lovejoy and Stephen Jay Gould, among others, was selected and compared as sources. From this bibliográfic review the article described processes of knowledge involved in these historical drifts of the idea of science in modernity, for example, that the botanist of a leaf or fruit, deduced the tree; the paleontologist of a tooth or a bone, it deduced the animal's origin and composition; the nosologist of a sign, it gave a pathology; the geologist of a rock, it observed the earth; the geographer of a temporary flock, it obtained the spatial distribution; the filologist of the whole-life of the words found the origin and composition of the culture
