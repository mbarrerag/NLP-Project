# THE NATURE OF BIOLOGICAL INDIVIDUALS RESISTS IN INTERACTION

In classical evolutionary theory, organisms play a leading role by considering the biological entities on which the mechanism of natural selection operates. However, organisms are only one among several entities that make up the hierarchy in which biological systems are organized. This hierarchy results from the evolution of the evolutionary process, which begins with self-replicating sequences of short chains of RNA, is continued with molecular aggregates that interact to regulate their replication in a mutualistic manner, to later result in the diversity of life forms that we know today, some of which depend on the close cooperation of organisms. On this cooperative interaction depends the survival and reproduction of each of the organisms that cooperate, as well as that of the same groups that they make up. These groups of organisms that cooperate can be of the same species, in this case we find different social arrangements, which range from their most modest expression, to which a mother that cares for her progeny, to the other complex societies observed in different species of hymenoptera, birds and mammals, including ours. Cooperation can also be between the organisms of different species, such as the one of the one of the one of the one of the other, and the other., the identity of biological individuals results from the set of interactions between their constituent parts and those they establish with their environment. We could say then that the biological individual acquires meaning in terms of interaction. This argument allows us to propose the hypothesis that possibly the objects of selection are not both individuals as such, but the interactions of which they are part. In these terms, individuals could be understood as by-products of those interactions, as ephemeral concentrations of processes, whose limits in space and time are diffuse. This contextualizes the concept of individual under a system perspective and leads to abandoning the essentialist concept of individuality
