# The altitude as a determinant of the quality of the fruit with emphasis on the Andean tropics of Colombia. A review

ABSTRACT Due to global warming, the high areas of the tropics have gained importance for the planting of fruit trees. The climate in these areas is characterized mainly by having low temperatures and higher solar radiation. A systematic literature review in four bibliographic databases found 22 studies that determined the quality of the fruits evaluated at least two altitudes. At higher altitude the development of the fruit was prolonged and in most cases, a higher fresh weight and size were found, however, the firmness of the fruit decreased. The intensity of the fruit color increased due to the higher radiation in high areas. In most cases, the total soluble solids of juicy fruits were increased with altitude, probably due to the increase in photosynthesis due to the higher solar radiation, while the total acidity did not show a clear trend with the increase of the elevation. With the ascending altitude the content of the antioxidants (mainly phenolic compounds) in the fruits and, especially in their epidermis, as reaction to the increase of ultraviolet light (UV).
