# Protection through patents on the invention of gene therapies in Colombia with a public health focus: CAR-T case study.

The CAR-T gene therapy, an immunotherapy to treat cancer by genetic modification of T cells to attack and destroy cancer cells, has marked a new era in the fight against cancer. However, access to therapy is uncertain due to its technical complexity and high prices. For this reason, the importance of building local capacities in research, development and innovation (R & D & I) that allow to expand in the medium and long term, in terms of supply and price, the availability of therapy. In terms of intellectual property, freedom of operation is essential, as it allows the local industry to make use of technologies without the risk of third party demands. In this regard, the Agreement on Trade-Related Intellectual Property Rights Aspects (TRIPS) establishes flexibilities that can be used by the countries members of the World Trade Organization (WTO) to protect public health when designing their intellectual property regulations, including the formulation of strict patentability requirements and patenting exceptions. Thus, in this work, it seeks to identify whether in the international, Community and national regulatory system applicable intellectual property on pharmaceutical inventions, which exist mechanisms for the development of the protection of pharmaceutical inventions, including the strict patentability and the protection of the protection of the protection of the subject matter of patentability
