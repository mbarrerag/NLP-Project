# The Usonian System. Variations in Frank Lloyd Wright's Moderately Cost Housing

The aim of this research is to examine the series of houses with disabilities designed between 1936 and 1959 and how they were affected by the crisis after 1929. Concerns about minimum housing in the United States were inevitable, seeking to provide a solution that involved economic, constructive and social aspects; considering the architect's perspective to address the issue of affordable housing, as well as the conditioning that these aspects were carried out in the face of the development of this type of projects, especially by reducing the spatial and material elements to the minimum necessary, making housing more efficient. Premises are established from the context to define the priority needs of the period and the characteristics of housing with a moderate cost, which serve to filter and choose the cases of studies; which will then be subjected to analysis variables that investigate structural, compositional, programmatic, technical and spatial components. Finally, a transformation of the projects associated with different changes in the context is established by means of household employment, by means of crisis 19th century and internalization. the object of study which will later be subject to analysis variables that researched structural, compositional, programmatic, technical, and spatial components. Ultimately, a transformation of the projects associated with different changes in the context is established over the course of 30 years, configuring a system of relationships which adapts to each of these changes to respond appropriately to these adverse economic conditions
