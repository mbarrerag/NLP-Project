# A history of commitment to agriculture and knowledge: more than a century of growing knowledge between grooves and letters, in the 111 years of the Faculty of Agrarian Sciences and the 85 years of its magazine

The history of the Faculty of Agrarian Sciences is an economic narrative of collective effort, educational vision and constant contribution to the agricultural development of Colombia. Its roots go back to the creation of the School of Tropical and Veterinary Agriculture, established by Ordinance No 11 of the Departmental Assembly of Antioquia on March 23, 1914. This institution was born from the conviction that agriculture constituted the basis of the genuine progress of the nation, responding to the call to modernize the agricultural methods and train professionals committed to the transformation of the countryside. The official opening of the School was carried out on October 10, 1916, initiating work with 104 students enrolled. This figure soon fell to 84, reflecting the initial difficulties of consolidating a solid and attractive educational offer in a social context where the migration to “white glove” professions was a marked trend. Despite this, the School maintained its firm purpose: to train professionals able to extract from the Colombian soil all its richness through the use of modern agricultural science procedures, as expressed by the journal El Colombiano in its October 7, 1916 edition. The spirit of the School did not delay in translating into student initiatives. The school, however, persisted in its mission of dignifying agricultural work, highlighting its fundamental value for collective progress. The 1930s brought new challenges. In 1930, the school had to change its headquarters, leaving the property of Joaquín Santamaria and making the relevant arrangements for its return. This same year, international experts were incorporated who contributed to strengthening the technical capacities of the school. However, the budgetary difficulties forced in 1931 to replace them by national professionals trained in the institution itself, such as Francisco Luis Gallego and Ramón Mejía Franco. The academic consolidation of the school was reflected in 1934 with its annexation to the University of Antioquia through Ordinance No. 34 of the Departmental Assembly. This incorporation meant an institutional and financial support that allowed the school and the Experimental Station of Tulio Ospina to become more efficient in the context of other programs of the university, enriching its capacity of research and training.
