# Geochemical exploration of endolytic protective pigments in plasters: Astrobiological implications

The geographical position of Colombia in latitudes close to Ecuador has as consequence a constant exposure to ultraviolet radiation throughout the year. This phenomenon, accentuated by altitudes that reach 5200 m.s.n.m in the Eastern Cordillera, makes the country a suitable place to investigate the microbiological distribution in lytic substrates and its possible applications to the field of astrobiology. In regions characterized by extreme environmental conditions, endolytic microorganisms find in the translucent rocks, a refuge that protects them from thermal variations, aridity and intense UV radiation. These rocks, often associated with evaporitical deposits such as plaster, calcite or halita, provide some microorganisms with the opportunity to carry out photosynthetic processes thanks to the presence of the minerals. However, even inside the mineral, these continue exposed to considerable levels of radiation, which they develop a group of pigments that act as a shield against high energy radiations (mainly U.V.). We seek to contribute to the understanding of microbial ecology in lithic substrates, and to promote the development of future research focused on the search for life on the modern surface of Mars
