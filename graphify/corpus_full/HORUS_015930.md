# READING THE START OF THE PHILOSOPHYC RESEARCH OF WITTGENSTEIN

The central purpose of this text is to suggest that there are intimate links between the unconventional style in which Wittgenstein's philosophical investigations are written, some purposes that are pursued in them and certain reactions or changes that may cause them in the person who reads them. It is about the reactions and changes that Wittgenstein deliberately wanted to provoke, as a form of therapy. First, he would have wanted to incite them in himself, to combat certain tendencies of his to philosophize in a certain way that he came to consider compulsive and unhealthy; but also in those who, sharing to some extent those symptoms, read his "observations (or annotations" (Bemerkungen), as he himself called the short paragraphs he wrote. By suggesting these connections, he also tries to show that the style of these philosophical observations is not merely a sumptuous and expendable ornament, or worse, a defect that makes it difficult to understand what Wittgenstein writes, but rather a form of writing that accommodates Bian for the purposes he pursues with such observations
