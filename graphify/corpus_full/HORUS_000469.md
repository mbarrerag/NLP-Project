# Design of a circular business model oriented to the use of the waste of uchuva cape in the Colombian company Ocati

The circular business models are fundamental for the effective implementation of the Circular Economy (EC) at the organizational level, since they allow to transform the way in which the companies create, deliver and capture value, minimizing waste and maximizing resource regeneration. However, the transition to EC is marked by uncertainties and depends both on the specific context of each organization and the expectations and requirements of its stakeholders. Thus, Design Thinking (DT) emerges as a key framework to drive this transition, facilitating its integration through an incremental and focused approach to the stakeholders. Moreover, the uncertainty generated by the innovation processes towards circular models requires that the companies develop experimentation capabilities. In this way, the DT allows to overcome such uncertainty with the focus on the user and the empathy; then, the employees (internal client) increase their openness to the change model and face the risk. Ocati, a company that produces and markets fruit and vegetables, faces the challenge of managing their organic waste from a circular perspective, particularly the uchuva layer. To address this problem, a circular business model oriented to the use of the market (business model) is designed, using the analysis, 6 phases of the market, and vegetables, facing to manage their organic waste. Design Thinking emerges as a crucial framework for driving circular transitions, facilitating the integration of circular practices through an incremental and stakeholders-centered approach. Additionally, the uncertainty inherent innovation processes towards circular models requires that companies develop experimentation capacities. DT helps overcome this uncertainty with its user-centered and empathy-driven approach, allowing employments (internal clients) to increase their opportunity to change and willness to take risks.
