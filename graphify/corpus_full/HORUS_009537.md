# Decrypting the genetic architecture of complex traits in plants

The complex features are those whose inheritance does not follow simple and predictable patterns. They are not governed by a single locus, but are determined by several loci and, moreover, are influenced by the environment. Most of the agronomic traits of interest such as resistance to biotic and abiotic stress, performance, among others, are complex traits, governed by multiple genes throughout the genome. The study of the genetic architecture of complex traits is based on the identification of the number of loci associated with a trait, the individual contribution of each loci to the trait, the heredability and the degree of influence that of the environment on the phenotype. This review provides the most relevant concepts for the study of genetic architecture of complex plant traits. The methodologies that allow to identify the loci and candidate genes, which govern this type of traits such as the QTL mapping by binding and the mapping by association are described. In addition, the incorporation of these loci into predictive strategies of the phenotype such as marker-assisted selection and genomic selection, presenting the benefits and limitations of these approaches are presented.
