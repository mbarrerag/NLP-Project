# The universe in a cube: taxis in three series of houses of John Q. Hejduk

This Thesis of Mastery expands the concept of order, articulated as taxis in the classical tradition and understood as fundamental act of architecture, because the taxis structure an internal order, that is, establishes a logic of organization of elements and spaces in the interior of an architectural work that are conceived within a finite and non-contradictory system; likewise, the taxis define a beginning and an end of the order, translated into the definition of edges of an architectural work. The recognition of the taxis depends on the clear differentiation between interior and exterior. And, finally, the use of the taxis in architecture, the structuring of an internal order and the differentiation of the work of the exterior implies that it is conceived as an autonomous totality; this by means of the study of three series of houses developed by the architect John Q. Hejduk, realized between the years 1954 and 1974, given that in them are inverted the internal borders of order with the entire architectural work; they are empty systems of content. Of equal importance is the introduction of ambiguous definitions between interior and exterior in a single family home.
