# Degradation of pollutants present in oil industry waste water using an ascending flow anaerobic bioreactor (UASB) at laboratory scale.

This research evaluated the removal of two pollutants from oil waste water in a bioreactor UASB and to achieve this, the study was divided into three stages. In the first stage, the main contributors of the toxicity of hydrocarbon production water were determined by a national and international study of its composition. The results showed that aromatic compounds such as toluene and phenol, salinity and drilling fluids such as ethanol contribute greatly to the toxicity of water produced. In the second stage, the stability and efficiency of a bioreactor UASB during the progressive adaptation of anaerobic granular sludge to 17 g/L sodium chloride (NaCl) was evaluated. The results showed that the process was not affected by concentrations of NaCl below 9.0 g/L but subsequently the resistance and stability of the granular sludge was reduced, causing deterioration of the yield. The bioreactor was stabilized with a total DQO removal and DQO soluble concentrations of 58.71% and 75.17%, respectively.
