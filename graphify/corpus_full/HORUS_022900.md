# Criptic, systematic and historical biogeography diversity of the genus Manerebia Staudinger, 1897 (Satyrinae: Pronophilina) in the neotropics.

The butterflies Pronophilina Reuter, is one of the subtris of the Satyrini tribe, are recognized as one of the most diversified butterfly groups in mountainous environments and have high levels of endemism. However, the precise taxonomic determination of species in many genera of Pronophilina has been affected by the cryptic diversity and taxonomically confused taxon as is the case of the genus Manerebia Staudinger. This genus is a group of Andean butterflies, which is distributed from northern Argentina to Venezuela, and has a higher cryptic diversity and a higher phenotypic variation (polymorphisms). Several new species have been described during the last decades, and others still hope to be described. In addition, our analysis emphasizes the importance of using the integrative taxonomy framework for the detection of cryptic diversity in regions such as the Neotropics. We generate the first phylogenetic hypothesis for the genus Manerebia based on data from mitochondrial sequences (IOC) and using phylogenetic tools. Nine clades are proposed for the genus Manerebia along the Central and Northern Andes, being the northern Andes the zone with the greatest richness for the genus. Our analysis allowed us to clarify some of the phylogenetic relations within the genus at the species level. Finally, our study explored the biogeographic history of the genus Manerebia estimating times and rates of diversification of its lineages and using a biogeographic analysis to reconstruct its evolutionary history. Our results allowed us to infer that the time of divergence of Manerebia was between the late Miocene and the Pliocene, and most of the existing lineages had already appeared in the Pleistocene.
