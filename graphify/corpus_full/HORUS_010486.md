# VOCUATION IN CANCER CHILD: REVISION OF SCOPE

Immunosuppression caused by cancer treatment in children represents a high risk for the occurrence of infections that are immunopreventable. For this, the most effective strategy known is vaccination; however, the research is not clear in the recommendations of its administration in this population, so, the objective of this review is to integrate the scientific literature available on vaccination in childhood cancer to identify vaccines, their types, adjustments to vaccination schemes, therapeutic and secondary effects, identify trends in research on vaccination in childhood cancer and generate recommendations for the practice of care of children with cancer in the vaccination area. Searches were carried out in databases such as PubMed and Scopus, among others. Articles published in journals indexed in the English, Spanish and Portuguese languages, between 2016 and 2021 were identified. The results were organized in questions that guide the proposed objectives, where it was shown that the administration of boosted doses is recommended in all vaccines studied, but with immunological tests carried out before their administration. The majority of studies allow neither the necessary time or conditions for their administration are determined
