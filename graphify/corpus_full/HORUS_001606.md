# The aporophobic discourse between tourism and expulsion migration in Medellin and Colombia

In recent years, Colombia, and specifically Medellin, have been opening up more to global dynamics, due to the relative change of the international image they have achieved; going from being spaces highly stigmatized by external visitors to being attractive destinations due to their cultural, natural and historical variety and social transformation. These conditions have allowed the country and Medellin in particular, to participate widely in various manifestations of global mobility. Some of these manifestations are shaped by tourist practices, but also the rapid Venezuelan migration that has taken Colombia as one of its main destinations and transit zones. In this sense, the objective of the article is to compare the discourse that has been created in Colombia and Medellin with respect to tourism and expulsion migration. For this purpose, a qualitative methodology and Critical Discourse Analysis are used as a research method, employing techniques such as interview and content analysis. The main findings show that the differentiation between the discourses of one and the other phenomenon is based on an economic vision, where private interests prevail, which from the institutional discourse seems to respond to common interests. In other words, mobility that is supposed to be aligned with a higher economic income is encouraged and supported, while mobility that seeks to take advantage of local economic resources is informally hindered and stigmatized. In this respect, it is concluded that in the case of Medellin, and Colombia in general, the relationship between tourism and expulsion migration reflects aporophobia rather than xenophobia
