# A Ch'ixi museum is possible: Striving new fabrics for museology

The following text is aweaving of notes, thought, questions, and a few instruments based on practice. These notes stem from reflections and insights draft from sociology, philosophy, and the museumlogical practices of Latin America—knowledge I chose to put into practice in collaboration with the Inga and Kamëntšá Peoples of the region Alto Putumayo, within the framework of a mine of thought (collective thought) to co-develop a comprehensive diagnostic for the Ethnic Community Museum of the Andes. This diagnosis takes into account the form of an ancestral age weaving and unity Inga or Šhombeach Kamëntša—serving as testimony to a process of the power of the poor word around policy of non-people, sacrifice third branch and branch branch branch of industry. by the actions of the present. It is about mingas of thought around the debates emerging from the territory, about programs, projects, and movements that go far beyond the result of the traditional work traditionally assigned to the museums, and that instead constitute a new way of doing museumlogy: a baroque, richly layered museumlogy brit from epistemes, beliefs, traditions, and accountings with different forms of life—heterogenous, multicultural, indigenous. This was the central thought upon which I based the entire methodology of the winning project A Plan for My Museum: Grant for the Development of a Museum Planted on a Comprehensive Diagnostic of the Ethnic Community Museum of the Andes (Sibundoy Valley, Alto Putumayo, sacred Land of the National and Kamënt and the Peoples) suggests that black and white that cohavita, overlaps, but never mixes nor merges, in the distance is a grey, a mestizo that accepts and inhabits the white world and the Indian world. Bringing to collation the ch’ixi as a specific space-time within the world of museums, is to elucidate the political and non passive power of the work of a community museum, is to understand that the museum is part of the category of spaces that shape a revolution of the definitions, a meeting around the memory of the past but the action of the present, mingas of thought around debates that arise in the territory, programs, projects and movements that go beyond the simplistic work of a museum and form a new way of making museum, a mosology bigarada that is born from the mastem, belief, tradition and encounter with the different forms of life, heterogenous, multicultural, indigenous.
