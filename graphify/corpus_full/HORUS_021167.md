# Evaluation of a multi-enzymatic fixed system for the production of GOS from whey

The whey is a waste of high availability and low cost, generated in the dairy industry during cheese production (9kg/kg of cheese). Its high sugar content (40g/L), salts and proteins (5g/L), generates eutrophication and microbiological imbalance by pouring into bodies of water. Thus, efforts have been made to evaluate it, for example, taking advantage of its organic load to generate biomass, produce fermented beverages or obtain other compounds such as lactic acid and biohydrogen. Under this premise, another possible use of whey is the production of Galacto-oligosaccharides (GOS), generated from enzymatic catalysis with beta-galactosidas. In fact, by its prebiotic nature and profit in gastrointestinal health, GOS has generated the interest of the nutraceutical industry. However, the reversible nature of the reactions and enzyme inhibition caused by the secondary products of catalytic activity (glucose and galactose) proposes, the use of each free-use, the present productivity limited to 20-40%. This is, with glucose oxidation from the beginning or after a period of initiation of the transgalactosylation reaction by betagalactosidase enzymes. Finally, the stability and retention of catalytic activity during the operation of the co-immobilized system in multiple reaction cycles will be addressed
