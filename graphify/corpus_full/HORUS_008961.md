# THE CONTINUITY OF PRE-OPERATORY PHARMACOLOGICAL TREATMENT TO THE CONVENING CARE AGREED CARE.

The dynamics of care in health care demand increasingly timely nursing interventions, in the case of surgical procedures, are constant the concerns that arise in the nurse regarding which therapy is continued or suspended and the previous time of suspension of the medications in order to guarantee an adequate preoperative preparation of the patient. It is a fact that patients who are programmed frequently present pathological conditions and demand chronic consumption of medicines, recent studies estimate that about 50% of patients are in this situation. The administration of some medications or their abrupt suspension can generate complications especially of cardiovascular type in the perioperative period. (1) The convenient or convenient care Care(2) is a proposal of innovation in health that aims to generate greater efficiency in the field of health care (3), and the perioperative pharmacological care is a projection of this initiative. Nursing from its role of guidance, educator and integrator has a great field of action and relevance in this scenario, with the implementation of a comprehensive drug reconciliation and the appropriate care. From this reflection, it is proposed as an initial phase of a research in progress to establish an integrated review with the purpose of literature23 3. Medicinal products with suspension conditioned by the subcategories: IECAS, calcium channel blockers, Enoxaparins with low molecular weight, rheumatological. Accurate knowledge of the considerations in preoperative drugs is part of the competence of the nursing professional in the relevant preoperative preparation of a patient; a transversal activity in the different care settings in clinical care; it is imperative to assume the responsibility of making a robust drug reconciliation, ensuring information on pharmacological management in the transitions of care scenarios and identifying the guidelines given by scientific evidence regarding the administration or suspension of medicines in the surgical patient
