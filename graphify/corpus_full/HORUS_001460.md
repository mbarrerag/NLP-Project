# ROLE OF TRUCK STOPS IN URBAN FREIGHT PLANNING USING GPS DATA: A CRITICAL EXAMINATION OF URBAN LOGISTICS IN THE CONTEXT OF CURRENT CHALLENGES

Freight transportation is vital to a country's economy, yet it often results in negative externalities, such as congestion caused by truck stops obstructing sidewalks and roads. Understanding freight activity and addressing these issues requires accurate and cost-effective data. However, many planners depend on expensive data sources or models based on assumptions. Technological advances, such as the Global Positioning System (GPS), offer a valuable alternative by enabling real-time tracking of goods' velocity, location, and time. This study leverages GPS data from truck routes in urban areas of Belgium to develop a methodology for analyzing stop duration, location, and type. By examining correlations between stop duration, location, and frequency per truck, the study identifies factors contributing to conflicts with vehicles and pedestrians. The findings, which address various data collection challenges and focus on urban freight behavior, are increasingly pertinent. They offer practical implications for public policies and initiatives to improve freight transport planning and urban logistics
