# Impact of light-matter coupling on correlations across the classic-quantum boundary in quantum electrodynamics

This thesis begins with an exhaustive introduction, paving the way for the subsequent adaptation of four articles.After this adaptation, the analysis narrows its focus to deepen the nuances of each article. In this analysis, we have explored several interrelated aspects of the interaction between light and matter in quantum systems. Focusing on the role of the different representations of quasiparticles, the quantum dynamics of emitters sets within cavities, the transition from phase of states consistent to quantum correlated states, and finally, the dynamics of polaritonic vortexes using a completely quantum approach. Our findings have provided valuable knowledge about the behavior of these complex quantum systems, as well as their possible applications and future directions in research. We begin with the comparative analysis of the representations of quasiparticles in a system shaped by two quantum points immersed in a cavity. This shows the importance of selecting an appropriate basis for effectively capturing the system and behavior of the quasiparticles. In this contribution we discover that the base of polaritons, consisting of states of photons and excrutons, allows us to capture the physical relationship between the various devices. interaction between quasiparticles and their coupling forces. We identify that the set of emitters achieves coherence slightly before the photonic field, highlighting the subtle interaction between light and matter subsystems. Our findings have also revealed the critical role of quantum correlations in determining the overall behavior of the system, emphasizing the importance of studying the interlinking properties in different regimes. Finally, when analyzing the dynamics of polaritonic vortexes using a completely quantum approach, we have found that quantum overlap between light and matter leads to a richer family of trajectories of the vortex core. Quantum exchange coupling of individual arousals results in interference effects in the trajectories of the vortex nuclei in both components, with more complex structures serving as solid evidence of quantum interlinking between the polaritonic components
