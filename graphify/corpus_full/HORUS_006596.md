# The late incorporation of the provinces of Choco to the Hispanic Monarchy: Indians, settlement and evangelization, 1668-1680

Between the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries the Spanish conquistadors tried to incorporate the provinces of the Chocó to the Hispanic Monarchy. It was a strip of land that stretched between the Andes mountain range and the Pacific Ocean and characterized because it is an extremely humid tropical forest, but rich in deposits earphones. In that region, the Iberians sought to apply their classic model of territorial expansion that consisted in dominating the native societies through mechanisms such as war, the settlement of urban centers, the establishment of a system of encomiendas and mining exploitation. However, after the second half of the seventeenth century, it was evident that in the Chocó this model had failed and it was necessary to implement a new policy of subjugation of the Indian, based this time, on the combination of evangelization, the founding of peoples and the exploitation of gold minerals
