# CONCENTRATION OF SODIO IN SUDOR AND THEIR RELEVANCE FOR THE DETERMINATION OF HYDRATIATION LEVELS

A suitable hydration in the sports and physical activity context is vital for performance and health, since even small losses of body water affect performance. In the literature we find studies that focus on the measurement of hydration status by means of the rate of sweating; however, this is a dynamic process, which varies between individuals according to their physiological and sociodemographic characteristics, and is a measurement that merely focuses on the evaluation of the water content, leaving aside the loss of electrolytes, which is evidenced in the sweat, and how this impact the performance. The literature records that the loss of fluid from 1 to 2 % reflected in the body weight can compromise physiological and cognitive functions, which can cause a reduction in performance; also, an overhydration without the adequate contribution of electrolytes carries hyponatremia, which also affects the sports performance. In some sports methods, it can present high rates of dehydration or severe hyponatremia, which can cause cardiovascular alteration, kidney damage or other pathologies, even reaching death. Until the moment there is no scientific consensus on what is the best way to evaluate the state of sport., demand to remove the athlete from the sporting context, which is why measurements are not carried out directly during sports activity. A common field method for determining lost sodium are regional absorbent patches (Patterson et al., 2000; Baker et al., 2009); with these, the concentration of sodium eliminated from the whole body can be predicted using simple algorithms and pocket gauges (Baker, 2017). Currently, in the Colombian sports field there is no guide or protocol for measuring sodium concentration in sweat that guides professionals, trainers and sportsmen with respect to hydration strategies, with a view to preserving, improving or enhancing their sports performance. This chapter seeks to provide an approximation to the methods of measuring sodium in sweat, as well as to present a proposal for a sodium measurement protocol for its application in the field, developed in the framework of the practice of Master in Sports Physiotherapy and Physical Activity of the National University of Colombia
