# EVALUATION OF BASIC RED 46 REMOVAL ONTO THE NON-CONVENTIONAL ADSORBENT CORN COB THROUGH A STATISTICAL DESIG.

Introduction and methodology The discharge of colored effluents in water sources represents a serious environmental issue. In particular, the presence of BR46 dye is frequently associated with carcinogenic effects. Besides, this pollutant reduces the penetration of sunlight that dramatically affects the photosynthetic processes and, therefore, the aquatic biota. On the other hand, corn cob is an abundant and low cost byagricultural waste in our country with a production of approximately 369,629 metric tons per year, of which most are improperly discarded causing pollution to the surrounding environment. Currently, there are different physical and chemical methodologies for the treatment of colored effluents. Some of these include flocculation, advanced oxidation processes, heterogeneous photocatalysis, ozonation, and filtration. In general, these methods have several difficulties in comparison to the adsorption process since compounds more toxic than the precursor dye can be generated, they tend to be expensive, or their effectiveness on a large scale has not yet been proven. For the above reasons, the use of corn cob as a non-conventional, abundant, and low-cost adsorbent for the removal of BR46 is a relevant topic research. In this context, in this work the most appropriate conditions were determined to achieve an efficient removal of this azo dye. A full 23 factorial design with a central point was implemented to evaluate the effect of adsorbent dosage, contact time and initial concentration; the removal percentage was selected as the response variable. The design and the experimental analysis were made using Statgraphics Centurion XV.II software, version 15.2.06, free evaluation edition. Results and conclusions The variance analysis for the adsorption process is represented by a Pareto chart, in which the factors are arranged in order of their statistical significance. It was found that the most influential factor is the adsorbent dosage, with a positive effect on the removal percentage, followed by the initial dye concentration, and the contact time, with a negative and positive effect, respectively. Furthermore, the interaction between dosage and the initial dye concentration also had a significant positive effect. The other interactions did not have statistically significant effects on the removal percentage. The highest removal percentage of 92.7% was obtained in the treatment under the following conditions, 4.0 h of contact time, 10 mgL-1 of initial dye concentration, and 4.0 gL-1 of corn cob dosage. However, this treatment has no statistical difference with the treatment with the same values in dosage and contact time but with a higher initial concentration of BR46, which achieved similar removal percentages. This indicates that the initial concentration effect is minimized when the other conditions are met. The described results suggest that the studied CC-BR46 system is outlined as a promising alternative that allows to use the waste from different agro-industrial processes in such a way that they benefit the environment
