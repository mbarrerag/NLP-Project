# Exploration of the speciation processes of the parasitic wasps of the genus Chelonus (Microchelonus) Szépliceti, 1908 (Hymenoptera: Braconidae: Cheloninae) in the Galapagos Archipelago, Ecuador.

The Galapagos archipelago is a system of isolated ocean islands that allows the colonization and diversification of the species enhancing adaptive radiation. These processes can lead to morphological differentiation and the formation of species. The genus Chelonus (Microchelonus), present in the Galapagos Islands, has 56 species registered for the Neotropics. The objective of this work is to characterize the taxonomic identity and the degree of morphological differentiation of the species of the genus Chelonus (Microchelonus) present in the Galapagos archipelago in relation to several characteristics of the islands that can influence processes of differentiation and eventual speciation of the group. They were identified, by means of taxonomic keys, 114 specimens. 23 measures of linear morphometry were taken, analysis of main components, of conglomerates, discriminatory function and PerMANOVA. Their degree of variation within each island was characterized. They were determined associations of the morphological differentiation of the populations with age and geographical distance of the islands, by linear regressions.
