# CONTRIBUTING TO THE FORMULATION OF PUBLIC POLICES THAT FOSTER APPLIED CE INNOVATIONS

The innovation spaces arise with physical or digital spaces that provide tools and technologies that strengthen the capacities to innovate and, in this way, catalyze innovation itself.Thanks to the nature of these spaces, it has been said in literature that they can facilitate the creation of eco-innovations that contribute to the creation of circular economy projects. A perfect example of this type of spaces is the Lorraine Fab Living Lab, the result of an academic project carried out in the ENGSI (École National Supérieur en Génie de Systèmes et Innovation), where by means of the use of additive manufacturing - also called 3D printing - recycled plastic is transformed into new products that meet the needs of the community. Taking this type of spaces as inspiration, the aim of this work is to review in a systematic and methodical way in the literature how public policies support or encourage the emergence of innovation spaces with impact in circular economy. These initiatives of living labs or smart cities are eventually driven by large actors such as the European Union countries in which the success of this technology will not be expected to be achieved
