# Bats of the Colombian Caribbean: evaluation of diversity and intraspecific morphological variations in a moisture-burning gradient

Neotropical bats exhibit a wide range of feeding strategies that can be closely associated with different environmental and climatic factors, which translates into its high taxonomic and functional richness. The variation in climatic conditions such as temperature and precipitation is reflected in changes in primary productivity and environmental heterogeneity. All these changes can be gradual in space and result in variation in patterns of bat diversity. The lowlands of the Colombian caribbean present a climate gradient between the arid areas of the north of La Guajira, up to the super humid pluvial areas of the south of the department of Cordoba. This gradient of humidity carries a gradient in environmental conditions such as the structure of the vegetation, so the variation in the conditions along the Caribbean provides different resources that favor the presence of bats with various strategies of forage, so this region can be ideal to study how the bats have been adapted morphologically to different environmental conditions and determine patterns of morphological variation with relation to humidity. Given the above, this the the the the the thesis will seek to answer the questions 1. the richness and composition of the low-life of the species of the region. The results will be validated by means of a repetitive analysis and information on the pure form will be obtained by means of a general analysis of Procrustes, after which the assumptions that allow the statistical analysis to be developed will be tested
