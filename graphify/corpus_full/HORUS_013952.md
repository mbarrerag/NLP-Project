# Evaluation of corrosion of steel, embedded in mortars made from recycled concrete aggregates previously improved by phosphate treatment.

Scientific literature allows to identify that recycled concrete mixtures improve their mechanical performance when recycled concrete aggregates treated with Diamonic Hydrogen Phosphate (DAP) are used, mainly due to the refinement of pores and sealing of cracks of the characteristic recycled aggregates because they present higher porosity and water absorption compared to natural aggregates. However, the effect of DAP treatment on steel corrosion in reinforced concretes is unknown. In this sense, this project proposes to evaluate the durability of mortars made from recycled aggregates improved from DAP phosphate-based soil fertilizer. The proposal is framed in the study of electrochemical, physical and mechanical properties of recycled mortars. In this sense, it is proposed to perform DAP treatment to fine aggregates of recycled concrete. Subsequently, it is proposed to evaluate the resistance to linear polarization to measure the corrosion rate of embedded steel in recycled mortars. In addition to determining the dynamic module, the permeability and mechanical resistance.
