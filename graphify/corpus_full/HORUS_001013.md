# Grouping of elections to increase the preference for savings

This paper investigated how the election group influences the savings choices through two experimental studies in which hypothetical scenarios were used. In the first study, participants were presented with a general case in which they decided how to distribute their income between savings and expenditure. In the second study a more specific scenario was used, focusing on how much participants would save to buy a motorcycle, compared with other personalized transport options. In both studies the hypothesis was that savings would be higher in grouped conditions, especially with nine options grouped against three. The samples included 102 and 41 participants, respectively. In the first study, the results showed the expected effects of the election grouping only among male participants, while no significant differences were found in the overall sample. In the second study, the election grouping resulted in less savings in the grouped conditions, an effect that was also evident among men. These findings suggest that the election grouping can influence on financial decisions of different forms of context and sex of the participants.
