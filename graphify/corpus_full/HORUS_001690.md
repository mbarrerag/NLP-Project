# Naturalize architecture: morphological procedure in 5 bellveder terraced, in the Bogotá Sabana

The relationship between architecture and nature has been previously conceived as a separation between two opposing realities, where the fundamental principle of architecture is defined by the operations of safeguarding and orienting. Complementary to the above, the hypothesis of this research proposes to expand this perspective by adhering as a fundamental notion of architecture the purpose of preserving the link with nature and the environment in which it is located, by means of the formulation of the morphological procedure called Naturalizing the architecture that integrates within the project the qualities of the natural environment that are translated into formal operations. The research is developed from the logical-formal analysis of five Belvederes landed through two aspects. The first aspect, is identified in the analytical process of abstracting qualities specific to a natural site of inclined topography, the Cooperative Los Cerros, Torre C de Las Residencies The Park and the edifico Herrmann, which reveal the naturalization procedure through two aspects. The first aspect, is identified in the abstracting of qualities specific to a natural site of inclined topography translated into morphological procedures that define the project. The second, is evidenced in the process of the Belis and formalized and later. veped through the logical-formal analysis of five terraced belvederes located in the urban-geographical landscape of the Sabana de Bogotá: the Geronia Building, the Christian Foundation for Housing, the Los Cerros Cooperative, Tower C of Residences El Parque, and the Herrmann Building. These buildings revealed the process of Naturalize architecture through two aspects. The first view is identified in the analytical process of abstracting qualities inherent to a natural site with sleeping topography, which are translated into morphological processes that define the project. The second is evidenced in the processes through which the formal structure of the terrace bellvedere is recognized as inseparable and natural part of Bogotá’s urban landscape
