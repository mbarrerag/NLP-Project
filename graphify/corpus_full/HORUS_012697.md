# Bayesian network methodology for decision support in foreign geotechnical engineering

Recent advances in engineering have increased the community’s expectation that civil works will work safely. Occasionally, some of these works fail due to human errors or unpredictable material behavior. Forensic engineering is the branch of forensic science responsible for investigating engineering failures. Scientific methods used by forensic engineering ensure that conclusions on the causes of a failure come from reliable research processes. However, in the case of geotechnical failures, the uncertainty inherent to soil and rock materials, difficulties in collecting evidence and the multiplicity of fault scenarios (hypothesis) pose a challenge to identify the true causes of failure. Consequently, conclusions related to the causes of geotechnical failures sometimes appear arbitrary and biased because they are based mainly on the judgment of experts. Probabilistic Bayesian tools can support decisions on the causes of geotechnical failures. This theory presents a Bayesian hypothesis of support for forensic engineering decisions using two probilistic techniques and the assessment of Bayesian biases using the technical networks
