# POVERTY ESTIMATOR IN SMALL AREAS WITH MEASUREMENT ERROR IN VARIABLES

In many situations, reliable information about poverty in certain geographic regions is often limited due to the lack of coverage of national surveys. Therefore, precise estimators cannot be obtained in the samples of some regions. In these cases, special methods how the estimation small area can be applied. In this study, we propose a small-area estimation methodology to predict poverty indicators when independent variables in the model have measurement errors. We use the SIMEX procedure to estimate model parameters and the maximum likelihood method to estimate the true values of the variable with measurement error. We considered a functional model which assume that variable with measurement error is fixed, that is, its probability distribution is not considered for the estimation of the model parameters. Additionally, we used the household survey conducted by statistical agency of Colombia, to estimate poverty indicators by locality in the city of Bogotá using explanatory variables at the area level and with measurement error. Two statistical models were adjusted each included as independent variable, the rate of child malnutrition and the rate of intrafamily violence. For each model, the incidence of poverty by locality was estimated. The results of this study show the effect of measurement error in the quality of estimators in small area models
