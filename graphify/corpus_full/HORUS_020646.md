# FRACTURES OF INFERING MEMBERS

The proximal end of the tibia has several important anatomical characteristics, it is responsible for articulating with the distal end of the femur, forming part of the knee joint. In its proximal epiphysis are bone structures where structures of clinical importance are inserted, such as the anterior cross ligament and the patellar tendon. Its proximal physiology is the most active of the tibia and it depends on 55% of the longitudinal growth of the bone (1). Added to the distal physiosis of the general femur 65% of the longitudinal growth of the lower extremity (2). It is crucial to take into account the relationships that it has with the distal portion of the popliteal artery, which can be injured in highly displaced metaphysial fractures, and with the peroneal nerve (3)
