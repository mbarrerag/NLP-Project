# Use of physical modelling tools in the design of fire-protection systems for an electric transformer in an underground hydroelectric Power Plant.

The designs of the fire protection systems in the underground generation plants go beyond the purely prescriptive. The above is due to the fact that, in addition to the risks of the operation of the rotating and electrical equipment, there is a difficulty to evaluate the time required for the evacuation of the staff due to the long distances between the access portal and the machine house. Therefore, it is necessary to make designs based on performance with the purpose of decreasing the level of uncertainty left by the prescriptive designs and to verify that the personnel can evacuate properly. In this work of degree of the thermal power transformer with an estimated rate of heat release of 8746 kW, for which five different fire scenarios were analyzed. Initially it was characterized based on the prescriptive method a system of protection and of the risks of fires in the hydroelectric plants, specifically in the case of the use of the smoke transformer and the electrical design. The smoke-reported by the physical simulation tools with those calculated by the prescriptive method for the definition of the smoke extraction system was finally evaluated. The functionality of each of the physical modelling tools to adapt to the proposed scenarios was assessed. FDS allowed simulating the five scenarios; with CFAST it was artificially necessary to adjust the HRR to represent the flood extinction system. In the case of FLUENT it was only possible to simulate, within a computation time similar to FDS and on a personal computer, two scenarios that were presented in a stable state. Although CFAST is the easiest physical modelling tool to use, FDS was confirmed as the standard of use in the fire simulation as the complexity of its mathematical development allows it to better characterise the fire. While FLUENT has the potential to simulate a fire, its application by specialized professionals in the fire safety area is limited to simulations in a stable state within the computation capacity normally available for this type of analysis in the industry
