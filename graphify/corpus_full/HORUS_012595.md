# Paleoecological reconstruction of human impact on the Colombian Subandean forest during the Holocene: Insight from analysis of ecological community structure

La Pochola an archeological site located in Santa Rosa de Cabal (Departament of Risaralda, Colombia), was occupied by humans from the Early Holocene to the Late Holocene, establishing housing sites, however its unknown whether human disturbance occurred at La Pochola. Herein we carried out a paleoecological reconstruction and use ecological community approach (α and β-diversity) in order to document human vegetation disturbance Palynological reconstruction suggests that during the Middle Holocene there was anthropogenic disturbance of the vegetation around the site, increasing α diversity and decreasing β - diversity. Disturbance is associated with the use and management of useful plants, indicated mainly by the presence of maize (Zea mays) pollen and by starch grains recovered from stone tools. Because of we found pollen, starch grains of maize a domesticated plant on the palynological, and archeological records we suggest that human population during Middle Holocene employed crops in this region of Cauca
