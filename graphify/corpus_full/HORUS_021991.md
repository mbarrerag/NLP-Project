# Development of oral mucopenetrant systems as a strategy to increase the bioavailability of routine flavonoid in a Physalis peruviana calcium extract.

Physalis peruviana L., is a plant widely cultivated in the South American Andes with medicinal properties. Its calyx are used in traditional medicine for its properties as anticancer, antimicrobial, antipyretic, diuretic and antiinflammatory. Rutin, the most important flavonoid extract of P. peruvviana chalices has been used in the treatment of various diseases. However, the greatest limitation associated with routine is its low oral bioavailability, caused mainly by its instability, low aqueous solubility and limited mucopetration. The objective of this research was to develop two mucopenetrant systems (autoemulsifiable system and polymeric microparticles) in order to increase the oral bioavailability of the routine present in an extract of P. peruvviana. Initially and as part of the pre-formulation of the systems, the characterization of the polyclides extract was carried out. The strategy to increase the mucopenetrative of the microparticles was carried out using Pluronic F127 while for the autoemulsifiable system was the incorporation of the copolymer PDMSHEPMS in the formulation. It was observed that the autoemulsifiable system is more promising for the extract of P. peruvviana chalices as it allowed for a greater mucopenetration, in addition to its preparation is simpler and more cost-effective with respect to the microparticles. The incorporation of PDMSHEPMS in the autoemulsifiable system increased the mucopenetration of routine 1,9 times, being a novel and simple strategy to favor the mucopenetration of this type of systems.
