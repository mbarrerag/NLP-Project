# COLOMBIA HYDROLOGY. WATER CYCLE IN A COMPLETE GEOGRAPHE

The book synthesizes results of three decades of research carried out by Colombia on hydrology, climatology, atmospheric and oceanic sciences, water resources, climate change, deforestation and the environment in Colombia and the American tropics. The book advances in the study, understanding, quantification and prognosis of the hydrological processes of Colombia, strongly interwoven with its climatology, geomorphology, biogeochemical ecology, and with various dynamic and thermodynamic mechanisms of atmospheric and oceanic circulation. Chapter 2 reviews the global and continental contexts that frame the hydrology of Colombia. It discusses relevant ideas of the planet's climate system, its feedbacks and scales, and its spatial-temporal variability. That chapter studies the context and significance of the natural variability of the Earth's climate and addresses the current role of global warming and global environmental change, caused by human actions. In addition, such chapter studies the long term average balances of water and energy of the planet, and examines its latitudinal and on different scales of time, and discusses the important role of clouds in such dynamics. This is the origin of hurricanes on the North Tropical Atlantic and the Caribbean Sea. Chapter 4 quantifies the long-term surface water balance, anywhere in the country’s hydrographic network. To do this, a digital model of the terrain is used on which the network of channels is traced using modern computational mapping methodologies. For the estimation of the long-term water balance, annual average rainfall fields (maps), real and potential evapotranspiration, and runoff, at a spatial resolution of 5 km are estimated.
