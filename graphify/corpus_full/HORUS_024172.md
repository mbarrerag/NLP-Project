# Impact of resident participation and its role in clinical outcomes of patients undergoing cholecystectomy in a university hospital

Colecistectomy is one of the most performed surgical procedures worldwide, so that their learning is increasingly necessary for physicians resident in training, but without compromising the safety of patients. The objective of this study was to determine the impact of the participation of resident physicians in the main clinical outcomes of cholecystectomy. A prospective cohort study was conducted, which included patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy, from June 2019 to July 2020. Statistical analysis was carried out to describe frequency, central tendency, dispersion and bivariate analysis measures for outcomes of interest. 482 patients were included who underwent cholecystectomy, 475 of them via laparoscopic. 62.5% were women and 76.2% were performed of urgent character. In 96% of the procedures, the participation of a resident was counted as a co-operative physician. In the bivariate analysis, no statistically significant difference was found between the participation of the resident and a negative impact on the clinical outcomes of the relevant variables
