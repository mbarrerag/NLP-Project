# THE MIDDLE MAGDALANA, BETWEEN EXTRACTIVIST ENGINE, CONFLICT AREA AND BUILDING A TERRITORIAL IDENTITY

For the Colombian State, the Magdalena Medio does not exist; it is an area within the confines of five departments (Antioquia, Bolívar, Boyacá, Santander and César), formed by a number of municipalities that vary according to institutions, that delimit it in different ways. The logic that prevailed in the conformation of the internal political-administrative borders of the country gave the Magdalena River a divisive role (as an interdepartmental limit), ignoring its articulating function for the inhabitants and the coastal communities.The construction of a territorial identity proper to the Magdalena Medio is nevertheless a recent and unfinished process.From a naturalistic geography, Vergara and Velasco at the beginning of the 20th century does not mention the particularity of the middle channel of the Magdalena and only identifies the raudals of Honda as a boundary between the Alto and the Bajo Magdalena (Vergara and Velasco, 1901, p. 361). In purely hydrographic terms, the middle valley of the Magdalena begins to be mentioned in the middle of the 20th century.
