# Subjective processes of doctoral students in the context of academic productivism

In this thesis, together with three doctoral students, I carry out an analytical and interpretive approach to their subjective processes in relation to their academic life and academic productivism.First we contextualize how doctoral training has developed in the world, Latin America and Colombia, from sociohistorical, legal, institutional, political, ethical and demographic perspectives.Next, we explain how academic productivism is expressed, conflicted and resisted in the world, the Global South and Colombia.Then, based on their life stories, we explore the problems they encountered in their academic life and the ways in which they assumed them through their feelings, practices and representations, as well as the dynamism between inventions, adaptations and iterations that make up their subjective processes.Finally, we propose some criteria for living a good life in the academy, coming from the values ​​of artisanal activity, from movements in favor of enjoyment;of caring for oneself, others and the world, and analytical mediation.In this way we contribute perspectives to the social sciences and humanities on academic life, subjective processes in
