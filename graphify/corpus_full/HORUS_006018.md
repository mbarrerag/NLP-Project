# Mimosa púdica, a tool for teaching biology in an Embera Katio community

In this thesis, an educational strategy was designed, applied and evaluated in a cultural context to teach the biology of the plant Mimosa púdica, known for its medicinal properties to treat some ailments and diseases in the Reguardo Gito Dokabu, Municipality of Pueblo Rico, Risaralda. Initially, semi-structured interviews were conducted to students of the indigenous educational institution Dachi Dada Kera in the dialect Embera Katio to know the uses of medicinal plants. It was consulted in the literature to identify the places of the world where the plant is used in cultural and ancestral contexts, in addition to its biological use and its movement. Methodologically, the design and implementation of an educational material that includes work guides, practical laboratories and field outputs and a taxonomic classification of the plant was carried out, with the participation of the student community, teachers and the Jaiban. The results of the evaluation showed that the method generated greater motivation and more significant learnings for students when integrating their cosmovision with the biological and scientific aspects of the Mimosa púdica plant. The activities consisted of 3 learning and a degree and a tenth activity, applied. worldwide cultural and ancestral use of the plant, in addition to its biological use and movement. Methodologically, the design and implementation of an educational material including work guides, practical laboratories, and field trips were proposed, leading to a taxonomy classification of the plant with the participation of students, teachers, and the Jaibana. The evaluation results showed that the method designed generated great motivation and more significant learning for students by integrating their worldview with the biological and scientific aspects of the Mimosa púdica plant. The activities shared 3 learning guides and a final activity, applied to 19th-grade students. A mixed approach with sequential exploratory design (DEXPLOS) was used to analyse the results
