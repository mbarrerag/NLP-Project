In this article we analyze the plausibility of the standard hypothesis about the social and cultural transformations that the economy of illicit crops has brought to the peasant sectors that share in it. According to this hypothesis, coca cultivation, assimilated to a drug-trafficking activity, leads to the search for easy money, waste and consumerism, individualism and the culture of illegality. Based on broad quantitative and qualitative evidence, we maintain that the standard hypothesis is fundamentally wrong. We present the reasons and mechanisms that lead us to such a conclusion
