# Lightly wobbly, tremebundous wak'as: Deep Causes of Terror in the Great Andean Revolt (1780 - 1783)

In this extensive essay, a historically unpublished interpretation is advanced on the cultural forces that determined the uprising and violence of the Andean indigenous population during the Great Revuelta from 1780 to 1783. (It is emphasized that it was a revolt and not a rebellion, based on the fundamental meaning that implies the concept of return or kuti for the Andean Indocampesin societies.) To this end, a general theory of terror as a force is produced, a fact that has been largely neglected until now from the social theory. From this, it is sought to demonstrate that the Great Revuelta was determined by a singular form of terror, as it is the case of the destructive forces exercised by the wak’a.
