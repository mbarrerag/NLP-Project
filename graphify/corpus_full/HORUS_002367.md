# RELATIONSHIPS BETWEEN FIRE INCIDENCE AND VEGETATION DYNAMICS IN CHAPADA DAS MESAS NATIONAL PARK, MARANHÃO - BRAZIL: AN APPROACH FROM PHENOLOGICAL METRICS DERIVED FROM REMOTE SENSING

The Cerrado is one of the main Brazilian biomes, being considered one of the most important biodiversity hotspots on the planet.Although fire plays a very important role in the functioning of these ecosystems, recent anthropogenic changes in natural burning regimes represent a significant threat to their conservation.This study seeks to determine the effects of fires on the different phytophysiognomies of the Cerrado, using data from the NDVI historical series derived from MODIS between 2000 and 2018, in the Chapada das Mesas National Park (PNCM) region.Initially, a classification of plant phytophysiognomies in the area was generated through the analysis of high spatial resolution images (Dove PlanetScope), using the random forest algorithm.The fire history (between 2000-2018) of the area was obtained based on the time series of Landsat images.The NDVI images were processed in the TIMESAT program, generating metrics related to the vegetation's phenological cycle (start date, peak, end of the growth period, duration and maximum productivity of the cycle).It was identified that the fires have an impact on the phenological cycles of the vegetation, changing the start and end dates of the cycle and maximum NDVI levels of the vegetation.The results highlight the potential of using data from remote sensors to use the vegetative phenology of plants as an indicator to evaluate the effects of fire on vegetation dynamics, generating information that can contribute to better management and monitoring of fires in the PNCM, being one of the riches of the cerrado biome existing in the State of Maranhão
