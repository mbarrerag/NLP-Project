# Resocialization in contexts of cultural diversity. Approaching punitive practices of the San Lorenzo Indigenous Reserve.

Indigenous peoples within their jurisdictional powers to punish have resorted to deprivation of liberty as one of the punishment devices, which may occur either in State prisons or in harmonization centres. Seconds are spaces constructed from the uses, values and customs that they hold as ethnically differentiated groups; and therefore focus on the restoration of the offending indigenous person in situations of greater freedom and relationship with the community, avoiding his isolation through total confinement. However, the State prison continues to be an option for the offender indigenous person of conduct that seriously undermines the harmony of the communities. Indigenous people who are sent to the State prison face a context that does not offer dignified living conditions leading to the loss of their cultural identity. In this sense, the Constitutional Court has built, based on the provisions of liberal criminal law, the concept of ethnically differentiated resocialization that operates as a limit to the sanctioning power of indigenous peoples. Accordingly, this investigation analyses the influence of liberal criminal law on the sanctions of indigenous communities, the judicial emancipation that has been achieved by the management of the centres of ethnically differentiated and ethnically differentiated approach
