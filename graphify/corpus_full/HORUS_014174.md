# Constitution and society: regulatory debates on care and AI

In the framework of the research lines _x0093_democracia, constitución y derechos fundamentales_x0094_ and _x0093_contemporary constitutional law_x0094_, the Research Group Constitutional Law and Human Rights will focus on developing the sublines: i) liberal democracy in the digital age, ii) regulatory framework and AI; iii) and right to care: from hope to regulation. Thus, this item will briefly describe the conceptual framework, the problematization of which the project starts, the objectives set out in function of the latter and its methodology. In the form of a conceptual framework, with respect to the field of the AI, for greater clarity, are defined: (i) algorithms as sequences of well defined and finite steps that allow to solve a problem; (ii) AI as an advanced system that uses algorithms and models used for the identification of trends (Liang, 2019); (iii) and digital environment as the information space in networks and the Internet, which is generated by the interactions of the Internet through the information and communication technologies, In this context, in which the two questions arise (i) This will allow a critical analysis of the doctrinal and jurisprudential flows on freedom of expression in digital environments, and thus will formulate a critical position of its own, with emphasis on the Inter-American System of Human Rights. However, with regard to the last line of research, it is necessary to note that care supports welfare and social reproduction. This makes them a fundamental component of the economy and culture of our contemporary societies. Moreover, they are embodied in several international instruments such as a right of children, adolescents and older persons, among others. However, despite the ruling T-583 of 2023, Colombia remains in debt to recognize and regulate them comprehensively as human rights. The gender and economics of care line aims to promote a broad academic conversation around the construction of an adequate dogmatic right to care, which guides the regulation and implementation of the National System of Care. To this, the proposal feeds from the information collected by the Gender Seeder and Economics of Care. Thus, a qualitative and theoretical methodology will be used to address the right to care and the national care system
