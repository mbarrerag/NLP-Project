# High-quality genome assembly and comparative genomics of Pseudocercospora ulei GCL012, the causal agent of the South American leaf light (SALB) in natural rubber tree Hevea brasiliensis: Towards the prediction of molecular components associated with its pathogenicity and virulence.

Pseudocercospora ulei is the causative agent of the South American mal of rubber leaves (SALB), the main threat of Hevea brasiliensis, a native species of the Amazon that represents the commercial source of natural rubber. H. brasiliensis cultivation is an important economic alternative for Latin American countries; however, the strategies of control of the SALB disease are inefficient due to the difficulty of traditional phytoimprovement in this perennial species and also to the lack of genetic data on this fungus. Our main objective was to understand the molecular mechanisms governing the basic biology and the process of pathogenicity of P. ulei. For this, we sequence, assemble and note its genome through a sequence of complete genome shotgun using long and short readings. We construct the largest functional I. ulez functionality of the Mycospharelaceae family with 98,3 Mbps which comprises 214 scaffolds with a value of 2.8 Mbps and a BUSCOGN integrity of 97.5%.
