# CONGENITAL SYNPHILIS CONIRMED BY PCR AS A RESULT OF TREATMENT FAILURE FOR SYPHILIS IN PREGNANCY. CASE REPORT

ABSTRACT Introduction. Congenital syphilis is an important public health problem and to prevent it it it is necessary to diagnose and treat gestational syphilis early. In this case the pregnant woman received the treatment of choice (benzatinic penicillin), but this did not prevent fetal infection. Presentation of the case. Newly born male, child of a mother with negative serology for human and positive immunodeficiency virus for gestational syphilis diagnosed at week 21 (VDRL test with 1:4 dilution and positive rapid treponemic test) and treated with three doses of 2 400 000 IU of benzatinic penicillin. In childbirth, the mother presented VDRL with dilution 1:1 and the newborn was diagnosed with congenital syphilis by presenting VDRL with dilution 1:4, rapid positive treponemic test, high levels of aspartate aminotransferase, hypostenuria, proteinuria, haematuria and leucocyturia, conditions that were resolved after receiving treatment with crystalline penicillin during 10 days.
