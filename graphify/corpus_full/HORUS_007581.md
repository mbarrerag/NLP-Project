# Contribution to the study of fixed oil of the fruit of the umari (Poraqueiba sericea Tul.) as a protector of the skin barrier in cosmetic emulsions.

Colombia has a wide variety of plant species, which are beginning to be investigated [1]. To a large extent, the knowledge of this biodiversity is known by local and indigenous communities that have made use of it for different applications, whether in traditional medicine, in the preparation of typical dishes, in accessories useful for hunting or even in the topical application for cosmetic purposes [2]. Among these plant species of Colombian diversity, the majority is found in the Amazon region where they live more than half of the known species in the planet [2]. One of the species of interest corresponds to Poraqueiba sericea Tul., of the family Metteniusaceae, whose common name is umari, guacurí, nemona or n+cao communities depending on the species in which their cultivation takes place. This species is sown very frequently in the chagras of indigenous communities that live in the Amazon, especially from the communities of the Uitoto and Muinane [2]. The species in its entirety generates great interest because of its seed is extracted a medical juice, its dry leaves are used as a traditional product.
