# TSLP levels and disease control in the patient with asthma diagnosis, systematic review and meta-analysis.

Asthma is a heterogeneous disease, characterized by chronic inflammation of the airway, with variable respiratory symptoms related to limitation to air flow. In asthma with type 2 inflammation, epithelial cells stimulate dendritic cells and other cellular groups such as innate lymphoid cells (CLI), by means of cytokines called “alarminas”, which polarize the immune response to this type of inflammation in the lung, one of these cytokines is the study of the thymic stroma (TSLP) by its acronyms in English, which is involved in the physiopathology of asthma, It has been evidenced in several studies that high levels of this cytokine are related to poor disease control. To evaluate the levels of the TSP and disease control in the patient with asthma diagnosis. Methods of search: A search with a complete cut-off date for 6 July 2020, in the databases of this cytokine/Pubmed, EMBASE, CENTRAL, LILACS, CLINS and Clinical trials included.gov and grey. LTFS in patients with asthma compared to controls (2.76 MDS 95% CI 1.99-3.53), and higher levels in patients with moderate-severe asthma compared to mild asthma (0.81 MDS 95% CI 0.58-1.04). Negative correlation was found between the levels of LTSS and FEV1. Based on the findings of the studies included in this review, it is considered that there is sufficient evidence to affirm that the levels of LTSS are higher in patients with asthma and that these are increased according to the severity of the disease.
