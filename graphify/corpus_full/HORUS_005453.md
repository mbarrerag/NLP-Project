# FORAMINIFERAL COMMUNITIES IN METHANE SEEP ENVIRONMENTS: INSIGHTS FROM THE SOUT CARIBBEAN SINÚ DEFORMATION FRONT

Investigations into foraminifera associated with methane seeps in continental shelf environments, particularly in tropical latitudes, have been limited. In the context of the South Caribbean Sinú Deformation Front, characterized by fluid seeps and mud diapirs, we conducted a comprehensive study on foraminiferal communities. The research focused on a methane seep field located on the external shelf, spanning depths from 47 to 166 meters. Our study encompassed the collection of 18 surface samples and a series of cores (piston and box). The sediments in this region vary from mud-dominated areas near the continental margin to muddy sands at intermediate locations and sandy muds at the shelf edge. In each sedimentary regime, a nucleus core was selected (cores MSH-02, MSH-10, and MSH-21). Our analysis involved the separation and identification of benthic foraminifera for each sample (surface or cores). Our findings revealed a reduction in foraminiferal abundance in the muddier stations. Surface samples were predominantly characterized by foraminifera with hyaline walls, with prominent species such as Cibicidoides wuellestorfi, Quinqueloculina candeiana, Reusella spinulosa, Discorbinella betheloti, and Elphidium excavatum. Shannon and Simpson indices indicated high diversity across all sampled stations. Within the sediment cores, we observed a sharp decline in the abundance of calcareous foraminifera, coinciding with an increase in mud content and the prevalence of agglutinated foraminifera. Core MSH-02 exhibited an abundance of ostracods, signs of pyritization, and the presence of ancient species suggesting ascent of deep sediments. Cores MSH-10 and MSH-21 revealed remnants of bacterial algae below 20 cm depth, while core MSH-21 displayed a distinct nodular layer at 50 cm depth. Furthermore, foraminiferal shells in both surface and deep samples exhibited authigenic carbonate overgrowths. Ongoing dating and geochemical analyses are expected to provide further insights into the dynamics of this methane seep ecosystem and the biogeochemical processes within the sedimentary column. This research advances our understanding of foraminiferal responses to methane seep environments and contributes to a comprehensive interpretation of this unique filtration zone
