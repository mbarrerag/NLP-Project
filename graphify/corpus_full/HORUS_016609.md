# OF BASIC RENT AS CONTINENCE TO BASIC RENT AS A RIGHT

In view of the initiative that has been proposed to adopt a basic emergency income, given the impact that the pandemic of the COVID-19 has had on the most vulnerable sectors of the population, this work aims to lead the re-exition and discussion on basic income, beyond its contingency properties, towards an argument that transcends this framework to place it in a more comprehensive perspective, such as the prism of a theory of justice and human rights. From the claim and postulate of human dignity as a sine qua non condition of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the understanding of citizenship as the proper substrate of modernity, the idea of basic income would be based on the provision of the material conditions (Raventós, 2007) for the exercise of citizenship, the effective enjoyment of human rights and the freedom of persons. It would be part of one of the three aspects established by T. H. Marshall on the concept of citizenship (1950), that of social citizenship which, combined with civil citizenship and political citizenship, would enable people to fully exercise their rights, and to establish a basic income as a universal income
