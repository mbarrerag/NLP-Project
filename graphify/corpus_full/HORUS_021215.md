# Design of amphifylic polymer systems modified with cyclodextrins sensitive to stimuli and evaluation of their toxicity and potential as drug vehicles

In order to mitigate the disadvantages that limit the clinical use of substances with pharmacological activity against cancer and other diseases, the use of nanostructured vehicles such as polymeric nanoparticles (NP) has allowed the use of new therapeutic agents, decreased the toxic effects of conventional drugs used in chemotherapy and increased the selectivity in the delivery of these.[1] These polymeric materials are characterized by low toxicity, high biocompatibility and being able to assemble themselves in an aqueous environment, which confers the ability to encapsulate drugs, favouring their solubilisation and increasing their chemical stability. [2] The design of systems for the controlled release of drugs sensitive to chemical stimuli, takes advantage of the particularities of cancer cells such as their accelerated metabolism leads to the generation of extra and intracellular microenvironments different from those of a normal cell, characterised by lower pH and increased concentration of _active oxygen species (EROs), among others.
