# Effect of intake of a drink with glycerol and sodium on hydration state, time to exhaustion and maximum aerobic potency of Colombian endurance cyclists

Dehydration is a critical factor that can significantly compromise aerobic performance, so ensuring optimal hydration becomes a fundamental strategy. This can be achieved by the intake of drinks designed to compensate for losses of liquids and electrolytes caused by sweat. Alternatively, the consumption of glycerol has shown a hydrating and even hyperhydrative potential, which makes it an attractive option for athletes. In this double blind cross-sectional clinical trial, Colombian cyclists were included, who were initially characterised by an incremental test using ergoespirometer. During the test days, participants consumed three types of beverages: glycerol (GLI), sodium glycerol (GLINA) and placebo (PLB). Subsequently, they performed a continuous pedaling phase for one hour, remaining at their first ventilatory threshold, followed by a cronoscale in the field equipped with a portable ergoespirometer. GLINA seems to optimize the use of muscle glycogen in high intensities; and although the results in hydration variables were not significant, the GLINA drink appears to have the potential to reduce sweating, the specific gravity of the urine and the sodium losses by sweat. These findings suggest that the combination of glycerol with sodium could be beneficial both for hydration and for performance in resistance sports. .
