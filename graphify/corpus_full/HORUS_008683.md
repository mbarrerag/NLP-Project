# Friedrich List and the national political economy

This thesis aims to present and analyze the thought of F. List, passing through its Renaissance mercantilistic origins, its configuration from his exile in the USA in 1825, until his work of maturity, The National System of Political Economy (1841). Likewise, I explore his influences both in the USA and Germany, passing through Japan, China, South Korea and Latin America, which became countries with a great economic development, excepting the latter, under a process of industrialization oriented by a developmental state. In the first two chapters I address the analysis of the works of List, Petition (1827), Outlines (1827), and National System (1841), and Land System (1842); in chapter 3, the economic stages; in chapter 4, the influences of List, both on its critics, Marx, for example, and on the thinking of modern economists. In this sense it can be drawn an intellectual genetic line between the work of List, the German historical school, and the German-American institutional school. In the last chapter 5 studies the contributions of Prebisch and the less-supporting model.
