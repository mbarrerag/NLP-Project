# It is said to walk on men as on horses. Images of freighters of men in the 19th century (1801-1853)

This thesis examines the meanings attributed to the images of men's freighters in the first half of the 19th century, using tools of cultural history, visual culture and digital humanities. Thus, a route is reconstructed in which the figure of the freighter changes its forms of representation, its registration technologies, the conditions of acceptability of the audiences to which it is directed and the senses that it articulates when interacting with different actors and contexts. Thus, it was possible to verify how the freighter was integrated into a vertical Andean landscape, showing interactions between different natural phenomena in the representation of Humboldt and in the images of Natural History of the colony the late (1801-1810). Then, it is analyzed how the freighter became a human type that carries out activities in the landscape that it crosses, in the context of the travel literature (1819-1850). Finally, it was examined how it became an emblem used in scientific companies and in public opinion during the period of political intermination of liberal reforms, allegorizing the relationship between physical disconnection and lack of social cohesion (1851-1853). It was possible to ascertain how the Carguero scholarship integrated into an Andean vertical landscape, showing interactions between different natural phenomena in the early 19th century, both in Humboldt's representation and in the images of Natural History (1801-1810). Then, it was examined how it was used by a human type engaged in activities in the landscape it journeys, within the context of travel literature (1819-1850).
