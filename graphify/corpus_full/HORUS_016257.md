# Evaluation of intermediaries of electrocatalytic degradation of hydrocarbons present in waste water in the petrochemical industry, using gas chromatography with mass spectrometry.

The oil industry in its drilling and extraction processes requires a high water consumption which inevitably is contaminated with hydrocarbons and must be treated for disposal. Pollution is also generated by spills, leaks or bad operational procedures requiring large investments and cutting technology for removal. In this work electrocatalysis was used to degrade the toluene molecule, which is one of the pollutants present in wastewater. The current conditions and reaction time were optimized, and the best electrolyte was selected to obtain better percentages of degradation. The results obtained showed that the highest percentage of degradation of toluene was 94% in 40 minutes of reaction, plus the identification of benzoic acid and acetic acid as intermediaries of electrocatalytic degradation was achieved, thus demonstrating that using the SPME technique for the extraction of molecules and its analysis by chromatography of gases coupled to mass detector (GC-MS) it is possible to evaluate volatile compounds present in water. This methodology was applied to degrade a real sample of contaminated water from the oil production process of the Colombian industry, showing a light graded oil in 905 minutes
