# Philosophical pedagogy through questions as a phenomenological exploration of experience: anecdote, philosophical diary and student experiences

This research aims to show to what extent philosophy is not a mere subject but a way in which students describe, explore and dialogue with their lived experiences. To achieve this objective, the phenomenological-hermeneutic method is presented as a tool of inquiry of students and teachers. The phenomenological experience requires that subjects learn to see their world of life, that is, to be a reflexive scheme of meaning of their subjects of interest. Thus, it is necessary for subjects-students to return to the original moment of their understanding of the world. This pre-reflection moment, away from ideologies or assumptions given by the teacher, should emerge in their anecdotes of daily. In this sense, the inquiry is transformed into a process of temporality and perception of the world of social and family life of subjects. This is the first moment of research work. The second moment will be the affective constitution of the narrated. This pre-reflective moment, away from ideology or assumptions given by the teacher, should surface in their anecdotes or diaries. In this sense, the inquiry becomes a process of temporality and perception of the world of social and family life of the subjects. This being the first moment of the research work. The second moment will be the effective constitution of what is narrated. And the third moment is constituted by the hermeneutical experience, insofar as the later is integrated into the temporal and affective processes in the narratives and anecdotes of the students. These three moments will be, in this way, the horizons of this research work
