# Machine learning for quantum systems

The study of quantum systems requires exponentially large computational capacities depending on the size of these quantum systems, so supercomputers quickly become insufficient for the study of quantum systems of theoretical and technological interest. Today, and for only a decade, the main computing area has flourished_x0096_the machine learning, in its most well known flavor, the deep learning_x0096_the object of study also requires exponentially large computing capabilities depending on the size of these objects: the probability distributions that describe the data. The last five years have witnessed enormous advances in the introduction of techniques of machine learning in the study of quantum systems with the pioneering work of Carleo and Troyer (2017). This symbiosis is natural: the machine learning models can help to study the central object of quantum mechanics_x0096_the quantum learning states of the physical systems. The other direction is also surprisingly: the ideas and techniques of quantum mechanics can inspire the mechanical and dynamic models of mechanical design. Vicentini et al. (2022). In this proposal, alternative methods that have been successful in machine learning competitions, such as gradient booting methods, are explored. These methods focus on the use of decision trees, which are structures with the potential to describe the nature of various quantum states, including quantum computer states. Also, the study of physical systems is planned through quantum machine learning methods, for which it is necessary to understand how to optimize variable quantum circuits to prepare states in quantum circuits used for quantum computing. On the other hand, the domination of techniques as variable quantum eigensolver (Kandala et al. 2017) is also required for the study of low-excitation states of physical systems.
