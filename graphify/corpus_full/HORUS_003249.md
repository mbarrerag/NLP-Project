# BIOCARBON, AN ALTERNATIVE IN COFFEE NUTRITION

Colombian coffee is recognized worldwide for its mild flavor and aroma. Its cultivation generates a large amount of waste such as fresh pulp, which leads to environmental, health and economic problems. Obtaining biochar (BC) by pyrolysis of coffee pulp and its incorporation into the soil can be a complement to the mineral nutrition of the crop and is considered an alternative to mitigate the environmental impact due to the excessive use of fertilizers (Lehmann and Joshep , 2009). The objective of this work was to evaluate the effect of the application of biochar obtained from coffee pulp on the physiology and agronomic behavior of the Castillo el Tambo variety coffee crop (Coffea arabica L.). The research was developed in two stages: i) vegetative, using three-month-old plants, carried out in greenhouse conditions at the National University of Colombia; and ii) production, using a three-year-old commercial coffee crop, carried out in Chaparral, department of Tolima. Four doses of BC (0, 4, 8 and 16 t ha-1) and four levels of chemical fertilization (CF) (0%, 33%, 66% and 100% of nutritional requirements) were evaluated. Three groups of variables were recorded during the experiment: i) physiological parameters of seedlings and coffee trees; ii) physical and chemical characteristics of the soil in a commercial coffee crop; and iii) quality parameters of roasted beans and coffee drinks. The results indicated that: a) in the greenhouse, a positive effect was found in plants with 8 t ha-1 BC and fertilization levels of 66 and 100%. b) a positive effect was observed in coffee trees treated with 8 t ha-1 BC and 100%. c) 16 t ha-1 BC favored soil pH, bulk density, aggregation state and microbial respiration compared to 0 t ha-1 BC. d) Applications of 8 and 16 t ha-1 BC and 66%-100% chemical fertilization recorded greater sensitivity to the aromatic compounds of roasted coffee beans in the electronic nose. BC amendments between 8 and 16 t ha-1 and FQ between 66% and 100% increased the total soluble solids content, reduced the pH and increased the titratable acidity in roasted coffee bean beverages. In conclusion, 8 t ha-1 BC of coffee pulp can be an alternative to complement the nutrition of seedlings and coffee trees. Applications between 8 and 16 t ha-1 BC can support coffee soil management strategies and help the use of solid waste. BC as a complement to chemical fertilization showed a positive effect on the aromatic profile obtained for roasted coffee beans and cup quality attributes
