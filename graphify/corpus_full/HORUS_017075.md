# Method for segmentation of brain magnetic resonance imaging using a neural network architecture based on care models.

In recent years, the use of models based on deep learning for the development of advanced health systems has been increasing due to the excellent results that they can achieve. However, most of the proposed deep learning models use, to a large extent, convolutional and pooling operations, which causes a loss of valuable data focusing mainly on local information. In this thesis, we propose a approach based on deep learning that uses global and local characteristics that are important in the process of segmentation of medical images. To train the architecture, we use three-dimensional (3D) blocks extracted from the complete resolution of the magnetic resonance image. These were sent through a set of successive layers of convolutional neural networks (CNN) free of pooling operations to extract local information. Then, we send the resulting characteristic maps to successive layers of self-attendance modules to obtain the overall context, whose output was later sent to channeling the decoder mainly composed by layers of upsampling. The model was trained using the set of data Mindboggle-101. The experimental results showed that the modules of self-attention allowed structures, whose structure was most delayed was the most successful
