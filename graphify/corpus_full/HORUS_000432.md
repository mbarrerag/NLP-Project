# De-constructed physicalism: exclusion, generalization and levels of reality

The criticisms of Isabel de Bohemia a Descartes on the possibility of causal inter-substantial interaction contributed decisively to the ossustion of the Cartesian ontological dualism and inaugurated an argumentative route that emphasized the intimate metaphysical relationship of dependence or determination between physical and mental properties and that would eventually result in a form of ontological monism being physicalism one of its most sophisticated formulations. However, the problem of mental causation in this metaphysical framework persists due to the causal exclusion of mental properties by the base or determinative properties. Even, the problem of generalisation ends by showing that this type of exclusion extends to any type of causal relations involving properties other than physical properties. This panorama ends by changing the understanding of ourselves as rational agents sensitive to mental states and the relationship of the special sciences with fundamental physics. This research examines a critical element in the development of the problem of causal exclusion and generalization: the model of levels of reality and metaphysical relationships of determination/dependence between physical/non-physical properties; indicating that both depend on the model of the physical sciences as fundamental. This picture ends up up to disruprupting the understanding of ourselves as reasonable agents sensitive to mental status and the relationship of the specific sciences and sciences such as fundamental sciences.
