# TREHALULOSE SINTASE PRODUCTION BY RECOMBINANT YEAS FOR SUGAR CANE MOLASSES BIOREFINERY

1. INTRODUCTION The excessive sucrose intake is related to health disorders such as cardiovascular disease, diabetes, obesity, cancer, osteoporosis, and dental infections. Trehalulose is a disaccharide, sucrose isomer, naturally found in small amounts in honey, pollen, and sugar cane. It is a promising substance to replace sucrose as sweetener because of its greater sweetening power compared to other sucrose isomers of sucrose (70% of sucrose sweetness) and its structural similarity which does not generate residual flavors. On the other hand, due to its slow metabolism, trehalulose contributes to the prevention of dental caries, to mitigate type-2 diabetes, to weight control, and also it has prebiotic effects (Tian et al., 2019). These characteristics suggest a wide range of potential applications in the food, cosmetic and pharmaceutical industries. The objective of this study is the development of strategies of genetic engineering and bioprocess intended for an enzymatic process for the production of trehalulose from sucrose. These advances could be applied in the sugar cane molasses biorefinery. 2. METHODOLOGY A bioinformatic analysis was carried out to find and optimize the sequences of the trehalulose sintase. Using standard tools (BlastP, HMM, MEGAX, etc.) multiple alignments of enzymes ¿ from different sources¿ involved in trehalulose biosynthetic pathway were conducted. Also, conserved and variable residues were determined in each collection, especially those involved in active sites. These nucleotide ORF were synthetized in silico and cloned in the Pichia pastoris extracellular inducible (pPic9K) and constitutive (pGapZa) expression system, so, after yeast transformation, several collections of recombinant clones were generated carrying the genes coding for the enzymes of different origin. Trehalulose sintase production assays were performed in 500mL Erlenmeyer flasks containing 100 mL of medium containing sucrose, yeast extract, peptone and other salts, during 72 h at 28 °C and 200 rpm. Protein extracellular expression was analyzed by SDS-PAGE. Enzyme activity was assessed by reducing sugars determination -DNS- using sucrose as substrate. 3. RESULTS Three microorganisms Pectobacterium carotovorum (GenBank KF317210.1), Pseudomonas mesoacidophila (GenBank DQ304536.1) and Lonsdalea britannica (GenBank CP023009.1:c317068-315272) were selected as sources of enzymes for the potential production of trehalulose from sucrose, based on previous reports and the bioinformatic analysis purposed. P. mesoacidophila is the most studied microorganism as a producer of these enzymes (Goulter et al., 2012). P. carotovorum KKH 3-1 has a high bioconversion yield (97.4%) of sucrose to its isomers with optimum activity at pH 6.0 and 40°C, convenient for industrial trehalulose production (Nam et al., 2014). L. Britannica has not been reported as a producer of these enzymes, although its genome contains a gene with high homology to these proteins. Considering its novelty, it was of interest to evaluate the expression of L. Britannica enzyme. After an exhaustive bioinformatic analysis, associations between particular active site residues and product specificity were established. Finally, those genes were modified and arranged for their expression in the P. pastoris yeast heterologous system. After many cloning and expression stages, several clones¿ collections carrying the coding sequences were selected for further studies. Subsequently, enzyme production was analyzed in shake flasks, on pursuit of trehalulose sintase producer clones. Among them, after recovering the supernatant of P. pastoris PC1 (carrying P. carotovorum gene), SDS-PAGE analysis revealed the expression of a protein which molecular weight corresponds to the expected for trehalulose sintase (76kDa).
