# LOOSE NUMBERS: POLITICAL CENTRALISATION AND STATISTICAL FRAGMENTATION IN COLOMBIA, 1886¿1930

This chapter explores the efforts of Colombian conservative forces to centralise the production of official statistics between 1886 and 1930. The analysis follows closely the transformations on the fields of demography and health statistics. These fields are privileged arenas to gather clues on the obstacles the authorities faced in normalising the production of numbers due to the combination of geographic constraints, the scarcity of trained cadres, the population's distrust in statistics, political violence, bureaucratic overlapping, and financial problems. By looking at the complex interaction among physicians, parish priests, municipal and regional authorities, bureaucrats, and the global statistical system, this chapter explains the main institutional paradox of the Colombian statistical system under conservative rule: an increasing fragmentation and divergence under decisive centralising efforts
