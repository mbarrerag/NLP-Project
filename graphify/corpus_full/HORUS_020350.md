# Socio-economic consequences of sleep, stress and voice problems associated with the working conditions of university teachers

The teaching population, is recognized as one of the largest groups of professionals who use their voice as a tool. Indeed, studies indicate that the most frequent pathologies in teachers are otorhinolaryngological. Additionally, it is reported that teachers have poor quality sleep, which may be generating in them a reduction in concentration levels, difficulties in carrying out their work activities, and voice problems. Another health problem associated with the teaching working conditions, with a report between 43% and 72%, is stress. These three problems may significantly affect the quality of life of the individual and generate costs for the worker and society in general. With this in mind, the present research was intended to determine the socio-economic consequences of the problems of sleep, stress and voice associated with the working conditions of 29 teachers of the National University of Colombia, Bogotá.
