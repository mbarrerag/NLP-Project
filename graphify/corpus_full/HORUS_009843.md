# THE MEANS OF COMMUNICATION AND ITS TRANSFORMATIONS IN PERSPECTIVE OF CONSTITUTIONAL STORY, BETWEEN THE COMBAT FOR FREEDOM OF EXPRESSION AND THE FOURTH POWER

The chapter analyses the media and their transformations in perspective of constitutional history, which the subtitle specifies by emphasizing the dichotomy Between the fight for freedom of expression and the fourth power. Throughout its three major sub-chapters, the first reviews the legal handling of the printed media in the Old European Regime between the invention of the printing press and the late twelfth century, between the widespread prior censorship in public licensing systems and a relative freedom of opinion in contexts of religious peace. The second begins with the Great enlightened-liberal transformation and the birth of the modern constitutional state around 1800, under the maximum constitutionalized freedom of the press, with its lights and shadows throughout Europe and the Americas. The rise of the new audiovisual media in the twentieth century is deepened as well as the problems related to attempts to include the media in propaganda apparatuses of various governments. The third and last sub-chapter examines the ambiguous tendencies of the incipient post-constitutionalism of the twenty-first century and debates both the role of the `fourth power in the real constitution and the media digitalization and the new social media of the Internet era
