# SCIENCE FOR CACACULTURE

The cocoa grain obtained from the species Theobroma cocoa L. has been dynamizing the world economy, result of the increasing demand in the international market and the employment in various products of the food industry and pharmaceutical, which are factors that have stimulated the sowing of the cocoa. The increase in the demand for cocoa butter and consumption of chocolates are aspects that motivate the production of cocoa. The Theobroma cocoa is an original species of the humid tropical forests of South America, in the upper Amazon basin, which includes three countries: Colombia, Peru and Ecuador, where high genetic diversity has been found and molecular studies showed the presence of ten genetic groups (Motamayor et al., 2002, Motamayor et al., 2008), and based on the richness of the alloys, has been found high diversity in the High Amazonia from the south of Peru, as well as in the border land between Colombia, Peru and Brazil. The type of fruit possibly required by large animals and human domestication for its dispersion, since in the mountainous region of Peru and Ecuador has been found a clear group of cocoa (Thomas et al, 2012), the product has been used by the wild soil, in Colombia, and Brazil. The document contains results of research carried out by the Agrox tree, the cultivation of the crop, the putting of science to the service of agriculture, the production of cocoa is mostly produced on small farms in agroforestry or polyculture systems, the demands of buyers of cocoa beans that request stable volumes and quality are arguments that in the last decade have encouraged research in this crop. The International Cocoa Organization (ICCO) for some 15 years has stated that among the fundamental factors that impede the increase in cocoa production, are the lack of knowledge and resources available in the producer countries to address the aspects of the problem, therefore, it encouraged the producer countries to improve their knowledge in basic aspects, such as genetic resources and their behavior (International Cocoa Organization ICCO, 2007), aspects that offer fundamental information for the proposal of strategies for the management of the crop. Cocoa is a traditional crop, with simple management in agroforestal systems, but the current conditions of demand in the world market make necessary the improvement of cultural practices and the establishment of new production systems with the application of appropriate technologies aimed at raising the yield and obtaining a quality cocoa grain
