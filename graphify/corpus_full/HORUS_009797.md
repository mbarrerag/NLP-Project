# EXPLORING THE CONDUCTIVE POLYMERS ADDITION IN ELECTROSPUN NANOFIBROUS SCAFFOLDS FOR TISSUE ENGINEERING APPLICATION

Conductive materials have emerged as important materials in tissue engineering due to their ability to influence and control cellular behavior. Electrospun membranes composed of fibers and nanoparticles with electrically conductive additives have shown considerable promise in this field, and recently researchers have been exploring their potential for tissue engineering applications. The purpose of this study was to fabricate a nanofibrous polymeric scaffold incorporating polypyrrole (PPy), a high-stability and electrically conductive polymer, with polycaprolactone (PCL) and polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) nanofibers by means of electrospinning technique. Briefly, the polymeric solutions of PVP and PCL were prepared in ethanol and chloroform solvents, respectively. Then, PPy was chemically in-situ polymerized and incorporated with PCL and PVP nanofibers. A sonication method was used to enhance the dispersion and homogeneity of the polymer solutions. Vertical electrospinning equipment with a syringe and a needle tip, a syringe pump, and high-voltage source power was employed. The most important electrospinning parameters were the applied voltage and solution flow rate. Further, the most important parameters for PPy synthesis were monomer, oxidant concentration, and polymerization time. To evaluate the properties of the scaffolds, Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM), Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR), conductivity, contact angle, and tensile tests were performed. The addition of PPy influenced the morphology of the scaffold, resulting in a reduction in the average nanofiber diameter. The chemical composition of the scaffolds was validated using FTIR, which also demonstrated how insulating and conductive polymers interact. Furthermore, even with small amounts of PPy added, tensile strength and conductivity measurements showed significant improvements. Results were compared with physiological values. Our results highlight the potential of electrospun PVP and PCL nanofibers with a conductive polymer, PPy, as a promising scaffold for tissue engineering, especially in nervous or cardiac tissues. The resulting scaffold exhibits properties that could enhance cell behavior, integration with host tissue, and scaffold stability for long-term tissue regeneration. Additionally, the electrospinning technique used in this study allows for the creation of a highly porous and interconnected nanofibrous network, which could facilitate nutrient and oxygen transport for cell proliferation, suggesting that this approach could be a valuable material for tissue engineering
