# Identification of criminal events from audio signals using wavelet correlation modes

For many years, safety in urban environments has been a concern for the inhabitants of all cities of the world. Due to the insecurity that exists to a greater or lesser extent, governments in different regions of the world are continually looking for mechanisms to prevent criminal acts. This study presents a method that seeks to prevent crimes using audio signals that can be captured in an urban environment. Therefore, an emerging technique, called wavelet correlation modes, is used in order to represent audio signals in a compact way, after which a method based on machine learning is implemented to classify audio signals into two categories: those associated with an event or violent crime or those associated with a common event (non-violent or non-criminal). In the study carried out, it was possible to conclude that wavelet correlation modes allow for the classification of this type of signals with a precision greater than 80% and with execution times less than 1 second, using a maximum of 4 characteristics for model training. In the study carried out, it was possible to conclude that wavelet compression modes allow the classification of this type of signs with a precision greater than 80 % and with execution times less than 1 second, using a maximum of 4 characteristics for the model training. The tests were carried out on a laptop with a 64-bit operating system, x64 processor, Windows 11, AMD Ryzen 5 5500U processor, 16 Gb of RAM
