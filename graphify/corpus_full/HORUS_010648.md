# SESSION P154 - EEB01 MICROBIAL ECOLOGY (SATURDAY) EEB-SATURDAY-1112. MICROBIOME BIODIVERSITY ACROSS LIFE CYCLE OF CORN AND RICE STRAINS AF FALL ARMYWORM (FAW), SPODOPTERA FRUGIPERDA

Spodoptera frugiperda (J.E. Smith) (FAW) is a polyphagous pest that causes losses in a wide variety of economically important crops. It exhibits two strains (corn and rice) with genetic, ecological and behavioral differences. The insect-associated microbiome could play a relevant role in its evolutionary and adaptive success. Information related to Microbiome in FAW is limited and the differences in the structure of bacterial communities according to strains is unknown. In this work, we studied the bacterial composition, abundance and diversity associated with corn and rice strain tissues of FAW during its different life stages development through high throughput sequencing of bacterial 16S rRNA gene. Firmicutes and Proteobacteria were the dominant phyla during the insect metamorphosis in both strains. The highest observed richness of ASVs (Amplicon Sequence Variants) was founded in eggs samples. Illeobacterium, Ralstonia and Burkholderia genera showed similar relative abundances between adult males and eggs. Carnobacterium genus had a significant abundance in the pupal stage in corn and rice strains. Enterococcus genus was analyzed in all samples, showing an increase in relative abundance from the egg stage (< 5%) to becoming the dominant taxon in mature larvae. The Principal Coordinate Analysis (PCoA) based on the Bray-Curtis dissimilarity analysis indicated that there are no statistically significant differences between corn and rice strains bacterial communities ([PERMANOVA] F-value: 0.076874; R-squared: 0.002948, p-value < 0.977). However, it was observed that the composition of ASVs varies significantly between samples from different life stages of insect development ([PERMANOVA] F-value: 6.9355; R-squared: 0.61184; p-value < 0.001). Klebsiella, Dermacoccus, Lachnoclostridium, Faecalibaculum and Leuconostoc genera were identified as the most predominant in the rice strain, while Arsenophonus and Weissella had a significant abundance in the corn strain. The exploration of symbiotic bacterial role of FAW will allow the development of new alternatives to pest control
