# Local knowledge and practices for EFL curriculum design in a rural school in Bogotá, Colombia

This study reports on an action research that was carried out in a rural primary school waccupition (Colombia) on how knowledge and practices of the local context of the school contribute to the curriculum designs that are fundamental to learning and teaching English as a foreign language. The project focused on reviewing the implications that arise from the design and implementation of a curriculum unit for teaching English as a foreign language, for which it was based on the need to reflect on the institutional curriculum, from a critical perspective. The data were collected through a demographic survey, a group interview, the teacher's research journal and documented evidence that were analyzed using the theory based on categorization analysis. The results indicate that the curriculum unit, in addition to integrating practices and knowledge from the rural context of students with the foreign language, provides interesting reflections on the retextualization of English in local ways of thinking and seeing the world. It also supports the development of English skills in accordance with the standards and national competencies, reinterpreting the meaning of the school curriculum. In conclusion, besides inquiring in its truly critical sense, the study proposes a model of design from and the English school and its implications for the English.
