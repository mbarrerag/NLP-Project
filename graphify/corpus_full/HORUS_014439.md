# Effect of Epstein-Barr virus gp85 protein-derived peptides on the expression of cytokines in peripheral blood mononuclear cells.

Cytokines are proteins primarily involved in intercellular communication during immune response. Some cytokines may inhibit tumor development and progression, and these effects appear to be related to the modulation of antitumor response. Previous studies have identified molecules capable of regulating their expression, including a peptide derived from the site of binding of the glycoprotein gp85 of the Epstein-Barr virus to human leukocytes, named 11438. In this study, the effect of this peptide and its analogue (33210) on the production of pro- and anti-inflammatory cytokines, at the level of anti-inflammatory cytokines, at the level of MRNAm and protein, on peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) healthy, as well as the induction of phenotypic changes in this cell population was assessed.
