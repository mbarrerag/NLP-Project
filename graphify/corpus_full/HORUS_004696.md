# Violence invisible in the integrated mass transport system: Travel experiences in Barranquilla

This final master's degree study studies the travel experiences in the Integrated System of Massive Transport (SITM) of Barranquilla, analyzing how they are determined by infrastructure, urban environment and identity, social, economic and cultural factors. The main objective is to understand the configuration of invisibilized violences in the urban space through these experiences. A multidisciplinary approach has been adopted, using qualitative and quantitative methods, including mobile ethnography, questionnaires and semi-structured interviews. Focusing on the metropolitan area of Barranquilla, characterized by its intense conurbation and interdependence with its surrounding territories, research includes a diverse, but limited, sample of users of the SITM. The experiences of travel from mobility as an object and justice is considered in the approach of this study. The findings reveal that the experiences of travel in Barranquilla are deeply influenced by elements beyond the physical infrastructure, including socio-economic and cultural aspects. The study examines travel experiences with a focus on urban mobility as the object and considerations justice in mobility as the approach of this study. Findings review that travel experiences in Barranquilla are properly influenced by elements beyond physical infrastructure, encompassing socioeconomic and cultural aspects. Patterns of exclusion and segregation are identified, posing significant challenges for governance and urban planning. The study conclusions with recommendations for the urban governance to promote inclusion, justice, and sustainability in urban mobility, employing the need to integrate a variety of citizen experiences and needs into urban planning
