# INTERNAL MASS BALANCE VERIFICATION IMPROVES MACHINE LEARNING-BASED MODELS

Recently, applications of machine learning in chemical engineering have regained strength as a research issue. Data-driven algorithms can capture the relevant non-linear behavior of complex systems, however due to the lack of phenomenological basis, they have low interpretability and take the risk to make predictions without physical, chemical, or biological meaning [1]. To compensate for these disadvantages, the data-driven model such as a neural network could be supplemented with a first principles model. This work contributes to the development and assessment of machine learning strategies as a tool for biochemical process modelling. Enzymatic production of galacto-oligosaccharides (GOS) from cheese whey, with the effect of metal ions, was considered as study case. This system was chosen because its phenomenological modelling is an unsolved issue due to the complexity of the reaction mechanism [2]. In this research a simple multilayer perceptron neural network (MPNN) and a hybrid model with the MPNN plus a mass balance were used to model the concentration profiles for substrate and products during enzymatic GOS production, and a comparison of these results against a phenomenological based model were performed. Due to the need for data-driven models to be trained with large amounts of data, an initial dataset containing 144 data points from 18 different experiments was artificially increased by an interpolation procedure. With the increased dataset, python's libraries PyTorch and scikit-learn were used to train the simple MPNN and the hybrid model. For training, k-folds method was used. 5 Folds were made, each fold contained 13 experiments for training and 5 for testing. The performance of the model was evaluated with the R2 metric for each fold. The simple MPNN obtained an R2 score of 0.9238 for training and 0.8765 for testing, and the hybrid model obtained 0.9670 for training and 0.9087 for testing. Additionally, from a qualitatively assessment, the hybrid model avoided the illogical behavior and improved the smoothness of the generated profiles. These results show that with a set of experiments and using an interpolation method, it is possible to obtain enough data to train hybrid models for complex systems without the need for intensive mathematical descriptions of the physical or chemical phenomena that in some cases aren't still completely known. The implementation of the hybrid models confirmed the possibility that the data-driven algorithm predicts complex and unknown behaviors, like kinetic mechanism, and a mass balance corrects possible disturbances giving as a result a very powerful model.
