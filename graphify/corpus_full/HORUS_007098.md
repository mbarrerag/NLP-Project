# Characterization of unwanted loneliness and its relationship to mortality in cancer patients

To describe the phenomenon of unwanted loneliness (NDS) in patients with cancer and to establish their relationship with mortality. A mixed study was carried out composed of: the validation of scales by means of the Classical Theory of Measurement (CMT) and the method of Response to the Item-Rasch; an observational and analytical study of prospective cohort; and a qualitative study based on the grounded theory of Strauss and Corbin. The study population included patients with various types of cancer (mama, prostate, cervix, gastrointestinal and haematological) with a view to the analysis of the patient. The analysis of the scale was repeated monthly and every six months during two years. In addition, 13 in-depth interviews were conducted using a non-probabilistic sampling, sequential, by convenience and theoretical. In the analysis plan of the prospective cohort (n=400), the analysis of the variables was performed, the partial and the partial analysis of the scale and the initial analysis of the patient was performed. Population. Patients with moderate or high levels of loneliness presented a higher risk of mortality, while surgical treatment and high functionality were associated with higher survival. Factors such as educational level, marital status, type of cancer and functionality also influenced the experience of loneliness. The qualitative study identified additional components of the NDS not included in the UCLA-LS3 scale, such as the "administrativesolitude" generated by the bureaucracy of the health system, which negatively affects the quality of life of patients and contributes to the pauperization of health. It was also observed that loneliness is linked not only to premature death, but also to suicidal behaviour, while patients tend to prefer to face cancer and death in hospital settings due to the emotional and psychological impact of loneliness. This study, a pioneer in Colombia, allows a comprehensive understanding of the NDS in patients with cancer and its impact on mortality. The results highlight the need to adjust current scales to measure the NDS, incorporating the specific experiences of this population and the barriers of the health system.
