# ADAPTATION OF PLANTS DURING DOMESTICATION: A GENOMIC PERSPECTIVE OF THE DUAL DOMESTICATION OF LIMA BEAN (PHASEOLUS LUNATUS L.)

Lima bean (Phaseolus lunatus L.) is the second most important Phaseolus crop for human consumption and, compared to common bean, it shows a wider range of ecological adaptations along its range of distribution from Mexico to Argentina. These adaptations, in special to heat and drought stresses, make Lima bean a promising crop for food security under predicted scenarios of climate change. At least two domestication processes occurred in Lima bean, one process in Mesoamerica and another one in the Andes. Interestingly, during domestication both Mesoamerican and Andean landraces evolved similar traits of the domestication syndrome (DS), mainly larger pods and seeds, reduction of pod dehiscence, reduction of cyanogenesis, changes in growth habit, among others. Therefore, Lima bean is a species of great scientific interest not only because of its ecological adaptations but also because it provides an opportunity to study the molecular basis of convergent phenotypic evolution. A better understanding of these adaptations may lead to the identification of the responsible genes and their use in future breeding activities
