# TECHNICAL, ECONOMIC AND ENVIRONMENTAL DEEMEMINATION AS A TOOL FOR SUSTAINABLE TREATMENT OF TEXTILE INDUSTRIAL EFLUENTS

This study compared the environmental impacts associated with sequential processes Coagulation-Floculation-Fenton-Neutralization (CF-F-N) and Coagulation-Floculation-Electro-Fenton-Neutralization (CF-EF-N), implemented under optimal operating conditions, for the treatment of an industrial textile effluent (ARnD), as an integral part of the Sustainable Development Goals number 6Clean Water and Sanitation, 9Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure, 12Responsible Production and Consumption, 13Climatic Action. An analysis of the life cycle demonstrated the competitiveness of the EF process against the traditional Fenton. EF was highly dependent on energy consumption (90%), mainly due to the electricity required for electrolysis, thus generating a total HC of 14.10 kgCO2-Eq/m3. On the contrary, in Fenton, energy consumption was low. However, high amount of reagents required, mainly H2O2, negatively impact the process, resulting in a total HC of 20.23 kgCO2-Eq/m3.
