# Definition of minimum energy performance standards for LED lighting systems technologies

This study develops a specific labelling model for indoor LED light sources in Colombia, considering parameters of energy performance, visual and circus. It evaluates the application of the Minimum Energy Performance Standards (MEPS) to these sources and evaluates multicriterial models to establish the labelling program. As a result, the importance of disseminating visual and circadian effect of lighting in human well-being among professionals and lighting designers in Colombia is evidenced, highlighting how the lighting characteristics can influence health and performance. Likewise, the study evaluates the impact of establishing the MEPS in Colombia. By comparing a MEPS of 80 lm/W with a base scenario of 60 lm/W, it is estimated that a significant accumulated saving of 8140 GWh of energy and a significant reduction of 3 million tonnes of CO2 emissions in a period of 10 years. These results support the implementation of energy efficiency policies in the interior lighting sector in Colombia, showing how the adoption of more ecological measures can have a positive impact on the conservation of energy resources and in the proposed reduction of the carbon footprint of the country. In conclusion, this study supports the use of the internal lighting and the import of disseminating the visual and circus effect of lighting on human well-being among professionals and lighting designers in Colombia was found, imphasizing how lighting characteristics can influence health and performance. Furthermore, the study evaluated the impact of implementing MEPS in Colombia. When comparing a MEPS of 80 lm/W with a baseline scenario of 60 lm/W, significant energy savings of 8140 GWh and a considerable reduction of 3 million tons of CO2 emissions over a 10-year period are estimated.
