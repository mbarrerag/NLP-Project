# Development of essential oil-based encapsulation with potential insecticide activity

The conventional synthetic insecticides used in agrochemical processes generate great concern at present due to the collateral problems that have been evidenced of contamination and affectation in human health and all life forms. In the search for alternatives that counteract this problem, essential oils have been positioned as an attractive option due to their proven insecticide activity, while possessing benefits such as their ease of being biodegradable and being considered safe for human health. However, the high volatility of these complex mixtures of plant secondary metabolites also represents a limitation when it is necessary that the insecticide effect is maintained for prolonged times as required in an agro-industrial crop. In order to cope with this difficulty, it has been proposed as an alternative the encapsulation in different matrices where essential oils have the ability to modulate their release and make it effective for longer periods reducing their volatidisation and strengthening their alternative use for the replacement of conventional pesticides. In this direction, in this project it is proposed to study the capacity that the essential oils of orange, lemon, improve, mint and romer as botanical insecticides have to obtain from a pre-capsulation and controlled process of the organic oil
