# Identification and characterization of neoantigen T lymphocytes specific to healthy donors for cancer immunotherapy.

Neoantigen-based immunotherapy allows stimulating the immune system of the patient with cancer by inducing a T-Linfocyte-directed antitumor response (LT). Neoantigenes are generated by somatic mutations in DNA that produce changes in the amino acid sequence and that are exclusive to tumor cells.The selection of immunogenic neoantigenes is made by means of in-silic tools that predict the affinity and timing of the neoantigene to the HLA molecule, then these are evaluated in in-vitro cell culture systems, while maintaining patient cells, however, the reported frequency of neoantigen responses is still low. Therefore, this study proposed two different approaches to identify and characterize immunogenic neoantigens.On the one hand, the implementation of the I-cell culture systems of healthy donor cells was proposed to evaluate the immunogenicity of neoantigenes in-vitro. On the other hand, the use of docking and molecular dynamics was proposed to identify the molecular characteristics associated with the immunogenicity of neoantigens. The results of this study showed that the presence of professional presenting cells such as DCs and an enrichment of virgin CD8 LTs was necessary, since it was in this culture that specific LTs against neoantigens were detected, although in a low proportion. However, these results were only observed in 2 of 4 evaluated donors, indicating that additional tests are necessary to determine that this culture system is the one indicated. For the second approach, a concept test was performed with two neoantigene complexes (one immunogenic and one non-immunogenic) to evaluate the use of docking cells and molecular dynamics as screening tools for the identification of immunogenic neoantigens, allowing to determine that an immunogenic neoantigen should form a stable MHC-peptide complex in time. This study demonstrates the high complexity that represents the use of healthy donor cells and the computational tools of immunogenic docking and dynamics, however, these two approaches are promising since they would not only allow the improvement of the selection of immunogenic neoantigens but also have the potential to identify specific TCRs against these cellular antigens (based modification)
