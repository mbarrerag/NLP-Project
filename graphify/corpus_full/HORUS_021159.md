# Maternal-fetal cellular communication key element in reproductive health

The implantation of blastocyst is a process characterized by continuous communication between the trophoblast cells and the cells of maternal deciduousness, for there to be a successful pregnancy there must be a tolerance of the maternal immune system to the fetus, while the immune competence of the mother is maintained. The cells of the immune system represent between 40 and 50% of the cells of the deciduous during pregnancy, and within this population the natural killer cells of the deciduous (dNK) constitute about 70% of the total immune cells. These cells produce growth factors, angiogenic factors and cytokines necessary in implantation. Among these growth factors is the factor similar to insulin-IGF2 which is a key peptide in fetal growth and maternal-fetal communication, although the mechanism has not yet been described.
