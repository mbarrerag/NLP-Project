# Design Methodology Assay For Determination Of Wear In Limes Of Endodontics Manufactured In Alloy Nickel-Titanium

At present, and in accordance with technological advances in the field of dentistry, the use of nickel-titanium alloys (NiTi) is recurrent in rotary tools such as endodontic files used in the preparation of the root canal of the tooth. Initially, these tools were commonly made of stainless steel and facilitated a deformation that adjusted to the geometric requirements of the root canal, preventing the development of a correct intervention. On the contrary, nickel-titanium alloy files, having superelastic properties, allow the instrument to be curved according to the geometry of the root canal, generating the process to be carried out more quickly and with better results; however, in the canal preparation process, the NiTi file is subjected to axial loads, bending and torsional stresses that cause the piece to break after a certain number of uses, which at this time are unknown due to the fact that the manufacturers recommend that the files be for single use, however, due to the high commercial value of each one, endodontists tend to increase the number of repetitions, ignoring the permissible limit of uses. An endodontic file mock-up test is presented, as well as an experimental test methodology through which it is sought to simulate the use of the file inside the root canal using controlled parameters in such a way that the force, the loss of material, the roughness and the change in geometry of the file as its number of uses increases in order to determine one or several recommended methods when measuring wear in endodontic files made of Nickel Titanium alloy
