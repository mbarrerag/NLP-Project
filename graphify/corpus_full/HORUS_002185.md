# Silicon Drench Application as a Plant Nutrient Tool to Mitigate Heat Stress in Rice Seedlings

Silicon (Si) is a beneficial element that facilitates plant adaptation to abiotic stress conditions, including heat stress. The aim of this study was to evaluate the role of Si through the application of two silicon compounds, namely, potassium silicate (SiK) and calcium silicate (SiCa), at different commercial application rates (1 L ha −1 and 2 L ha −1 for SiK; and 1.5 L ha −1 and 3 L ha −1 for SiCa) to alleviate the effects of elevated day/night temperatures (HT = 40°C/30°C) in two rice genotypes [Fedearroz 67 (tolerant) and Fedearroz 2000 (susceptible)]. Two experiments were conducted. In the first (E1), four treatment groups were established in Fedearroz 67 plants: control (CK = 30°/25°C), HT without silicon, HT with SiK at 2 L ha −1 (drench), and HT with SiCa at 3 L ha −1 (drench). In the second (E2), treatments (CK, HT, HT + SiK at 1 or 2 L ha −1 , and HT + SiCa at 1.5 or 3 L ha −1 ) were applied to Fedearroz 67 and Fedearroz 2000 genotypes. In E1, Si drench increased stomatal conductance (gs: 363.8 mmol m −2 s −1 ), Fv/Fm ratio (0.69), and chlorophyll content (28.5 SPAD units) compared to HT Fedearroz 67 plants (145.7 mmol m −2 s −1 for gs, 0.46 for Fv/Fm, and 16.8 SPAD units). In E2, SiK had a higher relative tolerance index (RTI) in HT Fedearroz 67, with no differences in HT Fedearroz 2000. In conclusion, SiK or SiCa can mitigate heat stress effects on rice in tropical environments during high temperature periods
