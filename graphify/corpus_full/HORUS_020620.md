# Sociolinguistic study of Japanese immigrants in Colombia: the case of the Nikkei community located in the department of Valle del Cauca.

This research study addressed the case of the community of Japanese descendants Nikkei resident in the department of Valle del Cauca in Colombia, in order to understand the result of the linguistic contact generated by the Japanese migrations in the region at the beginning of the 20th century in the current state of the languages spoken currently in this community. Due to the dynamics presented in a language contact scenario, it was sought to know what impact these have had in favor of and against the ancestral language of the community, Japanese, and if these had fostered phenomena like bilingualism. To this end, it was thought to carry out a research of mixed approach with qualitative predominance of cross-sectional type, descriptive scope and mixed ethnographic design to explain the findings found. The data obtained was obtained by the language of the dominant context, Spanish. Starting from the sociology of the language, it was carried out a study of the functional language, the functional part of the functional group has been determined by the functional group of the language, the functional group of the individual language, the functional group of the individual. Such dynamics have had in favor and against the ancestral language of the community, Japanese, in connection with other linguistic phenomenon such as bilingualism and language shift. To this end, the perception of identity that the Nikkei has in connection with their linguistic attitudes was analyzed to understand the value that they assigned to the ancestral language in contrast to the connection they have with the language of the dominant context, Spanish. To explain the findings, it was proposed to carry out the analysis of the sociology of language, a mixed approach approach with qualitative predominance of a transversal type, descriptive scope, and mixed ethnic design
