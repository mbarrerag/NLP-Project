# COLLECTIVE WATER MANAGEMENT, A READING FROM SOCIAL WORK

This article is a contribution to thinking about the work of Social Work in an environmental perspective, through experiences of collective water management. The results presented interpret the solid community feeling in the territory that arises when people with their own resources build irrigation channels, pools, storage tanks and install pipes to distribute water for human consumption. Community experiences such as collective water management contribute theoretically and methodologically to the research-intervention exercise carried out by Social Work in the environmental dimension. Likewise, the research values the sociopolitical contribution of inclusion and social recognition that has the collective strategy of access to water for human consumption, between rural and urban residents marginalized from the State care for access to public water service
