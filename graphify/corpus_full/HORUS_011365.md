# IMMUNOLOGY AND SKIN CANCER

The concept of the process of formation of cancer in tissues is born in the face of the possibility that a cell that should enter a programmed death process presents some changes in its genetic composition that impact on its functionality and ends up in the first instance perpetuating its cycle and later multiplying altering normal function of the tissue where it is present, where it ends up presenting a denaturation of its function and generating a destructive process that initially occurs at the local level, but that by migration can lead to other tissues and finally to organic tissue damage. The tissues in the skin and cells as such are not exempt from this phenomenon, which leads to the most known skin neoplasms that may have a local behavior, either slow or infiltrating as in basal cell carcinoma, others, with other capacities of differentiation and local and regional invasion, such as squamocellular carcinoma, up to tumors of high proliferative rate with metastatic progression such as melanoma
