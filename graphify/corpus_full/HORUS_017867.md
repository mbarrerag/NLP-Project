# IDENTITY LOST IN THE DOMESTIC ARCHITECTURE

This reflection revives a topic that has been the subject of theoretical debate in the fields of architecture from the eighties to today and that has detractors and advocates who advocate, either for a global architecture of homogeneous conditions, or for one that incorporates local values in buildings. Such a notion of link between architectural products and the place where they arise is analyzed in this text as a dialogue conducive to producing feelings and sensations of cultural identification that allow, or not, the construction of architectural identity. The particular contribution lies in the application and analysis of ideas on space supports for domestic privacy
