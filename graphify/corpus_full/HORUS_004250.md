# UNVEILING FREIGHT-RELATED SPACE-SHARING CONFLICTS AND THEIR IMPACTS ON THE VALUE OF STREETS

Increasing demand for scarce urban streetside creates space-sharing conflicts, compromising cities¿ liveability. Conflict assessments predominantly adopt traffic-centric perspectives, neglecting place-making functions of streets and the role of freight delivery operations in this regard. This paper investigates space-sharing conflicts between freight and other streetside users, aiming to understand challenges in creating liveable streets. Based on empirical evidence from the West End of Central London, a framework is proposed to systematically identify these conflicts, and explore impacts on the health, social, economic, and environmental value dimensions of streets, from the freight operators¿ perspective. The framework suggests four types of freight-related space-sharing conflicts: collision risk, traffic obstruction, space overuse, and pavement damages. Findings highlighted the unintended pressures the identified conflicts create on freight operators, exposing them to high-stress levels and endangering both their own and other street users¿ health and safety. The framework also informs space allocation decisions for efficient last-mile deliveries while minimising negative impacts on liveability. Analysing freight delivery operations and their interactions with other users introduces a novel approach to city logistics that traditionally studies freight in isolation. Additionally, the paper contributes to urban planning, providing insights for right-of-way decisions that include the freight perspective'often overlooked in urban studies
