# Expression and functional characterization of a Geobacillus stearothermophilus polymerase I DNA

The DNA polymerase of Geobacillus stearothermophilus (ADN Pol I Bst), is a member of the A family of polymerases, which has characteristics of strand displacement that favor its application in methods of isothermic amplification. In this work the expression and functional characterisation of a DNA Pol I Bst is described, starting with the sequencing of the plasmid by means of Oxford Nanopore technology, to confirm the coding sequence of the protein, followed by an analysis in sillico, with the purpose of determining the 3D structure and active sites of the protein. Subsequently, the expression, extraction, purification, determination of the catalytic activity and analysis of functionality by means of Loop Mediated Isothermal Amplification (LAMP) at 65 °C per 60 min, using as the RNA substrate of SARS-CoV-2. The results reflect a coding sequence of 576 amino acids belonging to the large DNA fragment Pol I Bst, which has molecular weight of 61,8 kDa. A concentration of 2g/ml of total protein was obtained, which is demonstrated by DRN, with a total activity.. This is followed by in silica analysis to determine the protein's 3D structure and active sites. Subsequently, expression, extraction, purification, determination of catalytic activity, and functionality analysis were performed using Loop-Mediated Isothermal Amplification (LAMP) at 65 °C for 60 min, with SARS-CoV-2 RNA as the substrate. The results revealed a coding sequence of 576 amino acids belonging to the large fragmentation of Bst DNA Pol I, with a molecular weight of 61.8 kDa. A protein concentration of 2 mg/ml was obtained, exhibiting enzyme activity of 606.4 U and a specific activity of 3.0×105 U/mg.
