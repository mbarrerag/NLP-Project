# CONSANINT AND FREQUENCY OF MOLECULAR MARCARDERS FOR GENETICAL DISORDERS IN COLOMBIA’S LUCERNA

The cattle Lucerne is a genetic resource of Colombia, with characteristics of adaptation to the lower tropics, specifically to the Valle del Cauca. It was officially declared as Colombian breed in 1983. However, like other Creole resources, it has been little disclosed and exploited, especially by the increase of production with specialized breeds, which made it difficult to expand and exploit, with the possible consequences of decreasing diversity and increasing inbreeding. The objective of this work was to estimate the coefficient of endogamy by genealogy and genomic frequency of the Lucerne cattle, to describe the genealogy and to estimate the frequencies of some markers for genetic disorders in the population, as a contribution to breeding and improvement of the breed. The information of the two main cattle (Natural Reserve the Hatico and Hacienda Lucerne) was used. The information included 9526 animals registered between 1968 and 2019. The analysis of the Ptygósel package was used for the analysis of pedigree. The analysis of the Plyl. 94 animals were selected for the extraction of DNA and the genotypal population with the Bovine GGP. The Lucerne population presented low levels of endogamy and markers of genetic disorders, suggesting an important level of diversity, which are in line with the FAO system on diversity of domestic animals that states that the breed is not at risk. However, endogamy control should continue to be implemented in the selection programmes to improve milk production in low tropic conditions, as an important productive alternative for Colombia's dairy livestock
