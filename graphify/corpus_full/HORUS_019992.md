# GUIDE TO THE CAMPUS OF THE NATIONAL UNIVERSITY OF COLOMBIA HEADQUARTERS AmazONIA

This tinamú had not heard it before in this headquarters, did the professor Gary Stiles, ornithologist of world recognition, that visited our headquarters Amazonia in 2013. It is something usual for us on the campus of the Amazonia headquarters of the National University to see and feel the birds every day, to hear the parrots issuing so-called stridends and backpackers doing their deployments, or to listen to the tinamúes and the roadkeepers singing in the twilight. It is common to observe guars throughout the campus collecting fruits, or micos bebelectche freely moving among the trees. However, it is not so usual to realize that the forest that possesses the Amazonia headquarters in its more than 13 hectares, is the result not only of the arrival of the University to the region, but of the few years of conservation decisions of several generations of people who do about three decades of this city, as the professor and only the amazonic ecosystem. It is like the recovery of the forest allowed not only many species of animals to continue to inhabit the area, but that many other Colombia began to arrive and continue to the campus after many years, as the professor, as the city. The guide also presents a list of the species of birds registered on campus to date, as a joint effort of several decades of birdwatchers who have contributed to the recognition of the headquarters avifauna. among these people are gary stiles, achilles gutiérrez, elais cuao, esteban carrillo and gc. The taxonomic sequence follows that suggested by the South American Check List Committee (SACC; Remsen et al. 2022)
