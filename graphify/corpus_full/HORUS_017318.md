# CHAPTER 29 INFECTION BY VIRUS OF THE HUMAN PAPER AND EMBARAZO

Human papillomavirus (HPV) infection is considered the most common sexually transmitted infection in the world with an estimated prevalence of 20% to 46%, can cause vaginal, vulva, penis and anus cancer, as well as some head and neck cancers, anogenital warts and recurrent respiratory papillomatosis. Additionally, it has been reported that HPV infection is acquired through non-sexual routes and that one possible route is mother-to-child transmission during pregnancy or during childbirth; HPV has been found in amniotic fluid, placenta, ovular membranes, in fetuses resulting from abortion, in newborns and young children. The following items are developed: Introduction Human papillomavirus Natural History of the disease Pregnancy Implications for pregnancy Implications for the fetus/nenate Early detection of HPV infection in pregnancy Management of HPV-associated lesions during pregnancy Considerations for the way of childbirth Prevention of HPV infection References
