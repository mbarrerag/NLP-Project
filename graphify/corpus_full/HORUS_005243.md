# Determinants in the coordination of actors and their effect on the performance of the hospital supply chain in a Lean environment

The coordination in the management of the hospital supply chain (CSH) is essential to improve the quality and efficiency of medical care. This research evaluated empirically the relationship between the determinants of coordination in the CSH, as well as its effects on performance mediated by a Lean environment. The study is quantitative of explanatory and predictive type, developed in three phases: The CSH was characterized between providers and health institutions, identifying the determinants of coordination; the PLS-SEM was used to examine the relationships of the model, using data of 108 Colombian IPS of high complexity; the relationships of moderation, mediation and predictive capacity of the model were evaluated, and a framework of improvement strategies based on results was proposed. The determinants: institutionality, information and ICT, human and social capital influence the relationship between the context and the vision. The coordination model has a positive effect on the performance of assistance, financial and technical. In addition, coordination influences the adoption of Lean practices, and these media in this relationship. The legal nature of the IPS moderates the relationship between the determinants and the vision. The research contributions contribute to the theory of the management and the use of the management theory of the Lean, and the Colombian IPS. Ultimately, moderation, mediation relationships, and predictive capacity of the model were evaluated, culminating in a proposed improvement strategy based on the findings. Determinants such as institutional framework, information and ICT, and human and social capital influence coordination in HSCM. Coordination exercises a positive impact on performance in clinical, financial, and technical aspects. Moreover, coordination influences the adoption of lessons practices, with these practices mediating this relationship. The legal nature of IPS does not moderate the coordination-performance relationship, but it does moderate the relationship between determinants and coordination. The research implications advance supply management theory, incorporating network theory-based view. Further exploration from alternative relationship comparative determinants and coordination
