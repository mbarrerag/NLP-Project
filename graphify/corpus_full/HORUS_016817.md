# EVALUATION OF MIXED BACTERIAL CULTURES UNDER CONTROLLED COMPOSITION THAT INDUCE MICROBIAL CALCITE PRECIPITATION FOR USE IN CEMENT-BASED MATERIALS

Microbiologically induced calcite precipitation (MICP) by ureolytic bacteria in cementitious materials have shown great potential to obtain biotechnological products that improve their mechanical properties and assist crack repairs. Few studies evaluate the effect of mixed cultures, although these are outlined as an alternative that could improve repairs in less time, besides to a greater microbial adaptation to the material conditions. The aim of this study was to evaluate the efficiency in calcium carbonate (calcite) precipitation, the consolidation capacity of sands and the change in the abundance of mixed MICP bacterial cultures. Four bacteria with high precipitation capacity (Psychrobacillus psycrodurans S17, Rhodococcus qingshengii S1, Glutamicibacter arilaitensis M3C3 and Arthrobacter crystallopoietes M4C20) were used to prepare 16 treatment assays with similar cell concentration. Calcite precipitation, pH variation and ammonium production were evaluated during growth in liquid medium, while changes in bacteria abundance by qPCR. The consolidation capacity of mixed cultures was evaluated using Ottawa sand columns. Mixture M4C20+M3C3 showed 99% calcium precipitation in liquid medium after 12h, pH 9 and generated the maximum ammonium production (2.81 g/L). However, bacteria abundance analysis indicated that strain M3C3 had a higher individual growth, agreeing with the consolidation assays, where its sand column showed the highest resistance to compression. Although a fast calcium carbonate precipitation was observed for some of the mixed cultures, this did not improve the consolidation properties of sand columns. Some antagonistic and synergistic interactions in mixed cultures were evidenced, indicating the need to further evaluate the associated metabolic products
