# Effect of the enriched environment, after maternal separation, on microglial morphology and concentration of corticosteroids, in adolescent Wistar rats.

In rodents as in humans, maternal separation during lactation (MDL) affects the neurodevelopment by being a stressor factor, affecting metabolism, behavior and learning. On the other hand, exposure to an enriched environment (AE) activates the central nervous system by providing physical, social and sensory stimuli that would lead to a reduction in stress and adaptation of neurobiological responses. To analyze the effect of 15 days of enriched environment, after maternal separation during lactation, on the quantity and morphology of microglycemia and the concentration of corticosteroids, in rats Wistar males and females during early adolescence. 78 offspring of Wistar rats divided into two groups, with and without MDL. On the 22nd they were separated by sex and treatment, and located in an EY or standard for 15 days. Animals were anesthetized and perfused on the 37th day, they took a serum sample to measure corticosone and removed brain tissue.
