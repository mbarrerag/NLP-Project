# Living with malignant sarcoma in lower limbs: teen experience

To learn about cancer is considered a devastating situation that divides the life of those who suffer it and generates changes at the physical, mental, emotional, social and spiritual level.Among the malignant neoplasms, are the osteosarcoma and Ewing sarcoma as the two types of sarcomas that are most representative in adolescence and in anatomical location in lower members.The adolescents who suffer from sarcoma face a situation that threatens their life or their integrity and that exceeds all the tools they have to confront it, which takes them to the limit and generates a vital crisis.This situation has been extensively addressed by different researches from the analysis of the physiopathology, the diagnosis, the treatment, the rehabilitation; while others study the management of the symptoms, the quality of life and the experiences of the adolescents from the vision of the adults.However, few studies that address the life experience of adolescents with malignant sarcoma in lower members from their own voice. To understand the experiences of adolescents living with malignant sarcoma in lower members. A qualitative study, developed under the methodology of the members based from the constructivist paradigm with adolescents, 11 years. The theoretical approach emerged from the process of comparing the categories: adjusting and living my new life. Finally, from the process of constant comparison of the categories, the theoretical approach emerged from which, as contrasted with the preceding literature, the following six central concepts of the resulting substantive theory were defined: Vulnerability; Motivation; Social support; Confrontation; Resilience; and Emancipation. The construction of the experience of adolescents with sarcoma in lower limbs was consolidated given the transition from leaving the perception of invulnerability of adolescents to the development of their emancipation. This emancipation, beyond meaning the separation of their family nucleus, implied the differentiation of adolescents as autonomous, empowered, adaptable, motivated, resilient, aware of the social support they count with, capable of developing decisions and aware not only of their own vulnerability, but also of their capacities to address the vital crises coming from them.
