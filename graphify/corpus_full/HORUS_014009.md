# UNa snake rola: Refuges as a mitigation strategy possible effects of habitat homogenization and climate change on Atractus crassicabatus population on the campus of Bogotá headquarters of the National University of Colombia

The snake Atractus crassicabatus or snake sabanera, is a species of endemic colubrid of the departments of Cundinamarca, Boyacá and Santander, in the Colombian Oriental Cordillera, with an altitude distribution of 2000 to 3000 meters above sea level. Thanks to its distribution, it is the only species of snake present in the city of Bogotá, and has become a flagship species of savannah. It is a medium snake, of night habits, with black and yellow colors. Being an ectothermal organism, it regulates its body temperature with the climatic conditions of the environment. It is common to find it under rocks, trunks or in soils rich in organic matter and covered by grasses in urban places such as parks and potreros. This snake uses this type of refuges to withstand the strong fluctuation of daily temperature, characteristic of the high mountain environments. A. crassicaudatus presents a series of threats, as the destruction of its habitat in urban settlements and climate change. These threats are not exception in the campus populations of the Bogotá de la Universidad Nacional de Colombia, where the snake is affected
