# THE HAMMER, THE TREE, AND THE FOREST: AN INTRODUCTION TO THE ZOOM IN - ZOOM OUT¿ (ZIZO) STANCE IN ERGONOMICS

The context of our Ergonomics and Human Factors (EFH) interventions is vitally important. Ergonomists are invited because someone (i.e., the organization, owner, key stakeholder) has defined a problem to be solved. In the parlance we used earlier), these are Matters of Fact (MoF). We are compensated for solving these hypothesized or causal relationships among actors in the workplace. We bring our ergonomics theories and tools, which have a strong track record for resolving the predetermined MoF. While our knowledge and experiences are valuable, they can also be limiting. Those in the system may recognize the MoF but also have their own interests, Matters of Concern (MoC). If we fail to uncover these different matters, we may fail in our attempts to improve the system. In other words, it is limiting to define the problem if it has been predetermined by the stakeholder that invites the ergonomist. Some authors call this process re-defining the request. The objective is to introduce a structured but flexible stance that enables looking in and out, back and forth, for understanding MoC in relation to MoF, in order to enhance the possibilities of understanding and intervening in working systems. This is a qualitative-interpretative work that builds up from the practical experience of two experts in ergonomics with different backgrounds, an ethnographer and an organizational behavior professional. The discussions included how to deal with MoF in relation to MoC, and tackled the challenges faced by professionals oriented to making decisions (designers, managers) and professionals focused on understanding the situation (ethnographers, researchers). Ergonomists are in the middle, and the key point is to answer the right questions. However, many of the problems to be solved are often complex i.e., lack clear and concise causality; little or no opportunity to do trial and error because situations constantly change and every try changes the system; there are multiple elements (stakeholders) who interact and have unpredictable behaviors within the bounds of the system dynamic; there is uniqueness and interconnectedness, among others. We propose the ¿zoom in and zoom out¿ (ZIZO) stance. We understand it as a semi-open, semi-closed process, that through an open-minded approach, pragmatism, and a constrained number of iterations, focuses the attention of the researcher / ergonomist in different scales of the situation to ¿capture¿ MoC beyond MoF. A ZIZO stance moves between ergonomics and ethnography, moving back and forth in a controlled and emergent way, observing behaviors, detecting patterns, and inferring practices. To be able to face this process, the professional needs to develop certain skills, and the situation needs to fulfill certain conditions, at least to a certain degree. Upon initial entry into a new complex environment (like a forest), it is useful to have basic skills, knowledge and tools to survive, and if we are there for the specific purpose of solving a problem. If we narrowly focus on a predetermined problem (a tree) we will zoom in on the problem and come armed with a specific tool (a hammer). If we do, we may miss the bigger picture of that tree, its relationship to other trees (patterns), and the forest (practices). Moreover, the preselection of the tool causes the user to look for its use; if you are a hammer, everything looks like a nail. Alternatively, we can enter the forest with our stance and first, simply observe the larger ecosystem, the elements of the system and how they interact. Build these into working hypotheses, then test and revise these ideas before deciding a course of action and what you will need to achieve that goal
