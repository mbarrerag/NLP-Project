# Evaluation and characterization of commercial fungi and proteolytic bacterial consortium applied for rice straw degradation

The agricultural waste represents a primary source of essential nutrients for the soil. The decomposition of this material in the field introduces a series of compounds and nutrients that improve soil fertility. Rice straw (RS) is an important residue in the rice context, and its application as a multitil during the fallow periods is a common practice. The incorporation of microbial consortia capable of degrading rice straw (RS) during the operations of covering the soil with rice straw has the potential to improve the cyclaje and the mobilization of nutrients. In vitro experiments were carried out to elucidate the interactions between several consortia. On the basis of these results, a consortium (the commercial consortium of Trichoderma plus the bacterial strain IBUN 2717) was selected to further investigate. The results demonstrated the effectiveness of the consortium selected as a degrader and indicated the presence of minimum antagonistic relationships between its fungal and bacterial members.
