# Evaluation of chemical and thermal methods to improve some physical and mechanical properties in concretes made with polymeric aggregate

The manufacture of concretes and mortars using Cemento Portland Ordinary material (OPC) requires significant quantities of natural petreate. Natural petrea aggregates, derived from quarrying, in particular those obtained by washing techniques, show adverse effects on natural ecosystems, including water sources, air quality and deforestation. Consequently, there is a pressing need to investigate new materials capable of replacing natural aggregates. This study proposes the use of residues of catalyzed polyester resin, a byproduct of the manufacture of poles for electrical networks, as a possible substitute for natural aggregates in concrete. Despite previous demonstrations indicating that the use of these resins and plastic materials as aggregates results in a decrease of the resistance to compression of concrete, the efforts are directed towards treatments aimed at improving the interaction between polymeric aggregate and cement paste. To achieve this objective, both MPA and SBR were systematically investigated both chemical alternatives (polymeric modification with latex and SB) as thermal alternatives (post-cured to temperatures of 23, 50, 80 and 110 oC).
