# Meshing methodology for numerical simulations of turbulent flows at low computational cost.

In this research work, we built parametric structured computational meshes for the numerical solution of two turbulent flow cases. The main purpose of this work was to analyze the influence of certain mesh parameters, in the numerical prediction of turbulent flow variables. To this end, UDNS simulations were carried out with low computational cost meshes, which were defined from mesh parameters such as mesh sizes, aspect ratios and refinement sections. The first selected case consisted of a supersonic circular jet ejected from a nozzle, for compressible flow; and the second case of interest was based on an internal flow through a channel with an abrupt section change, for uncompressable flow. First and second order turbulence statistics obtained with numerical experiments performed using different mesh topologies were analyzed, but the results were validated with experimental data and numerical results SLE and DNS reported by other researchers. It was found that by means of the parameter adjustment of the mesh, it is possible to obtain spatial discrepancies of low computational cost, but that allow for the prediction of such numerical results
