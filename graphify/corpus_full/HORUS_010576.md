# ABBUNDANCE IN CIRCUJAN FISH POPULATIONS (ACANTHURIIDAE) IN AN INSULAR ENVIRONMENT OF COLOMBIAN CARIBBEAN

In coral communities, herbivorous fish play a major role in the maintenance and control of algae that are the basis of the trophic chain in coral reefs. Among the herbivorous fish of coral reefs, the Acanthuridae family is among the most abundant. The effects on the reefs of this family may vary according to their densities, the stability of the ecosystem and their interactions, so determining the state of their populations is of interest. In the archipelago of San Andrés, Providencia and Santa Catalina (ASAP) lack information on the current state of abundances and densities of this group. Therefore, by visual censuses of species of the genus Acanthurus the population states in coral communities of the ASAP were estimated and the population differences between the sites evaluated (San Andrés, Cayo Bolívar island, Cayo Serrana Island and Providencia), which evidenced a total of 6075 individuals besides a positive relationship of abundances in the islands of San Andrés and Providencia with respect to the islands of the Bolivar and Serrana cayos.
