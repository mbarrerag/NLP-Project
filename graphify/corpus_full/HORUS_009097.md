# CONMEMORATIONS AND REPRESENTATIONS OF BOLCHEVIQUE REVOLUTION — THE RED OCTOBER OF COLOMBIAN COMMUNICISTS (1920-1938)

This chapter studies the commemoration of the Bolshevik Revolution and its enthronement as part of the revolutionary ritual calendar of Colombian socialists and communists, between the 1920s and 1940s. Mainly, it seeks to answer three basic questions that serve as the structuring axes of the text: What is it commemorated? How is it commemorated? What are the main symbols associated with the celebration? The first question asks both for the experience and for the horizon of meaning that social actors gave to the October Revolution and the changes experienced in line with the development of the workers' movement and the left. The second question analyzes the forms that the commemoration takes: parades, evenings and political acts, identifying the characteristic aspects associated with the political culture of the lefts, and the limits that the repression imposed on the development of public commemorations. Finally, to answer the third question, the figure of Lenin and the red flag is examined as symbols for excellence of the revolution
