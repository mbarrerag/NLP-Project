# Impact of the load model on the dynamic voltage performance of an electrical power system

This thesis evaluates the impact of different load models on the dynamic performance of voltage in electrical power systems subjected to disturbances, using the IEEE system of 39 bars as a case study and performing simulations in the software DIGSILENT PowerFactory. Several models are explored, including the model WECC (Western Electricity Coordinating Council), Model ZIP (Constant, Current and Constant Power), Model Exponential, Model Complex and the combination of Induction Motor with ZIP model. The load models were parametrized by optimising their parameters using the case-scale Nelder-Mead, comparing the dynamic response of each model with that of the WECC model. The study focuses on how the choice of the system specific dynamics that others fail to represent. This analysis highlights the practical relevance of the load modeling in effective conditions of the models, guiding the critical importance of selecting and adequately parameterizing the load models, demonstrating that each model has the ability to capture specific dynamics of the system that others fail to represent. The study focuses on assessment how the choice of load model influences the voltage response of the power system during failures. The results highlight the critical importance of proliferation selection and parameterization of load models, demonstrating that each model has the capacity to capture specific system dynamics that others may fail to represent. This analysis underscores the practical survey of load modelling for voltage stability and guide the implementation of more incurable and effective models in dynamic analyses of power systems. In doing so, it contributes to a more reliable modelling of power systems, enhancing more dependent system planning
