# HYDROCOLOID ADDITION EFFECT (HYDROOXIPROPILMETHYLCHELLULOSE HPMC AND GOMA XANTAN) AND TREHALOSE ON GLUTEN-FREE PAN QUALITY

The breadmaking industry faces the challenge of waste due to the rapid loss of sensory properties, especially in gluten-free products. These products, replaced by gluten-free flours, have a reduced useful life and present challenges in their formulation. To address this, technologies such as the use of frozen masses and the addition of technological ingredients to preserve the mass and quality of bread have been explored. Trehalose, a natural disaccharide, and hydrocholoids, polysaccharides and proteins acting as colloids in water, are additives that can improve the viscosic and structural properties of bread. However, their interaction with other ingredients can significantly alter the mass. Therefore, it is essential to study the effects of these additives in different matrices to validate their functionality. In a recent study, three additives were used independently: hydroxypropylmethylcellulose, xantane rubber and trehalose, in the formulation of bread molded rice flour. The aim was to compare the results with each additive and a control to evaluate their impact on the quality and useful life of bread.
