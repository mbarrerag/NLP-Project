# Crafts at the crossroads of global tourism

This work aims to analyze the relationship between tourism and the transformation of the artisan practices in Ráquira, Colombia. Through a qualitative approach, interviews were constructed that were carried out to two local craftsmen and were complemented by an exhaustive review of the literature. The design of the research allowed to identify how the growing tourist demand for souvenirs has influenced traditional techniques of pottery, transforming local practices. The study site was Ráquira, known as the "artificial capital of Colombia", where tourism has been gaining a fundamental relevance, and where there is a growing cultural mercantilization. To analyze this phenomenon, the perspectives of artisans in the face of these transformations were explored and framed in processes of heritage and global tourism. The results show that, while tourism has generated economic benefits, it has also imposed pressures that affect the cultural sustainability of handicrafts.
