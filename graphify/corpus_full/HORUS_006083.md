# PRESENCE AND AOUTANCE OF PEOPLE IN TWO HAIRS OF JOSENE MARIA ARZUAGA

This research paper analyzes how the first two films by Spanish director José María Arzuaga, filmed in Colombia in the 1960s, build a very particular look at Colombian social reality. Arzuaga filmed these works in the period between two years that have been considered opening or closing in national film trends: 1961 is conceived as the closing of the renewal of Colombian cinema in the middle of the century; 1966 has been catalogued as the beginning of the break provoked by explicitly militant cinematography. Moreover, the director arrived in Colombia in 1960, in the first stage of the National Front (1958-1974), an alliance between liberal and conservative parties, the most powerful and traditional, aimed at alternating the presidency and distributing the bureaucracy millimeterly. Arzuaga was thus in no one's land in the political and film industry. This article shows how Arzuaga presents the absence of political and social consciousness in those who are exploited by developmentalism in the country, and how this is expressed aesthetically in the making of his works
