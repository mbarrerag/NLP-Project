# The rule of the right to water and food security in the Afro-Colombian community of the Pacific Coast

The social injustice rooted in the African diaspora in Colombia has left a persistent footprint at present, generating conditions of vulnerability and inequality. The Colombian Pacific Coast, despite its wealth in biodiversity and water resources, reflects these social and economic gaps. This research proposes the hypothesis of a direct association between the violation of the right to water and the food insecurity in the Afro-Colombian community of the Pacific Coast. And the objective is to explore this association, using a quantitative descriptive approach. In the quantitative phase from the National Survey of Nutritional Situation (ENSIN) 2015, the data on (in)security and access to water of 1,595 households with Afro-descending headship of the Pacific Coast were analyzed. To enrich this data, in the qualitative phase, five interviews were conducted with Afro-Colombian social leaders and knowledgeable local context, to characterize some knowledge and experiences around the right to water and their relation to food security. As a result, it was revealed that 78.6% of the Afro-Colombian population studied experiences food insecurity and the 33.3% do not have access to the aqueduct. It is a significant association between the violation of the right and the need of water. partnership using a CUANTI-quali sequential descriptive exploration approach.
