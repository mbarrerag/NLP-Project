# Dielectric behavior of Ho3+-doped Y3Fe5O12 garnet-type ceramics at high temperatures

High-quality Y 3-x Ho x Fe 5 O 12 (x = 0, 0.1, 0.5, 0.7) ceramics were prepared via solid-state reaction. The monophasic garnet-cubic-spinel lattice formation of the pristine Y 3 Fe 5 O 12 and the successful substitution of Y 3+ with Ho 3+ ions were verified via analysis of XRD patterns using Rietveld refinement. The substitution generated a small but sizeable structural lattice distortion, which seemed to be concomitant with slight variations in the values of the Fe(a)-O-Fe(d) bond lengths and bond angles. The dielectric properties were carefully studied in the 300–553 K temperature range and the 40 Hz–5 MHz frequency range using complex impedance spectroscopy. The results showed two relaxation processes taking place at the grain boundaries and grains. The relaxation seemed to be controlled by thermally-activated charges. The substitution of Y 3+ with Ho 3+ ions shifted the peaks associated with the grain boundaries towards lower frequencies. Although the complex impedance spectra showed depressed semicircles, careful cross-examination of permittivity, electric modulus, and impedance representations suggested that the relaxation processes were mainly Debye-type. The pristine sample showed relatively large dielectric dispersion (∼10 2 ) at 300 K and low frequency, probably due to the electronic exchange between Fe 3+ and Fe 2+ centers. The energy activation for hopping of charge carriers across grain boundaries and within the grains was determined from the fit of the Arrhenius-type law to the impedance, electric modulus, and dielectric loss (Tanδ) data. The values of the activation energy for the hopping across grain boundaries turned out to be higher than those for the hopping within the grains. No anomalies in the conductivity and relaxation frequency were observed near the ferromagnetic transition temperature, which pointed to a weak magneto-electric coupling effect. The small polaron hopping model was fit to the DC resistivity data recorded at temperatures below 300 K. The nature of the hopping conduction and the strong electron-phonon interaction was confirmed
