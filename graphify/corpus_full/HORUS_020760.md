# Goodbye to the POT: Conceptual system of territorial computers for the Savannah of the Bogotá River

This Thesis develops an idea on territorial power from two denunciations: one, by Orlando Fals Borda, according to which, in Colombia, one cannot think regionally because politicians and owners are not willing to yield their territorial power; and another, by Raymond Unwin, according to which, as long as there is no institutional power over urban growth, the demons of construction anywhere will continue. For Bogotá, the problem is manifested in that part of its growth occurs in neighbouring territories, which leads to the paradox of a capital whose future is beyond its control, in a region of which much is spoken and by little is done. As the problem arises in the municipalist system of territorial planning, the logical solution is to replace it with a regionalist system. To achieve this, it requires the Congress of the Republic to create a territory and a system of planning, which is now nonexistent. For this, the Thesis proposes a system of six concepts through which to structure such creation: i) a geographical-territorial concept, Sabana of the Bogotá river; ii) a concept of regional planning, common reserves; iii) a regional concept of planning, a regional planning and planning; ads to the paradox of a Capital City which future is out of its own control, in a region that which is widely talked about and for which little is donated. As this problem originates in the municipalist system of land use planning, the logical solution would be to replace it with a regionalist system.
