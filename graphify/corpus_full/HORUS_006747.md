# Comparison of methodologies used to address the problem of missing data in longitudinal studies

This study compared different methodologies used in the literature to address the problem of missing data in longitudinal studies. Three conditions were considered proposed by \cite{rubin1976} for the generation of missing data: Lost Completely to the Azar (MCAR), Lost to the Azar (MAR) and Not Lost to the Azar (NMAR). Two longitudinal databases were used from the Open Data Portal of the Colombian State, which were adjusted by mixed linear models. Subsequently, missing data were generated under the three above mentioned conditions and different methods of imputation were applied. The models were compared using the Mean Squared Error (RMSE) and it was found that the choice of the method of imputation could have an impact on the interpretation of the model's findings and recommendations were made for further research. In addition, the variability in the accuracy of the model by department was analyzed and significant differences were found between the methods of imputation. It was concluded that the choice of the method of imputation could have an impact on the interpretation of the model's findings and the results were made for future research. and different imputation methods were applied. The imputed models were compared using the Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) and it was observed that the Last Observation Carried Forward method (LOCF) performed better in most cases. In addition, the variability in model accuracy by department was analyzed and significant differences were found between the imputation methods. It was concluded that the choice of imputation method may have an impact on the interpretation of model results and recommendations for future research were made, such as exploring other imputation methods and considering the impact of imputation on the assurance of model predictions in longitudinal studies. In summary, this study highlights the importance were made, such as explaining other imputation methods and considered the impact of imputation on the accessibility of model predictions in longitudinal studies
