# MISCELLANEOUS CITIZENS: HYP HOP AND CITIZEN TRAINING AT COMMON 13 MEDELLIN

The bombings against the civilian population, promoted by the Colombian State in alliance with paramilitary groups (2001-2002), marked the history of Commune 13, popular territory located on the slopes of the city of Medellín. Comuna 13 has faced processes of structural, political and criminal violence for almost 50 years. Its stigmatization as a space in conflict has justified military incursions as anti-subversive strategies. More recently, urban interventions based on the model of social urbanism, promoted by the municipal government, have sought to pacify the population through sophisticated infrastructures. This chapter aims to analyze the forces and folds of power present in the citizen's practices. Based on a methodology that links ethnographic work with microhistory, the research reveals the configuration of divergent citizenships in hip hop groups. The chapter shows how they have been configured by factors such as disputes for public spaces as political scenarios, interpellations to images of legitimate citizens, claims of rights anchored in territorial history, experiences of citizenship linked to the body and emotions, experiences linked to collective work, and the defense of universal claims of the world
