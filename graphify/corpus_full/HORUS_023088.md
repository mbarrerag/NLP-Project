# PETROGRAPHICAL CHARACTERIZATION OF THE CALCOSILICATED ROCKS OF THE ALLELUY COMPLEJE

In the axial zone of the Central Cordillera of Colombia, in the municipalities of Neiva, Palermo, Santa Maria and Teruel, department of Huila, the unit called Complex Aleluya, which is constituted by metamorphic rocks pseudoestracified, mainly by marbles and in a smaller proportion by metarenites and quartzites cut eventually by dioritic and and anesitic diques. In this study, through the characterization of twenty samples collected in seven mines belonging to the Aleluye Complex, by means of petrographic analysis and geochemical analysis, these materials are classified into four lithodrals: Pure calcitic marbles, which are characterized by a size of medium grain, are composed by more than 90% of maclad calcita in paragenis with tremolite; Calcitic marbles with dolomite, are a size of fine grain, are anhedrales with subhedrales and are composed by more than 95 % of calcita and domite, the calcita being the largest proportion; The dikes can be correlated to the Cuarzomonzodiorita de Páez, given their calcoalcal character. Furthermore, according to the Tectonic environment discrimination diagrams, these intrusives were generated in an environment of volcanic arc. Impure calcitic marbles present mineral paragenesis of the type Calcita (with signs of deformation) ± Tremolite ± Olivino, whose petrographic characteristics allow to deduce an event of regional metamorphism, which was preceded by a meta-synthetic event of dolomitization recorded in the Calcitic Marbles with dolomite. Subsequently, the metamorphic sequences were instructed by the dikes without generating a substantial change in the mineralological composition of the marbles
