# INTERCONEXION TO POTENCE NETWORKS

The power system has evolved due to external causes, some technical and economic and others due to socio-economic and environmental factors. Such changes affect the quality of the electricity service and therefore to the end consumers. Such a transition from the electrical system, which is currently based on fossil fuels, to a modern system requires development, design and planning, which allows for managing the distribution of energy generated on the basis of demand and the uncertainties inherent in the raw material. An alternative to managing this system is the aggregation of distributed generations (GD), microgrids (MR), storage systems (ESS), demand management controls and controllable loads near energy consumption points. There are strong incentives for the use of distributed generations or the development of micronetworks interconnected to the network, to reduce greenhouse gases and improve the efficiency of the electricity system, such as its reliability, quality, competitive energy policies, participating actively in the electricity market and postponing the improvement of the transmission and distribution system, taking into account that for each new demand or point of consumption in the region or area, an infrastructure is needed for the transport of energy
