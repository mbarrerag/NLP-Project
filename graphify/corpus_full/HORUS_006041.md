# The Magdalena River: settlement, trade and daily life, 1560-1639

This research studies the way how the Magdalena river and its tributaries became the main routes of transport and the axis of the articulators of the economy of the New Kingdom. The chosen period extends between 1560 and 1639, since this period allows to analyze not only the consolidation of Hispanic societies along its watershed, but also the transformations result of this population, like the native population and the modifications that were established around the work of the Indians. In the development of this research were used documentary sources of the General Archive of the Nation (Bogotá) and the General Archives of the Indies (Sevilla), among these highlights the review of several Visits of the Earth and Ordinances. Throughout the investigation it was possible to show the definitive establishment of the Magdalena river as a way of transport of goods and of people work between the Caribbean and the interior of the New Kingdom; and the transformations that were generated in the population and the economy that facilitated the development of the fluvial trade. This problem was addressed from several fronts: population and conquest of the river, trade and daily life.
