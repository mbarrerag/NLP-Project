# Health and work conditions in the development of telehabilitation activities: A perspective of Colombian professionals in occupational therapy in times of pandemic and post-pandemy

With the declaration of pandemic by COVID-19 in March 2020, different aspects of the daily life of the people were transformed, among which the work process is included in its different forms and conditions. Moreover, in the health sector, new work was implemented in the context of this new reality, seeking to facilitate the promotion and care of health remotely. Therefore, mechanisms such as Telemedicine or Telesalud, with origin in the mid-20th century, were positioned as an alternative of great importance, permeating the different areas of the health sector, including occupational therapy, with the adoption of the Telerehabilitation modality. Faced with the transformation of the world of work and health, it is the workers who must face the changes in the working conditions and health, putting in tension their professional work and the way they relate to the work process and the environment. Methodologically, this research is placed under the critical approach of the Workers' Health (ST) of the Latin American Social Medicine (MSL), look that defines the working process as a main determinant of the health-disease process collective and individual, placing the worker. social and social conditions, including the work processes in its different forms and conditions. On the other hand, in the health sector, novel intervention modalities had to be implemented in response to this new reality, seeing to facilitate health promotion and care remotely. As a result, mechanisms such as Telemedicine or Telehealth, which originated in the mid-twenties century, had posited those elements as high imported alternative, permeating variable areas of the health sector, including Occupational Therapy, with the adoption of Telerehabilitation structure. It highlights the risks of new work forms, the implications for occupational tasks, and the management of health, work, and employment conditions that contribute to the precariousness of the health-work process in the context of a health crisis. Likewise, it also identifies the potential of Telerehabilitation as a useful modality for managing health care during emergency periods, which would require the assurance of differentiated health and working conditions for its proper implementation, managing the feedback of all actors involved
