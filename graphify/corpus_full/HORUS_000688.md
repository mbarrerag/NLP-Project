# Sequential experimentation orders in factory designs

A basic principle of experimental design is to carry out experimentation in a completely random way, with the aim that the possible effects generated by external factors do not affect estimates. However, this approach may be complicated and costly, due to the resulting amount of level changes in factors, besides that a random order does not guarantee the elimination of bias. In this doctoral thesis, a proposal is presented to construct experimental sequences in complete and fractional factor designs, seeking a balance between decreasing the number of level changes and the bias associated with unknown factors. This approach, called the method of allocation-expansion, adapts the problem of linear programming to generate the systems, and then extends the properties obtained to designs with a greater number of factors and levels.
