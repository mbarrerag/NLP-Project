# The general anti-tax abuse clause as a legal and constitutional limit to the freedom of business in Colombia.

The general anti-abuse clause provided for in Article 869 of the Tax Statute is a mechanism that allows the State to ignore and reclassify the effects of legal acts or businesses that taxpayers celebrate with the aim of avoiding, reducing or modifying their taxes; and thus to require payment of the taxes not caused. However, this figure has been of little use to the Directorate of National Taxes and Customs -DIAN - since its scope is in the grey zone between legitimate planning of business activity and tax avoidance or tax fraud, so that there is a dispute between the right of entrepreneurs to plan their business activity and the tax administration's control activity in respect of the scenarios for applying the general anti-abuse clause. Delimiting the scope of application of the general anti-abuse clause in Colombia goes through examining its application in other countries of Latin America and the European Union, for the purpose of concluding that it can be conceived as a tool for State intervention in the economy, by limiting the autonomy of the private contractual will and the freedom of a company with criteria of reasonableness and proportionality
