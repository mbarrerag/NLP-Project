# BOND STRENGTH OF UNIVERSAL ADHESIVES TO DENTIN TREATED WITH CROSSLINKERS

To evaluate the effect of the dentin collagen crosslinkers, Myricetin (MYR) and Proanthocyanidin (PAC), on the bond strength of a universal adhesive used with self-etching technique. Exposed dentin from the occlusal surface of 20 healthy human third molars collected under informed consent was used. The experimental groups were: (G1) negative control: conventional technique following the manufacturer's instructions with Tetric N-Bond Universal adhesive (Ivoclar); (G2) vehicle control: 100% ethanol; (G3) crosslinking control: 1% glutaraldehyde [GA]; (G4) proanthocyanidin [PAC] 6.5%; (G5) myricetin [MYR] at 600 uM all applied for 120 s. After the surface treatments, and the application of the adhesive in self-etch mode, composite resins (Tetric N-Ceram, Ivoclar) of 4 mm height were built, and light-curing of 20 s/layer (Bluephase C8). Restored teeth were sectioned to obtain sticks (1 x 1 x 8 mm) for microtensile bond strength (μTBS) testing. Half of the specimens (n=15) were tested on a universal testing machine (0.5 mm/min) after 24 h storage at 37°C in relative humidity, and the other half of the samples (n=15) after 10,000 thermocycling cycles. The type of failure of the bonded interface was qualitatively evaluated by stereomicroscopy. The data were analyzed using ANOVA and Tukey tests to determine significant differences between groups (p 0.05). After thermal aging, G1 and G2 showed a significant decrease in μTBS of approximately 50%; while GA, PAC and MYR maintained the μTBS value close to 80% compared to the immediate values (p>0.05). The use of crosslinkers contributed to a significant reduction in adhesive failure mode. The effect of the pretreatment with crosslinking agents did not affect the μTBS to dentin and contributed to preserve the values after a prolonged thermal aging process. Collagen crosslinkers can be an alternative to improve the behavior of the adhesive interface with the use of universal adhesive systems with self-etch technique
