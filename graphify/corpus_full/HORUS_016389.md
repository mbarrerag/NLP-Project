# Search for Mycobacterium TB CtpF membrane transporter inhibitors by virtual screening based on pharmacophorus and in vitro activity determination.

The emergence of strains of Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb), multi-resistant and extremely resistant to drugs, and the co-infection with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), have dramatically increased mortality from tuberculosis (TB) in the world. This situation forces the need to find alternative therapeutic targets and the identification of new more effective drugs against TB. In this sense, the TAPase membrane proteins type P, could be relevant targets, since they are known are relevant in the ionic homeostasis and in the survival of Mtb during infection. CtpF, a CtpF, a plasma membrane ca2+ ATPase, is a protein that is activated in conditions of hypoxia, oxidative stress and infection, suggesting that this transporter is part of the strategies used by the tuberculosus bacillus to evade the immune response. Considering the importance of CtfpF in the viability of the bacilla tuberculosus, in this work the search for new compounds with potential inhibitive activity of the transport of Ca2+ mediated by CtpF. In this work, the analysis of the CtpF was based on the in the functional mode of the Two of these compounds on the activity Ca2+-ATPase of membrane vesicles showed that ZINC09731847 (IC50 = 7.6 μM) and ZINC04030361 (IC50 = 3.3 μM) effectively inhibit CtpF activity in plasma membrane. The results of CMI (25,0 μg/mL) activity Ca2+-ATPase, cytotoxic activity (27,2%) on vero cells and hemolysis on human red blood cells (0,2%), helped select the compound ZINC04030361 as the most active candidate against plasma membrane ATPase CtpF. The results obtained in this work represent a step forward in the search and development of new anti-TB compounds targeted at alternative targets, such as membrane proteins involved in the ionic homeostasis of Mtb
