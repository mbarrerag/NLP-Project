# A method for obtaining formal software specifications from knowledge representation languages

Software analysts use knowledge representation languages to characterize knowledge coming from stakeholders in the engineering phase of requirements. These languages comprise software models based on unified modeling language and structured methods, including entity-relationship diagrams, class diagrams, cause-effect diagrams and conceptual graphs. While these models are used to represent basic features of a software system effectively, these models still have limitations in representing behavior and components of complex software systems. Formal specifications include text, mathematics and logically based notations to represent knowledge related to a specific domain. Software analysts use formal specifications to mitigate the impact of the ambiguity inherent in traditional knowledge representation languages, facilitating computational processes, inference and non-ambiguous knowledge validation. However, the generation and validation of formal knowledge specifications requires experience from mathematical and logical fields of software and stakeholders. Software analysts use effective communication and validation of the software domain. In this Doctorate Thesis, we propose a novel approach to close the gap between formal languages and the language of representation of knowledge.
