# Issallation and CHARACTERIZATION OF COLOMBIAN ENDEMIC ENDEMIC CAPACITY OF DEGRADING TOLUENE ISOLATION AND CHARACTERIZATION OF ENDEMIC CAPABLE BACCTERIA OF DEGRADING TOLUENE

The monocyclic aromatic hydrocarbons: benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene and xylene (BTEX), present in crude and refined oil, are part of the compounds with the most impact on the environment and human health, due to their cancer-genous, mutagenic and highly toxic nature. This research aimed to obtain and characterize bacteria capable of degrading toluene. Three samplings of soil contaminated with hydrocarbons from the Cauca Valley were carried out in three conditions: diesel, accidental spillage and mechanical workshop. Bacteria capable of growing in steam phase toluene were evaluated as the sole source of carbon and were characterized at the morphological, biochemical and molecular level. For molecular characterisation, the biologically-based bacteria were amplified, dried and analyzed with bioinformatics tools the 16S ribosomal gene. The use of direct toluene with 1% concentration as the sole source of carbon was evaluated. 29 bacteria with the capacity to metabolize toluene were managed. Biochemical and mole-cular characterisation identified with the isolated bacteria of soil as Ptropomona and isolated soils were found to be the best source of carbon
