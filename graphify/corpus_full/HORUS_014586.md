# Identification and quantification of polymorphs by X-Ray diffraction, in-situ ramp thermal for: Febuxostat, Nimesulide, Clindamycin Phosphate and Carvedilol

The study of the thermal properties of active principles, as well as the identification and quantification of polymorphs, is a common practice in the pharmaceutical industry. The examination of the behavior of active principles under thermal treatment, such as mass loss and the identification of phase transitions, transition temperatures, or the presence of exothermic or endothermic processes, determines the stability of the active principle and indicates the appropriate pharmaceutical form for medications. Thermodynamic properties were investigated using Thermogravimetric Analysis (TGA), coupled with Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC), in order to identify a wide range of processes: dehydration, re-crystallization, phase change (polymorphs), structural breakdown, molecule degradation, carbon loss, and melting point. Morphological analysis was conducted using Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) of the active principles, qualitative analysis of the active molecule, and the identification of functional groups and rotations through Infrared Absorption Spectroscopy (IR). The structure and quantification of the active principle and polymorphs were studied through X-ray Diffraction for all stages of thermal treatment. The temperatures of transformations were successfully identified, and X-Pert Pro will be programmed in a thermal chamber (in-situ), capturing diffraction patterns that allow us to identify the appearance of polymorphs and any other transformation identified in thermal characterization techniques. Four active principles were studied: Febuxostat, Nimesulide, Clindamycin Phosphate, and Carvedilol, with the aim of mastering characterization techniques, meeting international standards, studying the infrastructure available at the Faculty of Sciences of the National University, and determining the capacity to offer a comprehensive drug analysis service to the pharmaceutical industry
