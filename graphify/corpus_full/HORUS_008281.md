# AUDIT SELVA: FINANCE, BUROCRACY AND DISABILITY IN JOSEL'S VORAGINE

This article argues that the aesthetics of the imbalance of La vorágine by José Eustasio Rivera points to the impossibility of disciplining the accumulation of capital through writing. Throughout the nineteenth century, the Creoles imagined that money and lyrics, guided by the invisible hand, would be able to manage the rebellious American matter and turn it harmoniously into values of change. In opposition to this liberal dogma, the formal experimentation of Rivera’s novel, published in the euphoric 20s, suggests that capitalism is not a balanced system as its apologists argue, but a turbulent whirlwind characterized by its volatility. Arturo Cova’s unstable daily parody of futures promised by financial prospects and evidences the limits of capital by showing that it, even when unleashed with more severity, encounters a materiality that stubbornly refuses to be reduced to an abstract unit of account
