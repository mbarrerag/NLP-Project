# High-performance Zn-Zr-Al(O) mixed oxides catalysts for CO2 sustainable cycloaddition with cyclohexene oxide

The cycloaddition of CO2 with cyclohexene oxide provides a promising method to convert CO2 into valuable products, such as cyclohexane carbonate, helping to reduce greenhouse gases. However, this process often relies on costly, nonrecoverable homogeneous catalysts. In this study, Zn-Zr-Al heterogeneous catalysts derived from layered double hydroxides were synthesized by co-precipitation. The Zn/Zr molar ratio significantly affected the physicochemical properties of the catalysts, such as the crystallite size, surface area, CO2 adsorption, and surface carbonate species. Higher Zr content improved surface area, microporosity, CO2 capture, and catalytic performance. Under solvent-free conditions (50 bar CO2, 70 °C, 24 h), low conversions (<20 %) were achieved without a cocatalyst; however, the addition of tetrabutylammonium bromide (TBAB) as a cocatalyst increased the conversion to 40 %. The optimal Zn/Zr ratio of 3 resulted in conversions of 88.8–97.6 % and selectivities of 95.8–96.1 % at 85–100 °C. Increasing the pressure (10–30 bar) boosted the conversion to 97.2 % with a selectivity of 97.1 %. The catalyst maintained a conversion of 94.3–96.2 % and selectivities of 94.6–95.9 % over three cycles, demonstrating its potential as a stable, reusable, and eco-friendly system for CO2 cycloaddition
