# Critical review of the benchmarks for the construction of procedural guarantees in transitional justice and post-conflict criminal law scenarios.

The country's penal system has contributed to the social and violent conflict in Colombia in the twentieth century and the course of the twenty-first century, and therefore the effective exercise of transitional justice should not only be concentrated on investigating and judging itself with strict adherence to the guarantees associated with due process and those responsible for international crimes committed in the context of the armed conflict, but should also allow for a critical review of the deficit role that the judiciary has played in managing conflict, as well as deepening the scope and content of various procedural guarantees instrumentalized in this type of scenario, such as the principle of legality, whose courses have been relaxed in the light of the study of the causes of the conflict attributable to the functioning of the criminal system, the rule of law and the affirmation of the validity of human rights. The strengthening of procedural guarantees associated with due process, the result of a profound critical review of these legal and criminal institutions, in light of the study of the causes of the conflict attributable to the functioning of the criminal system, can allow the consolidation of structures of material legitimacy that contribute not only to the success of the transition, but to the functionalization of the system of the ordinary penal orientation and the post-conflict, the national consolidation of the conflict
