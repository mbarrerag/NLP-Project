# Phylogeny of the family Withiidae (Arachnida: Pseudoscorpions) and evolution of the male reproductive system

The evolution of the animal genitals has been an interesting research topic since Darwin proposed that sexual selection acts on the morphological traits. In this context, taking the pseudoscorpions of the family Withhiidae as model to study the influence of the selective pressures on the variation of the male genitals, 1) we characterize the morphology of the male genitals proposing declarations of homology between genders after a detailed examination of specimens, 2) we explore the internal phylogenetic relationships of the family Withhiidae using morphological and molecular characteristics, 3) we date the divergence of the clades using three fossils as calibration points, and 4) we explore the rates of change of the characters of the male genitals. We find a strong support for the monophilia of Withidae as well as for two internal clades, the neotropical group and the non-neotropical ones; as for the brother group, there is no conclusive evidence as the molecular data suggest Atemnesidae, but the combined data, morphological, point to Cheliferidae. The results suggest that the latter arose in the group.
