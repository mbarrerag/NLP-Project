# Development of a new integrated assessment methodology to design and select the best strategies for reducing emissions from urban passenger transport.

In cities of emerging countries, demand for passenger transport has proliferated, creating socially and environmentally unsustainable transport systems. Finding the best transport options for these communities raises substantial problems for those responsible for transport policies. Thus, the main objective of this thesis was to develop a new methodology for integrated assessment (MEI) to design and select the best strategies for reducing emissions for urban passenger transport. This MEI considers the inventory of direct emissions, the inventory of indirect emissions, the total cost of ownership and strategies for reducing emissions. First, the estimate of the inventory of direct emissions of air pollutants (PM2.5, NOx, SO2, CO) and greenhouse gases (CO2-Eq), includes emissions from exost, and from abrasion and resuspension or Wear and Resuspension (W&R). The latter does not usually include in the existing MEI. In this work the COPERT model was adapted to local conditions to estimate direct emissions from combustion and EMEP and EPA methodologies to estimate emissions from W&R. Second, the inventory of indirect emissions from the current or future economy is the basis of the life. The main contribution of this research is the development of a more effective MEI for policy makers to address solutions around different modes of transport in environmental and economic terms
