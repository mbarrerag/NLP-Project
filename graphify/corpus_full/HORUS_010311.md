# BIOMINERALIZATION AS A TOOL IN THE REFUPERATION OF PROPERTIES OF A COMPETITED RESIDUAL SOLE UNDER STORAGE

The soils of the tropic region, when experiencing marked climatic variability, are exposed to strong periods of drought that make them highly susceptible to forest fires. Added to this, the anthropic interventions, whether by renewal of crops, controlled burns, or accidental fires, affect them strongly. Colombia is not alien to this problem, and a large portion of the territory presents an important degree of susceptibility to forest fires. Extreme temperature variations cause changes in some of the geotechnical properties, but the vast majority of research does not focus its efforts on the study of the effect of such changes on the hydromechanical behavior, and much less on the application of regenerative treatments for the recovery of these soils in an environmentally friendly way. Therefore, in the current chapter, a methodology is proposed that allows analysis and determination in laboratory, by adding a B4 medium to the native bacteria, the effect of such treatment on the geotechnical properties of a compacted soil, subjected to the maximum temperature that can record a soil in a fire.
