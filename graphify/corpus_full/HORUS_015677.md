# A SECRET MISSION FOR JAMAICA TOME. IMPERIAL AGENTS AND SPIES IN THE CARIBBEAN CONTEXT, 1780-1781.

In this chapter the idea we defend is that, in the middle of Anglo-Spanish confrontation in the second half of the eighteenth century, the Hispanic empire made bold military and espionage efforts to know closely the British imperial functioning and the role played by its colonies in the Caribbean to maintain a flourishing economy. To this end, the crown deployed a body of sailors, spies and officials illustrated in different spaces of the Caribbean and its coasts that were able to unravel the British military capabilities and their successful ways of increasing wealth and increasing their productive capacity. To this end, the crown deployed a group of sailors, spies and enlightened officials who fulfilled missions of great transcendence for the interests of the Spanish crown is Don Francisco de Saavedra, who, among many things, announced the British claims to articulate an interoceanic route between the Caribbean and the Pacific by the coasts of Nicaragua, intention that was corroborated some years later by the English colonel Robert Hodgson, arrested by the Spaniards in the vicinity of the Mosquitia coasts.
