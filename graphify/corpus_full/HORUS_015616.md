# Fungal capacity to remove drugs present in irrigation waters in western savannah of Bogotá (Colombia).

The district of Riego de la Ramada is located in the Sabana de Bogotá (Colombia), captures waters from the upper basin of the Bogotá river and due to the urbanization in surrounding sectors, residual waters are generated that reach the irrigation channels of the district without any treatment. In its content are several drugs (claritromycin, carbamazepine, clindamycin, gembifrizole, ibuprofen, lidocaine, lincomicin, losartan, benzoleggone, etc.) and personal hygiene products, and also contains coliforms in concentrations greater than those permitted by Colombian legislation (decreto 1594 of 1984), which causes the irrigated crops in the district to present biological risk for human health. Within the biological treatments, fungi are an alternative for the remediation of water thanks to the variety and inespecificity of their enzymes, therefore the objective of this work was to evaluate if the fungal treatment is effective to improve the quality of irrigation water in the district of Ramada. (isolation 9) It is concluded that fungal strains with drug removal activity (clindamycin and clarithromycin) were obtained, as well as for total coliform inhibition and Escherichia coli. It is recommended that complementary studies be carried out to deepen the knowledge of the processes and the application of fungi as bioremediators of water and in particular to improve the water quality of the irrigation district of Ramada
