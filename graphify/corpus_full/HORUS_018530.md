# Methodological approach for the analysis and design of courses in higher education based on Blended Teaching.

Higher education currently faces the challenge of responding to the needs of an educator who must be able to be trained anywhere, at any time. Scientific literature has been presenting hybrid learning as an alternative to meet this challenge. This thesis represents an effort to realize this opportunity. The proposed general objective is a methodology for the design of hybrid courses in higher education, with application to the area of computer science, which considers elements for evaluation and allows contrast with other teaching structures. This objective was fulfilled through four contributions: (i) a systematic review of scientific literature that analyzes the evolution of Blended Learning in the last 10 years, (ii) a conceptual approach to a Blended Learning model from the teaching perspective (Blended Teaching), (iii) the formulation of a model for the design of a course of the Blended Teaching modality in higher education and (iv) a matrix to assess the instructional configuration of a hybrid research course. For its development a single case study was used that allowed to evaluate and improve in practice the work developed. The results of this exercise allowed confirming the importance of the research communities, a constant transformation of knowledge (i) to assess the teaching and a hybrid research course
