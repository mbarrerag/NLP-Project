# SiO2 nanoparticle to optimize the colloidal suspension stability and filter cake buildup in water-based mud.

The agglomeration and the filtration process in colloidal suspensions occurs in many natural phenomena and engineering applications. The most common problem of colloidal theory is the stabilization of a dispersion; reduction of the agglomeration by controlling the forces between particles. If particles are agglomerated, the sizes of agglomerated increase, increasing the speed of sedimentation (or deposition) randomly on the surface. The drilling fluids are composed of a base fluid, water and suspended solid particles, calcium carbonate - CaCO3. In most cases, polymers are used to favor the dispersion of the solid material. However, factors such as the contamination of the mud, the size, the concentration of solids, the changes of pH, etc., can alter the surface load and affect the colloidal stability and alter the properties of the perforation fluid. An alternative to the current improvement of the properties of the perforation fluids is mainly used in the solid. are retained on the porous surface due to the affinity between the rock silica groups and the active sites of SiO2 NPs. Therefore, SiO2 NPs may interact in the following order with each evaluated element: Polymer <Roca. In the case of polymer, it interacts more with rock, followed by NPs and then CaCO3. The NPs do not generate significant changes in the rheological profiles of the WBM. However, the cedence point and gel resistance, which are reinforced at low shear rates, were improved with the presence of NPs, the forces of attraction predominate. The lower the distance between the polymer and SiO2 NPs, the greater the attraction force between molecules. This study provides a broader picture of the role of SiO2 NPs in improving their properties and the design of perforation fluids for field application. Strategies and methodologies are proposed for the application and scaling of WBM with NPs in well drilling
