# Evaluation of the operation of a membrane bioreactor (MBR) for the treatment of leachate from a sanitary filling

This study evaluates the efficiency of the membrane biorereactor (MBR) in the treatment of leachates of a sanitary fill in Colombia, by analyzing physical-chemical parameters, operational monitoring and laboratory tests, with data collected between 2016 and 2024. The correlation between the permeate flow rate and key variables was identified as the relationship between DBO/NH3, the concentration of solids (SSLM and SSVLM), the volumetric index of sludges (IVL), the alkalinity, total nitrogen removal and soluble DQO. The results showed that an adequate process of nitri and denitrification is essential for the optimal functioning of MBR. The parameters causing permeate fall were determined, such as the relationship of DBO/total nitrogen, the concentration of SSLM and SSVLM, and the age of the sludge, as well as the variables resulting from this fall, including the IVL, DQO soluble and temperature. In addition, indicators of operational faults were identified, such as the decrease of alkalinness below 1600 mg/l below 1600 mg/l, the treatment below, the age of the sludge, the total and the above above, the increase of the total and soluble COD was identified. The results demonstrated that a proper nitrification and denitrification process is essential for the optimal performance of the MBR. The study identified the parameters causing permeate decrease, including the BOD/total nitrogen ratio, SSLM and SSVLM concentrations, and sludge age, as well as the resulting variables such as SVI, soluble COD, and temperature. Additionally, critical failure indicators were established, such as alkalinity dropping below 1600 mg/l, total nitrogen exceeding 600 mg/l, and nitrates risking above 4000 mg/l.
