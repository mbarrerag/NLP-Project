# Catalysis of the hydrogenation of levulinic acid to γ-valerolactone with gold nanoparticles supported in mesoporous silica.

Currently, obtaining products of industrial interest from renewable sources has acquired relevance, due to the environmental impact generated by traditional methods of production and the imminent depletion of fossil sources.This has given rise to various processes of valorisation, with which products of high added value are obtained from biomass.However, one of the main limiting factors in this valorisation is the production of products with direct application in industry, because the transformation of biomass by hydrolysis, thermochemical processes, fermentation, among other methods, results in the production of compounds known as platform molecules, which, while they are a step forward in the valorisation process, require an additional transformation process that allows the production of products with direct application.This is the case of levulinic acid, as a platform obtained from lignocellulotic biomass, which can be valorised towards γ-rolactone, a compound of interest to the food industry and biofuels.By means of this master's thesis, the obtaining of γ-valrolactone from levulinic acid, where it is used by means of a nano-dependent reaction using a hetero-generate catalyst, is performed using a hetero-generous catalyst. a total conversion (100%) of levulinic acid at temperatures between 135 °C and 177 °C and times between 2 h and 5 h, and selectivity to γ-valerolactone of 99.49% using as reaction conditions: catalyst 5% Au, 135 °C and 3.5 h
