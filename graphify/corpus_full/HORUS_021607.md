# Public health in prison: a crisis explained from a critical sociopolitical perspective, Colombia, 1993-2018.

The Colombian prison is immersed in a profound institutional and health crisis. This crisis persists due to the operation of a set of conjunctures that are a legacy of the institutional trajectory of the prison system. Consequently, the prison tends to behave as a pathogenic, unjust, unequal and non-sustainable social space. In fact, this crisis limits the ends of the penalty, violates human rights, deteriorates health, encourages destructive processes in the way of life and allows the transfer of health risks. Therefore, this thesis proposes a sociopolitical and historical reading of its configuration process leading to the analysis of the trajectories, legacies and implications for health and life. Explaining from a critical sociopolitical approach how and why the process of configuration of the Colombian prison has affected the persistence of a crisis with serious implications for public health. Qualitative design of a documentary, historical, critical and interpretative character
