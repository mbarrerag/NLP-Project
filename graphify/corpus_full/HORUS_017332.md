# WHITE VOTE AND ORGANIZATIONAL PROCESS IN THE REPRESENTATION OF NEGRITUS IN COLOMBIA: ELECTIONS 2018

The special constituencies for indigenous and Afro-Colombians have been questioned, from the moment they enter into force, for not promoting the participation and political representation of these population groups. However, individuals throughout the national territory are voting in these constituencies when they might not. The 2018 elections present an even more interesting pattern; that is because the national total for the blank votes in the ethnic constituencies of the House of Representatives was higher than the percentage of votes per list or party. This pattern presents an important subnational variation since in the traditionally indigenous or Afro-Colombian departments the white vote is much lower than the list or party vote; instead, in cities like Bogotá, Medellín and Cali the blank votes are much higher than the votes by lists or parties. This situation generates a series of approaches related to the institutional design of these districts and its effects on the representation and political participation of ethnic minorities in the Colombian Congress. In particular, this chapter asks why there is a subnational variation in the votes of ethnic constituencies and what are their effects on the representation of ethnic minorities in the national politics.
