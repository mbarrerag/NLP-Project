# POLICE MEASUREMENT: AN APPROXIMATION FROM SOCIAL REPRESENTATIONS IN COLOMBIAN POLICIES

Mediation as a strategy for resolving judicial conflicts has expanded to several areas of law, including training of the police to contribute to coexistence in relations between citizens. The aim of this work was to explore the social representations of mediation in a group of police (n=108) who received a four-hour training on police mediation. A free partnership format was used on police mediation and possible uses of this technique in police work. As results, the prototypic analysis found that the central node of police mediation is structured around concepts of "solution," "conflict," "dialogue," "listen," "does the analysis of similarities find that the concept of "agreement" is also an important element of the central node. On the uses of mediation, tools such as dialogue and listening are mentioned, which serve neighborhood problems, rills or intra-family violence, and as a way of preventing the conflict between the parties from escalating to a level of greater severity
