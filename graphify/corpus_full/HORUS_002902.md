# ASSESSMENT OF A SIDRURGICAL INDUSTRY WASTE AS A CATEGORATER IN DEGRADATION OF AMOOXICILINE BY FENTON AND PHOTO-FENTON REACTIONS

Amoxicillin is a ß-lactam antibiotic widely used to treat bacterial infections. Approximately 78% of amoxicillin is excreted unchanged via urine [1], and its wide use has converted amoxicillin into one of the most commonly detected antibiotics in wastewater [2] because of this it is essential to find effective treatments for its degradation. In this study the activity of two solid materials as catalysts in the degradation of amoxicillin was evaluated. The first catalyst is a residue of the steel industry consisting of a mixture of minerals (hematite, goethite and siderite). The second catalyst is magnetite, synthesized by the mixture of 13 mmol of FeSO4·7H2O with 35 mmol of FeCl·6H2O, dissolved in 15 mL of H2SO4 0.5M. To this mixture was added to 40% and was added to a pH of 9. The growth of the crystals was allowed for 24 hours at 70°C, then filtered and washed the solid with the water of the acidised solution until reaching a neutral pH and 24°C was achieved. Thermo UltiMate 3000 with a Kynetex C18 column (2.6 μm, 100 × 4.6 mm) and a UltiMate TM 3000 to 230 nm multiple wavelength detector. The catalytic evaluation suggests that amoxicillin is strongly adsorbed on the magnetite with a removal rate of 10%, which could be related to the pH of the reaction (6,50), where the magnetite has a more positively charged surface than the iron residue, which makes the interaction with the carboxylate group of amoxicillin stronger.
