# Mineralogical and fluid characterization of the Providencia, Las Verticales and Carla deposits in Huila, Colombia

The District Minero Segovia-Remedios (DMSR) is located in the eastern flank of the Central Cordillera of Colombia, in the department of Antioquia. This district is widely recognized for its significant gold mineralization, being one of the most relevant at national level. In the systems Providencia, Las Verticales and Carla, within this district, the petrographic analysis identifies three different hydrothermal events: Event 1 is characterized by the presence of pyrite (Py), marcasita (Mrc), pyrotene (Po), galena (Gn), calcopirita (Ccp) and ciedium quartz (Qz), with Py forming from the transformation of the Mrc and Po. An event of cisilla separates the event 1 from the event 2, which causes a highly fractured Py and arsepirita (Agrea) sample. In the event 2, the mineral assembly consists of pyrite (Py), sphalite (Sp), and carbon (Au), with the analysis of the calcopirita (Cpp) and arsepirita (Ag). High concentrations of gold linked to iron availability. The petrographic data support this relationship between mineral assemblies and gold mineralization. In addition, the results of LAICP-MS show that the mena forming fluids are a mixture of magmatic hydrothermal fluids and basin brine, as evidenced by the concentrations of rubidium (Rb), sodium (Na) and manganese (Mn) in the systems. The isotopic analysis of Pb-Pb reveals a heterogeneous source for mineralizing fluids, with contributions from the superior crust and an orogenic origin. The gold mineralization in the DMSR appears to be associated with the second magmatic pulse of the Batolith of Antioquia, linking the intrusion of porphyric dikes with the precipitation of gold .
