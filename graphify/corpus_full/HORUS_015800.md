# SENSITIVITY TO MERCURY AND ENVIRONMENTAL LEVEL OF EUTROPHICATION IN FRESHWATER MACROINVERTEBRATES

Sensitivity to mercury and environmental level of eutrophication in freshwater macroinvertebrates Duvan Nieves-Ruiz 1, Sebastián Reynaldi2* 1 Departamento de Geociencias y Medio Ambiente-Facultad de Minas, Universidad Nacional de Colombia sede Medellín 2 Departamento de Ciencias Agronómicas, Facultad de Ciencias Agrarias, Universidad Nacional de Colombia sede Medellín *Corresponding author: sreynaldi@unal.edu.co; phone: (+57)3147021137 Abstract. Based on the idea of the biotic ligand, the absence of organic matter in oligotrophic habitats suggests tolerance in the species of these habitats. We compared sensitivity to mercury, as HgCl2, in freshwater macroinvertebrates considered and not by the Working Group method of Biological Monitoring (BMWP) method. The BMWP classifies freshwater macroinvertebrate by the eutrophication level of their habitats. Taxa found in oligotrophic habitats receive the highest BMWP scores, meanwhile taxa found in hypereutrophic receive the lowest BMB scores. We downloaded Median Lethal Concentration values (LC50) from the USEPA, Ecotox database and grouped by taxon and BMWP score using geometric mean. A routine R combined the "ssdtools" and "ggplot2" packages to construct a species sensitivity distribution curve (SSD) with the geometric mean of each taxon. The SSD curve included 42 taxa and 20 BMWP taxa. Ten of the BMWP taxa were among the 50% most sensitive, but only two BMWP taxa were among the 25% most sensitive. BMWP taxa were less sensitive to mercury. An R routine written with commands from the "dplyr" package placed the scores on the BMWP Taxes. The geometric means of the LC50 grouped by BMWP score did not show a clear trend when compared. The geometric means of the LC50 of the taxa showed high variability within the same score. The LC50 of the species showed differences of up to two orders of magnitude within the same taxon. The results did not show a relationship between mercury sensitivity and BMWP score. Also, the taxonomy cannot predict the sensitivity of macroinvertebrates to mercury. These outcomes show that the sensitivity to mercury in freshwater macroinvertebrates seems to be independent from the habitat level of eutrophication.
