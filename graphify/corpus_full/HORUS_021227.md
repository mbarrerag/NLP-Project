# Identification of triggers, motivations and barriers for the adoption of shared mobility services in the transition to a MaaS

The fourth industrial revolution or digital age has allowed advances in digital technologies being its increasingly widespread availability and opening the way to new ways of providing and accessing transport services.An example of this is the rise of transport network companies that have developed digital platforms to unite users and suppliers, and provide shared transport services, shared vehicles, shared bicycles and micro mobility.The concept of Mobility as a Service (MaaS) encompasses new trends towards digitisation and the trend towards the provision of shared transport and mobility services away from the dependence of the individual ownership of private vehicles, for example, the services offered under various types of agreements and operational models (Uber type, or DIDI type in our local context).These new services, technologies and emerging businesses can mean for our cities and our communities the reduction of negative externalities of the transport (e.g., congestion, sinisterality, atmospheric emissions of pollutants) that are caused by the use of private cars, as they increase the occupation and decrease the number of vehicles that are wheeled in the limited road infrastructure of the city. To reach targets of pollution and services, it is necessary. and the barriers to the future implementation of a MaaS service, analyzing current car sharing initiatives in the university communities of Bogotá, considering the perspective of both current and potential users and service providers. To achieve this objective, master and undergraduate students and SIMUN seedbed teachers intend to characterize the current offer of shared mobility services on university campuses in Bogotá in order to understand who the actors involved are, the operating characteristics of the service, their vehicles, and the technological support platforms, among other aspects; for this purpose qualitative research methods will be used. Additionally, the characteristics of the current demand will be analyzed from revealed preference surveys to determine the user profile and trigger factors and barriers that influence the use of the service, and the characteristics of the potential demand from econometric models considering latent variables to determine the motivating factors that influence the use of these services will be defined. Finally, with the results of the research, guidelines for the transition to MaaS shared mobility services will be defined
