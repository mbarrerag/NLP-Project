# CHAPTER 53: CRONE CRIME CRIME SYNDROME

Chronic delusional syndrome is a chronic mental disorder that affects the content of thought and generates as a consequence the appearance of permanent delusional ideation. It has the characteristic that it does not affect the functionality of other areas of mental examination, whose functionality is preserved. It manifests itself as a continuous delirium, systematized, irreducible to logical reasoning, which does not necessarily affect the functionality of other areas of the person's life, which are largely preserved. The variety of chronic delirant disorders is dependent on the type of altered content of thought: chronic paranoid psychosis (including the delusion of persecution, folie a deux, of claim or querulative, invention, idealist, professional isolation); passional delirium (including the cerotipic delirium, eroticoniac, grandiosity); sensory or referential delusion; interpretation delirium (including the somatic delusion, Sérieux, Capgras, Ekbom, Fregoli, Cotard, Digenernes); chronic hallucatory psychosis;
