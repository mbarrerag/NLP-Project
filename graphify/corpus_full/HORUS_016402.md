# Morphological characterization and capsaicin contents of introductions of Capsicum chinense Jacq. (Solanaceae) for use in genetic improvement programs.

The plant genetic resources constitute material-prime for the creation of more productive varieties, more resistant to pests and diseases and better adapted to the cultivation regions. Together with wild relatives, they are the repository of the potential genetic variability of the breeding programs of cultivated plants.The genus Capsicum, represented by ajies, peppers, chilies and pimentones, comprises a group of Solanaceae of high consumption worldwide, as in Colombia, with high demand in market places and supermarkets, occupying labor in field work and consumption centers, therefore, being constituted in crops of social interest. Five species of Capsicum are domesticated – C. annuum L., C. baccatum L., C. chinen Jacq., C. fruitescens L., C. fruitescens L. and C. pubescens has a strong and concentrated in the agricultural and industrial sectors. However, the wild and endemic species of tropical areas of America make up a genetic resource of economically valuable characteristics, which can be introduced in the cultivated varieties
