# COELLO, TOLIMA: LIVING PAST PAST

The concept of cultural heritage initially linked only to material objects recently incorporated the vision of the intangible and immaterial, with an appreciation of expressions, practices and experiences of folklore and popular culture. The new sense of living, intangible heritage is given through processes of differential appropriation of social groups with the capacity to represent a common heritage. This type of processes is called patrimonialization, by virtue of the binding significance of some social and symbolic capitals for social agents, through which they constitute alliances of kinship, neighbourhood and friendship
