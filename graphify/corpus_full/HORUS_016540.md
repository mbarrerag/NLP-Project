# MEDICINAL CANNABIS AND PHARMACOLOGICAL MEASURES IN COLOMBIA

This article describes, from an ethnographic perspective, the process of legalizing medicinal cannabis in Colombia.The story connects diverse agents and scales and is related to the emergence of "cannabis science" and the effects of anthropogenic agency on prohibited plants.It shows how, thanks to the internet and international seed trade, Colombian gardeners were able to set up small laboratories to produce oils, tinctures, extracts, ointments and other preparations in which, according to them, marijuana acts "holistically".These cultivators have partnered with groups of caregivers and patients with chronic and congenital illnesses successfully treated with cannabis.This alliance has been of great importance in consolidating the process of moral and symbolic purification that transformed the current pharmacopoeia, establishing a broad and promising market that the pharmaceutical industry seeks to monopolize. Although in the 19th century several studies were carried out to determine the psychopharmacological properties of hemp (Cannabis sativa L., Cannabis indica), studies on the therapeutic uses of this plant species practically disappeared with the prohibition of marijuana in the USA, a process that developed between 1911 and 1927 (Langlitz, 2013).It took a Bulgarian doctor based in Israel, Dr. Raphael Mechoulam, professor of organic chemistry and pharmacy at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, to begin a systematic analysis of marijuana and to be able to isolate its main active components.This scientist and his colleagues identified and tested the structure of cannabidiol (CBD) in 1963 and tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) in 1964.
