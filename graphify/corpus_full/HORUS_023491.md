# CACCO CHANGE

The accumulation of Cadmium in cocoa almonds is a subject of crucial interest to the community that produces, transforms and consumes it, both in exporting countries and consumers, and so on, the cadmium relationship soil cocoa chocolate plant and the legislation associated with it, both in Colombia and in other countries of the world. Soil is the fundamental resource for agricultural production, especially when it comes to obtaining food for the well-being of communities. However, it can become a source and reservoir of toxic substances, which could affect both plants and animals that consume them (Adriano, 2001: Bronick and Lal, 2005). Cadmium (Cd) is among the transitional metals belonging to the ll-B group of the periodic table and can be toxic to plants that absorb it from the soil (Adriano., 2001; Eutropio et al., 2016). Cadmium is recognized as a carcinogen for human beings, because of its bioaccumulation, and risk is generated when the amounts available in soil are taken by plants, affecting the yield of crops, and the quality of cocoa (Bocharya and 2008)
