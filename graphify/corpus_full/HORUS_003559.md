# PROFESSIONALS IN EARTH SCIENCES IN MUNICIPAL ADMINISTRATIONS, A CONTRIBUTION TO THE DEVELOPMENT OF THE TERRITORY: A CASE STUDY IN COLOMBIA, SOUTH AMERICA

The geographical location, economic vulnerability of a large portion of the population, lack of teaching of Earth Sciences in schools, lack of territorial planning, illicit extraction of resources, and lack of public policies adapted to the reality of the territories are the perfect breeding ground for what is observed daily in the news: loss of lives and economic losses due to lack of harmony between public policies, knowledge, geo-hazards, and environmental degradation in the regions. In this scenario, a legislative initiative has been proposed in Colombia that allows for the presence of a professional in Earth Sciences in the territory, who can permeate the social fabric and have a positive impact on the well-being of the population. This initiative has been accompanied by research projects at the National University of Colombia, Bogotá campus, including undergraduate theses, academic internships in a municipality, a short documentary, as well as more than 50 presentations in national and international events of different kinds, campaigns led by professional associations (Colombian Geological Society and Colombian Association of Petroleum Geologists and Geophysicists), communications and meetings with government officials and lawmakers, news coverage in national and local press, campaigns on social media, among others. The current reality calls for ensuring the presence of professionals in Earth Sciences in municipalities, with the aim of contributing to sustainable development through the application of technical knowledge in the exercise of oversight, advisory, and validation of issues related to geohazards, groundwater prospecting and identification, and mining-energy resources present in the territory, supporting the development and review of territorial planning and schemes, and promoting social appropriation of knowledge in Earth Sciences. We face challenges such as climate change and its consequences for society, energy transition, carbon neutrality, and all of them have in common the knowledge acquired by geologists in their academic training. In this sense, the initiative also seeks to generate more and better knowledge of topics related to geology and its importance for society. The lack of knowledge in Earth Sciences prevents proper and optimal territorial management, resulting in conflicts in land and subsurface use, lack of timely mitigation of potential risks in communities, and ignorance of potential water resources. Therefore, building a country involves putting the knowledge acquired in the university at the service of society, and thus professionalizing decision-making in the territory with technical information support can lead to progress on the right path towards sustainable development
