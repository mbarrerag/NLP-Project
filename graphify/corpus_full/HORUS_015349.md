# Extraction of rare earth elements from monacita by deep euthectic solvents and their comparison with conventional organic solvents.

In this thesis we obtained a concentration of monacite [scan-earth element phosphate (RETs)] from residues of alluvial gold mining, which is one of the most critical minerals in the world, because it is a source of RTEs. RTEs are of great importance in the development of technological materials. In this thesis, the concentrate of monacite was subjected to a process of dephosphoration with sodium hydroxide and potassium hydroxide. It was investigated different conditions of leaching with HCl and H2SO4, which will allow the maximum extraction of cerium, lanthanum and neodymium, it was determined that the greatest extraction of these elements is achieved with H2SO4. It was established that the addition of 10 % (v) of H2O2 increases the dissolution of rare earths (to 93 %). The RTEs can recover to values of approximately 100%, by means of precipitation with oxylic acid.
