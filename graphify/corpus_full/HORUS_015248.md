# ANTIMICROBIAL SACKS FOR THE CONTROL OF PECTOBACTERIUM CAROTOVORUM PHYTOPATHOGEN OF POTATO

Considering the trend towards more restrictions on conventional materials, using biopolymers functionalized with antibacterial agents to manufacture smart packaging is a clean alternative that can mitigate plastic pollution and food losses due to postharvest plant diseases. In this study, surgical cotton fibers were functionalized with AgNps obtained from a native strain of Fusarium oxysporum with an antibacterial action against the potato plant pathogen Pectobacterium carotovorum causing soft rot of the tuber. Two methodologies were used to obtain the antibacterial fibers called A-AgNps-C and A-AgNps-RBi, which, in addition to being characterized by SEM-EDX, XRD, were evaluated as antibacterial materials. The parallel streak method allowed us to observe zones of bacterial growth inhibition when using the two types of fibers, finding percentages of bacterial reduction of up to 94% for A-AgNps-C fibers and 100% for A-AgNps-RBi fibers. Then, the AgNp's were immobilized on fique fibers to build fique sacks, which were evaluated against soft rot symptoms in tubers infected with P. carotovorum. The results show that the tubers stored in traditional sacks gave an average percentage of affectation of 25%, while the tubers stored in antibacterial sacks presented an average affectation of 7.8%. These results indicate that using fique sacks-biosynthesized AgNp's is highly attractive for managing P. carotovorum and that its commercial scaling is highly viable
