# EVALUATION OF LOW ENTHALPY GEOTHERMAL POTENTIAL IN THE CITY OF BOGOTÁ, COLOMBIA FROM GEOLOGICAL, GEOPHYSICAL, HYDROGEOLOGICAL AND REMOTE SENSING THERMAL ANOMALIES

Bogota in Colombia is a region favored for the exploitation of geothermal energy due to its geological location, thick sedimentary coverage, structural setting, and geothermal anomalies. In addition, recent advances in technology to produce electrical energy from medium to low enthalpy geothermal resources have made it possible to exploit them in such sedimentary basins. Based on the compilation and analysis of geophysical information, geological surveys, well information, and other inputs for groundwater exploration, there is a growing interest of evaluating geothermal resources in the Rio Bogota Basin, specially, those associated with deep aquifers beyond 1000m depth. During geothermal exploration around the world, multiple sources of geological, geochemical, and geophysical information are used; of which MV studies stand out as one of the most important tools for the evaluation of a region's geothermal potential. The National University of Colombia, through its Department of Geosciences, have been working on this topic, applying geoelectric, MT, seismic and field data potentials, along with surface temperature mapping (Land Surface Temperature LST) from a multisensory (Landsat -7,8,9-, ASTER, Sentinel 3) and multitemporal approach. and radiative heat flux products for the Bogotá Savannah, from remote satellite thermal sensors and Digital Image Processing (PDI) algorithms, and the hydrological and hydrochemical analysis of groundwater from surface and deeper aquifers. As a result, a geothermal model from the geological, geophysical, and hydrogeological information derived from the resistivity models has been generated, categorizing the potential geothermal prospectivity by integrating the available geological information, assessing the geological risks associated with the different elements of the geothermal system and proposing mechanisms for the mitigation of the identified risks. Additionally, the appropriate geothermal heat pump for the identified HVAC needs has been proposed, considering the required heating and/or cooling capacities, such as size the geothermal exchanger, which may be capable of capturing energy from the subsoil, taking into account the depth and type of exchanger (open closed loop), determining an optimal configuration of wells and probes
