# CLINICAL ADVANCEMENT OF THE PATIENT IN DECITO

The equine patient in decubitus represents a challenge for the clinician who handles it and requires specialized care and care. Multiple causes are related to the decubitus in horses, including neurological, musculoskeletal, toxic and/or metabolic diseases, and respiratory and/or cardiovascular. The history and clinical examination are fundamental for the diagnosis approach of the patient in pathological decubitus. The history provides critical information regarding the previous performance of the horse, accidents or traumas, diet and conditions of handling of the food, chronicity or acuity of the event, vaccination against diseases with neurological potential, among others. The general clinical examination seeks to determine the metabolic stability of the patient as well as to try to find the cause of the decubitus. Additionally, it is imperative to perform a neurological clinical examination and an adequate evaluation of the limbs and the axial skeleton to be able to include or discard musculoskeletal or neurological abnormalities as a cause of the decubitus.
