# Symbiosis between quantum physics and machine learning: Applications in data science, many-body physics and quantum computation.

This thesis explores the intersections between quantum computing, quantum physics and machine learning. In the three fields, the estimation of probability distributions plays a central role. In the case of quantum computing and quantum physics, a central study object is the quantum state of a system, which encodes a probability distribution (however, the opposite is not true, since a quantum state is a more general object than a particular probability distribution). In the case of machine learning, most of the supervised and unsupervised learning tasks can be considered as the estimation of probability distributions from a training data set, which can then be used to predict by sampling or evaluating such probability distribution. Due to the famous curse of quantum optics, present both in the machine learning and in the natural intrastability of Hilbert spaces, it has established that quantum theory and automatic learning have much to give and learn from one another. In this thesis, we show fruitful approaches to study basic states, stationary states and closed dynamics of quantum systems through neural quantum states. This knowledge transfer is not only produced in one direction: quantum physics can also contribute to machine learning with machine learning methods inspired by quantum. In this thesis we also present a framework that establishes an analogy between quantum state preparation and training, and also between quantum projective measurements and prediction. Our approach condenses classical data into the quantum state of a system. We succeed in proving that arbitrary probability distributions can be encoded in that quantum state with arbitrary precision, given sufficient degrees of quantum state freedom. In addition, we can condense arbitrarily large data sets into quantum states, allowing us to have a non-gradient training (in reality, without optimization). This framework of ours was also put into practice by implementing it in a real quantum computer for sets of quantum data.
