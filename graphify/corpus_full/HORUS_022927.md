# Approach to an indicator to estimate the magnitude of physical exertion in cultivation work

The most common social indicators of agricultural sustainability are working hours and seasonality of the workforce. The magnitude of the physical effort that workers invest in agricultural activities is not normally used as an indicator of efficiency and sustainability; for this reason, this study proposes the Agricultural Labour (ELB) effort as an indicator of this type. To calculate this, five degrees of labour effort (GE) and a classification of cultivation work with an assigned GE were established. To test the method, ELB was estimated at four potato production systems in two sites with different slopes. Results showed that the slope or inclination of the field has a more significant influence on the ELB than the level of cultivation technification. The non-technified system in inclined field (MuiNo) generates the largest ELB (4.67), corresponding to the maximum work effort. The medium-sized system technified on flat ground (PlnMd) presented the lowest ELB (1.69), corresponding to little work effort. It is expected that this indicator can be included in the social dimension of farm-scale agricultural sustainability analyses
