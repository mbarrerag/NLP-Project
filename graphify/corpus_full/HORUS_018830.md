# OF LITERATURA, FIVE WORKS FOR THE PHILOSOPHY

The book contains five essays in which its author addresses some problems of philosophy from literary texts (which come from different eras and traditions). The detailed reading of these texts uses a group of concepts frequently worked in philosophy: world, conscience, death, empathy and memory, among others. In most chapters, this group of concepts takes its place in the tradition of phenomenology and serves to highlight a way of conceiving the relationship between consciousness and the world: one in which there is no world beyond the capacity of consciousness to attribute meaning. The book is conceived with a dual purpose; first, to offer a justification of the philosophical value that some works of philosophy come to have and, second, to expand the interpretive scope of these works, so that it transcends the boundaries of literary criticism. Because of the latter, with the book, its author is separated from any interpretation that gives rise to a judgment on another type of value of each of works with which it works it works work; that is, of any form of interpretation that speaks of its literary or historical value for social life or its moral value
