# CONCEPTUAL MAPS AS TEACHING STRATEGY IN DENTISTRY

To describe the process of teaching high-difficulty content using conceptual maps. Quasi-experimental educational intervention with a before-after analysis, implemented in one academic year. Students who granted their informed consent were trained in the use of Cmaptools® software, received the content with different methodologies and made three concept maps throughout the course, which were shared to display the maturity and correct conceptual errors (Novak, 1972; 1998). The maps were stored in the Cmaptools® format and a recount of the constitutive elements ¿ (Concepts, Links and Propositions) ¿ was made in each map, calculating the variation (labeled conceptual maturity) of each student and the group for every period. 17 students completed the three maps. The initial one had on average 31 Concepts, 21 Links and 44 Propositions. The intermediate one changed to 52 Concepts, 34 Links and 75 Propositions and the final one ended with 90 Concepts, 61 Links and 124 Propositions. The group proportional variation between the initial and final one was almost 3 times more for the three elements. The greatest individual variation went from 26 to 183 Concepts (7 times+) and from 27 to 233 Propositions (8.6 times+). Students expressed their satisfaction and better internalization of complex concepts, since they understood them effectively during learning, incorporated them not as completed meanings but as structures under construction, involving other taught elements to produce new knowledge. They indicated the greater amount of individual work time required as disadvantage, compared with traditional pedagogies. Teaching of difficult content using concept maps gave participants the opportunity to use their prior and new knowledge, to contribute to the construction of the knowledge of their classmates, to provide constant feedback in the teaching/learning process, to integrate theoretical and practical concepts in their maps and to assimilate complex concepts applicable to clinical settings
