# DFT studies of model systems of Fe2+/3+-Aβ complexes and anti-aggregating properties of polyphenols and carotenoids in Alzheimer’s disease

Alzheimer’s disease, a complex multifactorial condition, involves a number of factors, among which the hypothesis of the role of iron in neurodegeneration processes and in the formation of amylodic plaques is highlighted. In this context, this study focused on the use of computational methods to predict the standard potential for reduction and the generation of reactive species by means of Fe-Aβ complexes. The results include the development of an advanced methodology for the treatment of iron complexes, as well as a proposal on the reactivity and formation of hydrogen peroxide. In addition, a specific methodology was developed to investigate the antiplatelet potential of curcumin derivatives. This same technique was applied to assess the antiplatelet activity of several carotenoids present in the red mamey, thus extending the spectrum of possible therapeutic interventions in the fight against Alzheimer’s disease. These advances represent a significant step in the understanding and treatment of this disease, opening new research pathways to combat its neurodegenerative effects. .
