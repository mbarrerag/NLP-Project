# MYCELIUM-BASED COMPOSITE WITH PITH OF MONTANOA QUADRANGULARIS SHULTZ BIP

Recent studies have pointed out the possibility of replacing some conventional materials with biocomposites, which are made from mycelium. This proposal suggests the development of a mycelium-based composite made with particles of pith of ¿arboloco¿ and mycelium of Ganoderma lucidum. The aim is to improve its thermal performance and thereby reduce the use of high embodied energy thermal insulating materials, such as fiberglass, extruded or expanded polystyrene [1]. The mycelium-based composite exhibited thermal conductivities ranging from 0.0432 to 0.0465 W/m K, with a difference of less than 8% between the two formulations in the dry state at a temperature of 22°C +/- 1°C. This data is close to the thermal conductivity recorded by widely used materials worldwide, such as glass wool [4], rock wool [5], expanded polystyrene, and extruded polystyrene [4], whose thermal conductivities range from 0.03 to 0.04 W/m K. The densities of the composite ranged from 0.0347 to 0.0905 g/cm3, resulting in low weight and a minimal contribution to the dead loads of the building. Its mechanical behaviour is comparable to the mentioned commercial materials, with formulation F1 exhibiting better performance at 0.18 MPa for 50% deformation, according to ASTM C165
