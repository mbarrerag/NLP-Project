Several jobs have recognized the importance of small and medium-sized enterprises (pymes) for the economic development of economies (Chung et al., 2008; Fahy et al., 2005, García et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2008; Okpara, 2009). These companies are responsible for a significant proportion of economic growth and the creation of new jobs in emerging economies (Alvarez et al., 2008; Cadot et al., 2012; Lyon et al., 2000; Robu, 2013). And in countries with highly competitive domestic markets such as Colombia, the number of SMEs involved in international business activities has increased (Javalgi et al., 2011, Manolova et al., 2002; McDougall & Oviatt, 2000; Ruzzier et al., 2006). SMEs, although they have a small number of workers (generally, less than 250 people) and a moderate turnover, are the backbone of most of the economies of the world and play a key role in the countries of emerging economies.
