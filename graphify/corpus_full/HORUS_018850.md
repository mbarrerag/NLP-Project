# Development of a continuous culture tool for the generation of mutants induced by exposure to antibacterial agents.

A continuous crop operated at 50% of the specific growth rate ( 0.2587 h -1 for E. coli; 0.2695 h -1 for S. aureus) was established, once the growth stabilized, Piperacillin/ Tazobactam was added to a concentration equivalent to 50% of the MIC until it reached growth stability. Then, the concentration of Piperacilin/ Tazobactam was increased to 75% of the MIC until stability and finally increased to 1MIC. With the proposed tool, the generation of mutant microorganisms, resistant to Piperacilin/ Tazobactam (32 times more resistant for both E. coli and S. aureus), with continued cultivation in operation for approximately 55 hours, of which approximately 40 hours corresponded to antibiotic exposure, which is a relatively short time and requiring low expenditure of material (expended culture broth per hour) with high biomass production (Piperaciline/ Tazobactam resistant microorganisms)
