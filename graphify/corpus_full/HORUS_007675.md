# Enzymatic exploration of candidates for polyethylene degradation.

The accumulation of plastic waste in the environment generates serious pollution problems, as it affects the ecosystem [1], and products resulting from incomplete degradation of these materials can lead to serious health problems for humans [24,5,6,7]. Several studies have documented the existence of microorganisms that possess degrading enzymes of plastics such as polyethylene terephthalate [20,22], polyethylene and polypropylene [22] in their metabolic system. Thus, it is intended to seek by using in-silico enzymes orthologous in other groups of microorganisms to identify an orthologist candidate for these enzymes. Hence, an in-vitro approach will be made considering the biological availability of the material, in the access collections. The isolation of the genetic material will be carried out for further amplification of the encoding gene of the candidate protein orthologist to previously documented plastic degrading enzymes [20,23]. Thus, the respective gene will be cloned, obtaining the transformants, which will allow the obtaining of the protein, which will be cryopreserve for the subsequent evaluation of the expression of the candidate recombinant protein.
