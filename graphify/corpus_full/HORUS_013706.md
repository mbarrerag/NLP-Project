# Agri-food marketing networks and short circuits

The agricultural and food marketing networks and short-circuits (RCCA) are a fundamental element for the planning and management of land, especially in rural areas. They stimulate local governance economies and processes and contribute to the sustainability of agri-food systems, by reducing harmful environmental impacts on conventional production systems. They emerge as an alternative to the commercialization of agri-food products in the face of long food marketing chains. To study the organizational processes that integrate producers, distributors and consumers of food, the use of ICTs that contribute to the social knowledge management processes present in biointercam spaces, is essential to generate knowledge and document these processes. The academy has not been unaware of these new dynamics, so many universities, networks and researchers have been accompanying these processes of local exchange for the social construction of markets. It seeks to respond to the challenges imposed by society to have agro-food systems in which food supply, with nutritional products, at affordable prices, with availability and opportunity to guarantee food and nutrition security
