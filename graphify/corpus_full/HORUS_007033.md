# "Who rules this territory?" Missionaries, Merchants and Soldiers in the Crises, Conflicts and Disputes over Authority in the Government of the Amazonian Border, 1896-1914

to discuss the establishment of the Capuchin mission and the disputes around authority and political power in Putumayo at the end of the nineteenth century and the first decades of the twentieth (1896-1914), from a perspective of the mission as a space that went beyond the “civilization of indigenous people” and that was inserted into the conflicts of society and the disputes over authority and political power. the studies of the mission in this period and the way in which its action has been interpreted are discussed, contrasting with the primary sources of the General Archive of the Nation and the Archive of the Diocese of Mocoa-Sibundoy. Originality: an alternative analysis to the role of the Capuchin mission in this period is offered based on an analysis focused on the study of the political and the disputes for authority through the strong changes that shook this border region (rubber economy, the thousand days war and the establishment of the Arana House). the Capuchin missionaries (from the mission essay and the Catalans who arrived in the country with the creation of the apostolic prefecture) fought for political power with the old forms of authority (rubber tappers as commissaries) and the new state agents (military) who settled in Putumayo with the creation of the Intendance (1905) and later special commissary (1912), due to the new role that was granted by the Vatican and the Colombian State as agents of development and civilization, which led to the political becoming a fundamental element of this company
