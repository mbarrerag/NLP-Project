# Stochastic model theory

The adapted logic of probability is a model-theoretical logic introduced by Keisler in 1979 that allows comparing stochastic processes based on the properties of universality, saturation and homogeneity of the structures (adapted spaces of probability) of this logic. We propose to enrich this theory of models of stochastic processes so that it is possible to compare not only stochastic processes but stochastic theories. As a starting point we take the open questions on definability into adapted spaces of probability. We investigate the types in this class of structures, their topology and Lindenbaum algebras of theories of adapted logic of probability. We complement more contemporary model-theoretical concepts as those coming from the theory of Shelah classification. We seek to coordinate the model-theoretical characterization of stochastic theories and their models with some notions of quantum mechanics as correlation, coherence, locality and contextuality. More specifically, we propose to specify in mathematical terms the structural differences between the dynamics of one or more grains of pollen suspended in a liquid and the dynamics of one or more "quantic" particles
