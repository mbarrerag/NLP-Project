# Analysis of genetic variability in seven exons of the human ACE2 gene and its relationship to the bloodline of SARS-CoV-2 in a group of individuals diagnosed with COVID-19

The interaction between the Spike protein of SARS-CoV-2 and the human ACE2 receptor is crucial in the development of the disease COVID-19. This study sought to evaluate the genetic variability of the viral lineage and seven exons of the human ACE2 gene, as well as to analyze clinical history and sociodemographic conditions related to the susceptibility and severity of the disease. Using sequencing technology, samples of nasopharyngeal hysopate were examined for the viral genome and blood samples of 50 individuals diagnosed with infection or viral pneumonia by SARS-CoV-2. These individuals were entered into the Colombian University Clinic, located in the city of Bogotá, from October 2020 to June 2021, and were classified into three groups of severity according to symptoms, diagnosis, respiratory failure and ventilation need as: mild, moderate and severe. Despite the absence of statistically significant associations between the viral lineages and severity of the disease, it was observed that the B.1.621 lineage predominates in mild cases, while the B.1.420 lineage is present only in moderate cases and the P.1 lineage was found in moderate individuals. however and sociodemographic conditions related to susceptibility and disease security.
