# METASTASIS OF BREAST CANCER IN THE JAW. CASE REPORT

Maxillofacial metastatic tumors are rare. Breast, lung, and prostate cancer are responsible for the majority maxillofacial metastasis. The jaw is the most affected site. Herein, we present a case of metastatic breast cancer in the jaw diagnosed at the Hospital Universitario Nacional de Colombia (HUN), where histopathological and immunohistochemical studwere decisive in diagnosis workup. Case Report: A 78-year-old woman with a history of HBP, T2DM, and left breast cancer (TXNXM0 E II B IHQ, ER 100%, PR 5% HER2 neg, KIG 67 10% R; 2017) previously treated with quadrantectomy, lymphadenectomy, radiotherapy and anastrozole (2 years). She was presented to the Head and Neck Service of HUN (2023) with a large symptomatic mass in the right mandibular region. Histopathological examination revealed metastatic breast carcinoma in jaw (CK7+/CK20-/ GATA3+/HER-2 neg., ER+ allred 8/8 score, PR neg. allred 0/8 score). She was treated with chemotherapy (Fulvestrant 250 mg [IM], Denosumab 120 mg [subQ], and Ribociclib 600 mg/3 weeks on) with positive results in follow-up controls. Metastasis to the jaw has been associated with the presence of hematopoietic marrow remnants in focal osteoporotic defects. Disseminated tumor cells (DTCs) corrupt the bone homeostasis by impairing the function of osteoblasts and osteoclasts. Elevated levels of PTHrP, IL-11, and RANKL and release of growth factors from mineralized tissue (i.e., FGTb, IGF, and BMP) lead to successful growth of tumor cells toward the destroyed bone (¿vicious circle¿). An integral and multidisciplinary approach is essential for diagnosis and treatment
