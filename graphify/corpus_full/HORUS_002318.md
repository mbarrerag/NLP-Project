# Deforestation and occurrence of childhood malaria morbidity in Colombia

Forests provide valuable ecosystem services, and some of them relate to the contribution they make to the well-being of people and to human health (Bernhard et al., 2023; Herrera et al., 2017; Jagger et al., 2022).However, these ecosystem services are not perceived immediately or directly, especially when compared with productive activities that generate income and increase the consumption of goods and services (Pascual et al., 2023).The increase in the area for productive activities, such as those related to agricultural production and livestock, is identified as relevant determinants of the conversion of forests into tropical regions, mainly in rural areas (Shvidenko, 2019).According to FAO (2015), deforestation is defined as the conversion of the natural forest to another alternative use of rural land. Deforestation is of global interest, since it is the second source of greenhouse gas emissions to the atmosphere (Audi et al., 2020; Sonwani & Saxena, 2022), after the problem of the environment of alternative use of rural land. Deforestation could increase the incidence of malaria in children under five years of age, since the disappearance of woody forest cover results in an alternative use of rural land, generating climatic conditions and habitat conducive to the reproduction of malaria-transmitting mosquitoes (Casman & Dowlatabadi, 2010; MacDonald & Mordecai, 2019).Previous empirical work provides evidence on the relationship between the loss of forest coverage and the increase in malaria cases (Aguirre et al., 2024; Fuentes, 2024; MacDonald & Mordecai, 2019), including causing death in children (Berazneva & Byker, 2017; Chakrabarti, 2021). In Colombia, evidence is reported on the relationship between forest coverage and malaria incidence (Brackman & Leguízamo, 2024; Hernández-Valencia et al., 2020).
