# «I CUT UP MUSIC»: QUANTITATIVE METHODS IN THE STUDY OF THE ITALIAN OTTAVA RIMA AND THE PROBLEM OF LITERARY EVOLUTION

The article discusses the use of quantitative methods in verse studies, its prospects, and how such methods are perceived in the Italian and international philological community. The starting point is the controversy surrounding quantitative studies of ottava rima, which has unfolded in recent years in the Italian scholarly press and resulted from a clash between traditional and new research approaches in Italian science. Neither the debate nor the arguments put forth by either side are new to an external observer familiar with the development stages of quantitative methods in other national traditions, particularly within the Russian school of verse studies. Resolving the differences in this discussion may be possible through a precise definition of the goals and objectives of quantitative research. The value of exact methods becomes evident when applied to the study of literary evolution and the history of poetic language, as well as in comparative studies. Examples of specific results in studying rhythm, poetic syntax, and rhyme illustrate the author's arguments
