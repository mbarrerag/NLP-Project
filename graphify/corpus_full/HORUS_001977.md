# Evaluation of natural coffee drying by forced convection

Coffee is the flagship product of Colombia due to its historical importance in society and its value in the world market. This value is defined by the quality of the product, which largely depends on the practices of post-harvest. In this work the process of drying natural coffee (dry profit), Caturra variety, using a forced convection dryer, was evaluated with the aim of analyzing the quality of the final product and finding the best adjustment of the process to models of kinetics of drying thin layer. The experimental design consisted of a factorial 2 2, where the parameters were the temperature (45 and 55 °C) and the air flow (35 and 46 cfm). The variables of response defined for the experiment were the cup test, the water activity, the pH and the degrees Brix. The results indicated that the cup score increased with lower flow and lower temperature, while the water activity decreased with the increase of temperature; moreover, the degrees Brix increased with the decrease of flow. The Midili models modified and Page showed a high correlation with the experimental data, allowing to establish a relationship between the coffee phase and the key. The response variables defined for the experience were cup test, water activity, pH, and Brix. The results indicated that cup score increased with lower flow rate and lower temperature, while water activity decreased with increasing temperature; in addition, Brix increased with decreasing flow rate. The modified Midili and Page models showed a high correlation with the experimental data, allowing the establishment of a relationship between dry time and mood content of green coffee. These findings provide key evidence to strengthen the knowledge of Colombia's coffee dry mill processes, raising the potential that has to reach quality standards
