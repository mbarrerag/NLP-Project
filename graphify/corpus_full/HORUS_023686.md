# Study of microbial diversity associated with cocoa soils with cadmium (Cd) presence and evaluation of its bioremediator potential.

The contamination of soils cultivable by heavy metals is a global problem, their accumulation can cause toxic effects that affect the quality and safety of the harvested products, the composition and functioning of the bacterial communities of the soil. Cadmium (Cd) is a heavy metal, which is found both in the terrestrial crust and in the ocean waters, is emitted to the environment as a result of natural activities (volcanic origin) as an anthropogenic (multiple industrial activities). The Cd present in the soil can enter the food chain through the roots and foliar tissue of the plants, whose rate of translocation and bioaccumulation depends on multiple variables such as the plant type and the physiochemical characteristics of the soils. The chocolate and its derivatives come from the fruit of the tree Theobroma cocoa L. (family Sterculiaceae) native to the tropical forest, whose plant is grown in the Amazon basin and the almonds.
