# DEVELOPMENT OF MOISTURE-ADSORBENT CELLULOSE AND CARBOXYMETHYLCELLULOSE AEROGELS WITH APPLICATION IN THE PACKAGING AND PRESERVATION OF FRESH PRODUCTS

One of the most recurring problems for the preservation of fresh foods is the accumulation of water condensation inside the packaging system. To improve the commercialization of these products, it is necessary to propose packages that minimize moisture accumulation while being sustainable and environmentally friendly. In this work, moisture-adsorbent aerogels were developed from cellulose and carboxymethylcellulose (CMC) from 1 to 5 % (w/w) in aqueous dispersions. CaCO3 and CaCl2 were used as chemical cross-linkers (5 % w/w). The cellulose and CMC aerogels were obtained beginning with a high-speed dispersion (15.500 rpm) followed by ultra-cooling (-45 °C for 24 h) and finally, freeze-drying (-75 °C for 24 h). Subsequently, the structures were characterized by measuring their spectroscopic (FT-IR) and calorimetric (DSC, TGA) properties in addition to their density, and moisture adsorption. To evaluate the application of the aerogels, a test was conducted by packaging 40 g of fresh parsley and 3-6 g of the best aerogel obtained previously, in polyethylene terephthalate (PET) trays with 2 micro- perforations (0.5 mm) at 8 °C and 80 % relative humidity (RH). The aerogel with the highest moisture adsorption capacity (Cads) was the one with Cellulose (5%) + CaCl2 (5%) with a dispersion speed of 15.500 rpm. For this material, a Cads of 0.58 g g-1 was obtained at 20 °C and 60% RH, which is 4 times higher compared to untreated cellulose (cotton) fibers. On the other hand, to describe the moisture adsorption in the aerogel as a function of the RH in the packaging headspace, a pseudo-second-order kinetics coupled with a BET adsorption equilibrium model were fitted to the experimental data. Finally, for the test with parsley, it was found that under refrigeration conditions without any other additional treatment, a shelf life of 1 week was achieved. For the active packaging with aerogel, the parsley was found in good condition for up to two weeks. The use of cellulose-based aerogels in the active packaging can contribute to the preservation of the characteristics of products such as parsley in a more environmentally sustainable way
