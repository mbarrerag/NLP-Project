# Effects of climate change on genetic diversity in representative terrestrial and sweet-acuic biomes species of Colombia's biodiversity.

Genetic diversity is the most basic level of biodiversity and plays a fundamental role in the survival and evolution of species. The ability of organisms to adapt to changing challenges, such as the complex climate change scenario, the growing fragmentation of habitat and the accelerated transformation of ecosystems, rests to a large extent on the richness of genetic variability present in populations. However, this genetic diversity does not distribute evenly in the geographical range that species occupy. Understanding the patterns of structuring of this diversity and the factors underlying its distribution is essential to design effective conservation strategies, initiatives of recovery of species at risk and the sustainable use of the resources that ecosystems provide. In this context, this project seeks to study the genetic patterns that reside in the key species of terrestrial and sweet ecosystems and the factors that underlie their distribution is essential to design effective conservation strategies, initiatives of recovery of species at risk and the sustainable use of the resources that ecosystems provide. In this context, the present project seeks to study the genetic patterns that reside in the key species of terrestrial and sweet ecosystems within the fragile bioacucultural biome of the Tropical Forest.
