# Metabarcoding of microbial soil communities in forest and moor ecosystems in the Cauca Valley.

The soil microorganisms in forests and moors are key axes in the maintenance of ecosystems due to their activities of decomposition, plant nutrition and intervention in biogeochemical cycles. In this research soil samples were collected in four locations in the central mountain range of the Andes in the Valle del Cauca, Colombia following an altitude gradient between 1000 and 3800 msnm. Subsequently the 16S gene of RNAr for bacteria and the ITS1 region were sequenced for fungi using metabarcoding. The analysis of taxonomic composition of microorganisms showed variations of abundances through the elevational gradient, being the bacteria Acidobacteriota and Proteobacteriota favored in the highest altitudes and Actinobacteria, Firmicutes and Verrucomicrobia in the lowest elevations. In fungi, Ascomycota had maximum allocation of readings in the highest elevation, Basidiomycota tended to dominate in the two lowest altitudes and Mortierellomycota reported the majority of its readings in intermediate elevations.
