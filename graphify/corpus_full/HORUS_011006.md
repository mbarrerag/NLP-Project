# MAGDALANA RIA OF CHARACTER TO THE COUNTRY: ABANDON, NEW USES, TERRITORIALITIES IN CONFLICT

The Magdalena River, the river of the Homeland, has a unique importance for Colombia: it has played and continues to play a key role in the configuration of the national territory due to its location, extent, physical complexity, biodiversity and variety of the territories it connects. Its basin, which covers 19 departments, 724 municipalities and 80% of the country’s population, contains the majority of the country’s economic activity, representing a contribution of 85% of the national GDP. For example, the fishing production of the basin represents 70% of the total continental fishing, while energy production is at 95% for thermal energy and 70% for hydroelectric power (Cormagdalena, 2007; AUNAP, 2013). These characteristics denote a distinction of Magdalena against the other two major rivers of the country, the Amazon and Orinoco, which are border regions and cross sparsely populated regions, lacking an articative role. This chapter will focus on analyzing navigation on the Magdalena River as an element of construction and territorial articulation, considering its historical character and recent proposals for revitalization by the State, as the Magdalena River Master Plan of Development has been.
