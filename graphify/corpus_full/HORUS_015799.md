# SENSITIVITY TO COPPER IN BMWP MACROINVERTEBRATES

Sensitivity to copper in BMWP macroinvertebrates Daniel Yepes 1, Sebastián Reynaldi2* 1 Departamento de Geociencias y Medio Ambiente, Facultad de Minas, Universidad Nacional de Colombia 2 Departamento de Ciencias Agronómicas, Facultad de Ciencias Agrarias Universidad Nacional de Colombia *Corresponding author: sreynaldi@unal.edu.co This work aims to determine whether the BMWP method could detect copper contamination in freshwater environments. The BMWP method ranks freshwater aquatic macroinvertebrate taxa by the water quality standard for human consumption of their environments with a rating scale of one to ten, excluding nine. The highest values of the BMWP scale correspond to environments with the macroinvertebrates taxa that live in the environments with the highest quality standards. In contrast, the lowest values of the BMWP correspond to the macroinvertebrate taxa that live in environments with the lowest quality standards. To evaluate the BMWP sensitivity to copper, we downloaded the CuSO4 concentrations that kill 50% of the population, or median lethal concentrations (LC50) determined after 96h in BMWP macroinvertebrate from the USEPA Ecotox databank. We grouped the LC50 by BMWP score, calculating the geometric mean for each score and performing a sensitivity distribution curve (SSD) with them. The SSD curve did not show a relationship between the BMWP method score and copper sensitivity. The sensitivity of taxa varied within each BMWP score when we calculated the geometric mean per taxon. Finally, species sensitivity varied within each taxon. These results show that the BMWP method does not help detect copper contamination. Also, they show we cannot monitor copper sensitivity at the Family or Class level because sensitivity changes from species to species without correspondence to their taxonomic relationships.
