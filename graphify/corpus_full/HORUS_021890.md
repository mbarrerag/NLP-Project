# Operational flexibility of the Colombian electricity system under renewable energy scenarios.

The energy transition to a system based on non-conventional renewable energy sources is characterized by a number of factors, but the energy generated from these is variable, difficult to predict and lacks inertia, and therefore integration is a challenge for operators. The flexibility of the electrical systems is the ability to manage the differences between supply and demand and can be quantified to measure the effects of renewable generation on electrical systems. By 2030, Colombia expects the entry into operation of multiple solar farms and wind farms, therefore, it is essential to assess how flexible the Colombian electricity system is to allow the participation of these projects. This thesis quantified the flexibility requirements of the Colombian electricity system based on changes in net demand and its ramps, considering multiple scenarios of generation and projections of demand. This type of analysis has not been massively carried out in the country and can be used by the system operator and decision makers to establish the road map for the energy transition of Colombia.
