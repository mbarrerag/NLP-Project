# THE STATE OF SOCIAL JUSTICE IN LATIN AMERICA.

At the end of the last century, in various Latin American countries, such as Colombia and Peru, the Social State emerged with a clear commitment to the well-being of citizens and to the minimum conditions of life in dignity. To achieve this end, the Political Constitutions that adopted this new form of State placed as a central pillar the rights of work and social security, as suitable mechanisms to fulfil the new social objectives of the State. However, from the very moment of the adoption of the Colombian Constitutions of 1991 and Peruvian of 1993, the law began to issue labour reform, to make the right of work more flexible and deregulated, and to allow the adoption of systems of individual capitalization in the field of social security. This became a first obstacle to the consolidation of the Social State.
