# REMOVAL OF PERTURBATIONS FROM OPTICAL-PICKUP-UNIT-BASED ILLUMINATION FOR DIGITAL LENSLESS HOLOGRAPHIC MICROSCOPY

Optical Pickup Units (OPUs) have arisen as a cost-effective illumination source for optical systems. Among them, Blu-ray OPUs, which are capable of producing spherical waves with a numerical aperture (NA) of up to 0.85, are particularly compelling for point-source-based techniques like Digital Lensless Holographic Microscopy (DLHM) [1]. Such techniques benefit from the high resolution given by the high-NA illumination [2], and the access to multiple wavelengths. However, this optical performance of the OPU lens is achieved by the implementation of a hybrid diffractive/refractive surface which introduces a strong ring-like structure in the illumination [3]. This perturbation cannot be removed via spatial filtering and leaves only the central area of the wavefront unperturbed, thus reducing the available area. In this contribution, a method to remove the ring-like structure produced by OPU-based illumination in DLHM is presented, thus allowing the use of the full area of the digital sensor. Multiple DLHM holograms of the sample are recorded by transversally scanning the sample plane, each one being affected by the presence of the ring-like perturbation. A corrected hologram is then generated by adding the individually perturbed holograms while considering their relative displacement against a fixed recording. It is not necessary to accurately know the displacement value beforehand, as it can be digitally determined by the normalized cross-correlation [4]. The reconstruction of the newly generated hologram allows the recovery of the full-field information of the sample, including the region previously affected by the ring-like structure. The method has been validated by imaging a phase-only USAF resolution test target and a biological specimen with complex inner structures
