# Digital Consumer Behavior: dependencies, relative strengthening value and literacy in the context of social media apps and mobile advertising.

The increase in the adoption and use of mobile devices by children, adolescents and young adults has led to an interest in research into the effects that social media apps and new digital advertising formats can have on these populations. The aim of this research was, first, to establish whether there is a relationship between the level of use of the smartphone and apps and a pattern of impulsive choice. Second, to identify the relative value of strengthening a monetary reward against different periods of use of social networks, and third, to establish the level of advertising literacy on mobile devices. To develop this work different methodologies were applied that included the application of online questionnaires, intertemporal choice tests and time records of use of mobile apps from a user panel. The results show, first, that the use of the apps does not allow predicting the choices in the intertemporal task, while the level of dependence on the perceived smartphone had a significant relationship with the average of impulsive choices.
