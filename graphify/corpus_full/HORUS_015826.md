# NIQUEL SUPERALLEATIONS CORROSION IN ACUOSATIONAL SOLUTIONS OF AMMONIUM BIFLOUR

Some metal components of gas turbines such as alabes, liners and nozzles are isolated with ceramic thermal barriers to reduce their temperature of service and protect the metal or substrate. Although these components have very high thermal resistances (above 900°C), when certain requirements are met, such as operating time or number of stops and starters, it is necessary to repair the parts and replace the thermal barrier for a new working cycle. To carry out this, it is necessary to remove the worn coating, perform mechanical repairs and prepare the surface for receiving the new coating. For the removal process, several methodologies are available between mechanical, electrical and chemical processes. One of the chemical processes involves the bifluoride salt of ammonium (NH4FHF), which in watery solution by its acid is highly corrosive. In the process, the parts are submerged in this solution for more than six hours at a temperature of 70°C. In this work, the corrosive impact of the NH4FHF solutions in excess of the working temperature is measured, and the industrial solution is observed
