# DURING TURACAN EXTREME CONDITIONS

The hurricane activity in the Atlantic and the Caribbean causes flooding due to the cyclonic tides and wave advance. This results in coastal erosion and damage to ecosystems and infrastructure. In deep waters, offshore platforms and commercial, fishing and tourist boats are also affected. These hazards affect the population and impact the economy and social development of the affected regions (Roldan, 2023). In this study we analyze the interaction between the atmospheric system (winds) and the oceanic (wave) atmosphere under extreme effects of Hurricane Emily 2005. Two main models were used: Weather Research & Forecating (WRF) to simulate the winds and Wave-Watch 3 (WWWW3) for the wave. Initially, a simulation of reference conditions was implemented where both systems were independently modeled. Then, the Oasis Coupler software was used to match the models, and from the 8° to the 8° of the model was used to connect the wind speed and the wind speed of the wind speed and the Charnock coefficient between WRF and WW3. Both simulations covered a spatial domain from 262°E to 303°E in resolution and from the 8°N. In conclusion, this study shows that the coupling between an atmospheric model and a wave model is necessary to obtain wind speed and significant wave height results close to the observed data. It is achieved by better estimation of the roughness coefficient over the oceans, taking into account different wave conditions at similar wind speeds. It underlines the need to integrate atmospheric and wave models to improve the accuracy of weather predictions in regions vulnerable to hurricanes, which is crucial to strengthening early warning programmes and planning for natural disaster response
