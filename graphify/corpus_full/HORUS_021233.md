# Genetic and cultural diversity associated with Amazonian cassava: source of genes for traits of agronomic and nutritional interest, culture and traditional knowledge

The origin center of the cultivated yuca corresponds to the region of the basin of the Amazon River, concentrating there the greatest diversity. This diversity has been conserved by the Amazonian indigenous communities since the domestication of their cultivation and favored by the development of chagras, in which the yuca has a leading role. In the diversity of Amazonian yucas, the source of genes can be found for the improvement of agricultural and nutritional characteristics. Without embrago, given the westernization and the devaluation of indigenous knowledge, agricultural practices such as those of the chagras have been lost. Chagrera women who guard the diversity of yuca are already of advanced age and have no one to bequeath their knowledge and varieties. In this way the conservation of the diversity of yuca in the Amazonian region is at risk. In the face of this problem, a central axis of this project will consist in developing different participatory research activities that allow documenting and re-valuating cultural diversity and indigenous knowledge around the charica as a refuge of the genetic diversity allows for the Amazonian and the development of the model. This project will take advantage of the information available in our research groups on the partial sequencing of genomes, GBS, of a set of Amazonian cassava varieties for the purpose of mapping study by association to cross it with that of phenotypification and identify candidate genes involved in these two traits. For the resistance to bacteriosis the group of varieties with the Xpm681 strain will be inoculated and the follow-up of symptoms will be carried out to calculate the AUDPC and determine the level of resistance of the varieties against infection. The expression of the candidate genes will be evaluated in the two most contrasting varieties (resistant and susceptible) at 48 hours post-inoculation.
