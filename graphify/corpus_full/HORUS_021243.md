# Changes in late positive potential and in the frontal alpha asymmetry associated with distraction as an emotional regulation strategy in young university students of both sexes.

Emotion is a multidimensional affective response linked to value judgments about stimuli, situations or events, considered of importance for the survival of individuals. One of the related themes that has generated greater interest in its application to multiple contexts is emotional regulation, defined as the modification of the affective response by applying a variety of strategies that produce changes at subjective, behavioral and physiological level. Of the strategies studied, distraction has shown great effectiveness in acting early in the modulation of the response generated by stimuli of different modality. In this study, changes in indicators of central and peripheral neurophysiological activity produced by images with negative emotional valence content after the application of a strategy of distraction. For this purpose, alterations in the late positive potential (PPT), the frontal alpha asymmetry and facial muscular response during the implementation of the emotional regulation strategy will be evaluated. These indicators have been proposed as biomarkers of the regulation of emotions in adults and children, but have been little explored in young adults, despite being an age in which many changes occur from the point of view, and social regulation are considered to be appropriate
