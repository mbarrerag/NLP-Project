# Control of an induction engine without speed sensors with active disturbance rejection for applications in electric vehicles.

The electrical vehicles (EV) must meet high standards of reliability and safety. One way to meet those standards is to develop sensor-free control strategies for induction engines (IM), addressing their existing challenges around speed estimation and robust control. This includes distortion introduced by filtering schemes, the effects of saturation, the complexity of the model and the variations in its parameters. Algebraic methods and active disturbance rejection control designs (ADRC) can meet those challenges thanks to inherent filtering capabilities and design flexibilities to address complex systems robustly. The aim of this document is to establish an algebraic estimator and an ADRC design without sensors for an IM in the context of electrical vehicles. Our approach is the following, first, we develop and analyze an algebraic estimation strategy for the speed of the rotor based on a classical dynamic model of IM. Subsequently, we design an ADRC scheme without sensors for monitoring and management of the IM based on generalized proportional controllers (GPI). We evaluate with simulations and experiments some properties and performance of the speed scheme without sensors and its estimation
