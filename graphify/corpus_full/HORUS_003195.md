# SOIL QUALITY: AN INDICATOR OF RECOVERY IN A NATURAL RESERVE IN THE COLOMBIAN ANDES.

The soils provide ecosystem services essential for the sustainability of ecosystems and biodiversity, it is crucial to evaluate their quality and health through the evaluation of ecological processes. Only some studies estimate the effectiveness of ecosystem restoration based on the evaluation of soil quality indicators (CS). This study evaluated the five most common soil use scenarios in Andean ecosystems in the Civil Society Natural Reserve (RNSC) The Magic Mountain, namely: natural forest, coffee, degraded soils, active and passive restoration. In this study, the physical, chemical and biological characteristics were analyzed in relation to soil use, establishing the baseline for CS indicators for different soil uses, determining the state of restoration systems at the site of study. The CS parameters were evaluated by ANOVA and test of Dunnett. Sus under passive and active restoration presented a lower apparent density, greater porosity, increasing the number of individuals and families of macroinvertebrates. Passive restoration of the soil presented low pH and aluminium values, increasing the potassium compared to other soil uses.
