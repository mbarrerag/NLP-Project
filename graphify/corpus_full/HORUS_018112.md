# QUALITY IN THE PROVISION OF INSURANCE SERVICES

Ensuring quality in the provision of health services has become a strategic organizational element that often allows for questioning where institutional efforts should be directed. While important structural and process elements have been considered, it is now necessary to go through the recognition of health outcomes of the population accessing health services, these results include negative outcomes associated with the occurrence of adverse events, as well as the experience that the patient experiences in transit through health organizations. The good care experience experienced by patients and families is the pillar of quality in the provision of nursing services. Achieving this level of quality requires the strengthening of a service delivery model that includes 4 elements: the technical, conceptual and communicative characteristics of the nurse; a care environment that ensures an adequate mix of technical and professional staff, shared decision-making systems, effective relationships between staff, shared power, organization of services and a functional physical environment; care focused on the person that allows the recognition of multidimensional needs and management from the recognition of individuality; and strengthening the patient's participation in what is now recognized as the patient's experience of providing care; and the patient's experience of providing care; and the patient's last-expended care;
