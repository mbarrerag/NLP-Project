# MATHEMATIC MODELS FOR CURRICULARY MANAGEMENT

Managing one or more curriculum programs involves making decisions that impact students’ learning. It is often necessary to take them even when there is no reliable information about the actual state of the program, the causes of their problems, and the potential impact of the changes that are made. This book helps to remedy that difficulty. It is not a glass ball that fills that lack of information. It is a collection of examples on how to build mathematical and software models that help answer questions about the flow and number of students, graduation times, teaching resources, evolution of a faculty, self-evaluations, professional profile, training objectives, learning paths and relationships between concepts. We have relied on concepts of discrete systems, diffuse arithmetic, ontology, aggregation operators, genetic algorithms, among others, to build models. Each area of knowledge has its specificities. We do not intend that the models presented here are extrapolable to any curriculum. The main contribution of each model is its structure, and the way it has adapted to represent a particular situation
