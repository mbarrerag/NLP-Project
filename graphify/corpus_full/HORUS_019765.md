# Reactivation of Chagas disease after autologous transplantation of hematopoietic parents. Case report and literature review

Chagas disease is a parasitic disease endemic in Latin America transmitted by triatominos. It is associated with risk factors such as poverty and rurality. After acute infection, one third of patients present a commitment to the heart, digestive system or central nervous system, while the remaining two thirds do not present this type of secondary commitment. Pharmacological immunosuppression breaks the balance between the immune system and the parasite, which favors its reactivation. Clinical case. The case of a 58-year-old man from a Colombian rural area, with diagnosis of multiple myeloma resistant to first-line drugs, which required a new chemotherapy and consolidation scheme with autologous stem cell transplantation. After transplantation, he presented febrile neutropenia. Initial microbiological studies were negative. In peripheral blood smears, trypstigotes were demonstrated and diagnosis of acute Chagas disease post-transplantation was diagnosed. Treatment with benznidazole was initiated.
