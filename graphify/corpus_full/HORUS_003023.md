# RECOMMENDATION 1. IMPORTANCE OF BIODIVERSITY AND HEALTH NEXUM FOR NATIONAL BIODIVERSITY PLANS AND STRATEGIES – PRIMARY HEALTH PREVENTION

The concept “One Health” summarizes a well-known idea for more than a century: human, animal and ecosystem health are interconnected and interdependent (World Organisation for Animal Health [WHOA], 2024). Wild species have hosted a wide variety of pathogens, to which human beings can be susceptible. However, it is essential to consider that human pathogens represent only a small fraction of the world’s parasitic diversity (Balloux and van Dorp, 2017). It is important to urgently address drivers of loss of biological diversity in order to reduce health risks, as suggested by the Kunming-Montreal World Biodiversity Framework (Subsidiary Body on Scientific, Technical and Technological Advice, 2024). Human activities have crossed natural barriers in an unsustainable manner, invading wild areas or extracting specimens for consumption, trade (mascots), or as derived or manufactured products, situations that facilitate risk of unsafe contact between animals and human beings.
