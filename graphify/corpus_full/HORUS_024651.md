# Association between the use of ultrasonographic guide for the placement of central venous access with the development of catheter-associated bacteremia in patients from the Intensive Care Unit of the National University Hospital of Colombia, 2019-2021

The placement of central venous catheters (CVC) by ultrasound guide has been growing over the last decade, demonstrating significant reduction of mechanical complications. However, it is not clear yet yet if there is a higher risk of infectious complications of the procedure. To establish if there is an association between the use of the obsonographic guide (US) in the placement of the CVC and the development of bacteremia associated to the catheter in critically ill patients of Intensive Care Unit (UCI). Study of cases and peer controls of critically ill adult patients in ICU, with indication of the CVC in the central insertion between the years 2019 to 2021. 250 patients were included, 76% presented infection COVID-19. Ultrasonographic guide was used for the insertion of the CVC in 72.8% and the most frequent use factor in the anatomic site was a vein subclavia in 56%. The most frequent agents of bacteraemia were Gram negative in 70%. A trend of association between the use of the US guide for the insertion of CVC in the CVC and the development of bacteremia (OR 2.16 (OR 2.16 (IC, CD, CC, CC, CC stream infection increase in critically ill patients in Intensive care Unit. Matched case and control study of critically ill patients in intensive care unit with central line insertion indication between years 2019 to 2021. We included 250 patients, 76% had COVID-19. Ultrasonography guide was used for central line insertion in 72.8% and subclavian vein was the main anatomical site used in 56%. Gram negative bacteria was the most frequent etiology in 70%. We documented a trend of association between ultrasonography guide for central line inserted and catheter related of bloodstream infection OR=2.16 (CI 95%, 0.89-5.21, p=0.085)
