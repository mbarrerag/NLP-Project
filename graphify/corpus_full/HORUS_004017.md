# Change in the carbonate system and organic carbon accumulation in water bodies adjacent to seagrass meadows in the Colombian island Caribbean

The carbon-in-stressed ecosystems considered strategic in the mitigation of climate change include seagrass grasslands, due to their high primary productivity and high carbon capture rates. This paper analysed the relationship between the organic carbon reserve in seagrass meadows of the island of San Andrés, international reserve of Seaflower Biosphere, and the dynamics of the carbonate system, in order to assess quantitatively the modulating effect that carbon capture in the seagrass could have on the carbonate system in the water bodies. Measurements of Total Alkalinity and Inorganic Carbon Dissolved on a grassland of marine grasses and on an adjacent point without grass during several climatic periods between 2019 and 2021. Additionally, biomass in foot, rhizoidal biomass and organic carbon content were evaluated in the sediment of the grassland. A strong seasonal influence was found, characterized by lower inorganic carbon values during the dry season.
