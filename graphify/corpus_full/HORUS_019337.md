# GENOCIDE COLLECTIONS IN THE BRITISH MUSEUM: HOUSE OF SUMBRKS, SEMIL CEST

Saved in Franks House, part of the British Museum, is a large collection of ethnographic artifacts from Latin America and the Caribbean, many of which remain undocumented or digitized. One of the first collections based on research grants initiated by the Santo Domingo Center of Excellence for Research on Latin America focuses, precisely, on a part of the Amazonian collection that has received a minimal interest: the collection of Murui-Bora objects, many donated by Eugène Robuchon and acquired during his travels in the Putumayo region of the Peruvian-Colombian Amazon in the time of the Fever of the Caucho (1900-1920). Oscar Román and Alicia Sánchez, both Murui Muina elders, were introduced to the Center by Juan Alvaro Echeverri, professor of anthropology at the Amazonian headquarters of the National University of Colombia, for their dominion and experience, with the hope that the group could develop a project that made these collections accessible to the descendants of the communities from which said objects were taken.
