# SOIL MICROMORPHOLOGY IN THE STUDY OF LAND USES IN PARAMO ANDISOLS

Paramos are endemic and climatically sensitive ecosystems in the northern part of the Andes, where changes in climate and land use have a strong impact on landscapes, flora and fauna diversity. Soils in the paramo ecosystems provide ecosystem services as carbon sequestration and water regulation; the soils in the Andean natural ecosystems are subject to high risk of degradation in relation to land use dynamics characterized by changes from natural ecosystems to cultivation and finally the establishment of grasslands. In this study Six soils profiles under different soil uses and geomorphological positions in the Guerrero paramo, Eastern Andes; were morphological, physicochemical, mineralogical and micromorphological characterized. In this study current changes in land uses with the transition from natural forest to pasture is consider from edaphological point of view. Changes in soils uses makes the soil vulnerable to degradation by compaction, erosion, and carbon dioxide emissions in relation to anthropogenic activities. The mineralogical results showed kaolinite and quartz than dominant in clay size fraction in all soils, followed by illite, vermiculite and interstratification chlorite-vermiculite and traces of imogolite, halloysite, muscovite and interstratification illite-smectite. The chemical composition showed acidic soils, with a high Al saturation and a low base saturation. Organic matter illuviation and iron and manganese nodules, coatings, and hydromorphical conditions with presence of abundant iron hipocoatings. The soil profiles morphology shown the presence of high organic matter content with tissues, macroinvertebrates, mit and and earthworm features; in not allophanic andisols where organic and ecological conditions are key factor. Soils are moderately evolved under a present scenario of forest clearance and expansions of grasslands in combination with paleoenvironmental conditions associated to volcanic ashes deposition and stone line presence leaving its mark inside soil profiles
