# Acupuncture for the management of patients with cancer-associated anorexia-cachexia: scoping review

anorexia-cachexia syndrome associated with cancer is characterized by weight loss, loss of appetite, asthenia, and nausea, among other symptoms. Its prevalence is high, especially in gastrointestinal cancer, and conventional therapies often have side effects, which is why acupuncture has been considered as a therapeutic alternative. The aim of this scoping review was to explore the evidence on the effects of acupuncture on this topic. this study corresponded to a scoping review. Electronic searches were performed in Pubmed, EMBASE, LILACS, Science Direct, Cochrane Library, BVS MTCI Americas, Korean Medical Article Database, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure, among others. The list of results was reviewed in several phases, articles were selected and data extraction was performed. 498 articles were obtained, of which six met eligibility criteria: one corresponded to a feasibility study, one to a randomized controlled trial and four to pilot studies. The use of acupuncture is feasible and safe, although the evidence is limited with multiple methodological flaws. Only one study reported ear pain as an adverse event. the present review points out the feasibility and safety of acupuncture in anorexia-cachexia syndrome associated with cancer; However, the evidence is scarce, preliminary and heterogeneous. Studies with greater methodological rigor, standardization in processes and a larger sample size are required to confirm the effects. The use of acupuncture in the context of evidence-based practice is suggested
