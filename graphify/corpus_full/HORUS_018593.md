# THE MEMORY SITIES ON AFRODESSENT STORY IN CHARTERGEN AND THE FIGURE OF PEDRO ROMERO

In recent years, a series of social mobilizations have taken place which, among other issues, have questioned what past we remember, who we exalt and who we silence through monuments, street names, plaques, memorials, celebrations, museums and different places of memory. From these questionings, we wonder about the memorial sites about the Afro-descendant history in Colombia, which are very scarce in contrast to the significant presence of Africans, Africans and their descendants throughout the territory since the sixteenth century. Given the persistence of racism and racial inequalities in the world and in our country, we consider it important to reflect on the silences about the Afro-descendant history in cities, monuments and public spaces, and thus to deepen the debate on how memories, sites and places of memory can be tools that contribute to social cohesion, national reconciliation, the anti-racist struggle and the construction of a more just and equitable society
