# HIGH NATURAL CONCENTRATION OF CHANGE IN THE SOUND RESULTS IN LOW DIVERSITY OF MICORRIZA HONGS FORMING ARBUSCULAR ASSOCIATED TO CACAAO (THEOBROME CACOAO L.)

The formative fungi of mycorrhizas arbuscular (HFMA) are symbionants that are present in the rizosphere of cocoa plants and the diversity of their communities is modified, depending on various factors such as the presence of cadmium (Cd) in the soil. The persistence of HFMA in soils enriched naturally with Cd could be an indicator of its ability to tolerate this condition. This research characterized the structure of the local HFMA community present in the rizosphere of cocoa plants in two soils with low (B-Cd: 0.1 mg kg-1) and high (A-Cd: 20.9 mg kg-1) concentration of Cd. Spokes of HFMA were identified by taxonomic keys and their abundance, wealth and diversity was determined in original soil samples and after their multiplication by trap crops. The HFMA communities were compared using the primary component analysis (ACP) and the fourth-stress indexes of alpha and beta.
