# Persistent left upper vena cava syndrome in adult patient: case report

The persistent left upper vena cava (VCSIP) is a venous congenital anomaly whose frequency is estimated to be 0.5% of the general adult population. Asymptomatic upper vena cava for most individuals and its diagnosis is often incidental, when diagnostic images are taken or vascular procedures are performed on the chest. Some patients may have dyspnea of effort with the entity, which is explained by the presence of short circuits from right to left. Knowledge of this anatomy in patients is fundamental for the proper planning of some types of procedures and surgeries. Presentation of the case: 59-year-old man with chronic renal disease stage 5, in whom left-jugular venous dialysis catheter implantation was performed. The technique was described without complications and was even performed under ecographic guidance by Nephrology. The catheter presented early dysfunction. Imaging studies (Path and angioTAC radiography) were performed that confirmed the presence of a left upper vena cava with drainage in the left atrium. The VCSIP is an unusual but important anomaly in the clinic, it can complicate intervention procedures or cardiotic surgeries
