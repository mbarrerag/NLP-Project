# Theoretical and experimental study of adsorption and catalytic reactivity of asphaltene fractions A1 and A2 over cubic NiO–PdO/CeO 2 nanoparticles

This study aims to analyze the aggregation state of A1 and A2 asphaltene subfractions and their influence on the adsorption and reactivity of ceria-based nanoparticles. To this aim, a molecular dynamics (MD) model was proposed to determine the effect of the asphaltene concentration and structure type (A1 and A2) on the aggregation behavior. Then, batch adsorption experiments were conducted using toluene as solvent and varying asphaltene concentrations between 100 and 1500 mg·L−1. Non-catalytic and catalytic experiments were executed at low (0.084 MPa) and high (6.0 MPa) pressures under air and nitrogen atmospheres to analyze oxidative and pyrolytic responses. Quantitative analysis from MD simulations revealed that the aggregate is higher for A1 than for A2 subfraction, explained by the higher density of A1. Adsorption experiments demonstrated that the affinity between A1 and CeO2 nanoparticles was greater than that of A2 and CeO2 nanoparticles, resulting in a higher amount of A1 asphaltenes adsorbed (1.65mg·m−2) than A2 (0.84mg·m−2). Oxidative experiments showed that oxygen chemisorption occurs on A1 subfraction, while no oxygen chemisorption was observed for A2. This led to lower activation energy for A1 decomposition in catalytic and non-catalytic oxidation. Non-catalytic pyrolysis of A2 showed higher mass loss at lower temperatures and less coke formation (8 % for A2 and 11 % for A1) at the end of heating, attributed to the different densities between A1 and A2 aggregates. The novelty of this work lies in the detailed analysis of the adsorptive and catalytic behavior of nanoparticles with A1 and A2 asphaltene fractions, providing insights that could enhance the reaction pathways of asphaltene aggregates in industrial applications
