# THE BIGGIE: DECODING THE BLOOD PARASITES THAT INFECT WILD ANIMALS IN COLOMBIA

Colombia is a neotropical country with one of the highest levels of biodiversity in the world. Despite major efforts to characterize it, there are areas, such as wildlife blood parasites, where information is scarce. Blood parasites are highly susceptible to spillover from wildlife to humans or livestock, causing zoonotic disease and economic loss. Our research group has devoted the last 20 years to surveys of wild fauna in different ecosystems. We have determined the host-parasite relationship by analyzing their distribution genetic and morphological characteristics. Some remarkable results are: a. Discovered nine species of blood parasites b. Generated at least 100 new barcode sequences and related them to their morphotypes. c. Reported one apoplast and nine mitochondrial genomes d. Identified the susceptibility of certain families of birds, reptiles, and amphibians to parasitic infection, e. Our studies allow us to decipher Leucocytozoon's specific distribution in South America f. We created the only biological collection of blood parasites in Colombia. In spite of these efforts, in our country and in most of the region, there are some areas where knowledge could be improved, such as the identification of vectors, which is essential for the completion of the life cycle, and the knowledge of transmission patterns. Today, significant progress and a holistic view of the problem can be achieved through multidisciplinary and inter-institutional cooperation. In order to prevent new epidemics and spillover, greater attention to wildlife and their pathogens is essential
