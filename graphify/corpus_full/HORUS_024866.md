# Nicotiana tabacum transgenic line expressing the phaC gene of Aeromonas caviae for the production of polyhydroxyalcanoates.

Polyhydroxyalcanoates (PHAs) are polyesters produced and naturally degraded by bacteria, whose properties make them similar to petroleum-derived plastics. The mass production of PHAs is costly, therefore the genetic transfer of key genes and their production in plants has been considered as an alternative, since these organisms have a low maintenance cost and why they can generate higher biomass. However, one of the main problems that have limited their production, is that plants generally present developmental and growth problems associated with sequestration of key substances for metabolism and directed towards the synthesis of PHAs. Recent findings have identified that their biosynthesis in peroxisomes reduces the negative effects due to the presence and abundance of intermediate compounds in the biosynthesis route of these biopolymers. For this reason, our objectives focused on obtaining genetically modified lines of Nicotiana tobacco var.
