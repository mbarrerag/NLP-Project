# SPODOPTERA FRUGIPERDA (LEPIDOPTERA, NOCTUIDAE) VIROME (CORN STRAIN) BASED ON RNA-SEQ: IDENTIFICATION OF POTENTIAL BIO-CONTROLS

Spodoptera frugiperda is a pest that causes devastating damages on corn, cotton, sorghum and sugar cane, causing millions of dollars in economic losses worldwide. Due to the wide distribution of the corn strain and the evidence of its increased resistance to insecticides, it is urgent to search more sustainable control strategies. Among these, the use of the microbiota and viruses associated with the insect presents a promising alternative. However, in Colombia, little is known about S. frugiperda-associated RNA viruses, limitating the development of strategies that offer new opportunities for its management. This study aims to characterize the virome of larvae S. frugiperda corn strain to identify viruses with biocontrol potential on the species For this study, three larvae of S. frugiperda (instar 5) were collected in Envigado, Antioquia. Their RNA was extracted using the TRIzol method (Invitrogen, California) RNA quality was verified and sequenced (pair-end) with the Illumina NovaSeq X plus platform at CD Genomics (New York). Bioinformatic analysis incluido evaluation of read quality with FastQC v0.12.1, removal of adapters and low-quality reads using Trimmomatic v0.36. Then, host sequences were filtered using Bowtie2 v2.5.3 to map to the reference sequence of S. Frugiperda, unmapped sequences were assembled using the rnaviralspades v3.15.5 tool. To identify possible viruses in the transcripts, BLASTn v2.14.1 and BLASTx v2.14.1 alignments were performed against a reference virus database. The viral sequences were classified according to their taxonomic identity. Among the viral sequences identified as possible biological controllers, the following genera were found: Betabaculovirus and Nucleopolyhedrovirus (family Baculoviridae). These viruses have been identified in other insects and are promising for S. frugiperda management. Then, MEGABLAST was performed to verify the identity of the viruses detected. Unfortunately, due to various factors that may have influenced data analysis, the verification did not match the viruses initially identified. Further studies are needed, possibly using alternative software or methodologies, to provide a more reliable virus with biocontrol potential
