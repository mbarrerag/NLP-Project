# Hand surgery with local anesthesia with WALANT technique. Experience with a series of cases

Abstract Introduction: Procedures in hand surgery require the use of tourniquet to avoid bleeding and, generally, use anesthesiology sedation to control pain and discomfort of the tourniquet. A decade ago, local anesthesia without tourniquet or sedation was used in hand surgery as a safe and efficient option for surgical interventions; this technique is known as WALANT. The aim of the work is to evaluate a prospective series of patients who underwent WALANT technique in hand surgery. It is a series of cases that characterize the performance of a local anesthetic technique for procedures in hand surgery without sedation and without tourniquet. The purpose of the study was to evaluate surgery time, intra- and post-operative pain and level of satisfaction. This work is accepted by the ethics committee. 96 patients were operated and 73 were more useful surgery of soft parts and 23 of bone parts. 92 patients (96%) reported being satisfied and without post-operative pain. In 4 cases, intraoperative surgery was necessary. No patient required tourniquet
