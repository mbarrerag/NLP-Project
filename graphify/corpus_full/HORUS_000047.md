# Development and characterization of a phloretin-loaded self-emulsifying drug delivery system (SEDDS) as outstanding strategy in topical acne treatment

Acne vulgaris remains one of the most prevalent dermatological conditions worldwide, partially driven by bacterial dysbiosis involving Staphylococcus epidermidis and Cutibacterium acnes. Since conventional therapies face increasing challenges due to antimicrobial resistance, phloretin, a natural polyphenolic compound with antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties, has emerged as a promising alternative. However, its poor aqueous solubility and chemical instability limit its topical application. This study aimed to develop and characterize a Self-Emulsifying Drug Delivery System (SEDDS) to enhance the topical delivery of phloretin for acne treatment. Formulations were optimized using solubility studies, pseudoternary phase diagrams, and a Central Composite Design, targeting droplet size, polydispersity index (PDI), and zeta potential. Stability under accelerated and storage conditions was evaluated. Antimicrobial activity against S. epidermidis and C. acnes was assessed by determining MIC and IC 50 values. Skin retention and distribution assessments were analyzed using Franz diffusion cells, confocal microscopy, and tape stripping tests. The optimized phloretin-loaded SEDDS exhibited a droplet size of 67.74 ± 2.42 nm, PDI of 0.12 ± 0.03, and zeta potential of −19.03 ± 3.36 mV. MIC values for both bacteria were reduced to 31.25 μg/mL with the formulation, compared to 75 and 37.5 μg/mL for pure phloretin. Confocal microscopy confirmed distribution within the stratum corneum and viable epidermis without dermal penetration. Tape stripping revealed a time-dependent accumulation, with the highest phloretin levels after 6 h. Overall, the developed SEDDS significantly enhanced phloretin's physicochemical stability, antimicrobial efficacy, and targeted skin retention, supporting its potential for safer and more effective topical acne therapy
