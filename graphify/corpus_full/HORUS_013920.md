# _x0093_I like Spanish_x0094_: Bilingual Acquisition of Spanish Verbs

This master's thesis project aims to investigate the way in which children of San Andrés (of mixed and root families) acquire and use the verbs of Spanish. In these Afro-Caribbean territories, there is a situation of social bilingualism that makes Spanish and Creole (the Creole language of the Raizal ethnic group) coexist in complex and dynamic conditions. Indeed, the raizal infant children, in general, can be considered potential bilingual (or in development), since they receive, from an early age, linguistic input in both languages. In fact, it is also frequent to hear, in early stages, bilingual linguistic productions (sometimes only in Creole; others, only in Spanish; and others, in the two languages). However, to investigate the acquisition and use of the Spanish language forms, the latter will be considered, the latter will have a functionalistic approach based on use, i.e., it is assumed that linguistic expressions have a profound relationship with the input received, the communitive intentions and the social motivations of use of these expressions. This experiment will include six nonce verbs, i.e. six ad hoc invented verbal forms to ensure that children do not know them in advance. These forms will be presented, spaced, every three weeks; and with them, the relative independence of verbal endings, i.e., will be measured how independent the past conjugations or endings -é (cant-é), -aste (cant-aste), -ó (cant-ó), etc. The two sociolinguistic characterization questionnaires will offer, for example, more general data on the environment, languages and dynamics of interaction in the neighbourhood in which children are found. Thus, the linguistic acquisition and comparisons of possible social patterns may be made
