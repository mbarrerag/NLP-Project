# CHICXULUB MEGATERREMOTE: EVIDENCE IN THE GEOLOGY REGISTRATION OF OCCIDENTAL EUROPE AND AMERICAS

The impact of Chicxulub (~66 Ma) against what is today the Yucatan Peninsula, in the Mexican Caribbean, coincides with the Cretaceous-Paleogen limit (K/Pg). The environmental consequences of this collision are the most likely cause of the last of the five massive extinction events of the history of our planet. Geophysical models suggest that the impact released energy equivalent to 1023 Julys, sufficient to generate meganetremotos (Magnitude 11 or more) and communal replicas in vast areas. To verify the validity of this hypothesis, we have examined in detail the sedimentary record of numerous sections K/Pg in Colombia, Mexico, the United States, Cuba, Spain, Netherlands, France, Croatia and Italy, in order to obtain physical evidence and reconstruct the consequences of this planetary event. Here, we show that it is possible to identify evidence of failure and fracture, in-situ liquefaction and deformation of mild sediment before, during and after the fall of ejectas derived from the impact of Chicx Faulub.
