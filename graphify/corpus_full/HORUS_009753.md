# Morphological analysis of sagitta otoliths as a tool for discrimination against populations of Mugil lyca Valenciennes, 1836 and Caranx crysos (Mitchill, 1815), Colombian Caribbean.

Both the black cojinúa (Caranx crysos) and the lebranche (Mugil lya) are important species in the artisanal fishery of the Colombian Caribbean, being C. crysos the fish with greater historical volumes of fishing landed. However, the overfishing and environmental damage are factors that have contributed to the decrease of its abundance. One of the main problems in its management is that it is not known if it is possible to differentiate stocks or populations of these species, which makes it difficult to implement strategies of fisheries management. Therefore, this study addressed the question of whether there are different stocks of M. lya and C. crisos in the Colombian Caribbean different according to its morphometric characteristics and the shape of the otoliths. For this, individuals from four locations distributed throughout the Colombian Caribbean were collected (Urabá, Tolu, Santa Marta and Guajira for C. crysos and the same ones for M. lyza less Urabá were analyzed. For this purpose, the morphometric sites, the olites rights of each fish were extracted and the variation were photographed and the results were photographed. C. crysos the identified stocks could be linked rather to oceanographic factors of the Colombian Caribbean in particular the dynamic interaction between the Panama-Colombia countercurrent in the south and the phenomenon of emergence in the north. It is established that there are statistically differentiable stocks of C. crysos and M. liza, which forms the basis of a geographic management focused both for its conservation and for its exploitation
