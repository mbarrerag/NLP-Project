# Detection of time series anomalies using unsupervised methods

This research work focuses on the analysis and comparison of various unsupervised models for the detection of anomalies in time series. These series are generated from simulated seasonal patterns and the introduction of anomalies using Markov chains. The time series combine cyclical behaviors and seasonal components, using adjusted cosine functions and values generated from Poisson distributions. The anomalies are injected by a transition matrix that alters the expected behavior of the series, simulating rare or atypical events. This approach allows generating data that mimic real situations in which anomalies are uncommon and difficult to predict. The models evaluated include Isolation Forest, Autoencoders and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), which were selected by their effectiveness in different contexts of detection of anomalies. Each of these models was subjected to an exhaustive evaluation using metrics such as accuracy, recall, F1-score, accuracy, as well as the false positive and negative rates.
