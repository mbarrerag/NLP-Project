# The Colombian Caribbean urban system: hierarchy and flows

This research aims to contribute to a better understanding of a classic object of study of geographical discipline: cities and urban systems. To this end, a combination of theoretical and methodological perspectives is proposed in the study of the phenomenon. In particular, this work deals with the urban system shaped by the relations of the three main cities of the Colombian Caribbean region: Barranquilla, Cartagena and Santa Marta; emphasizing the way spatial interactions on different scales construct the territory and blur the formal boundaries, giving a relational sense to the geographical space. The research question that guided the process was: How has the urban system of the Caribbean coast been configured from the multiscale networks of the cities of Barranquilla, Cartagena and Santa Marta in the 21st century? The general objective of the work is to explain the configuration of the urban system of the Caribbean region in the first two decades of the 21st century, using the analysis of the hierarchy and multiscale flows of the cities of Barranquilla, Cartagena and Santa Marta.
