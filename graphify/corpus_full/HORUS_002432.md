# Numerical simulation of biodegradation in sanitary fillers: adjustment of experimental data

The sanitary fillers remain economically viable for the disposal of Urban Solid Waste (USR), however, experiences of failures in various places in Colombia and the world, lead to soil, water and air pollution, damaging ecosystems, and biodiversity. Numeric models can help improve design by considering the biodegradation, hydraulic, thermal and mechanical phenomena involved in sanitary fillings. This work presents a simulation of biodegradation in sanitary fills, calibrating the parameters so that the results match the experimental data of the technical literature. COMSOL Multiphysics was used to implement the biodegradation model of McDougall, following the transformation of organic matter into volatile fatty acids (AGV) and methane production (CH4) via acetylogenesis. The parameters taken from previous references were recalibrated to fit the data of six U.S. landfills. The results of the variation of concentration with time for organic matter, the AGVs and CH4 continue satisfactorily the expected behavior and conform to the experimental data.
