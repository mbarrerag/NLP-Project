# PRIMARY CUTANEOUS LEIOMYOSARCOMA: A SINGLE INSTITUTION STUDY TREATED WITH MODIFIED MOHS SURGERY

Cutaneous leiomyosarcoma (LMS) is a rare neoplasm of muscular origin. It makes up 2'3% of cutaneous sarcomas and has a global incidence of 0.04%.1 LMS may be metastatic or primary. Primary ones can be divided into two histological subtypes: (i) cutaneous/dermal, and (ii) subcutaneous/hypodermal. The greater the thickness, the worse the prognosis. Rates of local recurrence between 24% and 37% and remote metastasis between 4% and 45% have been reported.1,2 The standard treatment is wide excision; however, Mohs surgery might have a useful role. Few LMS sets have been reported in the literature, and thus we decided to make a clinical-pathological description of the cases treated in our institution; the majority of them were treated with modified Mohs surgery (MMS)
