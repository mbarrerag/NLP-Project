# SAMPLE SIZES FOR THE APPLICATION OF THE CENTRAL LIMIT THEOREM IN POISSON DISTRIBUTIONS WITH SMALL POPULATION MEANS

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) is one of the most important in statistics, and for its application it is common to consider that sample sizes of n = 30 are enough to use this theorem. Nevertheless, this is untrue in general, in particular for distributions such as the Poisson that is asymmetric when the population mean is small, and there is no clarity around the right sample size in order to use the CLT in this context. Then, using statistical simulation with R, it is possible to study which pairs of sample sizes and means are suitable to use CLT through the generation of 500 samples for different 12 sample sizes and 80 populations means and performing a Lilliefors normality test, and repeating this 2,150 times for each pair of sample size and mean to calculate a rate of non-rejection of the null hypothesis using the p-value with a significance level of a = 0,05. Finally, it was found that for very small means, sample sizes greater than 1,000 are desirable
