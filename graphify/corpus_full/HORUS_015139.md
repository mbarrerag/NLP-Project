# CONSISTENCE OF LONG-TERM TRENDS AND HYDRICAL BALANCE IN AMAZON

The consistency of long-term trends in the water balance of 63 sub-basins of the Amazon River was studied. Precipitation, evaporation, runoff and water storage series in the soil obtained from information from remote sensors and flow stations were used. The series were completed by applying an adaptation of the methodology proposed by Kondrashov & Ghil (2006) for the period from February 1995 to February 2015. On the series, the decomposition was applied in empirical modes to filter natural variability and isolate the residue representing the average trend of the series. The Mann'Kendall and Sen tests were applied to determine the sign and magnitude of the trends. There was no generalized unidirectional trend for the Amazon basin in any of the variables studied. The consistency of the overall water balance equation in the short-term conditions and its approach to the long-term were evaluated, finding that in the long term the error in the balance tends asymptotically to a constant value, different from zero.
