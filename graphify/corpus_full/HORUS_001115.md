# Metabolite profiling-based analysis reveals up-regulated shikimate derivatives in carnation roots associated with the thiamine-induced resistance against Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. dianthi

Interest is increasing for understanding the effects of substances that activate a plant's natural defense mechanisms. Among these effects, vitamins such as thiamine have been reported as resistance inducers in plant-pathogen interactions, particularly by promoting the accumulation of defensive specialized metabolites. The present study analyzed the impact of foliar thiamine applications and Fusarium oxysporum f.sp. dianthi (Fod) inoculations of carnation roots, phenotypic responses, and phenolic metabolite profiles. We experimented under greenhouse conditions on two cultivars with contrasting resistance to Fod. We employed chemical profiling using HPLC-DAD-MS to identify key differences in phenolic metabolites among treatments. Thiamine application reduced disease incidence and severity, increasing the abundance of 6 metabolites in the susceptible cultivar ('Mizuki') and 21 in the resistant cultivar ('Golem'). Thiamine-mediated priming, followed by Fod inoculation, further increased metabolite abundance to 18 and 29 in the respective cultivars. Differential metabolites, including glycosylated, malonyl glycosylated, methylated flavonoids, and anthranilic acid derivatives, were identified and associated with antioxidant, antifungal, and resistance responses in carnation and other pathosystems. These findings demonstrate that thiamine treatment promotes the accumulation of specific phenolic compounds, enhancing carnation resistance against vascular wilt and positioning thiamine as a promising plant biostimulant through phenolic metabolite upregulation
