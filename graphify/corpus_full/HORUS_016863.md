# SCIENTIFIC JOURNAL OF APPLIED SOCIAL AND CLINICAL SCIENCE

From the pre-Hispanic groups that inhabited what we know today as Colombia, to the current rural and urban constructions, are examples of how human beings adapt to a territory, identifying techniques and materials suitable for conditions that mitigate the risks of their environment. This knowledge, transmitted from generation to generation, enriched on the basis of a trial and error system, the survival to natural and technological disasters, violence, displacement, among others, in many cases undocumented; shows us ingenious, practical and successful examples in contrast to the imposition of foreign construction systems, which generate conflicts with the environment and cultural appropriation, displacing knowledge that allows an adaptation to the place in conditions from the Guajira desert to the humidity of the Pacific, the Amazon jungle and the mountain and volcanic chains of the Andes. In this context, knowing the traditional techniques in depth allows us to understand how materials taken from the environment can, with simple but efficient solutions, give us great lessons in construction. With this article, we intend to share our experience, the result of research projects, publications and professional work, in specialties such as restoration and construction pathology; with the aim of contributing to the knowledge and conservation of tangible heritage in urban planning and architecture that still exist, and intangible in the knowledge of the inhabitants of our territory who continue these traditions
