# Pseudohypoparathyroidism associated with Gitelman syndrome. Case report

The pseudohypoparathythyroidism (SPT) is a genetic disease with a very low prevalence that causes resistance to paratohormonmon (PTH), with subsequent complications to the resulting hypocalcaemia. Presentation of the case. 37-year-old man with history of tetanic crisis since childhood and with an unpredicted diagnosis of periodic hypokalemic paralysis since 2013 who entered the emergency department of a second-level hospital in Bogotá D.C., Colombia, for experiencing paresthesias in hands and feet for two days, which worsened to painful intermittent spasms. The patient denied a family genetic history. Hypocalcaemia, hypomagnesemia, hypermagnesemia, hyperphosphataemia and hypokalaemia, as well as elevated levels of PTH (369 pg/mL), so that after the exclusion of several causes of hyperparathypothypothyroiditis secondary, the patient was suspected. In the laboratory examinations, hypocalcaemia was reported hypocalcaemia dependent on PTH, hypocalcemia dependent on PTH, compatible with PT.
