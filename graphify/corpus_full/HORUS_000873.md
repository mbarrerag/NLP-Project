# THE ART OF OBSERVING THE HEAD, THE LIGHTING OF THE FIRST OBSERVATORY OF THE ASTRONOMIC AMERICAS

The Astronomical Observatory of Bogotá, first in America built by Fray Domingo Petrés, at the beginning of the xixth century, carries an implicit load of knowledge that generate contributions and reveal facts, practices and constructive methods of the time. Carrying out a rigorous architectural survey, with the use of Laserscanning in this important work, becomes an invaluable source of new knowledge. The construction of the historical background allows us to show the origins of the construction and methods of graphic representation, through (1) iconographic sources, which contribute with a hypothetical timeline for restitution; (2) documentary inputs such as the descriptions of Caldas (1808) and the contributions of Gutiérrez et al., (1999), which record the use of manuals, as well as the expressive manner with which he built and determined his idea; and (3) the different comparative restitution versions, where ideal geometrizations of the observatory appear
