# Decrypting the morphological and molecular diversity of hemoparasites in focal samples of wild fauna deposited in the GERPH Biological Collection

Neotropical presents one of the richest avifaunas and herpetofaunas in the world associated with the great variety of ecosystems produced by the topographical and climatic heterogeneity. Colombia within this region leads important positions in number and richness of species of birds and reptiles, with a wide diversity of biomes, endemic species and potential vectors, all this makes Colombia a very promising country for research in hemoparasites in wild fauna. Parasites represent a high proportion of the world biodiversity, because many of the living organisms currently host more than one species of parasites, which has allowed to know estimates that these can represent more than half of the total species on the planet (Poulin 2011). Unfortunately, only a small fraction of this diversity has been studied (Valkiunas 2005, Telford 2009). In this context, great advances in the vasital and the decrease in the success of the avian malaria (Marjcyna) countries, since in principle, the birds, were used as models of antimalar and follow-up of the infection; subsequently came the documentation of the birds in Hawaii and the disease species and in the world of the disease and the diseased. The results of the research and healing of the GERPH Biological Collection have been possible to identify relevant species in which we will seek to focus on this project. We will include in this study the Tangaras, Catharus and vireos in the bird class, on the part of reptiles we will choose Iguanas and snakes. The reason is that we have detected high prevalence in species belonging to these groups, and whose morphological and molecular diversity is still to be characterized. The methodology will include careful review of the extended blood to be able to take photographs of the greatest number of parasite forms present, which will be analyzed by morphological keys and by morphometry (Valkiunas 2005, Telford 2009). Further amplification of nuclear molecular markers or mytochondria according to the case and analysis of phylogenetic hypotheses. We hope to obtain novel information in the form of molecular lineages, extension in species distribution, extension of geographical distribution, BarCode sequences or even new species of parasites. The results will be socialized in congresses, in the academic networks
