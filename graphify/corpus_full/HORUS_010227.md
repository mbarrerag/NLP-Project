# ECONOMIC POLICY AND CLIMATE CHANGE IN SOUTH AMERICA:FRIENDS OR FOES

In this study, we estimate the impact of foreign direct investment (FDI) and energy expenditure in the industrial and transport sector, on CO2 emissions for a group of Latin American countries during the period 1990'2018. By doing so, long-term relationships between these variables and the directions of causality between them are generated. Findings showed that in addition to economic activity and energy consumption, the flow of foreign direct investment to these countries partially determines the increase in CO2 emissions per capita, during the period 1990'2018. This provides evidence of the existence of pollution havens in Latin America. We carry out a robustness analysis to determine how the results might change for different combinations of the set of countries contained in the analysis, the results show that most of the results holds, and only few causal relationships are influenced by the composition of the sample
