# Overall survival and prognostic factors of patients with laryngeal cancer in Colombia: A retrospective cohort study from 2012 to 2017

Laryngeal cancer is a malignancy whose therapeutic approaches and outcomes are influenced by disease stage and patient characteristics. The objective of this study was to describe the clinical and demographic characteristics of patients with laryngeal cancer treated in a Colombian healthcare institution. A retrospective cohort study was conducted using administrative health data. Patients older than 18 years with laryngeal cancer treated surgically between 2012 and 2017 were included. Overall survival was analyzed using Kaplan-Meier curves and Cox proportional hazards models, considering age, sex, comorbidities, geographic region, and type of surgery as potential prognostic factors. The cohort included 1,546 patients, 74.3% men, with a mean age of 65 years. The 3-year overall survival rate was 78.5%, with better outcomes in women (89.6%). Survival was inversely related to age and comorbidities. Patients undergoing total laryngectomy or more extensive surgeries had a higher risk of mortality (HR: 2.09; 95% CI: 1.54–2.84). While adjuvant chemotherapy was associated with higher mortality (HR: 2.08; 95% CI: 1.57–2.76), radiotherapy did not show a significant impact. This study provides a national overview of laryngeal cancer prognosis in Colombia. The findings are limited by a lack of detailed clinical data. Strengthening cancer registries in Colombia is essential to improve disease management and patient outcomes
