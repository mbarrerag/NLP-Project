# VISUAL PATHOLOGIES EXPOSURE PATTERNS OF SICK BODIES

The COVID-19 pandemic has generated an abundance of technical images that have captured the public's attention and played a crucial role in scientific and medical communication. These images, which include drawings, photographs, X-rays and CT scans, have made it possible to visualise and understand invisible phenomena related to the disease. They have been used to diagnose, monitor and investigate COVID-19, giving scientists and health professionals a sense of control. From the first chest X-rays that revealed the characteristics of pneumonia caused by the virus, to electron microscopies that showed the detailed structure of the virus and damage to organs and tissues, these images have played a key role in understanding and studying the disease. However, technical imaging is not objective in nature. The choice of image capture parameters, the technology for image processing and the reading of the data define the understanding and visual representation of scientific phenomena. Moreover, imaging is expensive. The generation of these models involves expensive technologies and unequal access relationships. Therefore, geolocalised context, financial investment and materiality are essential when interpreting and judging the assemblages of circulation and consumption of which scientific images are a part
