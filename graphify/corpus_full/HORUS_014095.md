# Assessment of diets based on organic by-products on the production dynamics of the yellow flour worm (Tenebrio molitor) and the quality of flour for use as a supplement in the production of chicken for fattening

Insects are widely debated as an alternative source of protein and a substitute for soy and fish meal in feed formulations of feed from feed-stock animals. However, mass production remains expensive, so means are sought to reduce production costs. One way is to keep the costs of insect diets low. One such possibility could be the use of secondary currents from the food industry or agricultural by-products to formulate a diet for insects. This study seeks to evaluate different substrates that serve as food for worm larvae from flour (Tenebrio molitor). It is a comparative study where insects will be fed with 3 types of diets, corresponding to T1: Diet control, cereal processing by-products, wheat bran, T2: enriched substrate, wheat bran mixture 40%, 60% potato residues, vegetable residues (frijol and arveja), beer yeast, carrot. T3: alternative substrate, oat bran, maize bran, carrot residues, lettuce.
