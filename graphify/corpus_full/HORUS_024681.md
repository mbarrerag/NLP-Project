# COMPLEMENTARY FOOD

Complementary feeding (CfD) is important and important in human life, affecting the future, with effects on short-, medium- and long-term growth and development.1 The introduction of new foods is controversial, influencing cultural factors and practices and customs in each region, and there is a diversity of international consensus.2 This chapter aims to review some key concepts of AC, for example, how to introduce AC, benefits of the onset of AC and recommendations with scientific evidence. AC is defined, according to the World Health Organization (WHO), as the period during which other foods are administered alongside breast milk; and the European Society of Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Pediatric Nutrition (Espghan) as any solid or liquid food other than breast milk or adapted formula, according to age range. Breastfeeding (ML) and AC practices can be improved through a combination of interventions, such as baby-friendly hospitals, regulations for the marketing of food and drink to children, adequate counselling and support and solid communication of social change and behaviour, but continuous research is warranted3
