# Structure and function of herbivorous fish, Scaridae, in the reef areas of San Andrés, an ocean island in the Caribbean

The parrot fish (family Scaridae), are crucial for the health and balance of coral reefs due to their herbicide function and control of macroalgae. The understanding of their composition and structure is crucial to assess their ecological impact and guide conservation strategies. Despite their ecological importance these communities have been decimated. In this study they analyzed their abundance, biomass and distribution of sizes and their relationship with habitat. Visual censuses were used from 2013 to 2019, in four sites around the island of San Andrés. They recorded 18,676 fish, of which 16.7% corresponded to the family Scarus guacamaia, Scarus coelestinus the only absent. The most abundant species were Scarus taeniopterus, Scarus iseri, Sparisoma aurofrenatum and Sparisoma viride.
