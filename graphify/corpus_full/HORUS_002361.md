# MEDITAL REPRESENTATIONS OF COLOMBIAN NATURE IN NATIONAL GEOGRAPHIC MAGAZINE 1903-1952 A LOOK FROM CRITICAL THEORY AND DECOLONIAL GIRL

The paper's purpose is to analyze the media representations, the production context and the social practices that legitimized the discourses that circulated on Colombian nature between 1903 and 1952 in National Geographic Magazine. The theories that guided our research are inscribed in Stuart Hall's Theory of Representations, Critical Theories and the decolonial twist. The methodological design worked on Fairclough's three-dimensional Model with 9 reports on Colombia as a unit of analysis. The research found the following media representations: sublime spectacle, land of economic opportunities, desirable place for collecting scientific data, land of adventure and consumption, land of future, laboratory, adventure. The social practices that created and institutionalized this particular discourse on nature were positivism, pan-Americanism and manifest destiny in the context of the emergence of mass journalism, the development of photo-recorded technology, the emergence and institutionalization of new academic disciplines oriented by positivism
