# Poché content and vacuum construction: The dimension of intermediate space from the reversal of poché paradigm as a fixed body programmed

The term poché, a concept commonly used in the architecture workshops of the school of fine arts of Paris of the eighteenth century, referred to the technique employed by the architects with the aim of achurating with ink, the section of the walls in plant, as well as some auxiliary spaces that they considered prudent to hide from the drawing. On the contrary, the void, a predominant space in the architectural composition given its social relevance, was resulting from this operation, in which the program defined the form and spatial sequence. It is not unfortunate to think about the investment of the poché paradigm since the structuring reflection of this final mastery work is oriented to the construction of intermediate spaces, collective spaces, which function in an unregulated way and have origin in the void. In that sense, the non-regulated premium over the programmed looking for the prefiguration of the latter through a dual operation of full/vacue, program/no program, ambivalence that serves the delimitation of the intermediate space and the content. In this sense, the architectural object with educational use, are put into practice, grouping strategies, concatenation and sliding that solve the intermediate space inside the building and its structure. In this sense, the unregulated benefits over the programmed, seeing its prefiguration through a dual operation of empintiness/filled, program/non-program, ambivalence as a function to limit the intermediate space and the contained coverage. In this case, through an architectural object with educational use and, on a small scale, commercial use, the interaction of the building with the urban space, gives Rise to concatenation, sliding and grouping strategies that resolve the intermediate space and its potential programme inside the building.
