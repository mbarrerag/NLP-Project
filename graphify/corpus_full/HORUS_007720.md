# INDICATORS FOR SURVIVENCE IN LECHOTES BREAKDOWN AND USE TO EVALUATE AN INTERVENTION STRATEGY FOR IMPROVING THE VIABILITY OF MILK IN A TRADE GRANTEE IN THE HIGH COLOMBIAN TROPIC

The project focuses on identifying physiological and metabolic indicators that can predict the survival of newborn piglets, and evaluating a nutritional intervention strategy to reduce mortality during breastfeeding in a commercial farm in the high tropics of Colombia. As pig genetics has improved, it has also increased the size of litters, which increases the number of low-weight piglets at birth, which face a higher risk of dying or showing poor performance. This problem is related to limited energy reserves, difficulty in regulating body temperature and insufficient access to colostrum. The study includes two phases: first, the evaluation of physiological parameters in 600 piglets of 50 births, measuring aspects such as body weight, temperature, and vitality using specific criteria. In the second phase, the effect of supplementing the diet of pregnant sows with guanidinoacetic acid (GAA), administered at different stages of pregnancy, and its influence on the survival of piglets is expected to provide knowledge on key parameters that predict neonatal survival and validate important supplementation with GAA as an effective strategy to improve the viability of the nursing and the more important animal mortality
