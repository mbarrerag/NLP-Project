# TREATMENT WITH FLUDROCORTISONE IN A PATIENT WITH SALT-LOSSING BREAKDOWN ASSOCIATED TO MENINGITIS BY CRIPTOCOSIS

the salt-losing brain (CPS) is a confused etiology syndrome, responsible for a hydroelectrolytic disorder of hyponatremia, which is significant in patients with central nervous system (CNS) condition due to trauma or infections. to describe the case of a patient with atypical CPS, who required fludrocortisone as a novel option in his treatment. Presentation of the case: 65-year-old woman who entered the emergency room due to altered state of consciousness, severe hyponatremia and stable vital signs. She did not develop diarrhoea, emesis or fever. She was suspected of inadequate secretion syndrome of antidiuretic hormone (SIADH) and was managed with water restriction that impered the hyponatremia. After identifying hemoconcentration and polyuria, the diagnosis was given to CPS, which led to treatment with hypertonic solution supplemented with fludrocortisone, essential to achieve levels of safety. Finally, a lumbar puncture was performed that confirmed meconcosis by cryptococosis is a case of origin
