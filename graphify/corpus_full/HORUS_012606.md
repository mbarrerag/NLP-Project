# ‘...JA NACHAL PEREVODIT’, CHTOBY VYSKOCHIT’IZ SHABLONA...’: STIHOVEDY- PEREVODCHIKI A. A. ILJUSHIN I M. L. GASPAROV I I IH JEKSPERIMENTAL'NYE PEREVODY DANTE I ARIASTO

At the end of the Soviet era, the Russian-speaking public learned two new translations of the great Italian poets. In 1980 Alexander Anatolievich Ilyushin (1940-2016) completed his translation of Dante Alighieri’s Commedia. Mikhail Leonovich Gasparov (1935-2005), in the mid-eighties, had fully translated Orlando furious, by Ludovico Ariosto. The similarities between the two experiences were not limited to the moment of their creation, and to the fact that in both cases they were translations of outstanding monuments of the Italian epic. Part of the approach has to do with the personalities of the translators: both Ilyushin and Gasparov were not first and foremost professional translators of poetry, but rather scholars-philologists, more precisely, versologists. Both philologists recreated stylistic archaism in their translations and distanced themselves from the typical practices of the Soviet translation school they chose, in addition to the syllabic and free verse. The innovative character of these decisions included the rejection of the syllabic tonic verse, dominant in the Russian poetic tradition, in favor of two other "marginal" and translation strategies. The translation is analyzed by the article
