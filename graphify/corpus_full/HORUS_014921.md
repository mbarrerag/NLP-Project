High Andean or high-mountain ecosystems are characterized by providing essential ecosystem services, many of them related to soil; however, it is known that agricultural and livestock activities have led to progressive changes in the physical, chemical and organic carbon and nitrogen depletion of the soil, contributing to the loss of soil quality and increasing greenhouse gas emissions. This study assessed the influence of three plant cover: native forest, naturalized grassland of Pennisetum clandestinum and monoculture of Allium fistulosum, on some soil properties, in a high Andean ecosystem of Valle del Cauca (Colombia).Edaphytic properties, such as apparent density, organic matter, contents and storage of C and N were measured. It was found that the agricultural practices associated with monoculture influenced significantly on the edific properties of the soil, could generate changes at the physical and chemical level, which could increase the vulnerability of the soil to degradation.
