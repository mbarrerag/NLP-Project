# UPPER CRETACEOUS SOURCE ROCKS OF THE MIDDLE MAGDALENA VALLEY, COLOMBIA: BASIN MODELING AS AN UNCONVENTIONAL PLAY

A project sponsored by the Agencia Nacional de Hidrocarburos (ANH) was carried out by the Dirección de Hidrocarburos (DTH) of the Servicio Geológico Colombiano (SGC), in order to characterize the Cretaceous units as source rock reservoirs in the Middle Magdalena Valley Basin in central Colombia. This work integrates new data from outcrops, seismic interpretation, well-log correlations, facies analysis, fracture pattern and pore pressure analysis, organic geochemistry, XRD, heat flow, and petrophysical data in a 3D basin model. The Upper Cretaceous sequence, referred to as the "La Luna Formation" was divided into a transgressive cycle of three parts. The first interval (Salada Formation) features a Turonian maximum flooding surface (MFS) with good to excellent TOC values (2%-5% TOC), type II kerogen, and =0.7% Ro levels. The second interval (Pujamana Formation) corresponds to a retrogradational succession with fair to good TOC values (2%-3% TOC), type II/III kerogen, and =0.75% Ro values. Finally, the Coniacian MFS in the third interval (Galembo-La Renta formations) exhibits excellent TOC values (3%-6% TOC), type II and II/III kerogen, and =0.6 Ro values. Based on the seismic interpretation of the northern portion of the MMV Basin, we suggest three structural domains: The Northern Domain (ND) includes SW-NE strike-slip faults related to the formation of the Central Cordillera. Central Domain (CD) includes NW-SE transpressive strike-slip faults related to tectonic inversion influenced by the Central and Eastern cordilleras uplift, and Southern Domain (SD) includes N-S thrust faults related to the Eastern Cordillera uplift, reactivation of pre-Eocene faults, and folding of Cretaceous and Cenozoic units. Petrophysical modeling shows effective porosities ranging from 2% to 12% and organic porosities lower than 0.5%. Basin modeling reveals no higher than 90% transformation ratios for the organic matter in the Upper Cretaceous rocks, in the deeper parts of the basin. Also, the estimated basal heat flow is not higher than 60mw/m 2 with a "transient heat flow" model constrained by 40 1D calibrated models. From a geomechanical perspective, we identified several brittle Turonian and Campanian strata based on the higher concentrations of carbonates and quartz. Surface geology and outcrop data found that the Salada and the Galembo-La Renta formation had a greater natural fracture density than the Pujamana formation. Pore pressure analysis of multiple wells shows that wells in which the La Luna formation has been beveled by the Middle Eocene unconformity, have no sealing capacity, becoming a risk for the play. We conclude that unconventionals sweet spots are mainly controlled by porosity, thermal maturity, gas-oil ratios (GOR), and volumes of retained oil and gas. Our findings will help to identify potential unconventional trends as well as exploration opportunities in the MMV Basin
