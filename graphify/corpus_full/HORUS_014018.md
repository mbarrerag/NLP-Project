# In-silico study of micro-communities present in solanacea producing secondary metabolites as potential for application of principles of synthetic biology

The aim is to carry out an in-silicon study of microcommunities present in solancaceas producing secondary metabolites as potential for applying principles of synthetic biology. To this end, part of the basic transcriptomic information obtained in project 44598 of the GEME laboratory of the National University of Colombia, Bogotá headquarters in collaboration with the RN Theorical and Computational Group. A subset of nine species of native and medicinal plants of the clade Datureae for which a potential production of tropical alkaloids within the family Solanaceae is associated. Initially, a preliminary bioinformatics characterization of the taxonomy of the dominant microbial communities in the foliar tissue of plants will be refined. Subsequently, strategies based on the omical sciences where biomolyses related to the functioning of these micro-organisms will be applied. This information will allow to carry out a search of the specialized metabolism, particularly the tropane alkaloids, which can be useful in the field of synthetic biology. Boroceros (Género Brugmansia) are one of the most interesting cases of domestication of medicinal plants, since in Sibundoy there are medicinal cultivars with different uses and very unusual morphologies (Lookwood 1979 and Bristol 1969). It is believed that these medicinal cultivars are the result of the infection of plants by microorganisms or viruses that modify their morphology and biochemistry. This theory is supported by evidence of co-evolution between Borracheros and a type of virus called CDV (Chellemi et al 2011)
