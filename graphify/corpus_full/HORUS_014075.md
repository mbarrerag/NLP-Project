# Relationship between PD-L1, CD31 and Malignancy Criteria in Canine Mammal Tumor

Cancer is one of the main causes of death in the world after cardiovascular diseases. Particularly, breast gland neoplasms constitute the third most common tumor in humans and is the most frequent type of cancer in women and in non-sterilized canine females. The understanding of molecular and biochemical mechanisms in this tumor allows progress in the development of diagnostic, prognostic and treatment strategies by identifying biomarkers related to the carcinogenicity process. This research aims to evaluate the immunoexpression of PD-L1 and CD31 in breast gland neoplasms in canine females and to determine their relationship with criteria of malignancy. Five normal mammary glands and 30 canine mammary tumors (10 benign tumors and 20 malignant tumours). Subsequently, breast tissues will be subjected to the technique of immunohistochemistry (IHQ) using as primary antibodies PD-L1 and CD31, equally, the histopathological samples will be observed under the microscope by two veterinary pathologists who will perform their respective classification and graduation according to histological criteria.
