# Security in the Colombian Insular Caribbean. State Expansion in the National Intendancy of San Andres and Providencia, 1912-1930

Objetivo: analizar la visión sobre la seguridad en los discourses y las estrategias implementadas by the Colombian government to ensure sovereignty over the Colombian insular Caribbean. historical analysis of primary sources of the General Archive of the Nation and the National Library, especially of the intendants' reports, various documents of the Congress and the commercial press. Originality: it contributes to the discussions on violence and security in Colombia, which have had important developments in the last forty years, but have been focused on understanding the phenomenon as a product of the political conflict and the violence unleashed in continental Colombia, starting with the appearance of guerrillas and armed groups in the sixties, leaving aside what happened in the Colombian insular Caribbean. The Colombian State governments, with the creation of the National Intendancy of San Andrés and Providencia in 1912, had the purpose of exercising greater control over the insular Caribbean and at the same time strengthening the territorial integrity of Colombia. To achieve this, they used an incipient vision of state security, which initially sought to avoid what happened with Panama, that is, the dismemberment of national territories. This vision evolved discursively from 1930, with the arrival of the liberal governments to power, who incorporated geopolitical factors and the defense vision by proposing the development of a military infrastructure in the archipelago
