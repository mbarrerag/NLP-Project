# Synthesis and evaluation of antibacterial activity of Cu(II) and Ni(II) complexes with mixed glycine-based ligands and dicarboxylic acids

A large number of metallic complexes have the ability to inhibit bacterial growth. Cu(II) and Ni(II) complexes based on glycine (Gly), itachonic acid (Ita) and oxalic acid (Ox) were synthesized by conventional methods and their antibacterial activity was evaluated. The metallic complexes were characterized by TGA, FTIR, UV-vis spectroscopy and XRD. The metal stoichiometry:ligands (M:L1:L2) of these complexes is 1:2:2, and the coordination around Cu(II) and Ni(II) appears to be octahedral, with the ligands bound through the N atom of the amino group and O atoms of the carboxylate bridge group. These compounds are crystalline and stable at temperatures between 250 and 300°C. The metallic complexes were tested for their antibacterial activity against bacterial species Staphylococcus aureus, Bacillus cereus, Listeria monocytogenes, Salmonella and Eschergrama coli.
