# COLOMBIAN KAIPOS: Among the regimes of disintegrating, domining, racism, exclusion and discrimination and the long-awaited recomposition of the minimum shared SIMBOLIC ORDER.

The critical studies on discrimination, as a paradigm or complex approach, remain a novel branch of social knowledge. Equally novel is the specialized language that enables to refer to the unequal relations they deal with. This is not strange. While the processes and practices of discrimination have a long history in human experience, the vocabulary used to analyze, criticize and propose alternatives to it is of recent invention. Vocablos or terms such as structural discrimination, stigmatization, affirmative action, suspicious categories, hate speech, discriminatory practices, anti-discrimination law, racialization, capacitism, etc., have a few decades of existence, but some others, in fact, amount to a few years. Of course, intersectionality is one of these new words. The international agenda against discrimination appeared only from the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, 1948, and with the consequent creation of the International Law of Human Rights, while the political, legal and academic language on discrimination in the United States two was deployed with systematicality from the 1960s.
