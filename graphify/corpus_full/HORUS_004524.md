# POLICE NOTICES AND SURVEILLANCE PRACTICES: THE CALDES OF BARRIO IN THE VIRRENATE OF NEW GRANADA, 17th AND 18th

The purpose of this article is to analyze colonial surveillance and its relationship with police development. To achieve this purpose, the conceptual polysemia of the notion of police is reconstructed and is related to the introduction of neighborhood mayors in Santafé de Bogotá in 1774. Based on a comprehensive analysis of primary sources, the police are differentiated as a concept, practical and incipient institution (Police). Only with this clarity are their different articulations drawn in the surveillance practices in daily life in Santafé. The work points to the importance of historicizing the police in the seventeenth, eighteenth and nineteenth centuries and describes the emergence of a culture of police surveillance based on the construction of the enemy, the internalization of norms, the sectorization of urban space, the investigation of crime, the capture of suspects and support in the administration of justice. With this result, this investigation invites reflection on neighborhood mayors, in the midst of dichoronies, as a future colonial heritage in the body of modern police as guarantor of systemic privileges
