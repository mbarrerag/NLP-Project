# Obtaining a DNA-level reference material for the detection of Mycobacterium bovis by PCR of use to the Colombian livestock sector for the control of bovine tuberculosis

Bovine tuberculosis (BTB) is an infectious contagion disease caused in cattle by the bacterial species Mycobacterium bovis (Mb). This type of disease can generate high losses in the livestock sector at the level of meat and milk production, reason why the ICA in Colombia has developed strategies for its prevention and control. Traditionally the TBB is diagnosed by means of the standard reference test known as Tuberculin, which presents drawbacks of sensitivity and specificity. In this sense, priority is made in addition to serological tests, the development of molecular diagnostic methods such as those based on the PCR, since this technique provides greater sensitivity, speed and specificity. Currently there are different methods to identify and quantify M. bovis based on the PCR, such as qPCR, dPCR, and PCR material produced by the present system of units. However, the PCR methods could prove to the results obtained by these methodologies, since there are no different methods to identify and quantify DNA reference material as a comparison pattern to the international comparison of units.
