# A MATHEMATIC MODEL OF IDEOLOGICAL POLICY IN AN ASYMETERIC EXPRESSION CONTEXT

This study analyzes how social inequalities in freedom of expression affect ideological polarization, a phenomenon that can both foster civic engagement and deepen divisions and undermine democratic principles. Polarization is driven by factors such as economic inequality and media biases. Unlike previous models, our approach examines the interaction between groups in a context where a faction controls the media, creating an atmosphere of asimitri-cal expression. We propose a mathematical model with a nonlinear system of differential equations, which includes a constant interaction rate and a functional response of Holling II type. This model is a staunch proponents of plebiscite media. The results identify two possible balances: coexistence of opinions or dominance of a posture, determined by threshold values. Simulations and case studies suggest that the effective transmission of ideas influences more than simply increasing the number of moderately sympathetical models in the media.
