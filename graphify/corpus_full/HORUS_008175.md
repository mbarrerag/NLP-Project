# Quantification of expression of the A-master gene in bacterial and archaeal populations present in soil samples of a rice lot characterized by environments

The nitrogen cycle represents one of the most important biogeochemical processes for terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems. Microbial communities play a crucial role in the processes of transformation of nitrogen in soil, since they participate in various stages such as nitrify, of great importance for agricultural production. Within the molecular markers most used to evaluate the activity of ammonium oxidizing microbial populations, genes that encode key enzymes such as the A subunit of the monooxygenase ammonium activity (AMO) have been widely considered. However, it is not fully understood if the expression of this enzyme is directly related to the yield of the crops. In this context, the expression of the AMC-A gene from bacterial and archaeal communities present in a pre-dedicated rice lot was used for the analysis of the AMC. To quantify the abundance of accreases and oxidative bacteria from ammonia (AOAC and AOB, respectively) the expression of the AMC-A was used the techniques of the APCR in real-time (RT-qPCR) and digital PCR).
