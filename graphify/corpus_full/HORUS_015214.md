# THE POTENTIAL OF A AVIAR MODEL FOR THE STUDY OF BLOODLY PARISITS

The common pigeon (Columba livia) is an excellent model for the study of Apicomplexa parasites. This species of bird is invasive in Colombia, in some places is considered as pest and is commonly infected with Haemoproteus columbae. Recently, we characterized the cycle of said parasite Apicomplexa, phylogenetically related to Plasmodium spp. causing malaria, not only in humans, but also in chimpanzees, gorillas, birds and reptiles. To establish the experimental model, we standardized the growth of the parasite vector, the Hiposboscida fly in conditions of captivity. Two populations of pigeons were used: one of them naturally infected, captured in Bogotá (used for the isolation and maintenance of the colony of vector insects), and other doves free of tissues bought from specialized breeders (to perform follow-up to the natural infection). One of them was managed to standardize the conditions of growth from the isolation of hypobicides pup to obtain adults.
