# Early detection of biotic and abiotic stress using VIS/NIR reflectance spectroscopy data classification models: Application in Banana Gros Michel plants

The early detection of diseases and water stress (HSD) in plants is crucial for agriculture and food sovereignty in Latin American countries. In this context, methods of visible electromagnetic reflectance spectroscopy (VIS) and infrared (NIR) were used, which are non-invasive and have proven promising to identify biotic and abiotic stress in plants even in their asymptomatic phase. A relevant example of this is the infection of banana plants by devastating diseases, such as vascular wilt caused by the fungus Fusarium oxysporum f.sp. cubanian Raza 1 (FOCR1) and by the bacteria Ralstonia solanacearum Raza 2 (RSR2), which can result in losses of up to 100% in plantations. To address this challenge, a study was carried out in which the most effective reflection data of 240 banana plants in the municipality of Carepa, located in the department of Antioquia, Colombia. These data included healthy plants, those of HSD infected with FOCR1, with a different analysis. The asymptomatic period of banana plants is 20 days, with the ALD can achieve a correct classification percentage of 86\% on day 3 with methods of preprocessing of Asymetric Square Minimums (ACMs) and management of atypical data using the Stock Exchange Method (MB). Without taking into account interactions, the best methodology is obtained by employing an ALD with a similar precision. At day 6 post-inoculation, similar precisions are obtained with the ALD, being the most optimal when using the methods of preprocessing as the methods of Multiplication Dispersion Correction (CDM) when treating atypical data with MB. These results suggest that early detection of FOCR1, RSR2 and EH in banana plants, by using the spectrum of reflection and innovation can significantly improve with the appropriate choice of preproceding methodologies, selection of characteristics and classification of data. .
