# Robust system for predictive energy management in multi-microgrid distribution networks including renewable energy

This thesis addresses the challenge of modeling and optimizing the operation of multi-microgrid interconnected (NMG) systems under conditions of uncertainty. The micronetworks, as evolution of the electrical distribution systems, integrate distributed generation sources —mainly renewable — together with storage and load systems. However, the variability inherent in renewable sources and the behaviour of demand introduces important uncertainties in their operation. The work explores different methodologies to manage these uncertainties, focusing on the Robust Distributive Optimization (DRO) using the DRO-W approach to optimize their operation. In addition, the analysis is presented as an intermediate approach between stochastic programming and robust optimization, offering a more balanced, realistic and less conservative decision-making in the face of uncertainty. An economic dispatch model for isolated microgrids is developed, using the DRO-W approach to optimize their operation. In addition, the analysis is extended to multiple microgrid systems interconnecting systems, introducing the concept of transactive energy to evaluate their benefits in the efficient dispatch of energy systems.
