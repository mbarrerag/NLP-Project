# THE PUBLISHMENT OF MEDICAL ARTICLES IN COLOMBIA AND OTHER DEMONIES

Mr. Editor: We recently went into the world of writing medical articles, and first hand we experienced what is woven around this universe. Our first attempt to publish was discouraging. We discovered that this process is permeated by different interests that determine what can or not be written. We performed in the surgical field of oncology and, for this reason, we were intended to exclude from the debate of radiation therapy for the management of cancer. We will briefly comment on how this experience was. About the pandemic by the COVID-19 we reviewed the short fractionation schemes and the omission of radiation therapy in the treatment of breast cancer, options not only striking but necessary, considering the critical moment that the health systems lived in the world, where it was essential to redistribute the economic resources and the labor force to attend to the sectors of the population most affected by this contingency. We looked for the evidence on which these management strategies were supported and we reflected their results in two manuscripts, which we sent to a magazine specialized in oncology of our country. After a peer review process that lasted six months both texts were rejected. While the associate editor argued that ours were more "an article of opinion than of review, and that opinion articles are written by people who are experts in the subject." However, this experience allowed us to perfect the manuscripts and explore other options for their publication. After a new peer review process in a Spanish journal, both documents were finally published
