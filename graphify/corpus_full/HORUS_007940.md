# Complementation of Mycobacterium tuberculosis mutants defective in Type P ATPases: Construction of integrative plasmids with self-inducible promoters

Tuberculosis (TB) is an infectious disease, which is a positive factor caused by the bacillus acid, resistant alcohol Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb). One of the main strategies for TB control is the development of new vaccines that replace the current BCG, or Bacillus Calmette-Guerin, which has a limited protection of pulmonary TB in adults. In this sense, live attenuated vaccines are one of the main alternatives to replace the current BCG. on the other hand, bearing in mind the important role of P-type TAPases in the control of the electrochemical gradients of the micobacterial, these plasma membrane transporters are considered interesting targets for Mtb attenuation. In previous work of our research group, supplementation of defective mutants in P-type ATPases using an extracromotive plasmids, multicopic, showed a very increased expression of the supplemented genes during the stress and in vitro infection processes, which affects the normal recovery of the wild phenotypes and the conclusions of the research. The results were used to design and construct an integrative expression system of the ATPase type P, CtpF (carrier of Ca 2+ through the plasma membrane of Mtb), under the control of the promoter of Ag85A, for the supplementation of mutants of Mtb defectives in this transporter (MtbΔctpf), to be tested in preclinical trials for vaccine purposes. .
