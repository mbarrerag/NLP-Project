# Evaluation of electrothermal effects of low frequency electromagnetic fields on tissue associated with limbs of the human body

Given the nature of its operation, the transmission and distribution infrastructure of electric energy generates electromagnetic fields in the environment, which can generate biological effects ranging from perception to discomfort [1]. However, the limit of the level of exposure to these fields is not an international standard; instead, each country is responsible for establishing its limits of exposure. There is also literature that allows to approximate the effects of exposure to electromagnetic fields of low frequency in the human body. With this information, it is possible to evaluate the limit values of exposure according to the population of each country, even in the absence of a standard established for this purpose. This research starts from a review of the effects of non-ionizing radiation in the biological tissues. This provides the basis for the development of computational models of limbs for the evaluation of dosimetry at low frequency. The models use simple geometries such as circles (2D) and cylinders (3D) which contemplate different layers of tissues with their respective electrical parameters. Subsequently, these data are supplemented with the thermal parameters with the aim of approximating the electrothermic effects caused by non-ionizing radiation at 60 Hz.
