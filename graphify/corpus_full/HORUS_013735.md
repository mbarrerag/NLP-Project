# Receptor-based design, virtual screening and molecular docking studies of new human acetylcholinesterase inhibitors with potential therapeutic application

Alzheimer’s disease is the main cause of dementia and concerns for its accelerated growth, projected to 113 million affected by 2050. This disorder significantly impacts cognitive abilities, affecting the social and working life of individuals. The three current treatments, limited to three drugs: donepezil, galantamine and rivastigmine; face adverse events and multiple daily administrations, challenging the adherence of the patient. This project focused on developing acetylcholinesterase inhibitors through an in-silico approach. First, the pharmacophoric characteristics of these inhibitors were identified to carry out a search for molecules that met these in the ZINC database 12. The molecules obtained were subjected to a virtual screening, where more than 70% showed an energy of inferior affinity to that of the donepezil. Then, the pharmacokinetic properties of the best-tuned molecules were evaluated and the two most promising ones were selected. From these, seven optimized analogues were proposed that had greater affinity for the recipient with toxicological and pharmacokinetic characteristics . obtained molecules were put through a virtual screening in which more than 70% showed a smaller affinity energy than donated. Pharmacokinetic properties were evaluated in the molecules with higher scores, from which the two most promising ones were selected.
