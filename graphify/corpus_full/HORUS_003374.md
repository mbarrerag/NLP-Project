# STUDY OF THE POSSIBLE APPLICATION OF UREASE AND PHOSFATASE RESIDENTS FOR THE BIOMINERALIZATION OF CHANGE AND PLOME

Biomineralization consists of the formation of insoluble minerals through various metabolic processes, by living organisms of different domains of life. Fungi, although less studied, possess this metabolic activity for the transformation of metals and minerals. Some of these organisms are able to deposit carbonated compounds around their mycelium or to the environment by means of hydrolysis of the urea by action of their urease, a process called microbiologically induced carbonate precipitation (MICP). This mechanism is of great interest in biotechnological processes for strengthening materials or for the mitigation of pollutants. In the last decade, studies in fungal biomineralization have highlighted the importance and potential of these organisms for their biotechnological application, reporting high percentages of biomineralization of pollutants as lead by Geotrichum candium, Penicillium oxalicum, Penicillium chrysogenum and Trichoderma sp., all micromietes.
