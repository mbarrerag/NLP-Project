# CHAPTER 10: LIFE IN INDIGENOUS DISPLACEMENT IN BOGOTA: HARMONY, DISARMONY AND TERRITORY

This chapter constitutes an exercise to investigate how the forms of participation and inclusion of displaced indigenous subjects 292 are considered to be a matter of concern to the people of Bogotá. It is argued that displacement is the search for the desire to persevere in their being, and that this perseverance is motivated by a context that goes beyond armed conflict. Therefore, the immigration of these communities to the capital opens, thus, a non-obedious vector that, in answering the urban conditions that it requires them to correspond as an external force and, therefore, adapt, opens a way to rethink the conditions of existence in the city now as an immanent force. The present reflection seeks to generate a dialogue with the way in which these subjects think and present another possible city. This dialogue is articulated from the word disharmony, which emerges to describe the ailities that these subjects suffer in the city, but on which also a critique is woven to the urban conditions that they intend to submit, that is, to the forms of inclusion and belonging that the city opens up to them, and imposes to them to integrate the key words of urban force
