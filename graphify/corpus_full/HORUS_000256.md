# Structural and electronic insights of Ni–TiO2 coatings synthesized from electroplating wastewater for Cr(VI) reduction

In this study, Ni–TiO2 coatings were synthesized via Plasma Electrolytic Oxidation (PEO), using titanium sheets as substrates and a galvanic industry wastewater as the electrolyte. The duty cycle was varied as the main process parameter. The resulting coatings were thoroughly characterized in terms of their structural, chemical, and morphological properties using XRD, AFM, contact angle measurements (CA), SEM/EDS, and XPS techniques. Furthermore, the photocatalytic performance of the coatings was evaluated by studying the reduction of Cr(VI) present in real wastewater from the chromium plating industry, under light-assisted photoelectrocatalytic conditions. The effects of pH and the wavelength of the irradiating light were also evaluated. The characterization results confirmed the successful incorporation of Ni ions as segregated compounds within the titanium dioxide (TiO 2 ) structure. The stability of Ni and Cr atoms on the different TiO 2 surfaces was theoretically confirmed. Experimental findings revealed optimal Cr(VI) reduction performance under low pH conditions and with a smaller duty cycle, using a light source with a wavelength of 390 nm. Similarly, calculations indicate that TiO 2 surfaces accumulate charge in the proximity of Cr atoms. Industrial effluents, particularly those originating from the galvanic industry, represent a major source of environmental pollution due to their high concentration of inorganic substances and heavy metals, including hexavalent chromium (Cr(VI)), which poses serious risks to living organisms due to its toxicity. These results indicate promising potential for wastewater treatment in industries facing similar pollutant challenges
