# CHALLENGES IN THE IMPLEMENTATION OF BIODEGRADABLE ACTIVE POLYMERIC PACKAGING AND NATURAL ADDITIVES FOR THE AGRIBUSINESS

For decades, Colombian and world agribusiness has had polyethylene (PE), polypropylene, and chemical additives as great allies. Concerning the conventional polymers mentioned, these are used as packaging material, insect pest barriers, and active packaging, among others. For their part, chemical additives such as chlorpyrifos are used as broad-spectrum pesticides. Within many attributes, its use has made it possible to control, reduce or eliminate significant economic losses due to the attack of pests in the banana harvest, over-ripening during the export of gulupa, or the appearance of phytosanitary problems. However, the increasing number of restrictions on the use of non-biodegradable packaging, and the use of chemical additives in contact with food, lead to the conclusion that in just a few years, Colombian agro-industrialists will face tremendous difficulties in harvesting, storing, or exporting their products. In this work, the results of various investigations carried out in the last 15 years will be presented, which today are in the process of consolidating the first spin-off in active packaging in Colombia. Among the works are a) development of anti-ripening packaging for the handling of gulupa and banana based on PE, which allows preserving excellent organoleptic properties of the fresh fruit for up to 5 weeks, b) antifungal PE and PP packaging, with natural additives, that allow tree tomato storage for up to three weeks, c) development of biodegradable packaging with hydrophobic characteristics, based on PLA and PHAs, for post-harvest handling of banana, gulupa, chocolate, and dry cocoa, and d) use of natural additives incorporated into conventional and biodegradable polymeric films as pesticides for handling bananas
