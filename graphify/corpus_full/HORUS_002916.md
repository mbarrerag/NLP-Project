# Exploration of the seismic effect associated with wave polarization on the instrumented slopes of the Aburra Valley, using spectral analysis of seismic events and environmental noise

This study addresses how lithology, slope and soil rigidity influence the polarization of seismic waves in the Aburra Valley. Through an analysis that combines seismic records, spectral analysis and covariance matrixes, a quantification of seismic amplification in slopes and a detailed correlation between natural vibration frequencies in soil and topographic characteristics has been achieved. The study reveals that lithology and terrain slope are decisive factors in seismic polarization, with a tendency to align with slope in steep slopes and a significant influence of geology in softer slopes. In addition, soil stiffness emerges as a key element, modifying the polarization of waves in function of topographic characteristics. The research contributes to the theoretical understanding of the interaction between geology, topography and seismic response, also opening paths for predictive models in seismic engineering and risk mitigation. The findings highlight the importance of considering in an integrated way the topographic configuration and the soil rigidity for a precise assessment of the seismic response.
