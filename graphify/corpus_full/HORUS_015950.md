# USE OF A DESIGN OF MIXINGS TO ESTABLISH THE NITROGEN SOURCES OF A FERMENTAL ENVIRONMENT FOR THE PRODUCTION OF MICROORGANISMS AS AN INOCULANT ACTIVE SUBSTANCE.

The cost of raw materials for the production of bacterial inoculants can represent about 60% of the cost of production. Likewise, a bioeconomy scheme allows the use and sustainable use of biodiversity. Under this scheme was proposed the obtaining of an inocculant destined for the conservation of ensilage, based on Colombian native acid-lactic bacteria (BAL) and agro-industrial residues. In order to reduce the cost associated with the use of yeast extract as a source of nitrogen in a fermentive medium for the growth of Pedioccus acidiactici A40, the partial substitution of that source was proposed by incorporating other sources of nitrogen more economical. An experimental design of mixtures of three components was used: yeast extract, fermented liquor of maize and soy hydrolysed plant protein, evaluating both the microbial growth and the concentration of lactic acid at the end of fermentation. A more desirability function was applied to determine the area where the maximization of both the bacterial and the concentration of lactic acid was achieved.
