# HARDSHIP'S CLOSURE

The contracts are determined to be fulfilled (pacta sunt servanda) provided that the conditions established at the start, at the time of agreement, are maintained at the time of its execution (rebus sic stantibus ). Eventually the conclusion is taken into account outside the parties that prevent or hinder its performance after the agreement. These facts may be due to an excessive onerousness that affects the initial balance (compulsory or provident) in head of the signatory parties and that, in case of being determined in the contract or in law, they allow to arrive at a revision of the terms of this contract and even, in the circumstances, to the termination of the contract. For this purpose the Hardship clause, which we can assess in the greater part of the international contracts, in which it is established that as a consequence of a circumstance not foreseen by the parties, it is a significant change of the economic balance of the contract, making its execution especially onerous for one of them, which leads to the affected party to request its revision or in its defect the termination of the contract.
