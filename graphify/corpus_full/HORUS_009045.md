# LOCAL GROWTH LIGERANTLY BETWEEN MACHOS AND HEMBRAS OF THE COLOMBIAN BOWIN DOES SANMARTINERO?

The bovine breed Sanmartinero is typical of the flat foothill of the department of Meta, in the Colombian Orinoquia. It was forged by the natural and artificial selection carried out by the Jesuits during the seventeenth century, from the bovines introduced by the Spanish in the fifteenth century. The Sanmartinero breed is defined as mesolin, eumetric and orthoid, and although there is published bibliography on the biometrics of the breed, there is, to date, to the knowledge of the authors, work on its growth and development. The objective of this work was to measure the allometric growth of males and females of the bovine Sanmartinero. A total of 28 animals (16 males and 12 females) of an age range of 2 months to 9 years, of which a total of 25 linear variables were obtained by means of morphometric methods of the head, width and length of the face, width and length of the face, height of the cross, bictal diameter, dorso-estrol diameter, width of the chest, anterior cane, height, width and length of the groups, width and length of the head, width and length of the face, width and length of the statistically significant with age in males (R2=0.502; F10.5=4.014; p=0.069) but not in females (R2=0.451; F10.1=6.235; p=0.302); morphometric variables that did not show changes with age, for both sexes, were the corneal perimeters and the length of the left horn. The anterior cane perimeter appeared statistically not significant only in females (r=-0.207; p=0.517), being statistically significant in males, in this case, with negative values (r=-0.546; p=0.028). The results in the behaviour of the anterior cane perimeter with age would suggest that, in the future genetic improvement programme for this breed, special importance should be given to the metacarpal perimeter, since as age progresses, if there is no proportional development, animals with weak cane could present difficulties in movement and support of their body weight
