# Perception of a sense of humor in bilingual Spanish-French individuals monocultural and bicultural

This research explores the perception of humor between bilingual Spanish-French individuals, comparing the reactions of subjects that are considered monocultural with those that identify themselves as bicultural (French-Colombian).Situated in the context of studies on bilingualism and biculturality, and based on humor theories such as the Theory of the Opposition of Screen and the Theory of Incongruence, this research sought to answer the following question: Is the comic effect of humorous texts perceived differently by bicultural bilinguals compared with monocultural bilinguals? To investigate this issue, a qualitative methodology was adopted, using a questionnaire with 57 parodic headlines of the French and Colombian online press, evaluated by a Likert scale by 50 bilingual participants. The data were analyzed in a descriptive way, comparing the perception of humor in terms of bicultural autonomy, nationality and daily use of languages. The main results indicate that bilinguals identify as biculturals show a greater appreciation of humor in various thematics, including those with cultural components (the self-perception of biculturalism, nationality and the daily use of languages). the Incongruity Theory, this study encouraged to accept the following question: Is the commercial effect of humorous texts perceived differently by bicultural bilinguals compared to monocultural bilinguals?
