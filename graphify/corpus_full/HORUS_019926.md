# MINE SUSTAINABILITY IN MINING UNDERSTOOD AS A PROCESS OF TRANSFORMATION

The concept of sustainability has been implicitly present in different historical milestones, mainly through economic thought, which expressed its concerns regarding the availability of resources, their use and distribution, understood at every moment by economic thinkers. The term sustainable development (SD) emerged during the United Nations Conference on Human Environment in Stockholm in the year 1972. Later in 1987 from the Our Common Future Report, this term was defined as that development system that satisfies the basic human needs in the present, without compromising the possibility that future generations will meet their own needs (UN, 1987). The thinkers of the classical school of economics, highlighted particular concerns regarding the collection, use and distribution of resources. As raised by (Forstater, 2008) some of these concerns are identified from the classical school. Thomas Malthus raised concerns regarding population growth and poverty in 1798; in the nineteenth century David Ricardo proposed the theory of diminishing returns in 1817; John Stuart Mill presented his concerns about the distribution of wealth in 1848; Karl Marx theorized regarding the ownership of resources in 1867 and Stanley Jevons about Marginal utility in 1871
