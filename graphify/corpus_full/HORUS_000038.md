# Synergistic heat-moisture and enzymatic modification of starch blends: a case study on structuring cassava-based gluten-free baked goods

The effects of dual modification, combining heat-moisture treatment (HMT) followed by enzymatic hydrolysis, on the physicochemical, structural, and morphological properties of a binary blend of cassava and yam starches were evaluated. Initially, the native starch blend underwent HMT, which did not alter the granular morphology but reduced peak viscosity and improved the thermal stability of starch pastes. Subsequently, individual enzymatic treatments with α-amylase, amyloglucosidase, and pullulanase were applied. Dual modification increased solubility and water absorption capacity compared to the native starch blend. Scanning electron microscopy revealed exo-erosion phenomena, characterized by surface lacerations and cracks. Additionally, it altered the X-ray diffraction pattern, increased gelatinization temperatures, and significantly reduced the crystallinity index. Furthermore, the dual treatments HMT+AMG and HMT+PUL decreased breakdown and setback viscosities, reducing the retrogradation tendency of starch granules. Consequently, the dual treatments HMT+AMG and HMT+PUL emerge as promising alternatives for replacing sour starch in pandebono formulations, as they maintain the product's sensory acceptance without compromising key quality attributes such as expansion capacity and hardness
