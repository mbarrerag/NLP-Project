# ASSESSMENT OF A INFRUMENT TO TAKE MEASURES ASSOCIATED TO COOPERATION IN RATES BASED ON THE CRAWFORD MODEL

Only with research in nonhuman animals is it possible to fully understand the cooperative behavior in different species, including human. The mere fact of controlling language as one of the relevant variables is already a contribution since other aspects that have contributed to the evolution of cooperation can be examined. Answers to questions such as: do individuals from other species, such as rats and other mammals cooperate to solve problems or to help themselves? And, if they do, under what conditions? Studying the behavior of mammals, such as rats, in the laboratory, requires rigorous management of concepts and methods. Safe and Gutierrez (2006) and Guevara, Salamanca and Silva (2008) have designed instruments to study cooperation in rats based on Crawford (1937); their procedures, however, should not allow the coordination of rats in the performance of some instrumental task, indispensable requirement to talk about cooperation. Instruments, such as mentioned, will allow for close-to-listing with the concepts that give them origin. As argued in this presentation, the design of tasks that require the interaction of experimental subjects to achieve the coordination of the same ones in the development of a task, is the most effective way of measuring the cooperation.
