# The Simulated Bureaucracy of Reincorporation. A Feminist Ethnography of the Public Policy of Reintegration

This thesis sought to understand the limitations and tensions in the production of the Public Policy of Reintegration from a feminist analysis in the framework of the Peace Agreement for the Termination of the Conflict and the Construction of a Stable and Lasting Peace (2016).Based on a feminist epistemology, which favours the construction of the situated knowledge, a feminist ethnography of the state was methodologically carried out, including field and memory journals, together with fourteen in-depth interviews of working people within the framework of the National Council of Reintegration, from which emerging categories of analysis were constructed. As main findings were found: first, what I call a simulated Burocracy of the reincorporation, such as the configuration of an institutional scaffolding that did not allow for implementation beyond the established guidelines of the “DDR”, producing a ‘reincorporated subject’; second, the precarisation and ‘mysterification’ of the work that limited the implementation of the Public Policy of Reintegration, which in turn led to a ‘subjected public policy’, and, third, which described it as a gender approach and ‘suplining’. dded to four in-depth interviews with workers within the framework of the National Reintegration Council, from where emerging analysis categories were built.
