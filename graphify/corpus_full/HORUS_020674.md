# Development of a sync module for a MDF-GIS system for real-time detection, location and monitoring of atmospheric discharges

The atmospheric electric discharges commonly known as “rays”, constitute a natural phenomenon that has been present on the planet earth since its inception 3 billion years ago, being the tropical and subtropical zones where the activity of rays is most common. It is estimated that, at any given moment, there are about 1,800 electrical storms that are forming on the surface of the earth and that the average discharge intensity of a lightning is around 20,000 amperes. The lightning is an electrical discharge of atmospheric origin that in most cases occurs between cloud - earth (negative lightning), and is caused by the strong electrical field that exists in the positive load P located under the cloud and the negative load N from the base of the cloud. Once the storm cloud has been loaded to the point where the electrical field exceeds the local dielectric rigidity of the atmosphere, it is initiated to an atmospheric electrical discharge or lightning.
