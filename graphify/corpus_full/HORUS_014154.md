# Environmental enrichment and behavioral rehabilitation of primates in captivity

Colombia is the second country with the highest biodiversity and the third country with the highest number of primate species, however, poaching, habitat destruction and illegal trafficking represent the main threats to the conservation and preservation of this same richness in fauna. It is therefore necessary to have strategies that allow to improve the processes of rehabilitation of wild fauna that have been condemned to captivity for illegal extraction from early periods of its development to be maintained in contexts not reciprocated by their natural habitat. With regard to this problem, the work of the Rescue and Rehabilitation of Wild Animals Unit (URRAS) of the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine and Zootechnics of the National University of Colombia becomes vitally important, since it is charged to receive animals that have physical and/or behavioral injuries derived from direct or indirect contact with humans. Posterior to their arrival, it is sought the physical and behavioral recovery of animals that is achieved by means of the rehabilitation physical and behavioral, which is promoted by means of physical and behavioral adaptation that favors the effective adaptation to their habitation. These animal recovery and rehabilitation processes can be favored by means of environmental enrichment, which has been a strategy widely used as a zootility and psychological environment
