# PHOTOCATALYTIC OXIDATION OF GLYCEROL USING X/TIO2 (X= CU, AG AND CU-AG) UNDER LOW-WATTAGE VISIBLE IRRADIATION

A set of catalysts was prepared by the surface decoration of TiO2 (Degussa-P25) with a noble metal (Ag), a non-noble metal (Cu), and bimetallic (Cu-Ag) nanoparticles (NPs), which were evaluated in the photocatalytic glycerol oxidation under commercial led white light irradiation (1.8 W) and the assistance of hydrogen peroxide. Dihydroxyacetone and glyceraldehyde were obtained as the oxidation products. After 8 h of reaction, the Ag/TiO2 catalyst showed the highest photocatalytic activity (glycerol conversion of 74%) and dihydroxyacetone selectivity of 80%. However, the highest selectivity towards dihydroxyacetone was achieved with the Cu/TiO2 catalyst (86%) for a glycerol conversion of 55%. The results were contrasted with those obtained in the dark, where catalytic performance was lower, confirming the crucial role of NPs that induce a surface plasmon resonance effect (Schottky junction for metallic Ag) and heterojunction effect (for CuO and Cu2O) that allows the light absorption within the visible spectrum. Thus, these catalysts were active and selective under low-wattage irradiation and alkali-free conditions
