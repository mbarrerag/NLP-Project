# Validation and reliability of the instrument to evaluate measures to control tuberculosis infection

Tuberculosis is an infectiouscontagious disease preventable and curable factor of great interest for public and labor health.The health workers are a vulnerable group that presents greater risk to tuberculosis infection that the general population has achieved between 10 and 100 times greater, due to its occupational exposure during the healthcare process.In addition to the above, the limited implementation of tuberculosis control measures in health institutions contributes to the increase of the risk of infection by this disease.The scientific literature proposes, as an effective strategy to improve the working conditions in health workers, the development of studies with greater scope and methodological rigor that allow to develop relevant health interventions and evaluate them in a valid and reliable way. To determine the validity and reliability of an instrument to evaluate the measures of control of tuberculosis infection directed to health workers. Study of psychometric quantitative type that was carried out in different phases for the validation of content and for the validation of construct and reliability of an instrument on the implementation of the measures of control of tuberculosis infection directed to the health workers. The items that were not statistically associated with the corresponding factor, and following the guidelines given in the scientific literature, were eliminated. The Cronbach alpha was used for the reliability analysis, which indicated an internal consistency of the instrument of 0.997. the final version of the instrument included 34 items that were statistically and conceptually correlated to each factor. For this reason, this manifests acceptable psychometric properties, demonstrating that it is a valid and reliable instrument both to guide future research and to develop interventions and their replicability in the systems of safety and health management at work; specifically in the design, implementation and evaluation of epidemiological surveillance systems in relation to occupational exposure Mycobacterium tuberculosis in health institutions. .
