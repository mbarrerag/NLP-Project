# FLORAL phenology

Floral phenology is often confused with the phenology of flowering, perhaps due to the phonetic similarity between these two terms, despite being different phenomena from the conceptual perspective. The phenology of flowering refers to the times related to the flowering of a species; mainly, the period of the cycle (annual, biennial, or longer-term) the duration of the production of flowers (weeks, months), and the season or month in which the plants bloom (Ollerton and Dafni, 2005; Shivanna and Tando, 2014). In the meantime, the knowledge of these periods of time and the intensity of the flowering is very important in the ecology of the plant-pollining interaction and plant-frugivoro, since it determines the dynamics of the mentioned ecological interactions, which is affected by the availability and abundance of the resource (Morellato et al., 2016; Martins et al., 2021).
