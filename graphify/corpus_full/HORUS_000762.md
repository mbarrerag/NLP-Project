# A shared sea: towards an understanding of the Maritoire and the Maritoralities of the cross-border region of Central American Mosquitia and the archipelago of San Andrés, Providencia and Santa Catalina

This article presents a reflection on the sociocultural relations between the Miskito, Creole and Raizal peoples living in the Central American Caribbean Coast and the Archipelago of San Andrés, Providencia and Santa Catalina, and particularly on the marital and marital areas that have shaped them. I try to show how a cross-border and transnational region is formed from the various exchanges of these peoples, emphasizing the role of the sea. On this, I present a look at the configuration of the marital and maritalities of the three peoples, and sought to answer the question of whether it is possible to speak of a shared sea, from the perspective of the peoples who inhabit it. Finally, I reflect on the inability of the National States to understand the sea as the living space of the communities, and the implications of this on the well-being of the communities; and as a possible twist of this conception, it would allow us to raise in a different way the way Colombia thinks to its island border region and the relationship with its neighbours. The discussion results of the ethnographic field work carried out in the Archipelago in the last twenty years, as well as of the extensive literature review of the region
