# Adsorption calorimetry: a comparison between experimental measurements and theoretical modeling using gas MOFs

The determination of various thermodynamic parameters, including the isosteric heat of adsorption, is essential for the practical determination of the thermal performance of adsorption storage systems. Measurements of isotherms over wide pressure and temperature ranges are evaluated using the “isosteric method”. On the other hand, it is possible to assess the isosteric heats directly and quantitatively by means of the coupled calorimetric–volumetric method (AdCalV), in which both the heat of adsorption and the corresponding isotherm are measured simultaneously. In this work, the isosteric heats of ammonia (NH3) are compared for five samples of metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) synthesized in this study. These heats are measured experimentally. They are evaluated using the Clausius-Clapeyron (CC) model. Furthermore, the results are compared with isosteric heats derived from Dubinin-Astakhov, Toth and UniLan analytical adsorption models. The purpose of this comparison is to analyze the reliability and limitations of the simpler methods and assumptions. We measured isosteric heat of ammonia in five prototype metallic-organic frameworks: ZIF-9, MOF-199, MOF-101, MOF-177 and MOF-74, using both techniques. This investigation has shown that the isosteric heats obtained by the direct method (AdCalV) are in agreement with those calculated by the DA and Toth models over the whole range of the loads studied. However, depending on the specific MOF studied and the load range, the predictions of the models differ from the experiments. The order of NH3 adsorption capacity from highest to lowest is as follows MOF-199 (10.60 mmolg−1) > MOF-177 (7.85mmolg−1) > MIL-101 (5.85 mmolg−1) > MOF-74 (4.45 mmolg−1) > ZIF-9 (3.45 mmolg−1)
