# Detection of Hepadnavirus of domestic cats (DCH) in dogs (Canine-CDH) suspected of hepatitis due to clinical pathology

Hepadnaviruses are very important viruses, particularly in humans since among them is the causal agent of Hepatitis B, a disease that causes widespread morbidity and mortality in the world. In animals also have been associated hepadnaviruses with generation of hepatitis, particularly in wild species. However, during the last years the cases of hepatitis in domestic species, particularly in cats and dogs have increased. In this last species, it is known that its main etiology is the Canine Adenovirus type 1 (CAdV-1) that causes canine hepatitis, disease that has been controlled by vaccination and its incidence has decreased markedly in the world. In the case of cats there was no reference of a virus that caused hepatitis, in 2018 a new hepadnavirus called the Hepadnavirus of domestic cats (DCH), which is suggested as a possible agent of hepatitis in that species. The DCH has been reported in several countries of Asia, Oceania, Europe and North America. Recently, a new liver disease was reported also in dogs DCH that it is present from the presence of the virus DCH, and its first studies associated with hepatitis in that species
