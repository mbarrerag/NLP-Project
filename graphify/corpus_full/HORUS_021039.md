# The faces of resistance in the Center of Bogotá

In 2016, we witnessed in Bogotá the demolition of one of the most sonadas areas in the media headlines as cradle of perdition and death. While it was spoken of as the main pot of narcotics in Bogotá, this space was unknown by the majority of the citizenry. The local administration advanced the intervention of eviction in order to advance in the renewal and urban planning and displaced a large group of socially marginalized inhabitants, among which there were families in poverty, stripped by violence, _x0093_ciudadanos_x0094_ criminalised by their situation of street inhabitants, their work of street prostitution or their addiction to the consumption of SPA. Posterior to the demolition, the IDIPRON together with the National Museum promoted a project of reconstruction of the collective memory through the model of the L, which was constituted in a significant experience of research, action and participatory (IAP) that allowed to recognize the richness of the life that lived in the area, the affective links and shared history, and motivated the model of the space of the urban area, which was a unique research, as the participation of the different. At the same time, we propose a community articulation, often used in listening centers and in reducing risk and damage, which implies: on the one hand, that the exercises of IAP such as the collective construction of artistic pieces, the activities shared in the kitchen and in the sowing of the collective gardens will not be pre-designed by experts, but will be constructed in parallel with the community and in the territory (Gongora, Salazar and Borja, 2021); on the other hand, we are committed to the joint work with community peers, a figure that differs from the community operators, since the same conditions of vulnerability as the people with whom they go to work (Gutierrez, 2015). Finally, at the level of public policy, the research is to contribute to the same way of thinking, the research is to the same way of thinking about the different people who have lived (or live) the same conditions of vulnerability as the people with whom they go to work (Gutierrez, 2015)
