# EVALUATION OF CRACKS REPAIR IN CEMENT-BASED MATERIALS THROUGH PASSIVE TREATMENTS USING MIXED AND AXENIC CULTURES WITH UREOLYTIC ACTIVITY

Cement-based materials are widely used in construction industry due to their mechanical properties. With use and pass of time, these materials tend to crack leading to structures deterioration, generating the need for their repair or replacement. Different alternatives have been evaluated at the industrial level in search for repair strategies. Microbiologically Induced Calcite Precipitation (MICP) by ureolytic bacteria have shown great potential to obtain biotechnological products that improve materials mechanical properties and assist crack repairs due to passive or active treatments. The passive occurs when the active agent (bacteria) is applied from the outside in an existing crack, while in the active, the active agent (bacterial spores) is added to the mixture when the cement-based material is prepared. This study aimed to evaluate external cracks (0.3 +/- 0.5mm wide) repair effectiveness in mortar samples by bacteria with high precipitation capacity and mixed cultures of these bacteria with similar cell concentration (1010 UFC/ml). Mortar test tubes (40*40*160mm), with a sand/cement ratio = 2.75 and cement/water = 1:0.5, were prepared and cracks were induced by a four-point protocol bending. Eight different treatments (n=9) were used to repair cracks: four axenic cultures, two mixed cultures and two controls (culture medium only). A biopolymer was used in the treatments together with bacteria grown in culture medium that induce carbonate precipitation. Repair agents were applied every third day for 15 days at 30°C. Change in crack size was evaluated using a fissurometer and a stereoscope. Flexural resistance was evaluated to estimate and statistically compare samples resistance. All treatments showed 100% crack filling at day 8. However, treatments with axenic strain M3C3 and with the mixed culture composed of strains S17, S1 and M4C20, presented significant differences regarding flexural strength, showing the best results. For strain M3C3, the initial resistance of the repaired mortar specimens was exceeded by 30%, demonstrating the efficacy of this biotechnological process in crack repair and mechanical properties improvement of cement-based materials. Additionally, and consistent with preliminary assays in liquid medium, it was found that although a rapid precipitation of calcium carbonate was observed for several of the mixed cultures, some antagonistic and synergistic interactions were evident, indicating the need to further evaluate associated metabolic products
