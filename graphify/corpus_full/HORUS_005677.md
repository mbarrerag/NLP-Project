# ESTIMATION OF BODY MEASUREMENTS IN DOMESTIC CATTLE INFERRED FROM CRANIAL VALUES

The inference of body biometric data from skeletal remains is a first order need in the field of zooarchaeology. But in bone sets, it is often difficult to find whole pieces which can be give us a general view of the body conformation. The obtention of measurements in live animals can help to establish estimation inferring formulas from bony remnants. The present work is a contribution to the topic of estimating body measurements (withers height and body length) in domestic cattle from simple cephalic measurements. For this purpose, we studied a sample of Sanmartinero cattle, a breed which is typical of the piedmont of the Department of Meta (Colombian Orinoquia). Sample size was 100 animals (21 ¿ and 79 ¿; range age 2 months to 10 years). We have chosen the obtention of data from this unselected native breed with extreme adaptive traits, as creole breeds can be supposed to present biotypes which are closer to ancient animals than those from current highly selected breeds. A set of 6 linear head variables were obtained "in vivo" by standard morphometric methods: width and length of head, width and length of face, and width and length of skull. As body traits, we obtained body length (BL) and withers height (WH). Spearman's correlation coefficients were obtained, which were all statistically significative with body measurements. Bivariate regressions of head measurements to BL and WH were then stablished. Except for cranial width, which is not statistically significant in Wilcoxon's paired data test, BL and WH could be well inferred from simple skull measurements by means of simple lineal formulas y=a+bx, which are stablished in the paper. As the question itself therefore has no conceptual and mathematical difficulty (except the need to respect the studied lineal range) it depends solely on the good state of conservation of the cephalic bone remains
