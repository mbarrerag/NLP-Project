# Space-time dynamics of the Cagollo rot in oil palm genotypes in eastern and southern western Colombia as a basis for risk analysis.

The Pudrition of Cogollo (PC) is a disease that has impacted to important levels and negatively the sustainability of palm farming in Colombia. Up to the present time, management alternatives have been proposed, but in general, present certain variable and economic limitations for its implementation. They propose elements that seek to reduce the negative impact on production, leaving aside epidemiological models and risk analysis. In view of this circumstance, the present work had as its general objective to determine the spatial and economic dynamics of the PC and to quantify risk factors with edafoclimatic origin, genetic at regional scale, such as the response of the interaction of genotypes (Ténera and Hybrids O x G) in two contrasting regions (Tumaco and Villavicencio), basis for the analysis of the risk analysis of the plant risk. This work developed during the period between semester B – 2011 and B – 2014. In the first phase of the study, the response of the genotypes Tener and Hbrids against the PC was determined by the use of the area under the curve of the progression risk analysis of the stagn disease progress (ABECECPE) during the period B – 2011 and B and B – 2014 – 2014. In the first phase, The study also found that the progress curves were adapted to logistic and gompertz models, and that the spatial tools indicated low aggregation of the PC, along with slight dependence between foci, and a dispersion potential at most 30 m. Finally, a slight spatial time interaction was found. Our results present a focus to establish alternative management of the PC based on the analysis of epidemiological risk factors, with a spatial-time approach supported by the simulation of temporal dynamics. These can be applied to the design of integrated disease management plans, in addition to their academic value by using these tools in the management of epidemiological data from other pathosystems. Key words: survival curves, temporal models, spatial distribution, point patterns, postmodern epidemiology, new analysis tools
