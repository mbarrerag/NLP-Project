# CO2 CAPTURE METHODS, GREEN EFFECT GAS WITH GREAT ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACT

At present, one of the most important environmental problems of concern is global warming, mainly given by the increase of Greenhouse Gases Effect (GEI) in the atmosphere, especially carbon dioxide (CO2) [1]. Taking into account its impact on climate change, and the imminent need to reduce global anthropogenic emissions, there are different strategies that seek to capture this gas to reduce its concentration in the air. Some of these techniques are chemical absorption, physical adsorbation, capture through the use of membranes, cryogenic distillation, among others. The present review aims to provide information on the effect of CO2 on the environment and to show some of the capture methods currently employed for its removal from the air. To begin, the sources of CO2 emissions are natural and anthropogenic. The carbon cycle is the main source of natural origin and allows for the use of forests and forest surfaces for the purposes of grazing, and photosynthesis. The process involves the binding of a gas or liquid to a solid surface, achieving the specific separation of one or more species, thus, depending on the affinity between the surface of the adsorbing material and the molecules that are to be separated. The adsorbing process is regenerated by the application of heat or the reduction of pressure, so that the obtaining of CO2 is not a complex process. In recent years different adsorbing materials have been investigated for the capture of CO2, finding that the best ones are activated carbon and zeolites [7]. The method of capture with membranes is based on the difference of diffusion of gases in the pores, which depends on the difference of pressures between both sides of the membrane, favoring the separation when it is found to be at high pressures.
