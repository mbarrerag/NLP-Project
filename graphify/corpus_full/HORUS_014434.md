# Study of nicotinamide/nicotinate mononucleotide adenyl transferase of leishmania braziliensis (LbNMNAT): Towards the characterization of a chemotherapeutic white potential.

Leishmaniasis is a public health problem in countries where it is presented endemic [1]. The search for low toxicity drugs for the patient and the identification of new therapeutic targets, have been some of the strategies to fight against this parasitemia. The laboratory of basic research in biochemistry (LIBBIQ), has concentrated its interest in the metabolism of the Nicotinamide and Adenin (NAD+) Dinucleotide of Nicotinamide and Adeninide (NAD+) in Leishmania braziliensis, specifically in the central enzyme of its metabolism, the Nicotinamide Mononucleotide Adenililililil Transferase (LbNMNMLT). Resitting its characteristics as possible therapeutic target because of its fundamental role in the metabolism of the parasite and its survival [2]. Therefore, it was considered relevant to continue with the characterization of LbNMNNAT, a central enzyme in the metabolism of the NAD+. In this work, several biointhinformmatic and experimental methodological strategies were used. Promastigitis were initially used to evaluate promastigitis to evaluate the effect that some imidazolic derivatives would have an effect on its viability, T S210D, which corresponds to a specific region of parasite protein. The analysis of the effect of phosphomimetic mutation on enzyme adenylyl transferase activity, evaluated by direct enzyme trials (RP-HPLC), showed no differences with respect to native protein. However, it is necessary to explore the effect that this modification could have on other possible functions of Lb-NMNAT, which are under investigation such as chaperone activity
