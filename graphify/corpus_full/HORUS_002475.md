# EFFECTS OF LAND USES IN THE SOIL PEDOFEATURES TO THE ORINOCO OXISOLS.

Oxisols are known as soils from tropical and subtropical areas associated with savanna ecosystems in conditions of ancient landscapes. The research was carried out in the Taluma experimental farm, rural area of the Puerto López municipality, department of Meta in the Colombian Orinoco savanna. The average annual rainfall of 2,134 mm, distributed throughout the year following a monomodal pattern, dry season period is between December to March, with a rainy season from March to November. The mean annual temperature is 26.8°C, relative humidity ranges between 68.5% to 85% with wind speeds lower than 2 m/s. The area is a tropical rain forest (monsoon) according to Köppen-Geiger (Peel et al., 2007). The soils are Typic hapludox (SSS, 2022), acidic, with low contents of organic matter, phosphorus, calcium and magnesium and high saturation of exchangeable aluminum; located in the medium and high old terraces with slightly undulating topography between 0% and 3% slope (typical of plain areas in the Colombian eastern). Three land use have been selected: natural restoration, forests and crop rotation; soil profile was characterized to a depth of 1.3m to each land use. The soil samples were taken from the different genetic horizons for micromorphological and physical-chemical analysis. Preliminary, results show greater biological activity and presence of plant remains in the surface horizons, with a development of microstructures of subangular and crumbly blocks, greater porosity and presence of chambers and channels. Is common the presence of close porphyritic and chitonic distribution patterns, stipple speckeled b-fabric, reddish and brown micromass. The presence of abundant fractured quartz and iron oxides scattered in the soil groundmass is common. Despite the genetical physical, chemical and mineralogical conditions land use favors aeration, biota activity and soil structure
