# Metagenomic reconstruction of the communities of microorganisms involved in cocoa fermentation in two agroecological regions of Colombia

The microbiova of the fermentation of cocoa is a complex ecosystem, composed of a wide variety of bacteria, yeasts and fungi. Each of these microorganisms plays a crucial role in the degradation of the fruit pulp pulp, the transformation of the organic compounds and the production of secondary metabolites that directly influence the aromatic profile of chocolate. From the sciences, progress has been made in the characterization of the communities involved in fermentation, which has allowed some functions and dynamics to be diluted within the process. However, analyzing these microorganisms only from a general perspective of community is not sufficient, since it does not allow to differentiate accurately if they belong to the same species or population group. This study addresses the fermentation of cocoa in two agroecological regions of Colombia: the Santandereana mountain (MS) and the tropical humid forest (BHT), using a metanomic approach to reconstruct microbiogen genomes and analyze their functional and population diversity.
