# Optimization of control forces of a hybrid system based on TMDI and magnetorheological dampers for the reduction of response in structures subject to sixmic excitements

The mitigation of structural vibrations induced by dynamic loads is a crucial research area in structural engineering. Control strategies such as passive, semi-active, active and hybrid, offer different levels of effectiveness, but each one has inherent limitations. This research proposes a hybrid control system that integrates a Tuned Mass Damper Iner (TMDI) with a magnetoreological damper (MR), managed by a Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) combined with diffuse control (FLC). The TMDI improves energy dissipation through an optimized inertial mechanism, while the MR damper provides adaptive damping in real time with minimal energy consumption. The WOA optimizes the design parameters of the TMDI and determines the optimal control forces for the MR buffer, while the diffuse control dynamically adjusts its activation in function of variations in the structural response. The results show that the proposed hybrid system achieves a significant reduction in the structural response. By selectively activating the MR dampers only when necessary, the system avoids unnecessary increments of rigidity and reduces the energy consumption.
