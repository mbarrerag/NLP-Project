# Influence of social precedents and others’ behavior on adherence to protective behaviors against COVID-19

During the pandemic by COVID-19, the others influenced by their behavior in how one individual followed the protective behaviors against COVID-19. The framework of the analysis of the conduct proposes that there are background and consistent conditions that could explain because the people followed the recommendations. This thesis sought to investigate as some background and social consequences predicted adherence. Study 1 explored the relationship between the reported adherence, two contexts and four perceived social consequences. Study 2 explained how the social distance modulated between the reported adherence and the behavior of others, and three perceived social consequences. Colombian residents completed surveys based on the indirect functional analysis where they reported their adherence in several situations and the probability of receiving certain social consequences. Study 1 found that the adherence was greater in the context of home than outside the home. In addition, the consequences such as social approval had more effect in the context of home.
