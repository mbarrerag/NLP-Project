# The Problem of Government in the Political Critical Theory

Critical political theory conceives the governance of contemporary societies as a process of assigning collective ends realized through three modes of coordination: the systemic self-governance proper to the capitalist market, the consummate hierarchy in the bureaucratic-administrative power of the State and the asymmetric dialogical networks that account for the interaction between the different expressions of associativism inherent to civil society. In general, this point of view coincides with that of the so-called theory of governance. However, while for this one such modalities must balance each other "metagovernance" in such a way that hierarchy and dialogue are functional to mercantile autopoiesis, for critical political theory, on the contrary, dialogue, supported by a de-hierarchized state, must prevail over the market system
