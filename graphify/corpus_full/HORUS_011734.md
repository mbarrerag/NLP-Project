# ESTIMAL ATMOSFERIC FLOWS IN CONDITIONS OF NON-STAMINARY TURBULENCE

Urban growth, industrial and agricultural production have generated an increase in the daily amount of emissions of atmospheric pollutants, including greenhouse gases (GHG), which are the main causes of climate change (Mønster et al., 2019; Nieves et al., 2019). That is why it is important to have methods that allow quantifying GHG emissions, in order to understand and monitor the processes that generate them and the effectiveness of mitigation actions. Currently, the technique of covariance of whirls (EC) is used to quantify atmospheric flows resulting from random fluctuations of wind speed and scalars such as temperature, concentration of gases, among others, that occur in the small scales of atmospheric movement (microscale) (Aubinet et al., 2012).
