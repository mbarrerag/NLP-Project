# MAXIMAL UTILITY AND OPTIMAL NUMBER OF WORKERS IN AN AGROFORESTRY SYSTEM WITH MINIMAL FOREST RESERVE

Agriculture and forests are essential resources for humans, providing food and purifying the air, which is crucial for the health of the planet and its inhabitants. However, intensive agriculture can lead to soil degradation and deforestation in the search for more arable land. Therefore, it is fundamental to optimally allocate the workforce to maximize production utility, improve quality of life, and maintain ecological balance. This study focuses on maximizing the production utility of a population engaged in both agriculture and forest management, using the proportion of people working in the forest sector as the control variable. Two scenarios are analyzed: one where a minimum forest reserve is required and another where this restriction is not active. The research results indicate that preserving a portion of the forest resource ensures a minimum base for economic activities and the needs of local communities. Furthermore, when the minimum reserve restriction is active, greater utility is generated, highlighting the importance of conservation policies and sustainable management of natural resources
