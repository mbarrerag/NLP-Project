# Arabidopsis shows non-host constitutive resistance against Xanthomonas phaseoli pv. manihotis

The vascular bacteriosis of yucca, caused by the gram-negative bacterium Xanthomonas phaseoli pv. manihotis (Xpm), formerly known as Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. manihotis is the main bacterial disease that compromises its production. With the goal of generating a durable and broad-spectrum resistance to bacteriosis it is possible to exploit the natural mechanisms present in non-host plants. Arabidopsis is an extensively studied model plant, which is a non-host of Xpm. The aim of this study was to determine if non-host resistance of Arabidopsis is a consequence of the presence of physical barriers or if it depends on genetic determinants. In this work, the ability of Arabidopsis plants to respond to inoculation with Xpm was evaluated. None of the eight Arabidopsis ecotypes evaluated showed a hypersensitive response to inoculation with eight different Xpm strains. Although the presence of reactive oxygen species was not identified, a blockage in the growth of Xpm was found in Arabidopsis plants. Taken together, the results presented here suggest that Arabidopsis is not activating a response against Xpm and that the observed resistance may be a consequence of the physical barriers present in Arabidopsis that Xpm is not able to overcome
