# REFLECTIONS ON CONVIVIENCE

Today, as always, we must ask ourselves what we mean by poverty and why societies generally feel rejected by poor people. Although many describe the daily hardships of those human beings who must fight every day for their immediate and fleeting food and shelter, we continue to speculate about how to define such a circumstance. But the question remains: why is the poor rejected? Is the fear generated by the mistrust felt towards those who are not known to us, simply because they are not? Is it the fear that generates us to think that, at any time, poverty can come to us, and we prefer to evade its reality? Or is the impotent repudiation, because in the poor we see reflected the injustice and social inequality for which we are also responsible? Why do slander and hate speeches remain as a daily argument that confuses the debate with the unjust and unworthy treatment of rational beings? Whatever, behind all these concerns lies an ethical question that deserves to be taken up. This text presents some reflections that, from the ethical and moral point of view, should be incorporated as a basis for analysis in studies on the habitat, but on the basis of our own reflection
