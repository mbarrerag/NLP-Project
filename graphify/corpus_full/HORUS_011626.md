# THE MULTI-MENSIONALITY OF THE OLD: AGEING PERSPECTIVES IN THE AGENDAS OF THE 21st CENTURY

The ageing of the population is one of the greatest social achievements of recent times, but does not necessarily imply a better quality of life for people who are ageing. On the contrary, literature points out that issues such as personal and family income, educational level, place of residence, access to social and health services, among many aspects, can be determinant of how they age. The situation becomes more complex in developing countries because they have not been able to guarantee a basic level of rights to the population. The heterogeneity of old age, that is, the assumption that each person ages in his own and in particular and over the years becomes more unique, agendas an increasing demand for public policies, programmes, opportunities and services that meet the needs of older adults, respecting individuality, guaranteeing their independence and autonomy
