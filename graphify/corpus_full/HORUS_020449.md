# Effect of protein restriction as modulator of immune response of gastrointestinal tract of BALB/c mice infected with Leishmania infantum

The protein malnutrition is the most frequent cause of secondary immunodeficiency leading to alterations of the innate and adaptive immune system, generating colonization and proliferation of pathogenic agents, becoming one of the main risk factors for the development of clinical forms of visceral leishmania (LV). Using as the biological model Mice BALB/c, our research group showed that animals subjected to protein restriction and infection with L. infantum, present severe atrophys in lymphoid organs, alterations in the subpopulations of T lymphocytes and in levels of expression of specific chemotatic factors in thymus and spleen. These alterations suggest that a precondition of protein malnutrition affects the immune response to L. infantum, modifying T-cell migration and the ability to control the proliferation of parasites.
