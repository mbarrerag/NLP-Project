# MICRO AND NANOPARTICULOUS SYNTHESIS TO EFLUENTS FROM THE GALVANISED DECAPATE BY IMMERSION IN HEAT

The hot galvanizing process is one of the most commonly used techniques for protecting against corrosion of steel and consists of immersion of steel in a molten zinc bath. This process consists of several stages and each of them produces solid or effluent residues with metallic content, which must be treated before being disposed in landfills and public sewers. These residues are around 1 000 000 t/year worldwide. Safe disposal of these is of great importance for environmental protection. The most critical stage of this process is the pickling, which generates very complex effluents to treat due to the acid character and metallic content, but are of interest for obtaining value added materials. In this article micro and nanoparticles of iron and zinc are obtained from pickling effluents of the hot galvanized industry, using four different routes of chemical synthesis (co-precipitation and sun-gel), obtaining particles of different sizes, morphologies and crystalline structures
