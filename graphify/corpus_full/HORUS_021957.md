# Molecular and in vitro sensitivity to antibiotics and peptides derived from catelicidine as antimicrobial agents against clinical isolates of Staphylococcus aureus from the city of Bogotá D.C.

Staphylococcus aureus is a bacterium of great clinical importance, characterized by being the most virulent species within its genus and causing very diverse infections that can threaten the life of those who suffer them. However, its importance does not lie only in the diseases it causes, but in the high capacity that has evidenced to present resistance to multiple antibiotics within which βlactamáns are found, exemplified by resistance to methicillin. The isolates resistant to methicillin are a problem of public health worldwide, which initially were attributed to hospital environments, but that with the passage of time have begun to be reported more and more frequently in the community. In addition, resistance to other antibiotics with different therapeutic targets has been observed. The above exists the search for new alternatives that allow to control the bacterial growth of isolates that cannot be treated by the existing antimicrobials. This work aims to determine the effect of peptides derived from catalizydina in the growth and morphology of clinical isolates of S. aureus from the city of Bogotá, Dureus to perform the LL-37 and ATRA-1 have antimicrobial capacity in relation to this pathogen and that there are differences between the activity of these compounds and the profile of sensitivity to metyliline. Finally, we find that these peptides generate changes in superficial morphology as one of their mechanisms of action, however, it is not ruled out that they present intracellular effects that enhance their antimicrobial activity. With this work, we seek to contribute to the monitoring of bacterial resistance in the city of Bogotá, promote the development of new therapeutic alternatives that allow control of bacterial resistance in our region, as well as contribute in the characterization of the activity of synthetic antimicrobial peptides in S. aureus
