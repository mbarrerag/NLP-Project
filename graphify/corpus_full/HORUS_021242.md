# Preliminary study of the potential of yucca leaves as an alternative nutritional source and identification of white genes for the improvement of resistance to bacteriosis and nutritional content

The yuca is a plant native to the Amazon, domestic more than 10000 years ago. In the 15th century it was carried by the Portuguese to the African continent. But recently, in the last century, introduced to the Asian continent. In the whole strip of tropical countries that cultivate it consumption has focused almost exclusively on the root, which due to its high starch content represents one of the most important sources of calories for a billion people in the world. However, the nutritional value of the root is very low compared to that of the leaves, which are characterized by a high content of proteins and other minerals and vitamins. Despite this, the leaves are not consumed by the high content of cyanide content that they present and the lack of knowledge about the mechanisms for their elimination. Only in some countries of Africa (Congo, Sierra Leone) the leaves are used for the preparation of different dishes and consumed in a routine and commercial way. Several studies have shown that, with individual or combined forms of crushing, cutting and heating, can be eliminated up to 90% of the Cyanium content in the leaves.
