# Technical feasibility of recovery of GTD 111 degraded blades in service by thermal treatment and additive manufacturing.

The repair of the components of the hot gas route is crucial for economic reasons in gas turbines. In this work a characterization of the deposits applied by Laser Powder Bed Fusion (L-PBF) and Laser Powder Directed Energy Deposition (LP-DED) was performed on a first stage wing manufactured with GTD 111 DS superalloy. When deteriorating in operation due to the collision of external objects, the growth of cracks due to thermal fatigue and high temperature erosion, their useful life is drastically reduced. The process parameters for L-DED and L-PBF were established according to the integrity and geometry of the deposits. For both processes René 65 powder was used without preheating. Visual inspection and metallographic analysis was used to evaluate the solidity of the additive manufacturing deposits. The optical microscopy and high-scopying of the scanning microscopy was used to evaluate the microstructure of the layers deposited in the cross section and analysis of EBSD allowed to study the crystallographic texture.
