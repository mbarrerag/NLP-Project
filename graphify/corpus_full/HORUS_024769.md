# Association between decreased in vitro parasitic sensitivity and therapeutic response in patients with skin Leishmaniasis treated at Federico Lleras Acosta dermatological center in the city of Bogotá between 2006 and 2012.

Skin leishmaniasis is considered a neglected tropical disease, with a wide geographical distribution in the world and a significant incidence in socially vulnerable populations. It has an undervalued impact by society and health systems, with a not negligible disease burden. Despite having therapeutic schemes for 70 years, in recent decades the parasite has been documenting increasing resistance to the first lines of treatment and high rates of therapeutic failure. Work carried out in Latin America reports resistance between 10-20% and therapeutic failure rates to the first treatment line of 25%. However, the correlation between the decrease in vitro parasitic sensitivity and therapeutic response is unclear. A case and control study was carried out, with a total of 34 patients in a Bogotá reference center to determine the association between decreased in vitro sensitivity and clinical response to treatment. OR 0.71 (95% CI 0.085 – 9.39) was obtained, with which there is possibly no relationship between the variables. These results suggest that in vitro decreased sensitivity tests do not impact the decision-making in clinical practice, as a possible predictor of failure to treatment
