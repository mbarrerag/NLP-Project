# INTEGRATIVE PREDICTIVE MODELING OF COVID-19 HOSPITALIZATION RISK IN COLOMBIA: GENOMIC, DEMOGRAPHIC AND CLINICAL INSIGHTS

The COVID-19 pandemic has heavily impacted global health, leading to significant morbidity and mortality. In Colombia, the strain on the healthcare system underscores the need for reliable risk assessment tools. This study aimed to develop predictive models for COVID-19 hospitalization risk in the Colombian population by integrating SARS-CoV-2 genomic, demographic, and clinical data. We conducted a cohort study using data from 275 patients diagnosed with COVID-19 in Colombia, collected through 2021. Whole-genome sequencing of SARS-CoV-2 was performed, and demographic and clinical data were obtained. We stratified patients by hospitalization status, identifying relevant variables using significant p-values. Both univariate and multivariate logistic regression models were applied to determine significant predictors of hospitalization. Additionally, machine learning models, including Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Random Forest (RF), were employed to create predictive models. Variant analysis identified three distinct SARS-CoV-2 clades among the 275 participants, with one clade showing a higher proportion of hospitalized patients, although this was not statistically significant. Stratification by hospitalization outcome revealed significant associations with age (p < 0.01), chronic lower respiratory diseases (p = 0.02), diabetes (p = 0.03), and hypertension (p = 0.04). In multivariate and univariate models, `Abnormalities of breathing¿ had the highest odds ratio (OR=2.96, 95% CI: 1.02-8.6), indicating a significantly increased likelihood of hospitalization. Conversely, `Pain in throat and chest¿ (OR=0.39, 95% CI: 0.16-0.98), `Disturbances of smell and taste¿ (OR=0.53, 95% CI: 0.28-0.99), and `Malaise and fatigue¿ (OR=0.44, 95% CI: 0.2-0.98) were associated with a decreased likelihood. The best predictive performance was achieved by integrating demographic, clinical, and genomic data, with SVM and RF models showing high accuracy (AUC: 0.97 and 0.94, respectively). Clade features did not improve model performance. This is the first study to investigate the association between SARS-CoV-2 genomic features and clinical outcomes for predicting hospitalization in the Colombian population. Our findings underscore the potential of integrating diverse data types to develop predictive models for COVID-19 hospitalization risk, which can inform clinical decision-making and resource allocation to improve patient outcomes
