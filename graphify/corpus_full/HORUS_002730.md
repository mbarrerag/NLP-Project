# ‘Symbolism to formalism: the translation of the Spanish post-revolutionary post-Russia post-Russia’

The first post-revolutionary decades in Russia were not only times of great social and economic transformations, but also an era of cultural changes of great magnitude, conditioned, on the one hand, by ideological and political innovations, and on the other hand, by the evolutionary development of the culture of the previous period.In the last decades, the attention of the researchers has been increasingly focused on revealing the relations of continuity between the culture of the pre-revolutionary period and the post-revolutionary, in identifying the symbolic origins of the Russian Vanguardism and the formalist school, and also on determining the common vectors of the Symbolism, futurism and acmeism, which had previously been overlooked due to the controversial struggle between these currents. But many things remain in the shadow: for example, the interaction of the following symbolicism and formalism in the field of literary translation is studied very little in the early decades, the poets developed the symbolist method. Is the student of Salamanca, José de Espronceda, and of the works of Calderón by the prominent symbolist poet Konstantin Balmont (1919); the Cantar de mio Cid and the popular romances translated by the medievalist and formalist scholar Boris Yarjó (first half of the 1930s); as well as the lyric of García Lorca in the version of the Acmeist poet Valentin Parnáj (second half of the 1930s). From these examples, we will characterize the translations and the method employed in them, both from the structural perspective (the metro, the rhythm, the sounding rhyme, etc.) and from the pragmatic perspective (the reasons for choosing the material to translate; the paratexts that accompany the translations and their functions)
