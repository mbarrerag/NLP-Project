# Sizing of swallowing in patients with cerebrovascular disease (ECV) by means of GUSS test and deglutatory acelerometry in relation to healthy patients.

The deglucose composed by different phases is a complex process that involves coordination between several sub-processes and involves various anatomical structures ranging from the cerebral cortex to the periphery. The commitment of any of these sub-processes can lead to the emergence of a deglutatory disorder or dysphagia. In patients with cerebrovascular disease (ECV) an incidence of dysphagia of 78% in its acute phase is reported, with which, the early evaluation and the prompt implementation of feeding strategies according to the difficulties of the subject decreases the risk of complications in the health condition related to aspirational pneumonia. This evaluation may be of observational type by means of scales of deglutatory sieve, such as the GUSS test, or it may be of instrumental objective type. to compare patients with antecedence of CVD with healthy patients by means of the GUSS test for deglutinate screening adapted to the Colombian population and the deglutinate acture as an objective non-invasive physiological measure. This analytical observation study included 19 patients with CVD and 15 healthy individuals as a group of control of age and GUS was performed by sex
