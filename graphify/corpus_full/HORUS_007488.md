# Spodoptera cosmioides (Lepidoptera: Noctuidae) as an alternative host for mass rearing the parasitoid Palmistichus elaeisis (Hymenoptera: Eulophidae)

Different alternative hosts can reduce or even avoid pre-imaginal conditioning of parasitoids in mass production programs.Spodoptera cosmioides (Walker) (Lepidoptera: Noctuidae) may be an alternative host for the mass breeding of the parasitoid Palmistichus elaeisis Delvare & LaSalle (Hymenoptera: Eulophidae).The objective of this study was to evaluate the parasitism, emergence, egg-adult period, progeny size, sex ratio, and host tissue consumption per individual of P. elaeisis produced from S. cosmioides pupae in the laboratory.Newly formed pupae of S. cosmioides were weighed, placed individually in glass tubes and each was exposed to six newly emerged females of P. elaeisis for 48 hours.These pupae were transferred to plastic pots until P. elaeisis adults emerged.Palmistichus elaeisis parasitized and emerged 100.0% and 87.5% of S. cosmioides pupae, respectively.The egg-adult period of P. elaeisis was 24.28 ± 0.18 days, with 109 ± 2 individuals emerged per host pupa.Host mass consumption was 2.56 ± 0.01mg per emerged parasitoid.This is the first report of P. elaeisis parasitizing and developing in S. cosmioides pupae in the laboratory.The ease of rearing S. cosmioides with an artificial diet and the high parasitism, emergence and size of P. elaeisis progeny increase the suitability of this alternative host for mass rearing of this parasitoid
