# TITLE. Ancestral Territorialities, Historical Memory and Justice of Black Women in Tumaco. An approach to life-field-populated relations under the effects of Law 70 of 1993

The Semillero de Mujeres-Tumaco, linked to the research group PODEA (Research Group Law and Environmental Policy), in its work plan has defined the recovery of ancestral community practices (family, organizational, justice, care, food, among others) of the peasant, indigenous, root, black and Afro-descendent communities through the collection of historical memory in collaborative research, through field work with ethnographic orientation based on the observation of organizations, leaders and other actors in issues associated with the understanding of the life-field-city relationship, their socio-territorial conflicts and the alternatives that are built from women. This leads to conversations, reflections and approaches between the academy, women's organizations and the citizenship on the extractive, patriarchal and colonial forms that pervive and strengthen the processes of re-territorialization of knowledge, practices of care, organization and production of ancestral ways of living the planet opposed to the movements that promote the privatization of the land, the cultural dispossession and the predation of the nature (the women's) In order to carry out this analysis, qualitative methodologies will be used, including reflexive ethnography and social cartography. Techniques such as non-directive interviews, participant observation and mapping will be used to gather the necessary information. With these systematized data, the aim is to construct an academic article that follows the structure of the ethnographic text, with special emphasis on the gender perspective as an essential category of analysis. It recognizes the importance of focusing ethnographic work on women in order to understand in depth the social and cultural relations in the context of the Vive Campo Poblado Relationship. This will make it possible to highlight crucial aspects of social life, such as family dynamics, private space and gender relations, which can often be ignored in other research approaches
