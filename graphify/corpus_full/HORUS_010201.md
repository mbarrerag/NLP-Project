# Chagra system in degraded soils in a Ticuna community of the Colombian Amazon

The system of roza-tumba and burning, locally called chagra, is the system of cultivation most used by the indigenous cultures of the Amazon. It is a system of itinerant, transitory and subsistence polyculture. To be sustainable, this method requires that the cultivated areas have a resting period long enough to allow the natural recovery of the fertility of the soils. Given the pressure for areas available for production in densely populated reserves, these rest periods are not fulfilled, which compromises the sustainability of this traditional productive system and therefore the food sovereignty of the population of these communities. Few studies have addressed the causes and how the process of overexploitation of the soils occurs in indigenous communities of Latin America, as well as the alternatives that these communities develop to address this problem. The objective of this work was to document and evaluate the characteristics of the system of chagra and the state of the soils in a Ticuna community of the Colombian Amazon with high population density and high pressure on the soil for agricultural production. It was found that the production areas within the shelter have been developed and the soils have nutritional deficiencies to develop the areas of agriculture.
