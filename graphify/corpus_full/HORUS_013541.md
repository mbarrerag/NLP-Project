# Self-medication in oral health in Bogota: ethnographic study

Self-medication in oral health is a practice censored by biomedicine; however, it is a frequent activity that synthesizes the interaction of people with different models of health care, where the structural and symbolic fabric of the health field is materialized. The objective of this study was to understand the knowledge and practices about oral health self-medication in Bogotá. qualitative research with ethnographic approach. Tools such as participant observation, ethnographic interview and field diary were used. Seven families from Bogotá were chosen from an ideal typological sample. There are barriers to access to dental care that are increased through segregation mechanisms that are related to the urban structure of the city, barriers that motivate the use of various medications against ailments. The mouth has different meanings. Caries and its effects are recognized, and silks, pastes, rinses and brushes are used in its prevention, elements that come from the pharmaceutical industry. The most common condition is odontalgia and to treat it mainly analgesics of pharmaceutical origin are used. Self-medication in oral health is a conscious process that subjects perform from their own reasoning and resources, in order to prevent and treat their ailments. This practice is related to social representations about the mouth, with access to dental care services, and with the provision and obtaining of therapeutic supplies
