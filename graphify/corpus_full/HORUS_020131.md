# Optimization of the structural design of asphalt floors for streets and highways

The construction of asphalt pavements on streets and roads is an activity that requires the optimization of the consumption of large economic and natural resources. Optimization of the design of pavements meets contradictory objectives according to the availability of resources and the needs of users. This work explores the use of metaheuristics to optimize the design of asphalt pavements using incremental design based on prediction of deterioration and vehicle operation costs (VOC). The costs are proportional to the energy consumption and of resources and polluting emissions. The evolution of the design of asphalt pavements and the development of metaheuristic optimization techniques in this topic was reviewed. Four computer programs were developed: (1) UNLEA, program for structural analysis of multilayer systems. (2) PSO-UNLEA, program that employs metaheuristic optimization with particle swarming (PSO) optimization should be reviewed for the inverse calculation of pavement modules. (3) UNSOPAVE, program of incremental design of pavements based on the equations of the US MEPDG, and calculation of costs of the construction and vehicle operation based on the IRI.
