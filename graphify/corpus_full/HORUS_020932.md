# Relationship, distribution, taxonomy of tick species associated with wild mammals in endemic areas of rickettsiosis in Colombia. A closeness to understanding the vector-path-reservor relationship

The study of the diversity of invertebrates and their association to infectious processes as vectors, is a tool that allows to define the potential ecological relations and cycles of life of the infectious agents in the areas where they circulate. Approaches focused on the distribution, diversity and impact of pathogens transmitted by ticks in human and animal health have been made. Little studied the microbiome of ticks despite the effect that it has in several aspects including the transmission of pathogens. In the cycle of ticks and microorganisms, wild animals play a key role in the cycle of diseases transmitted by ticks as they serve as hosts and reservoirs. Various pathogens, associated with ticks, have been detected in Colombia as is the case of Rickettsia spp, related to fatal human organisms. Although some of the host organisms (wild mammals) have been recognized, the current knowledge is incipient and requires a deeper approach, to expand, complete and better understand the associated relationships between patients and diseases, diseases, diseases not pathogenic-micropathological
