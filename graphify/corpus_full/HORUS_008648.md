# ERRORS OF COGNITIVE CONTROL IN THE ALTERNATIVE OF IMPLICATE HOSTILE ACTIVITIES

To interpret biasedly neutral situations as potentially hostile can facilitate aggression. Research suggests that biased interpretations occur automatically and implicitly. This research seeks to establish whether training in executive functions reduces implicit hostile powers. Executive functions describe processes associated with control, planning and allocation of attentional resources to perform actions aimed at goals. A sample of ninety-eight university students was randomly distributed to two groups ex-posted to modified versions of the Implicit Associations Test (IAT), to assess levels of automatic hostile powers. The experimental group was trained through the Stroop Task, while the control group was exposed to a neutral task. Although the results did not confirm the working hypothesis, the results show that the IAT can be effectively adapted to measure aggressive implicit attitudes. Overall, this methodology can contribute to elucidate the influence of social factors versus cognitive factors in the generation of violence
