# GEOGRAPHIC ACCESS BARRIERS TO ORAL HEALTH SERVICES IN COLOMBIA

To analyze the number and distribution of dentists and oral health institutions on whether their location is considered as a geographic access barrier in a Colombian Department Methods: Mixed cross-sectional study carried out in 47 municipalities of a Colombian Department. Secondary Departmental and national sources were employed for the inventory of human resources and health institutions. Geographic access barriers (according to the WHO) were considered as if the travel time to the top level of complexity exceeded 4 hours. The average travel time and speed of public transport from the four most remote municipalities to the capital of the Department were logged, activity supplemented with participative ethnographic observation. The calculation of the travel time from the 47 municipalities to the capital was made using the average speed obtained, and by means of an official map that notified the distance and condition of roads (paved or unpaved kilometers) Results: The low level of complexity is offered in all the municipalities, medium complexity in 6 and high level of complexity only in the capital city; the number of dentists according to international references does not correspond to the population to be served, since 31 municipalities display a deficit of 4-10 dentists. The average speed registered in these trips was 52 Km/hour and 17 Km/hour in paved and unpaved roads, respectively. When applying this travel time model to all municipalities, assuming similar topographic and climatic conditions, there are geographic access barriers to oral health for 6 out of the 47 municipalities. From the qualitative component we find cultural and institutional barriers related to the insurance and health services provision system in Colombia. In addition to the deficit of dentists and the geographic access barriers, other institutional and cultural barriers emerged that could deepen the inequities in full access to the right to health in Colombia
