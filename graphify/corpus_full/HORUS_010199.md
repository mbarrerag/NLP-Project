# INFLUENCE OF MULTIPLE DREDGING EVENTS ON FISH ASSEMBLAGE IN A TROPICAL ESTUARY

The multiple dredging events have cumulative and synergistic impacts, which generate a long-term decrease in diversity. In this study, the periods before, during and after the dredging events of the years 2015, 2017 and 2020 in the Bay of Buenaventura, Colombian Pacific, were analyzed. The density and composition of the fish assemblage was analyzed by monitoring using an artisan trawling net, common in the area. There was a significant change in the assemblage of species in each of the dredging periods, which was explained by a high dissimilarity in the composition of fish species before, during and after each dredging event, where there was an effect dredging variable on the density of the species associated with the bottom. Although it was found that during the 2015 and 2017 dredging, and before the 2020 dredging, the densities of the catfish Cathorops multiradiatus, the sole Citharichthys gilberti and Achirus klunzingeri, and the croaker, Stellifer typicus, increased after the 2020 dredging, there was a decrease in density and the disappearance of 36 species, of which ~50% are important for the food security of coastal communities. Additionally, there was a decrease in richness and diversity from 56 species (before the 2015 dredging) to 20 species (after the 2020 dredging). The results were consistent with studies of the effects of dredging on biota in tropical and temperate estuaries. This research suggests a decline in species assemblage in a downward spiral, which represented the loss of ecosystem resilience after multiple dredging
