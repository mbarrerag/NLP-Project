# EDMOND JABES AND JAMES TURRELL, POETICS OF DESIGN

The desert, a privileged place of a word that does not take place, like that of the Egyptian writer Edmond Jabès. A privileged place of light without measure, like the one that has treasured for decades, the American artist James Turrell. That non-place, apart from its power of poetic and artistic capture, constitutes for those who frequent it or dwell it an arduous experience of time and space, of the body and of self-consciousness. The desert is enveloping: it bathes with its blinding and raw light; it dispenses the color always equal and almost tangible; it embraces with its silent emptiness; it shows the vitality of the wind; it confronts, in an abyss of mirrors, the weight of the sky and the thirst of the earth. The desert fascose and claims a category that concerns our relationship with the world and with others, but that it is not usually of frequent use, the category of place, that spatial segment that attracts and retains our attention
