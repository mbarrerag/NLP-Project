# Making hands with the land: Curatorial proposals from indigenous material practices to inhabit the Earth

This doctoral research explores the material practices developed together with indigenous groups of the Orinoquia and the Colombian Amazon, analyzing how their ways of doing shape ways of inhabiting the planet that emerge from, and at the same time enable, relationships between human and non-human.From an interdisciplinary approach that integrates art, anthropology and environmental studies, and through multi-situated participatory research-action methodologies that include the making, the travelling and the care-taking, the the thesis addresses the material processes as dynamic correspondences between human and their environment. Practices such as the fabric with the palm of moriche, the ceramics in roll and the manufacture of traps reveal relationships of interdependence between bodies, territories and materials, revealing dynamics that transcend the conventional categories of art and technology, while highlighting regenerative material approaches. Likewise, the fundamental role of indigenous women in the inter-generational continuity and in the transformation of these practices for the sustainability of life, where aesthetic capacities and forms of survival converge. In this intersection between the daily and aesthetic systems, the healing, enriched by the ethnography, is presented as a critical space for the social implications and vibilization.
