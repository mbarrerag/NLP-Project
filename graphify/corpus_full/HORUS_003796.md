# EXTENDED BRAUER¿S ANALYSIS OF SOME BRAUER CONFIGURATION ALGEBRAS AND ITS USE TO ANALYZE TOPOLOGICAL INFORMATION CONTENT OF BACH¿S CANONS

This talk deals with interactions between the theory of representation of algebras and topological content information. We recall that Rashevsky and Trucco [1, 2] introduced the concept of graph entropy to measure the structural complexity of several graph invariants. It is worth noting that the construction of entropy-based measures was originally called the topological information content of a graph. We also remember that Adler et al. introduced the notion of topological entropy [3]. On the other hand, Green and Schroll introduced Brauer graph algebras and Brauer configuration algebras to investigate algebras of tame and wild representation types. Whereas Ca'nadas et al. introduced the notion of extended Brauer to analysis to study data associated with the configurations defining such algebras, the quantitive analysis of the algebraic invariants of such algebras along with the analysis of the topological content information of their covering graphs is said to be an extended Brauer's analysis which is analogous of the topological data analysis [4]. In this talk, we use Brauer's extended analysis to analyze networks induced by note transitions in musical compositions [5]. The analysis is applied to some of the canons proposed by Bach in his Musical Offering (BWV 1079) [6]. Due to such procedures, it is noted that Bach's canons give rise to undirected networks with determined entropy and geometrical shapes.
