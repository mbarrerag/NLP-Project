# The role of care in rights-claiming processes: the case of women in the human settlement La Libertad (Yopal) in the search for housing (2016 and 2023)

The phenomenon of human settlements is the reflection of the existence of populations in conditions of extreme poverty or marginalization, whose rights and basic needs have not been covered. Those who create them, are usually families or persons displaced by the internal armed conflict, migrants or other groups in conditions of vulnerability. Therefore, their analysis and attention from public policies must be addressed from a human rights perspective. The settlements present _x0093_inadequate conditions of health and high rates of insecurity in terms of social encounter and intolerance of variety of cultures gathered in those places where they self-construct their housing without the minimum guarantee of quality of life_x0094_ (Romero, 2019, p. 58). In the case of human settlement, the situations described above materialize in the dispute that exists for housing, between institutionality and the community. This process has triggered constant evictions by the public force and unworthy living conditions for their inhabitants, recrudeción de los traficas, lack of public and social services, among others. In the framework of the formation of these human settlements, we take the special role of social claims in the housing. _x0094_ in order to _x0093_ emphasize the diversity, plurality and complexity of these works, from an open feminist perspective_x0094_(Arango, 2011, p.15) Additionally, the selection of this place allows us to question the tendency of academic research to focus on the country's large cities, leaving behind peripheral territories and alternative knowledge. Our research also addresses the analysis of a territory that is poorly analyzed (in which La Libertad is located) from its cultural and geographical context, to ways of inhabiting it. For this, we carry out qualitative empirical research whose methodological design combines rapid participatory diagnosis and normative analysis. Our analysis of empirical information collected combines elements from legal sociology with a feminist and intersectual perspective. Our research contributes to the analysis and characterization of the three working hours and the way in which care is related to the processes of exigibility of rights in the lives of these women. This triple day is often invisible.
