# Nonbreeding ecology of the Blackpoll Warbler (Sethophaga striata) in Colombia

The cross-border reinita (Setophaga striata) is a migratory bird Neartica-Neotropical that is rapidly declining, that reproduces in the northern forest of North America and migrates to South America during its non-reproductive period. Similar to other migratory species, different factors contribute to the decrease of their populations, such as the loss of habitat in non-reproductive sites. Currently, information on the ecology of S. striata in non-reproductive sites is scarce, there is no demographic information, nor use for different habitats in non-reproductive sites. In this study, we want to contribute to the knowledge of the ecological niche and non-reproductive ecology of S. striata in Colombia through the following questions: 1) How do the occupancy rates of S. striata at regional and local scale vary through the non-reproductive range in Colombia? 2) What are the characteristics of the vegetation (structure of the territorial habitat at fine scale) associated with the occupation of S. striata in agroforestal systems (citric, cocoa and silpastory and forest 3)? What quality of the War (extrico vs rural) rounds is scarce and no demographic information for different non-breeding habitats exist to date. In this study, we will contribute to the knowledge of the ecological niche and non-breeding ecology of the Blackpoll Warbler in Colombia, by addressing the following questions: 1) How do you October rates and abound vary at multiple scales (i.e., regional, landscape, and local) across the Blackpoll Warblers over-wintering range in Colombia?, 2) How does space use and home range size of Blackpoll Warblers range across different scales?, 3) Do urban or rural landscape represent habitats of higher quality for Blackpoll Warblers based on estimates of apparent survival in the Orinoco region?
