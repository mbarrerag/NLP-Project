# URRAL TERRITORIES IN COLOMBIA: DIVERSITY AND CONFLICTIVITY

In this document, we assume that today's rural Colombia presents a high heterogeneity, a diversity of situations and territorial configurations and that it cannot be held forever to an idea of "retardation." The countryside cannot be reduced to being the opposite of the city. "The countryside" refers first of all to a great diversity of rural territories, built over time from various processes and types of social relations. In a first approach to rural territories, it is emphasized that, as they were populating new territories, human groups have appropriated natural goods and built a way of life, in which the population density, consumption habits and uses of the land depend closely on the capacity and vocation of this one. Regardless of the fate of the produced, of their dominant orientation towards the market or to the self-consumption, the rural territories are characterized by low population and exchange densities, characteristics that clearly differentiate them from urban spaces. Thus, rural territories are essentially productive, when the urban spaces are more diversified in their functions and oriented to organize the commercial and consumption functions. This chapter proposes an approach to the rural areas, which clearly differentiates from their urban areas
