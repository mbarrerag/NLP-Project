# Human exposure to air pollution in the Abura Valley

This thesis analyses the variability of nine monitored air quality variables in the SIATA air quality network and assesses the potential dose of these substances for five model profiles of people under four levels of physical activity. The results of the analysis of air quality (a) show that the most critical sites are at the base of the valley and are highly contaminated especially by particulate matter and nitrogen oxides exceeding the limits suggested by the World Health Organisation (WHO) in a relative quantity of about 200 %; (b) ozone does not exceed the thresholds suggested by the WHO at daily or annual scale; (c) carbon monoxide concentrations are high at the few sites where it is monitored; (d) sulphur dioxide registers have very low spatial coverage. scale; (c) carbon monoxide concentration are high in the few places where are monitored; (d) sulfur dioxide records have very low spatial coverage. The analysis of the variability of potential doses for profiles and activity levels shown that the high doses are received by children 10 years old doing moderate-to-intense activity and adults doingintense activity. The diariol cycle of concentration has peaks at different times for different substances. These patterns of variability should be considered in the planning of activities to minimize the exposure of the population a high dose of these substances
