# nutrient removal capacity in swine wastewater by Colombian high mountain Cyanobacteria

The use of microalgae has been proposed as promising alternatives for the treatment of waste water at low costs, together with the production of oxygen and reduction of carbon footprints, we can obtain products of added value in the process from algae. The aim is to evaluate the removal of nitrates, phosphates and organic matter dissolved in synthetic residual water for the first time with high mountain cyanobacteria from Colombia. The capacity to remove at laboratory level with synthetic waste water and at reactor scale with residual waters of porcineza. The Colombian sweet-acoustic cyanobacterias that were used correspond to the order of the Synchoccocal microalgae and were deposited in the collection of the algae culture laboratory of the National University of Colombia under codes LAUN55, LAUN69 and LAUN71. The bioremediation capacity of the cytobic bacteria was compared to the highest levels of 15,4 mg/Lg and was reduced to 75% of the total number of organic matter
