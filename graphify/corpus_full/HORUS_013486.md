# Experts, Missions and State Knowledge: Harvard, Ford Foundation and Planning in Colombia 1963-1970

In 1963, a mission of economic advice from the Harvard Institute for Development, with funding from the FORD Foundation, arrived in Colombia to modernize the national planning system. The team arrived in the midst of a feeling of confidence and optimism, thanks to the direct support of the Ministry of Finance and the Directorate of the National Planning Department. However, within a few months of starting its work in Colombia, international experts encountered all kinds of problems, not only the instability of the Colombian government, but also the negligence, sometimes intentional and sometimes not, of the local bureaucratic body. This situation remained until the arrival in power of Carlos Lleras Retrepo, who committed himself from the beginning of his government, with an administrative reform of the Colombian State. These modifications included planning, allowing the recommendations formulated by the Harvard/FORD Mission to materialize in 1968. This document seeks to explore the difficulties experienced by the Harvard team during all these years, paying particular attention to the shock experienced by the American experts regarding their expectations of influence, and the real difficulties experienced in the daily practice with the local officials and the instability of the country.
