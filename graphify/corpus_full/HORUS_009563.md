# Effect of the maturity stage, genotype, and geographical location on the physicochemical characteristics of the cocoa bean during fermentation

Abstract An important aspect to compete in the world market is the quality of the cocoa bean, which is influenced by different factors ranging from genetics to post-harvest processes. The present research aimed to determine the effect of the state of maturity (stage 2, 3 and 4), the genotype (CCN51, ICS95 and TSH565) and geographical location (Algeciras and Garzón, Colombia) of cocoa on the pH, titratable acidity of the pulp and the grain and fermentation index of the grain during days 0, 3 and 7 of the fermentation. The response variables were analyzed through ANOVA variance analysis, multiple comparisons were performed by means of the Tukey test with a significance level α= 0.05. Additionally, a longitudinal analysis was performed since there are measurements over time and there may be autocorrelation of individuals over time, which allows contrasting the results by means of another analysis. The results showed that the factor with the greatest influence on the physicochemical characteristics evaluated was the genotype, which affects the pH of the grain and pulp and the fermentation index. Likewise, it could be evidenced that, of the three genotypes evaluated, CCN51 stood out for having less grain acidity, higher grain pH and fermentation index. Of the factors evaluated, the one with the greatest effect during cocoa fermentation is the genotype. The above allows to establish a solution to the heterogeneity of grain quality, the main problem of international marketing
