# Evaluation of cell viability in a biomaterial for bone regeneration with biobased components obtained from fish farming residues.

The demand for solutions for the repair of bone defects has grown significantly in recent years. Factors such as the increase in life expectancy, the higher prevalence of disability related to movement, bone trauma and changes in the population's life habits have led to the advancement of research into treatments that reduce morbidity related to defects in bone tissue. Among the most recent developments is the objective of achieving biochemical implants that promote the regeneration of bone tissue without leaving sequelae in the body that receives them. To do this, these biomaterials must have biological and physical properties that generate responses that correspond to the needs of the tissues to be repaired in the affected population. In this research, it is proposed to evaluate the cellular viability of a biomaterial for bone regeneration composed mainly of collagen and biobased hydroxyapatite, obtained from the residues of the benefit of tilapia
