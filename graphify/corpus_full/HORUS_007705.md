# _x0093_SAMPLING AND COSECHAR ANIMAL_x0094_, ANALYSIS OF THE ARCHEOZOOOLOGICAL DATA OBTAINED IN THE SULN LOSSES, THROUGH THE ETNOGRAPH AND ETNOHYSTORY OF THE COLOMBIAN AMOZONIC NOROCCIDENT.

The archaeological works carried out in the Cerro Azul site in the department of Guaviare have provided a large number of paleoecological evidence on the past of human activities, in direct relation to the natural environments of the Tropical Humid Forest of the Amazonian Noroccident for more than 12,000 years AP. These data, among which hundreds of thousands of Faunistic evidences were recovered, became the first data for the Colombian Amazon that demonstrate a complexity of relationships and interactions between human groups, ecosystems and their natural resources, in particular animals. However, the characteristics of this archeoo-faunistic material are a challenge to understand those relations between culture and nature that have been widely debated in other contexts. Therefore, the possibility of investigating in key concepts such as the domestication of animals, cultural forest and historical ecology, will allow dialogue with ethnography and ethnohistory, to advance in the historical and cultural understanding of human groups and animals, from a highly biodiverse region and with ecological and cultural complexities, where the understanding of this, can contribute to the interaction of the fadistic resources
