# Yam (Dioscorea spp.) cultivation and landraces with market potential in South America

Yams have been part of the culture of South American peoples for millennia. Lowland indigenous groups, descendants of African slaves, and immigrants from Europe and Asia have all cultivated Dioscorea spp., contributing to a rich continental agrobiodiversity. A high percentage of yams cultivated in South America are maintained by traditional farmers, who in gardens and backyards conserve varieties that are interesting for their uses and consumption. Because yams are also part of the so-called neglected and underutilized species, in many cases associated with subsistence agriculture, the production level in the continent is low compared to other regions of the world. Their consumption is also predominantly localized to certain regions, where they can be found in fairs and local markets. In this chapter, we will explore the current production of yams in South America, the main species used, the agriculture systems in which they are grown, the barriers that hinder their greater acceptance in the market, market prospects, and finally, their different uses in gastronomy
