# TRANSPIRATION OF WATER VEGETATION

Evapotranspiration is the most complex component of the water balance since it is not possible to measure it directly, so it is necessary to use other ways of estimating it. One of the methods widely used to calculate plant perspiration is using heat as a tracer of the flow rate of the sap into living plants. In this work the method of heat proportions, mainly used in woody species, was adapted to measure speeds of sap flows in frailegones, since it generates a high level of detail for low flows and even for inverse flows. Although measurements are made at plant level, it is possible to extrapolate the results at the level of the stand or ecosystem, considering the densities and the predominance of the species. The results obtained indicate that the frailedones reserve a large amount of water in their marrow, thus not dependent on the water available in the soil to transpire. It is estimated that an adult plant on a sunny day moves about 1.6 liters of water per day, considering the day of 24 hours, since they are also active during the night, recharging the reserves in their tissues
