# COMPARISON OF DORSAL GRASE AND OF THE LONGISSIMUS DORSII MUSCLE IN THREE POBLATIONS OF POCINES COLOMBIAN CRILOLS

The in vivo measurements of parameters of the pig carcass composition, such as the thickness of the dorsal fat (EGD) and the width of the Longissimus dorsii muscle (LGD), allow predicting the yield and the meat quality in the carcass and, with this, it presents an indirect selection of the breeding. Most of these works have been performed in slaughtered animals. Evaluating these parameters on live animal would be of great utility in the sector, and more in the pig cryollo, little researched in terms of yield and quality. As a contribution to this, the objective of this work was to evaluate in ¿vivo, using ultrasonographic techniques, EGD and LGD in 3 different Creole populations: traspatio (n=6; 3 males and 3 females), Casco de Mula (n=21; 14 males and 7 females) and Sampedreno (n=9; 2 males and 7 females) from 2 to 17,5 months old (n=36), bred in traditional holdings of different municipalities of the Meta (Colombia). From each animal was obtained between 2 and 3 males (EG and 7 females) and left (n= 2 years). well adapted, with high characteristics of rusticity, and being able to be fed with alternative sources to the use of balanced food, represents a great business option for rural areas of the country as an alternative to intensive pig production
