# SYNTHESIS, CHARACTERIZATION AND PRELIMINARY CYTOTOXICITY TESTS OF PALLADIUM(II) COMPLEXES WITH SCHIFF BASE LIGANDS

The Pd(II) complexes were studied as biologically active substances and have showed promising results in anticancer assays. This research focused on analyzing the cytotoxic properties of a series of new Pd(II) complexes with hydrazide-hydrazone ligands. General method for the synthesis of the compounds: by the reaction in methanolic solution between aldehydes derived from 1,3-disubstituted pyrazole and benzohydrazide in a 1:1 molar ratio, a series of hydrazide-hydrazone ligands (HH1, HH2 and HH3) were obtained with moderate yields. The reaction of each of these ligands with PdCl2 in a 2:1 molar ratio in methanol yielded their respective complexes (Scheme 1.). The compounds were characterized by elemental analysis, IR and Raman spectroscopy. Two absorption bands were observed in the IR spectra at 1641 cm-1 and at 1537 cm-1, attributed to the stretching frequencies ¿(C=Ohydrazide-hydrazone) and ¿(C=Nhydrazide-hydrazone), respectively. In the complexes, these bands were shifted to 1616 cm-1 and 1502 cm-1, respectively. These results suggest that the carbonyl and azomethine groups of the ligands participate in the coordination with Pd(II) ions. The Pd-O and Pd-N interactions were confirmed by Raman spectra, with bands at 409 cm-1 ¿(Pd-O) and 254 cm-1 ¿(Pd-N). Scheme 1. Synthesis of the Pd(II) complexes with hydrazide-hydrazone. In this work, the cytotoxic effect of the hydrazide-hydrazone ligands and their respective Pd(II) complexes was evaluated. The cytotoxic effect of these compounds on human prostate cancer (PC3), human brain glioblastoma astrocytoma (U87MG), and human lung carcinoma (A549) cell lines was determined using a methylthiazoltetrazolium (MTT) assay. The results obtained showed that the A549 and PC3 cells were more sensitive to treatment with the Pd(II) complexes than with the free ligands when their activity was compared. The experimental data suggest that metal complexes with hydrazide-hydrazone ligands containing the pyrazole heterocyclic represent a potential pharmacological prototype for the treatment of cancer cells
