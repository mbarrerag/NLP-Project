# Five: A Latin American project (1951-1972)

The Inter-American Center for Housing and Planning (five), founded in 1951 as a Pan-American program of technical assistance of the OAS, was an institutional alternative designed from the science of housing to address the urban and rural housing deficit. The development of this initiative exposed a complex political and social panorama that ended up turning the Center into a project of Latin American regional integration, thanks to a great network of academic, institutional, professional and community relations. This work aims to build an overview of the role played by the five in the planning of social housing in Latin America in the mid-century xx. In this sense, six analytical approaches are presented that allow understanding the relationship between housing and planning as an institutional background, the activities of the Center as an educational project, the formation of interprofessional networks, planning as a tool of development in Latin America, housing and rural extension, and technical experiments. Accordingly, it showed how social sciences became a mechanism of institutional decisions, considering urban and rural sociology as the way to find housing solutions through community participation.
