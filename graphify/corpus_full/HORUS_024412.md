# Analysis of decision-making guided affectively in a modified version of the Iowa random task

Traditionally it has been considered that the human being makes decisions primarily rational, however, contemporary studies in psychology indicate that the emotions influence implicitly on our choices, especially in situations of uncertainty. The Somatic Marker Hypothesis is a theory that explains the psychological mechanisms by which the emotions influence our choices. From this perspective, the physiological states underlying the emotions mark our choices as advantageous or disadvantageous depending on the rewards expected to be obtained. The somatic markers involved in decision making have been studied mainly by means of the Azar Task of Iowa (IGT). However, there appear to be several inconsistencies in the results of different experiments carried out with the same task. The literature suggests that there are methodological failures in the IGT that question the traditional explanations about the mechanisms by which the somatic markers influence the decisions.
