# Can spending on research and development decrease greenhouse gas emissions?

This paper seeks to explore the question of whether investment in research and development (R&D) can reduce greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions. To answer the question, we explored the relationships between four variables: R&D expenditure, GDP, GHG emissions and energy consumption by using a novel methodology of local projections with a longitudinal database for 34 countries from 1990 to 2017. The main results show that R&D investment has negative effects on GHG emissions in the early years, but then the process reverses and generates a positive effect after 10 years. This shows that there is a rebound effect in which the innovations generated by this investment can reduce GHG emissions but then incentivize higher consumption and economic growth that results in higher emissions. Thus, the results illustrate the Jevons Paradox
