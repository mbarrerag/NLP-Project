# Factors influencing body size in populations of Glossophaga longirostris Miller, 1898 (Chiroptera: Phyllostomidae)

The family of bats Phyllostomidae has experienced an increase in its diversity thanks to morphological studies and molecular techniques, and the genus Glossophaga has not been the exception. Currently nine species within this genus are recognized, distributed in Central and South America, being Glossophaga longirostris the species of greatest size in most cranial measures. For this species six subspecies are recognized, differentiated mainly by their body size. In Colombia, three of them are present: G. l. major, G. l. longirostris and G. l. reclusa, although there are few records in collections and studies. In work, the possible factors that influence the morphological variation of Glossophaga longirostris, as well as the systematic and distribution of the three subspecies present in Colombia, using three lines of evidence: morphological, molecular and distribution models. In the morphological evidence was based on the analysis of nine cranial measures of 103 individuals, using GlaMANOVA and a discriminating analysis of the morphological differentiation of the groups. The genetic distances between the clades were small, suggesting that the sequences analyzed belong to the same evolutionary lineage (species). However, the analysis of population differentiation (FST) showed significant differences between the OCTs, indicating the existence of differentiated populations that could be considered Independent Management Units. The current MD revealed differential distribution areas for each group. However, the analysis for the Ultimate Glacial Maximus suggested a connection between the projected ranges. Based on these evidences, it is concluded that Glosophaga longirostris in Colombia is represented by a single evolutionary lineage, whose populations present a morphological variation that could be the result of the current geographical isolation.
