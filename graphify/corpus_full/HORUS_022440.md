# SMALL REPLIES IN SOLLOW SOLVES ASSOCIATED TO THE RULE OF SALICILIC ACID IN KEY INTERACTION (Dianthus caryophyllus, caryophyllaceae)- FOD (Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. dianthi)

The vascular wilting caused by Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. dianthi (Fod) is the disease that most affects carnation cultivation. Understanding the nature of the interaction between the plant and the pathogen will allow the future development of new alternatives to control the disease. That is why it is sought to have experimental evidence that allows understanding the role of the signaling path of salicylic acid (SA) and enzymes associated with the resistance of the plant such as phenylalanine ammonium lyase (PAL), polyphenoloxidase (PFO), guayacol peroxidase (GPX) and phospholipase D (PLD), at the level of the sylplastic of the stem during the interaction with it pathogen. An in vivo trial was established using two varieties of carnation with different levels of resistance to the disease and was determined in symplasmous of the stem SA, MeSA (methyl salicyllate) and enzymes studied.
