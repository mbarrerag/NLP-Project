# Complementation and characterization of a Mycobacterium mutant tuberculosis defective in the ctpH gene, a P2-type membrane ATPase involved in the transport of Ca2+

In recent years the P-type ATPases of Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb) have been considered interesting attenuation targets for the rational design of attenuated mutants with vaccine potential against tuberculosis (TB). Among them, CtpH a P-type ATPase transporter of Ca2+ is highlighted for its role for the survival of Mtb in the face of stress conditions during the infection processes. In a previous work carried out in our research group Biochemistry and Molecular Biology of Mycobacteria (BBMM), it was determined that the deletion of this ATPase decreases the ability of the tuberculosis bacillus to face adverse environments such as toxic concentrations of Ca2+ and Na+, as well as the presence of oxidative and nitrosative stress generating species. Additionally, it was demonstrated by analysis of gene expression that ctpH would be fundamental to support or compensate the activity of other Ca2+ ATPases of Mtb.
