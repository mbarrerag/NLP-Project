# Impact of microbiome and climate variability on habitat behavior and preference in Aedes aegypti mosquitoes in three areas of epidemiological importance in Colombia

This project aims to contribute to the fundamental knowledge of the problem of control of arbovirus vectors, focusing the proposal in the study of the behavioral response of populations of the Aedes aegypti mosquito versus the preference of temperature within the context of climate change. For the approach of control of vectors, the aim is to study the relationship between the microbiome and the relation to the temperature profiles to obtain information that allow predict future scenarios of risk of vector transmission. The progressive increase of dengue cases in the departments of Amazonas, Antioquia and Cesar during the years 2021 to 2023 with a number of cases reported from 316 to 1023, 1329 to 5689 and 2192 to 5195, respectively, evidence a growing problem in public health in Colombia, associated with the climatic variability, migration patterns and expansion of the agricultural border. Thus, the project seeks not only to increase knowledge about microbial biodiversity in the vectors of Amazonas, Medellin and La Paz, but also a broader view about the influence of abiotic variables and their relation to the microbiome of the main vectors of arbovirus (dengue, Zika, chikungunuña, yellow fever) in Colombia; this could be fundamental to the design of new strategies for control of ETVs
