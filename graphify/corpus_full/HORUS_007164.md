# Evaluation of new technologies for the study of gene function in Pseudocercospora fijiensis with potential for the management of Black Sigatoka in banana cultivation.

This study evaluated the use of the technology RNAi and CRISPR-Cas9 to improve the management of the Black Sigatoka in bananas. Short-chained RNA sequences (siRNA) were synthesized that silenced the genes PfFus3, PfAC and PfCYP51 and were applied in ascopores and mycellial fragments. Long-chain RNA sequences (double-stranded RNA, dsRNA) were also synthesized and evaluated in the same fungal materials in in vitro conditions. The sequences of dsRNA with better in vitro inhibition and siRNA sequences were applied in susceptible banana plants, encapsulated in double laminar hydroxides (layered hydroxy hydroxy, LDH) and without nanoencapsular, and were used in the assessment of their protective effect against disease. In addition, the use of CRISPR-Cas was investigated for the editing of genes in P. pegensis, using the results of the NDL, LDH, and NBDL. The results of the study may be due to a partial edition in which only some nuclei of the fungus were edited. This study suggests that the RNAi and CRISPR-Cas9 were found to be in part successful, no modifications in the genomic sequence were detected by sequencing; however, changes in the levels of gene expression were observed. This result could be due to a partial edition in which only some nuclei of the fungus were edited. This study suggests that the RNAi and the CRISPR-Cas9 were not detected as tools for the management and study of P. fijiensis.
