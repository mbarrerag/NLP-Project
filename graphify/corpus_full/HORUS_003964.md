# MECHANICAL PERFORMANCE OF PORTLAND CEMENT MORTARS ADDED WITH RIGID POLYURETHANE FOAM WASTE

Rigid Polyurethane foam (PUR) is obtained from an exothermic chemical reaction between a polyester diol and a diisocyanate; it was developed in 1937 by Otto Bayer [1]. PUR has multiple uses such as insulation in walls, ceilings and home appliances [2]. The most relevant properties of PUR are low thermal conductivity, water diffusion resistance, weather resistance, chemical resistance and dimensional stability. Due to these properties, the use of PUR has grown significantly over the years [3], with the consequent generation of solid waste into the environment. For this reason, it is important to develop research for the use or recycling of waste of this material. In the present research, the mechanical performance of Portland cement mortars added with PUR as a replacement for sand in proportions of 0%, 30%, 40%, 50% and 60% was evaluated. Four PUR wastes generated in different companies, were characterized through chemical composition (X Ray Fluorescence), density (ASTM D-1622) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) tests. The mortars were evaluated through absorption, porosity, compressive strength (ASTM C109) and flexural strength (ASTM C293) tests; additionally SEM were applied. The results showed that PURs are composed of C, H and N, and in smaller proportions contain chlorine, silicon, calcium, iron. Apparent densities between 35.35 kg/m3 to 37.01 kg/m3 were reported, while for the PUR4 foam the value obtained increased to 78.64 kg/m3. The latter is due to the presence of additives in the material. On the other hand, the mortars added with PUR reported higher water absorption compared to the control samples (0% PUR). These results are similar to authors such as [4]. The results of the flexural and compression strength in the mortars with PUR showed an increase in both properties with curing time; but slightly inferior compared to the control sample. According to the results obtained, it can be concluded that the reuse of PUR waste in construction materials is feasible, which has positive effects on the environment
