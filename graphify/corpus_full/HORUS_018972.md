# Multimodal writing in the science classroom as a tool for learning the disease phenomenon.

Some of the difficulties of teaching natural sciences that have been detected in the last decades, are the high lexical content, the appropriation of a specific vocabulary and the ignorance of the rhetorical traits of the language of the school science. The above, has placed them as the focus of attention of some researches on the importance of introducing language to the classroom as a strategy of teaching learning of the Natural Sciences. Although many of these studies have focused on reading as a way of improving the understanding of the students, only recently, is it being given relevance to the writing, especially that related to the different modes of representation of a content, that is, the multimodal writing. The latter, has begun to call attention precisely, because the written writing, is no longer the focus of representation in the learning materials, its place is taking the fixed and moving images of the disease COVD-19, the main way of understanding. So, this research aims to document the importance of the multimodal writing in the classroom, through the answer to the following question: How the production of the multimodal texts of the physical disease from the eighth disease from the university, 19 years, The design of the activities was based on Sanmarti (2002). As a result, a sequence was obtained divided into four sessions, subdivided into initiation, promotion, synthesis and application activities. Each of the slogans was justified from the use of multimodal writing and language skills associated with the teaching of school science. The test data was analyzed with the Hake statistician to corroborate the gain in understanding and its relation to the level of performance obtained by Hake. To organize and facilitate the analysis of the data collected from multimodal productions and the interviewing of students, Atlas.ti® software was used, in order to construct a grounded theory based on Strauss & Corbin (2002). The above, it was possible to answer the problematizing question, based on four naturalistic generalizations that showed improvement in the understanding of texts related to the COVID-19 disease phenomenon, being related to the teaching-learning strategy combined with current and updated learning
