# Assessment of the degradation capacity of mass-use insecticides in Colombia by bacterial strains from the intestine of insects of agronomic interest and in public health

The search for new environments that host microorganisms capable of degrading recalcitrant molecules is continuously necessary in bioremediation and environmental recovery processes. Various studies have reported that intestinal microbiota participates in vital functions for the host such as nutrition, defense, vector capacity and development; they are additionally capable of degrading toxic substances. In Colombia, the main control strategies include the use of chemical synthesis insecticides, which cause a negative impact on the environment and health; other strategies such as biological control and transgenic crops have been implemented; however, because the development of mechanisms of resistance or tolerance to such practices has been observed, the implementation of new strategies for integrated management. Insects share a long history of association with bacteria, and there are several studies that demonstrate that the intestinal microbiota of these have been involved in the degradation of natural and synthetic organic molecules, including insecticides in this list. The purpose of this proposal is to evaluate whether bacterial strains from the intestines of insects vectors of tropical diseases and insects plague of crops, challenged in the field with high concentrations of insecticides and synthetics (insects in this list)
