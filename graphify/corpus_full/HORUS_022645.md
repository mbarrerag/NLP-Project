# SELECTION OF INTERACTIONS: FUNDAMENT FOR THE FORMING OF A BIOLOGICAL INDIVIDUALS DEFINITION

The concept of biological individual is central in evolutionary theory. The classic argument is that these are the units on which natural selection acts. Biological individuals can be molecular aggregates, organisms, groups that cooperate and symbiotes, among others through the different levels of the natural hierarchy. Among the features that characterize these individuals are: having a discrete origin and final, spatial location, and exhibit emerging properties that must be inherited; they must also reproduce in a sexual, asexual, or through an analogous mechanism; interact with their environment as a coherent whole and their populations change over time. Identity results from the set of interactions between their constituent parts and those that establish with their environment. Given the contingency of a large number of factors that intervene each individual has an exclusive, singular character; so, for example, although two organisms have identical genomes, it could be said that they are not complete clones. The biological individual then acquires a meaning in terms of interaction, which leads us to propose that possibly the selection objects are not both individuals and such, but the interactions of those that make part of these terms, the individual's processes and the above-influentities could be understood as the biological ones
