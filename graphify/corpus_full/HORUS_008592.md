# NEUROBIOLOGICAL MECHANISMS OF DISRUPTION OF THE MATERN VINCLE AND THEIR RELATIONSHIP TO THE CONSUMPTION OF DULUCORANTS

Breast separation during breastfeeding has been considered a stressful event that generates neuro-morphophysiological and behavioral changes such as anxiety and hyperactivity, as well as preferential vulnerability to alcohol consumption, sucrose and palatable foods. Considering the relationship between early stress and obesity and the increase in the consumption of sweeteners as an alternative to the use of glucose, the aim of this study was to carry out a review that integrates experimental advances related to maternal separation and consumption of sweeteners. A search was made for existing publications in both English and Spanish and 12 experimental studies were included for analysis in which maternal separation was carried out with subsequent evaluation of water consumption with sweeteners. Maternal separation is an experimental model that allows to evaluate the consequences of stress at early stages of life; the consumption of palatable foods cushions responses to stress and intervenes with neuronal mechanisms of reward, in addition to functioning as a measure of anhedonic behavior by means of the test of preference to sucrose.
