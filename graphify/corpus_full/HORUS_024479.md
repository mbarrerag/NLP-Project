# TRANSITIONAL JUSTICE FROM DOWN AND WITH GENDER PERSPECTIVE.

The paradigm of transitional justice is relatively recent, but it has had a wide development since the 80's and its importance continues to increase.The best proof of this is the process of institutionalization that has been verified through the creation of international and hybrid tribunals, truth commissions, and reparation programs.More and more, the countries that intend to make transitions to more democratic regimes adopt measures explicitly that aim to confront the atrocities of the past and guarantee the rights of the victims to justice, truth and reparation. This document is intended to contribute to the debate on how the atrocities that have occurred in the context of the Colombian armed conflict should be addressed. For this, it presents a theoretical proposal through which the dominant approach in the field of transitional justice is enriched. This proposal starts from the recognition that despite the legal standards that inspire and guide transitional justice, it is proposed to invoke the victims, not always take into account their realities, expectations and even the contributions that can be made. This happens, for example, with women victims of violations of human rights that are past in the regions, and are far removed from the construction of the processes that are intended to deal with the consequences. The second point, in close connection with the above, is that despite the contradictory nature of the situation, this paradigm has acquired great importance in our context. The best proof of this is the institutions created to promote the realization of the rights of victims, which, although they deserve multiple criticisms, constitute an opportunity to think and include those directly affected by the conflict. This opportunity makes the discussion on transitional justice relevant in Colombia. The document then focuses on generating proposals that will enrich and strengthen the dominant approach to transitional justice, even though we are not in the presence of a transition in Colombia. To develop this proposal, the document is divided into three main parts. The first is intended to describe in general terms what the dominant approach to transitional justice is. Part Two presents an analysis of the main limitations of this approach. Part Three and Four systematizes the elements that would enrich the dominant approach. Finally, it includes closing considerations
