# The Latin language reading and writing practices in Jesuit institutions of the New Kingdom of Granada

This research paper traces the use of Latin as an academic or teaching language in a Jesuit educational institution of the New Granada, and establishes a parallel with the so-called religious literacies that occupied different areas in which clerics and laymen were formed under ideals of promotion of Christian perfection. The work is based on two theoretical foundations: the literacy and the social history of written culture, which complement and give light on the way in which reading and writing were used in Latin language during the colonial period. From the confluence of the theoretical foundations, an instrument of analysis has been designed, taking as a basis postulates and categories arising from such fields of study, to review three bibliographic sources (Ratio Atqve Institutio Studiorum, Exercise of perfection and Christian virtues and Physica Specialis et curiosa) which represent different ways of relating to the books.
