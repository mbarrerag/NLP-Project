# OIL, ENVIRONMENTAL STANDARD AND TERRITORIAL ORDER IN MIDDLE-TERM

The recovery of mature oil fields implies an intensification of extractive processes and new techniques of production that are highly harmful to the environment, in particular the injection of large quantities of water. The article examines the production of the environmental norm and its articulation with the territorial order in the oil blocks of the former concession De Mares, in Magdalena Medio, Colombia. Based on interviews and workshops with environmental leaders, documentary review and field work, it shows that interpretations and uses of the environmental norm generate fragmentation in environmental management and, in turn, a high social conflict. The notion of legal destruction of the territory is discussed and it is proposed to rethink environmental regulation within the framework of a relational conception of the territory
