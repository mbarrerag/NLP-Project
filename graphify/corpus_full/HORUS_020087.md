# A computational methodology for the generation of genomic maps from fluoroscanning images

Fluoroscanning is a novel system for the rapid generation of genomic maps. Unlike previous systems such as optical mapping and nanocoding, Fluoroscanning is only based on the intensity of signals (which we call Fluoroscans) produced by dye fluorochromes when they adhere to DNA nucleotides. As part of this work, a series of steps are developed and evaluated that include image processing to extract fluoroscan signals quickly from fluorescence microscopy images, a parallel and highly optimized algorithm to simulate the physical principles behind Fluoroscanning and a methodology to guide laboratory experiments from that algorithm. As a result of our work, we were able to establish a workflow in which Fluoroscans real extracted from digital images were used to adjust the parameters of simulations, which were used to guide laboratory experiments and to generate a synthetic set of data on a genomic scale that will help the development of signal alignment algorithms for genomic mapping (text taken)
