# The movement of opposition to the government of Rojas Pinilla: 1954-1957

The government of Gustavo Rojas Pinilla ended on May 10, 1957. What happened during his presidential period changed the Colombian political context radically with implications until the present. This work argues that the ‘May Days’ were a coup d’opt opinion that civil society. Its main managers – the political and journalistic liberal elite – were effective in bringing together various social groups to build a movement that would overthrow the military regime by wearing down political, social and ideological susustents. The opposition was organized by means of personal letters and meetings, and was carried out by means of marches, banquets, books and press articles. It is argued that the Liberal Party’s elite sought to establish itself as agents mobilizing public opinion. To do this, they consolidated spaces to promote the opinion flows built by themselves. Hence, information circuits emerged that were very efficient in positioning a discourse against what the social interests meant and proposed the government of Rojas Pinilla. Thus, the opinion of the citizens was modified with respect to who should govern and how it should do it.
