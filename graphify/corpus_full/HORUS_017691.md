Insects have the potential to transform societies. Insects like black soldier flies may be produced locally on organic waste, providing protein as well as other nutrients for animal feed, thereby reducing dependence on high-cost imported protein sources and contributing to a circular economy. Indeed, insects may be used to replace conventional sources of protein in animal feed to improve socio economic conditions of small farmers in general and mitigate gender inequality, as has occurred in Kenya. In Colombia, a country currently in a post-conflict stage after over 60 years of war, we were able to help resolve a community problem: the high cost of fish feed through implementing a small pilot plant for production of black soldier fly larvae to feed fish, reducing the costs up to 23%. This project of implementing the use of insects as feed by communities of ex-combatants and others affected by the armed conflict is called Insects for Peace, and is an example of how black soldier flies could help reintegrate ex-combatants into the local economy and thus to contribute to peace through science. This project has shown that even when large-scale transformations are not possible, small-scale initiatives can make significant contributions. Societal transformations ¿ such as from war to peace ¿ unleash social, economic, and political challenges that need to be dealt with by many committed actors through multi-, inter-, and trans-disciplinary perspectives. Insects for Peace has been made possible through the collaborative efforts of scientists of a variety of disciplines as well as personnel from independent organisations leading to the support of post-conflict transformation in Colombia
