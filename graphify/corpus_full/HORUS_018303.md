# RADICAL GLYPHOSAT TRANSLATION TO PLANTS

Glyfosate is a herbicide used in post-merging foliar applications, which adsorbs to the different constituents of the soil, so that its activity is considered null once it comes into contact with the same. Due to this, the effect of exposure via radical to the herbicide in plants has been little studied. Despite the high affinity of the molecule with the soil, it is possible to desorption the herbicide to the rhizospheric area of the plants, a process that is favored with the addition of fertiliser sources phosphates. With the aim of recognizing the effect of the root exposure to the herbicide on the growth and development of plants with and without addition of phosphoric fertiliser, a test was proposed in hydroponic conditions, exposing rice plants to two types of solutions with monoamonic phosphate and glyphosate in sub-dose. The results show increases in the concentration of shikimic acid of the plants, decrease in the production of biomass and of the foliar area.
