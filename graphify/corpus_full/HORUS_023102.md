# Illustration of two mixed models for the modelling of a Craneo-Facial Measure of the Cselph-Damasque Cohort

Understanding craniofacial growth in humans is fundamental in many areas of knowledge and human work such as anthropology, biology, and orthodontics, among others. Usually, measurements with craniofacial characteristics are recorded longitudinally at certain time intervals. Predictors such as direction, speed, and acceleration of growth are essential to understand the nature of craniofacial growth and its possible behavior according to gender and age. For some decades, literature can find recommendations on the use of mixed linear models when the data are of longitudinal type because they are useful and precise tools to generate quality knowledge. This knowledge is relevant for decision making, especially in the fields of orthodontics and dental occlusion where you want to obtain optimal results during a treatment. Thus, polynomials of second order with random coefficients have demonstrated a very good performance in the model of cranial growth in Anglo-Saxon population. For these reasons, this work seeks to illustrate results of the implementation of two models of polypneums to a measure of facial growth
