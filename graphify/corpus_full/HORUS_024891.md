# THE STANDING IN ACTION IN THE CENTRO-PERIFERIA RELATIONS: THE CASE OF A COLOMBIAN RACE CA. 1930

The Colombian historiography identifies the fragmentation of the territory as a central variable to explain the configuration of the State and the nation. This fundamental rupture has been attributed to exceptional topographic and environmental conditions, on the one hand, and to the density of its population, on the other. Although it is accepted that the transport routes are a constituent part of the topology of the territory, little has been explored their participation in the construction of a fragmented country. A controversy between the central government and the department of Antioquia over the road standards that were to be applied in the 1920s serves as a pivot of analysis. In this regard, I suggest that the fragmentation of the Colombian territory is the result of the interaction between institutions, infrastructure and nature. The controversy, which involved engineers, politicians, trade unions, roads, construction companies, technical and material standards of construction, was closed through the threat of a greater division of the nation, effectively invoked by the local elites as a resource for political negotiation. I propose the notion of a State in action to characterize the essential tension inherent to a physical, institutional and symbolic national space under construction
