# Predictive heat transfer models in fibrous insulation at high temperatures

The modeling of heat transfer in fibrous materials is important for the design and improvement of thermal insulation systems. At high temperatures and low densities, thermal radiation is expected to be the main mechanism of heat transfer. Currently the most successful models to model heat transfer in insulation at high temperatures require the use of semi-empiric methods. The main limitation of this approach is that the parameters of the model should be determined from calorimetric measurements for each possible material and determined again if the morphological structure is modified, even for the same material. This research presents a predictive model for heat transfer by radiation based exclusively on physical and morphological properties. The model was validated using previously realized measurements of thermal conductivity effective in a low density insulation based on alumin. To distinguish the different mechanisms of heat transfer, measurements in vacuum and cryogenic temperatures were analyzed.
