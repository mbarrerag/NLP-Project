# Shallow landslide cluster propagation in tropical mountainous mountains. Case study Colombian Andes

Approximately one fifth of the land surface is considered to be vulnerable to at least one threat of natural origin, such as cyclones, droughts, floods, earthquakes, volcanoes and mass movements. The latter being one of the most destructive; there are several triggering factors. In the Colombian Andes, rainfall is the main trigger factor, according to historical records between 1900 and 2018 87% of the mass movements were detonated by rains, which are usually shallow and can evolve to faster and more destructive movements such as flows and avalanches. According to recent records, the occurrence of tens to hundreds of mass movements detonated by rains, It resulted in the occurrence of torrential floods, causing impacts on the population and infrastructure. In terms of spatial analysis, most of the studies carried out focus on estimating the most susceptible areas to the occurrence of movements in surface mass. But what happens when a mass movement spreads little bit more?
