# Higher Education Public Policy Thinking Center (project Hermes 48499 - already completed) - Higher Education Public Policy Research Group

On July 20, the national government, in head of the Ministry of National Education, presented to the Colombian society the drafts of the draft laws that will mark the educational reforms during the term of Gustavo Petro. On the one hand, a draft statutory law was published that establishes the fundamental right to education at all levels; on the other hand, a draft ordinary law was socialized that reforms integrally Law 30 of 1992. Although it is not clear the sequentiality and the way that the MEN will accompany both legislative procedures (it would not make sense to reform Law 30 to have to adjust it, again, to the provisions of the statutory law), the objectives of the government are clear: to advance the exigibility of the right to education from the initial education to the undergraduate education, and reform the law of 1992 to adapt it to the requirements of the contemporary societies. On this last aspect, the need to change a norm created 30 years ago in a historical, political, social and economic context very different from that of today. It has then, from the premise that the Colombian higher education system has remained hermetic and rigid in front of the changes in the last country. The education system has been notorious: SENA has been significantly strengthened without addressing the debate on the type of education that is taught there, the educational credit has been extended to access higher education, the _x0093_pyramide of higher education has been deepened_x0094_ with a wrong distinction between technical, technological and university careers, the programs _x0093_Ser Pilo Paga_x0094_ and _x0093_Generation E_x0094_ with cohorts of still existing students, the Quality Assurance System has been developed, the number of undergraduates has been selectively promoted and the National Qualifications System has been created. To this must be added another set of changes also produced within the framework of these government bets: the time of undergraduates has been reduced and the number of postgraduates has been increased in a strong way. Many of these initiatives have been criticized, but not of deep discussions within the academy.
