# MICROENCAPSULATION BY SPRAY-DRYING AS A TOOL TO PRESERVE THE BIOFUNCTIONAL AND SENSORY PROPERTIES OF PERISHABLE FRUITS.

According to the 2017 USDA Agricultural Census report, fruit and vegetable production was valued at USD 48.2 billion, accounting for 12.4% of total agricultural output and 25% of crop production. Specifically, one of the categories of significant commercial growth in the world agricultural sector is that related to fruits, which has shown a notable expanding business since the eighties. FAO has reported that the worldwide demand moved from USD$ 16.000 million in 1980 to USD$ 10 billion per year in 2019. Bananas, pineapples, and mangoes are the most commercialized fruits; however, many tropical fruits have shown an apparent relationship between their high consumption with a decreased incidence of lifestyle diseases, such as hypertension, diabetes, and hypercholerestolemy, among others. Vitamin C, carotenoids, and polyphenolic substances are key players. Due to their radical scavenging activities, they can neutralize the harmful effects of lipid peroxidation or DNA damage caused by free radicals. Despite the fruits organoleptic, nutritional, and biofunctional characteristics, many have the inconvenience of being perishable. To increase their shelf-life and ensure their commercialization, using microencapsulation by spray-drying has become a strategy to obtain added-value products with longer shelf-life without losing their natural characteristics. The recent results related to the development and characterization of microencapsulates obtained from Myrtaceae and Solanaceae fruits, exhibiting antihyperglycemic and antihypertensive activities, will be shown in the symposium
