# Genetic structure and connectivity of Thalassia testudinum in oceanic and continental environments of the Colombian Caribbean

Thalassia testudinum, a key species for the engineering of the marine grass ecosystem in the Caribbean, provides an essential habitat and sustains vital ecosystem services. However, like other marine pasture species worldwide, its populations face increasing anthropogenic pressures, which has led to significant decreases in its coverage and functionality. Understanding the historical and contemporary population dynamics of this species, which exhibits both sexual and clonal reproduction, is crucial its effective conservation. This study investigated the genetic diversity and connectivity of T. testudinum populations in oceanic and continental environments of the Colombian Caribbean, integrating data throughout the range of distribution of the species and considering the possible anthropogenic impacts. Microsatellite analysis of 240 samples from eight Colombian populations, compared to a previously developed regional data set covering 32 additional populations, revealed a complex interaction of geological, oceanographic, biological and anthropogenic factors that shape the population structure.
