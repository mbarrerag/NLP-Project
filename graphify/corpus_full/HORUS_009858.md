# HEALTH INDEX FOR BIOLOGICAL COLLECTIONS

The space was initiated a with a talk that had the objective to give the theoretical bases and general concepts about the Health Index of Collections (ISC). In this one emphasized the importance of the administration in the biological collections, which is an indispensable part of the curating process. It is evidenced that there is a lack of methodologies that allow to dynamize the different tasks that are done in the collections and that allow to show its progress and, indirectly, the work of the curator and the associated staff. The participants are urged to generate strategies of visibilization of the collections, given the current crisis that they face, as well as to pose new challenges that allow their positioning at the managerial and institutional level. Under this context the Health Index of Collections (ISC) was presented as a proposal made by McGinley (1993)13 and modified by Fernández et al. (2005)14, which consists in the maintenance of the collections, led only to create strategies that optimize resources and personnel to improve the care and use of the information. information associated with the level 6 to 10 was explained how the analysis is done on the priorities of the management of the collections, where: Priority 1, referring to conservation includes specimens found in Level 1; Priority 2, accessibility of specimens, includes those recorded in levels 2 to 4; Priority 3, corresponds to physical organization, and includes those found in levels 5 to 6; and Priority 4, refers to the rescue of information and includes and specimens located in levels 7 to 9. It was emphasized that in any collection there is entropy; a collection that lacks this is a collection that is not being used. Finally, a reflection was made on how much a biological specimen costs, taking into account the analysis from the project's postulation, field exits, personnel involved in each process, curatorship, use, among many other variables.
