# COPPER INTERFERENCE IN CADMIUM QUANTIFICATION BY SQUARE WAVE ANODIC STRIPPING VOLTAMMETRY

Cadmium quantification in cocoa samples using square wave anodic stripping voltammetry with bismuth film electrodes has barely been reported. Then, we have not found in the literature available, and as far as we know, the copper interference study on the cadmium quantification by this analytical method. Cocoa beans have copper concentrations of 40 to 60 mg kg-1 [1], while cadmium concentrations ranging from 0.03 to 10.4 mg kg-1 [2]. The present investigation found that at levels of cadmium of 30 μg kg-1, the cadmium stripping signal is diminished starting from a Cd2+ : Cu2+ concentration ratio of 2 : 1 (30 : 15 μg kg-1), and the signal is wholly suppressed when the ratio exceeds 1 : 8 (30 : 240 μg kg-1). According to the above, the copper must be removed before quantifying cadmium, which was made in this work by the selective copper reduction on a platinum plate electrode. The reduction was made at -0.40 V for 9.5 hours, which was enough to suppress the copper stripping signal from an in-house cocoa powder reference material, with content of cadmium of 3.2 mg kg-1 and copper of 47.2 mg kg-1 (ratio 1 : 15).
