# Staying is resisting: collective action against the plunder in the Eastern Hills of Bogotá. Community mobilizations in front of the Path of the Butterfly project (2016-2020).

This document analyses the collective action in the territorial conflict of the popular neighborhoods adjacent to the Forest Reserve Forest Oriental or Cerros Orientales of the city of Bogotá, D.C., on the occasion of the proposal to build the project Path of the Butterflys or Path of Firewalls of the Cerros. This would travel between 160 to 180 kilometers and connect the localities of Usme, San Cristobal, Chapinero, Usaquén and the municipality of La Calera. The analysis of this territorial conflict emerges in the context of a process of transformation of the urban space in Latin America, in which the intentions of consolidating cities linked to global integration and under neoliberal logic have as central axis of the discourse the accumulation, competitiveness and integration from tourism and the real estate sector to international trade and services. In this sense, Latin American cities, and particularly Bogotá, have deployed mechanisms of dispossession while emerging, from the inhabitants, historical processes of resistance based on territorial organization, the search for equitable and just ways of building the urban space, in opposition to the commodification of the territories
