# EFFECT OF TEMPERATURE IN THE LOCOMOTOR DESIGN OF THE ALTOANDIN LASGARTO ANADIA BOGOTENSIS (SQUAMATA, GYMNOPHTHHALMIDAE

The locomotion plays a crucial role in the activities of lizards, such as forage, predation and survival, which directly impacts their adaptability. Various factors influence this performance, highlighting temperature. This study focused on exploring the relationship between temperature and locomotor performance in Anadia bogotensis, considering two locations (3,000 and 3450 m s.n.m.) in the eastern mountain range of Colombia, during rainy times. Two components of the locomotor performance in two populations were analyzed, evaluating four different temperatures (14, 24, 28 and 32°C): the initial speed of sprint (start speed) and the prolonged sprint (sustained speed).The start-up rate (x= 11.23 cm/s) is different to the sustained speed (x= 6.16 cm/s) and the body size of the inhabitants could not influence these speeds, in the two localities. By comparing the start-up speed and temperature treatments we do not find differences between these two factors.
