# The genesis of domination from a historical-genetic perspective

The thesis sets out the principles of the historical-genetic theory of culture and the historical evidence that sustains them and then uses that theory to explain the genesis of political domination in the ancient states from the representations of political legitimacy that were generated during the period of primitive history of humanity. The thesis provides arguments to support that the forms of primitive representation of political authority were necessary for the development of domination when there were not yet the instruments of political coercion . Thissis presents the principles of the historical-genetic theory of culture and the historical evidence that support it. After this, thesis uses that theory to explain the genesis of political domination in ancient civilizations from the ideas about autonomy in primary societies.
