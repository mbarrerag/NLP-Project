# Effects of music therapy on symptoms of musical performance anxiety in college music students

The students of careers who demand the performance of presentations in public have a high incidence of anxiety for performance. Specifically, musicians are vulnerable to suffering anxiety due for musical performance (APM), understood as the experience of a fear marked and persistent by the execution of a musical presentation. Some researches affirm that the exhaustion produced by the APM can lead students to abandon their career. Since 2009 the inventory of APM (K-MPAI) has been implemented to quantify this anxiety and evaluate the effect of some treatments for its management. This study, carried out in Bogotá, Colombia, investigated whether the music therapy can contribute to the management of the APM in music college students, using as a tool of measurement the K-MPAI and implementing tools of qualitative analysis. To this end, a process of music therapy was carried out with 14 volunteer music students, distributed in 2 groups with which a weekly session of approximately 1.5 hours was carried out until completing 9 sessions per group, using the four methods of music therapy as a whole, 18 sessions of music therapy were carried out, completing 27.3 hours of practice.
