# SAMPLING AND SPACE-SEQUENTIAL MODELATION OF THRIPS (THYSANOPTERA: THRIPIDAE) IN CULTURES OF CUT FLOWERS IN THE HIGH COLOMBIAN TROPIC

The sampling of insects in crops constitutes the main strategy of entomological (monitoreo) monitoring to determine the species present and estimate the structure and dynamics of their populations.The monitoring is defined as the counting and estimation of densities pest populations as fundamental basis for the decision making of control, for the assessment of risk and development of early warning systems.Insect sampling should be precise, fast, economical, easy to learn by technicians and permanent and essential use in the phytosanitary surveillance and integrated pest management.The design of a sampling program of thrips (Thysanoptera: Thripidae) in the production of ornamentals that takes into account the allocation of resources, the cost, the characteristics of plants, the seeding plans and the cut-off times, as well as other aspects of the own management of the crop, such as fertilizations, irrigation or the pruns, among others, is critical to incorporate the sampling of thrips as an essential activity in the management of crops.A strategy that combines the advantages of sequential sampling, with mobile windows that are fitted and adjusted to the spatial patterns of population density and incorporates models and incorporates. The study of the predictions of the selected model by crop, together with the results of the best classification algorithm based on confusion matrices or by any index associated with the quality of the classification, are used to guide the control strategies, their evaluation and as an entry into early warning systems or risk assessments, since regions of no risk at the highest risk related to the constructed indicators associated with the presence of thrips or damage caused to crops can be mapped. The results of the simulation for different patterns of thrips dispersion in crops with staggered seed such as carnation and chrysanthemum, or permanent production as pink, reveal the advantages of the methodology proposed here, as well as its easy adoption in routine entomological monitoring of pests in crops or for research. In particular, this methodology constitutes a fundamental contribution to modular models of support to the control decision for evidence-based thrips, both scientific knowledge and observations of those who are responsible for monitoring thrips and their control
