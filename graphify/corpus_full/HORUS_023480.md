# CHANGES IN THE EXPRESSION OF CITOCHINS BY EFFECT OF PEPTIDES DERIVED FROM THE GP85 PROTEIN OF EPSTEIN-BARR VIRUS.

Introduction Cytokines regulate intercellular communication during immune response. According to their concentration, they can promote or inhibit tumor development. A peptide derived from the site of binding of the Epstein-Barr Gp85 glycoprotein to human leukocytes has been identified, which could modulate their response. In this study, the effect of the peptide named 11438 and its analogue (33210) on the level of protein and gene levels on the production of inflammatory cytokines in human leukocytes by RT-qPCR and flow cytometry, respectively, was evaluated. The peptides were determined to induce changes in the cytokine expression profile, given by a low-regulation of IL-10 and GM-CSF at the gene level, and an increase in the level of proinflammatory cytokine expression such as IL-6, TNF-a and IL-1ß at the protein level, depending on the time of stimulus and the concentration used.
