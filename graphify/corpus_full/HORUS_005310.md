# ANESTHESIC CONSIDERATIONS IN RETT SYNDROME WHAT YOU SHOULD KNOW. A PEDIATRIC CASE REPORT

Rett syndrome is a rare neurological disorder characterized by progressive systemic changes. It results from a mutation in the MeCP2 (methyl-CpG-binding protein 2) gene located on the X chromosome, which is an ubiquitous transcriptional factor. The prevalence of Rett syndrome is approximately 1 in 10,000 girls. Diagnostic criteria for typical Rett syndrome include a period of regression, followed by recovery or stabilization, and the presence of all four main criteria: loss of purposeful hand skills, loss of spoken language, gait abnormalities, and stereotypic hand movements. The overall survival rate for individuals with this condition is 78% at age 25 years. Unfortunately, there are no therapeutic alternatives for curing or modifying the disease, and management primarily relies on supportive measures. In this case report, we present a clinical instance of classic Rett syndrome in a pediatric patient, focusing on anesthetic management and highlighting the multisystemic impact of the disease to mitigate morbidity during the postoperative period
