# Longitudinal modeling of dengue cases in Colombia, using Poisson and ZIP models of mixed effects.

In this work of master's thesis, the modeling of dengue cases in Colombia by municipality is carried out by means of Generalized Linear Models of Mixed Effects (GLMM) applied to longitudinal data. Based on the fact that the nature of the data are specifically counting data, the models of regression Poisson, binomial negative, Poisson inflated zero (ZIP) and negative inflated zero binomial (ZINB) of mixed effects are considered; for the adjustment of the models, 266 municipalities of the country were taken that had the following explanatory variables: temperature, precipitation, population density and water quality index (IRCA); these models are addressed from a classical perspective. Additionally, the study of goodness of adjustment of these models, their analysis of residuals as well as the respective analysis of diagnosis of influence, bearing in mind that in this case each observation is nested within a municipality. In addition, the municipalities are classified in clusters according to the number of cases accumulated following the methodology kml (k-means for longitudinal data) presenting the model of influence (the example of the general tendency of influence)
