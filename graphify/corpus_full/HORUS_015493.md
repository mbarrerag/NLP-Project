# COMPASION AND AUTHOCOMPASION IN DISEASE PROFESSIONS IN HOSPITALS OF BOGOTA, COLOMBIA

Background and meaning: Compassion allows nurses to alleviate the suffering of other people,1 while self-pity is related to the relief of one's own suffering. These contribute to their mental health and work performance.2,3 Objective/targets: Identify the level of compassion and self-pity in nursing professionals in hospitals in Bogotá. Transverse descriptive study, participated 93 nurses from three hospitals, prior consent, diligenced the validated Spanish versions of the instruments: 1) Compassion Scale (CS), internal consistency: 0.89; 2) Self-compassion Scale (SCS), a Cronbach's (0.87 and test-retest coefficient: 0.92.4 Analysis of data with descriptive statistics; low levels (1.0-2.49), moderate (2.5-3.5) and high (3.51-5.0). Survey without risk, with ethical endorsements corresponding to them. 78,5% of participants were women, 50.5 between 26 and 40 years old. Although at the lower limit, levels of compassion are high (3,6), especially in women and over 26 years old, regardless of their religion and a little more (4.3)
