# Influence of drilling and blasting method on tunnel convergence in soft rocks

The convergence in tunnels is the result of the displacements or deformations produced around it due to the redistribution of efforts experienced by the medium when subjected to the excavation process. This convergence can be influenced by different factors associated with geological-geotechnical characteristics of the medium or due to the methods of excavation used, taking into account the latter, in the present grade work an assessment of the influence of the drilling and blasting process on the convergence of soft rock tunnels was carried out by means of the analytical method of rock interaction. For the development of the grade work the method of analysis of rock-support interaction was applied by Hoek & Brown, the factor of disturbance by perforation and voladura (Factor D) proposed by Hoek & Diederichs (2006) as well as the two equations for deformation module of the rocky mass, where, the first of them is dependent on GSI and the second, dependent module of deformation of the intact rock and GSI. Calculated the deformation module, convergence was calculated. the grade work, the rock-support interaction analysis method proposed by Hoek & Brown was applied, the drilling and blasting disturbance factor (Factor D) proposed by Hoek & Diederichs (2006) as well as the two equations for deformation modules of the rock mass, where the first one is dependent on GSI and the second one, dependent on deformation modules of the untreated rock and the GSI. Calculated the module of deformation, converged was calculated. Different grades of rock mass off soft rock were represented by GSI values of 90, 50, and 30, and disturbance factors between 0 and 1 were used to evaluate different degrees of disruption.
