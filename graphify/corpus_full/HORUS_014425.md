# Temperature measurement using FBG sensors in 3D breast cancer spheroids exposed to microwave radiation.

This document presents the results of the characterization of temperature in breast cancer spheroids exposed to hyperthermia (HT) treatments. Hyperthermia is an alternative to conventional cancer treatments, such as surgery, radiation therapy (RT) and chemotherapy (QT), which have potential functional, aesthetic, emotional and psychological repercussions that significantly impact the quality of life of patients. To achieve the temperatures required in hyperthermia treatments, two microwave radiation systems were used. A high-power scheme based on the use of a magnetron extracted from a microwave oven and a moderate power based on the use of solid state amplifiers. The test subject was spheroids of the MCF-7 cell line. For temperature measurement, a prototype culture plate was designed with FBG sensors embedded in its wells. This plate was constructed using modeling by molten deposition and was characterized under controlled laboratory conditions. As a result of the tests, temperature characterization curves were obtained in different radiation schemes. The performance of the sensors was not altered by being exposed to a strong interference environment
