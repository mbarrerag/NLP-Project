# TERRITORIAL IMPACTS OF POSCONFLICT IN COLOMBIA: A EARLY BALANCE

Introduction Colombia is a large country; in terms of population is the fourth largest in the continent and, at the present rate of growth, will pass soon to Argentina, being the third. Its position makes it a strategic passage between the Caribbean and Central America and, at least, the Andean mass of the South American continent. It has borders on Venezuela, Ecuador, Peru and Brazil, some of which are ripe for the insurrection. And it would be much more difficult to exert pressure on a Colombian revolution, than on the Cuban one. With these words it characterized E. Hobsbawm (1963, 249) the nascent movement of insurrectional in Colombia, calling attention to its deep anchor in the international context of the Cold War, but also the complexity that was apprehensive of the conflict derived from the wide extension of the territory and the porosity of its multiple borders. This acute observation would be ratified in the following decades with an armed conflict connected to the dynamics of the international geopolitics but also strongly territorialized. This last means that it was not generalized throughout the country, but was sectorized according to vulnerable and exploit areas by the illegal actors Go Colombia's combined with the difficulty of control by the center's structure and the central government This idea of compartmentalization of space has been central to political geography and analysis of territory and constitutes an excellent conceptual tool for evaluating the dynamics of regional and national change. In this sense, we consider, as a working hypothesis, that the signing of the peace process, including its three years of preparation and five years of application, has generated an important territorial mutation in Colombia. Describe, and explain, this territorial change is the aim of this paper. To this end, we will take as a reference the years 2000 and 2010 to examine the state of some demographic, economic and political components and then make a comparison with the latest data and to show the change in the phenomena and in the logic of the actors in conflict. The paper consists, in addition to this introduction, of three additional separates. The first corresponds to a theoretical reflection on the nature of the territory and its processes of change inserted, in addition to, in a dynamic of globalization. We then examine the geopolitical territorial situation of Colombia in the last two decades, including the examination of that territoriality in four of the dimensions highlighted Raffestin (1980); the materiality-resources; the power of internals (a-nationals)
