# Discursive traditions of the subfield of Colombian Afro-Pacific oral literature

The Afro-Pacific communities of Colombia, in the historical background of their existential experience in the territory of the coast, shaped a certain relationship with the literature that takes place in various social practices. Thus, aside from the institutions that make up the official literary field, in these practices arise different situations of oral enunciation that make use of certain discursive genres and literary forms such as the tenth, copla and romance. In this thesis we can address the aesthetic verbal expressions, and their respective situations of enunciation, to propose an idea of oral literary field. The hypothesis of which is that, through the analysis of the scenarios, the agents and social practices in which the enunciation of oral literature takes place in the Afro-Pacific communities, certain discursive traditions can be identified. Four main traditions in the subfield of the Afro-Pacific oral literature, the functional analysis and the popular laughter that comes from the in the home and the family, the spiritual that emerges from the incretism of the Catholic-Christian and African cosmovisions, and the mythical-lewards exist linked to the humor and the popular laughter. The understanding hypothesis is that, through the analysis of the settings, agents, and social practices in which the enunciation of oral literature takes place in Afro-Pacific communities, certain narrative traditions can be identified. Four main traditions were identified within the subfield of Afro-Pacific oral literature: the search for freedom and justice; care for home and family; spiritual traditions addressing from the syncretism of Catholic-Christian and African societies and landscaped worldviews; and mystical and legendary tradition linked to popular humor and nursing that fosters particular festivals
