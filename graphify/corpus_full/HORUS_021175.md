# Is the survival of tree species in the dry tropical forest explained by functional features of drought resistance?

From the analysis of hydraulic and foliar functional traits, specifically the resistance to cavitation, hydraulic architecture, wood density and loss of electrolytes of some species used in the ecological restoration of the Ituango Hydroelectric project, it is desired to understand the relevance of resistance to cavitation in drought resistance, and to compare the different mechanisms of resistance to drought between species that present different strategies such as: rapid growth (low densities of wood), accumulation of water and carbohydrates (in stems and roots), loss of foliage (deciduous or perennial phenology), among others. The hydraulic architecture and foliar traits are evaluated in trees of natural regeneration and resistance to cavitation under controlled conditions. Mainly, it is expected that species that evade drought loss of leaves and decreasing water loss by transpiration, do not develop a high resistance to cavitation; while perennial species develop a better resistance to cavitation when supporting lower water potentials and carbohydrates (Wolfe et al., 2016).
