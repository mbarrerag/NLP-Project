# A GLOBAL READING OF REGIONAL INTEGRATION

The world space is generally taken as the scene of regional integration units (or processes) to illustrate their similarities and differences or to know their uniqueness. But little attention has been paid to the links between these processes and their place in the construction of global space. The proposal of this chapter consists in apprehending the complex consolidation of regional integration as an empirical phenomenon of the contemporary world. Our reflection is based on an analytical approach of global history. It describes the origin of regional union in different areas of the planet, starting with the Second World War of the 20th century. Next, regional integration is highlighted as a phenomenon participating in the socio-historical process of globalization. Finally, it is argued that in the xxi century the relations between regional groups interregionalism and agreements that transcend institutionally constituted regions or not trans-regionalism suggest that, despite the recent crises of some processes, regional blocks continue to be structural elements of global politics and economy
