# EFFECT OF RELAXIN IN DIFFERENCE OF MESSENCHIMAL CELLS OF HUMAN DENTAL PULPA

PRECACES Relaxin (Rln) is a polypeptide hormone of the superfamily of insulin which helps reduce spontaneous contractions of the uterus, favour greater flexibility of the pubic area and facilitate delivery. It is produced by the corpus luteos of the ovaries in women and by the testicles in men. In addition to its physiological effects on male and female reproduction, it is known to affect the metabolism of connective tissue, especially in the cardiovascular system. In addition, it stimulates the turnover of collagen, angiogenesis and antifibrosis, by binding to specific Rxfps receptors. These receptors have been identified not only in reproductive tissues but also in bone cells such as osteoclasts, osteoblasts and osteocytes. Several studies have determined that the Rln stimulates the osteoclastic differentiation and regulates the activity of the osteoclastic cells mature. However, little is known about the effect of the relaxin on the differentiation of mesenchymal cells into osteogenic lineage. The mesenchymal stem cells showed morphological changes after seven days of treatment, with larger cells and more cytoplasmic prolongations. The treatment with relaxin at 20 and 80ng/ml led to a significant increase in formation of calcium nodules in the CPSD after 14 and 21 days, similar to those obtained by the differentiation method for induction of osteoblast formation. Also, the cytochemical signal for alkaline phosphatase was significantly greater in the PCSD treated with Rln than in control. The primary osteoblasts show a lower formation of calcium nodules and alkaline phosphatase after treatment with Rln. Treatment with Rln did not result in reduced proliferation of mesenchymal cells or of primary osteoblasts for 5 days. The Rln induced morphological changes, calcification nodules formation and increased detection of alkaline phosphatase treated in mesenchymal cells, which were ostentous in the induction of osteoblastic cells into primary calcification
