# Content of Total Organic Carbon Using Random Forest, Borehole Imaging, and Fractal Analysis: A Methodology Applied in the Cretaceous The Moon Formation, South America

Abstract This research presents an alternative approach to calculate total organic carbon content using cable records and machine learning techniques; specifically, resistive well images, their average resistivity and gamma ray recording are used to train a retrogressive model. The methodology was applied in the La Luna Formation, which has been reported as one of the main generating rocks in Colombia and western Venezuela. The objective of this work is to teach a machine how to recognize patterns between fractal features in well images and their total organic carbon content. The applied machine learning is based on techniques of learning by sets, in this case, a set of decision trees known as random forests. The data used have a total of 960 record measurements, which were randomly divided into 80% for training and 20% for validation. The result is equivalent to the curve obtained with a semilogarithmic regression of organic carbon measured in the nucleus against density record values. The accuracy of this method is high enough to be considered during petrophysical evaluations, showing a quadratic mean error of 0.44% and an image coefficient of correlation of any quality of Pearson will increase the methodology.
