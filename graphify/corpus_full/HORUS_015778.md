# Mixed oxides derived from hydrotalcitas as catalysts for the production of n-butanol from ethanol.

Ethanol is a primary alcohol obtained both by petrochemical and by biomass. Among the most important derivatives obtained from ethanol is n-butanol, which is mainly used as solvent and as a platform molecule. Currently, the production of n-butanol is carried out mainly by two methods, the ABE process (Aceto-Butylic-Etylic fermentation) and the OXO process (hydroformilation), however, both processes have drawbacks, for example, the ABE process presents low yield, difficulty in the presence of products and high toxicity of solvents, and for its part the OXO process presents high toxicity of the reagents, a high cost of the catalysts and a low profitability. Due to the above, the reaction of Guerbet as an alternative has emerged, which consists in the transformation of ethanol to n-butanol through condensation and dehydration as a result of a catalyst. The results of the analysis of the texture and porosity were found to be the characteristic peaks of hydrothalcitas in precursors and in mixed oxides the characteristic peaks of a profile associated with periclase (MgO). On the other hand, a texture and porosity analysis was performed, finding that these solids, according to the classification IUPAC are of type IV, characteristic of mesoporous solids with specific surface areas between 216 and 294 [m2/g].
