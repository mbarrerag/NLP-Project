# Behavioral and glutamatergic approach of a rodent model of autism spectrum disorder by prenatal exposure to valproic acid.

The disorder of the autistic spectrum (ASD) is considered a disorder in the neurodevelopment characterized by deficit in social interaction, communication and presence of repetitive and stereotyped behaviors. It is estimated that approximately 16% of the population under 15 years of age in Colombia suffers some type of developmental disorder, including ASDs. Colombia does not have official figures that establish the prevalence in the country of this disorder. Post-mortem and diagnostic imaging studies have been approximated to the underlying neurobiology of ASD, however, the animal models allow exploration in detail and in a controlled environment of neuroanatomical, neurophysiological and molecular mediators, related to the phenotype of the disorder. The evidence suggests that environmental factors and the genetic-environmental interactions contribute to the etiology of the autismal disorder, for example, the prenatal exposure to the epitometic acid, the epthroic acid (by its acronym: ADVP), is associated with a high incidence of autism in the births; in fact, the early embryogenis as a critical period for the development of the neurodevelopment disorders supports the development of the epeusomatic disease and the ecological disease. research supports the experimental use of the Autism Biomodel by prenatal exposure to PPV, demonstrating the apparent and construct validity of the model and its potential usefulness for the development of future lines of research that will deepen key aspects in the compression of the neurobiology of the ASD and the finding of therapeutic targets for the treatment of the disease
