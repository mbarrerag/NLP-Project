# BIOCOLONIALITY. DERIVES FROM THE LATIN AMERICAN AND CARIBBEAN DECOLONIAL THINK.

The biocoloniality, as derived from the thought of Latin American and Caribbean colonialism, is presented in this work as a step towards the biocentric turn of the decolonial turn and as an analytical and methodological tool that allows to understand the production of colonial subjectivities articulated in racial and epistemic hierarchies, in dialogue with relational ontologys. From the analysis of the discourse and practices of protection of traditional knowledge associated with biodiversity in Colombia, at the macro, meso and corpopolitical levels, we observe the particular ways in which power over biological and cultural diversity currently operates. The process by which the Colombian State has been becoming an ethnographical State is detailed; classifying, ordering, mapping and raising accurate information about territories, communities and peoples, under a heteroarchical logic - non-hierarchical-of power. In this process, the assessment of knowledge and human classification operates under a protectionist discourse
