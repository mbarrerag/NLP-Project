# Intricated justice (Phase 2): A localized analysis of justice in Latin America (Argentina, Peru and Guatemala)

Imbribrada Justice (phase 2) is a project of investigation of constitutional law compared to, located and contextual that aims to continue with the project initiated in 2020 (phase 1) in which important findings were made on the compatibility of constitutional justice with the political systems of three Latin American countries: Ecuador, Bolivia and Mexico. At this stage, this project seeks to continue to investigate the elements that allow us to answer the question: What is the model of constitutional justice most compatible with democracy in Latin America? covering three other countries of the region. In fact, in recent decades the discourse of the difficulty againstmajority, put the emphasis on the democratic deficits of constitutional justice giving doubts about the legitimacy of the constitutional judges to decide the constitutionality of the laws. In this sense, the Latin American countries that had recently incorporated in their constitutions an extensive catalogue of economic, social, cultural and environmental rights that were included in the same Colombian, as Colombia (1991), Ecuador (2008), Bolivia (2009), were challenged by the promises of justice offered by a questionable justice. In this sense, the peripheral countries that had constitutional actions that were not the same as the autonomous Colombia with the public action of nonconstitution, or the forced to the Mexican legal process. The aim of this project is to create new knowledge from, for and with Latin America, based on our own experiences with a new selection of countries representative of, in the case of Peru, the insertion of a semi-parliamentary political system, Argentina, the intention of a credible incorporation of the model of justice, the United States political and territorial system (judicial review) and Guatemala as a Central American country with a strong constitutional justice and an economically democratic state
