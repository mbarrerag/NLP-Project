# Space Prediction of Sales through Bayesian Approaches and Machine Learning Using Area Data

Sales are a critical indicator of the performance of a company and the correct projections of future sales allow the decision making on their budget, production, inventory and expansion. The incorporation of statistical tools that allow to understand the spatial-temporal variability of sales and that support the logistic planning of the company is of great interest to managers and administrators. The obtaining of sales forecasts through the implementation of models that take into account spatial autocorrelation and that evolve in time, allows the company to define strategies of marketing and production with particular characteristics on the places where it provides its services. This work focuses on comparing different statistical techniques and the Approximation of Laplace Annosa and Integrated (INLA) for the approximate Bayesian inference in the analysis and temporal, spatial and spatial-temporal pronostic sales of a company in the east coast of the United States from 2017 to 2022. It analyzes data of sales for a period of about 6 years, including as demographic covariable population of inhabitants in the states involved in the study. Initially, it addresses the analysis of the sales in the point of time of the United States from the point of view of the literature and the usual sales for the series. INLA allows easy extension of spatial models to the spatial-temporal case, enabling the insertion of spatial and temporal random effects, as well as effects of interaction between space and time. On the other hand, the relationship between the methods Bayesia- x nos and statistical machine learning is intrinsic, since the Bayesian analysis is a method of statistical automatic learning. For the spatial-temporal analysis of textile sales we compare the Bayesian approach methodology INLA and its new proposal for statistical automatic learning based on the variational correction of Low Range Bayes (VBC), which uses the method of Laplace and subsequently a variational correction of Bayes to the subsequent mean, which showed equal accuracy in the adjustments of models and their forecasts. The analyses are performed using the statistical programming language R and the R-INLA library .
