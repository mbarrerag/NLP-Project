# Comparative study of extraction methods and evaluation of protective effect of mandarin leaf extracts (Citrus reticulata) var. Arrayana against lipid oxidation in soy oil

The negative effect of synthetic antioxidants on health together with the interest of consumers to acquire natural products has contributed to replace synthetic additives with natural antioxidants. In this sense, the recovery of residues and agro-industrial by-products represents an economic and environmental alternative for obtaining bioactive compounds. Therefore, this study focused on valorization of mandarin leaves (Citrus reticulata) var. Arrayana by extracting antioxidant compounds using supercritical carbon dioxide (CO2 SC) to protect soybean oil against lipid oxidation. Initially, the most active and protrusive extracts were obtained with hexane, ethyl acetate and ethanol in order to verify the polarity of the compounds obtained that could have protective effect against lipid oxidation. Among the extracts, the hexane-based product offered the highest protection in terms of peroxide and p-anisidine index, indicating the action of non-polar compounds as antioxidants in oil. A CO2 pressure and a lower temperature allowed an extract with a higher concentration of bioactive compounds such as terpenoids such as linalol, thymol and tangeritin, carotenoids such as lutein, all-trans-β-carotene and chlorophyll a. Therefore, the extraction with CO2 SC of non-polar compounds of mandarin leaves proved to be a sustainable and effective method for obtaining natural antioxidants to improve the oxidative stability of soybean oil. .
