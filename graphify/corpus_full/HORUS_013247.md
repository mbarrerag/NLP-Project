# A machine learning framework to support multi-channel time series classification in BCI systems with preserved interpretation

The brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) based on electroencephalography signals (EEG) have gained significant attention in recent years as a practical approach to human-computer interaction.The paradigms of motor imagination (MI), where users simulate mentally motor tasks without physical movement, are widely used in the development of BCI. However, the construction of BCI systems based on EEG signals face important challenges due to the low signal to noise index (SNR), the non-stationary and non-linearity of the signals of EEG, as well as the inter-intrasubject variability that hinder the extraction of discriminatory characteristics. To address these challenges, this thesis proposes two novel methodologies for the classification of MI based on EEG. First, a second approach of dependent preprocessing signals, called Elimination of dependent Artefacts, and the individual subjects (SD-AR, by its acronym in English). In public databases, they demonstrate KREEGNet’s superiority over EEGNet and other cutting-edge methods. In addition, an interpretation of the model at individual and group level is carried out, using rendiming metrics for classification tasks and relevant functional connectivitys. In conclusion, the methodologies proposed in this thesis contribute to improving the reliability, interpretability and performance of EEG-based BCI systems for MI tasks. The SD-AR approach effectively addresses the removal of artifacts and improves the quality of EEG signals, particularly for subjects with weak motor skills. On the other hand, KREEGNet demonstrates notable improvements in performance and provides spatial interpretability, becoming a promising alternative to make interpretable BCI systems based on deep learning models.
