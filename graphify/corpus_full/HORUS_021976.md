# Development of type I collagen laminar biphasic ducts for use in peripheral nerve regeneration.

The lack of donor nerves has led to the development of nerve ducts to connect peripheral nerve edgings and to help prevent the formation of neuromes. Often, the standard diameters of these devices cannot be adapted at the time of surgery to the diameter of the injured nerve. In this work, supports were developed to form nerve ducts filled with an internal matrix with unidirectional channels covered by a multidirectional porous zone. To this end, two dispersions of collagen type I (5 mg/g and 8 mg/g) were frozen sequentially using different methods to obtain laminar cells (NC, P1 to P5) formed by an area with unidirectional pores (U) adjacent to an area of multidirectional pores (M). The physicochemical and microstructural properties of the supports were determined and compared Pneumatic cells were measured, as well as their biodegradability, the contents of residual glutaraldehide and its cytocompatibility. In addition, to the human ducts obtained by winding the lesions from the unidirectional to the multi-directional area were determined and the Pneumalization results were compared
