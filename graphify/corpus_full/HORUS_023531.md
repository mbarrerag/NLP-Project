# SELECTION OF APPROPRIATE VARIABLES TO ANALYZE RENEWABLE ENERGY STRATEGIC INVESTMENTS IN POWER MARKETS CONSIDERING DIVERSIFICATION ISSUES AND THE EFFECTS OF WEATHER AND COMPETITION UNCERTAINTY

In addition to the environmental benefits of introducing renewable energy in power generation, there is a benefit related to the diversification of the energy mix and improvements in system reliability and security. However, addressing diversification in power sources is not an easy task, and one of the main issues is to select the appropriate variables to analyze complementarity among resources. Specifically, this paper shows the preliminary results of a larger research project that studies renewable energy strategic investment decisions for generation assets in competitive power markets, focused in comparing hydro and wind power resources and validating with information from the Colombian power market. Wind and hydro resources are very volatile as they depend mainly on weather fluctuations. These resources also show varied degrees of complementarity [1]. Among the variables used to determine wind and hydro complementarity are physical flows (water flow, wind speed) and operating variables such as the capacity factor, defined as the annual generation of a power plant divided by the product of the capacity and the number of hours over a given period. In addition to the selection of a variable that represents complementarity, an appropriate methodology must be applied to estimate the benefits of diversification. In this case, a Game Option model is developed (integrating real options and game theory), and this paper shows the process of testing and selecting the variables to be included in it. A conclusion of this project is that capacity factor is the most appropriate input for this kind of models, since it allows a realistic simulation of volatility within the real option lattice, and it also helps to correlate operative and financial risk in cash flow analysis
