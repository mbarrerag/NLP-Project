# _x0093_Circulation and consumption of Chinese products in New Granada in the 18th century. A comparative look at the Pacific economy_x0094_.

During the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, the Nao of China or the Galeón of Manila has operated in an important economic circuit that connected the ports of Manila (Cabide), Acapulco and Lima, which had a significant impact on the economies of the Spanish viceroyalties. Specifically, the Viceroyalty of the New Spain managed to control the economic relations between China and Manila, which resulted in a very favorable exchange for China in terms of silver, in exchange for a wide variety of goods of great value, such as silk and porcelain, for the families of the New Spain.Despite the economic dynamism that was experienced in the Pacific region at that time, the historiography has focused mainly on the socioeconomic relations between New Spain and the Viceroyalty of Peru.Only in the last years, the most important regions of the American subcontinent have begun to receive attention in terms of consumption of the same Chinese goods that were in the main viceroyal territories.However, much of the Latin America and Portuguese did not appear in these circuits, despite the important evidences that demonstrate the Chinese products to those territories. Since the 18th century, there have been records of products with the name "Chinese". Perhaps the small number of mentions has discouraged the interest in investigating more about how these products arrived in the Neo-Granadino territory; however, the few mentions that exist are an important indication of the historical connections between New Granada and China during a very long period in which the Asian giant was the world's leading economic power
