# Evaluation of morphological changes in hippocampal neurons caused by facial nerve injury in rats.

Different models of peripheral nerve injury in rats have been used extensively to study the structural changes that occur, not only in the neurons directly affected, but also in other structures of the central nervous system related to sensoriomotor processing. The Group of Neurophysiology Behavioral, of the National University of Colombia, has been able to produce changes at the morphological, molecular and electrophysiological level generated in the primary motor cortex of vibrisas by the lesion of the facial nerve in rats. The Group of Neurophysiology of the Seportamental, has described a decrease in the long term plasticity in the synapsis of the coscululcidal CA3, CA1 of the hypocampal, and an increase in the microglial activation in the hypocampal of the animals with the axotomy of the facial nerve. In this work, the neuronal morphology of the corticonal cells was analyzed in CA3 and CA1 of the hippocampal regions, with the reversible or irreversible lesion of the facial nerve. For this purpose, the functional tissue was performed in the initial and the cerebral tissue. changes denote a general reduction in the complexity of the dendritic tree and the density of thorns, which tends to be reversed if there is recovery of motor function
