# EFFECT OF THE WATER LEVEL AND FINAL FERMENT TIME ON THE PROCESS OF COFFEE ELABORATION (ARABICOFFEA VAR. CATIMOR AND CASTILE) AND THE SENSORIA PERFILE IN TAZA - JARDIN - ANTIOCIA - COLOMBIA

This work was derived from the research project: viable and cultivable micro-organisms present in the wet fermentation process of coffee and its relationship with the coffee profile in the coffee cup finca La Antigua?, municipality of Garden, funded by the University of Antioquia, Colombia. Traditionally, coffee growers in the South West Antioqueño use the wet fermentation of coffee to obtain the typical flavor of Colombian coffee. The effect of fermentation variables such as the water-fruit ratio of depulsed coffee and the final fermentation time of grains, were studied in fermentation systems called minilots and evaluated the effect on the trilling processes, yield, coughing and the final sensory profile of the drink, applying a 32 factorial design. The results of ANOVA (a=0.05) show that no significant effect of these variables was found in the final score in the cup obtained. With these findings, strategies can be applied that seek to mitigate water consumption and optimize fermentation times during the post-harvest of coffee
