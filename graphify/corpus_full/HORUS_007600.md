# Identification of transcriptomic biomarkers related to oncogenic processes in oropharyngeal cancer of HPV-positive patients

The infection with the human papillomavirus (HPV) is the most common sexually transmitted infection in Colombia and the world. Depending on the serotype responsible for the infection, HPV can manifest itself as warts or induce carcinogenicity. It is important to note that high-risk serotypes are associated with various types of cancer, including cervical cancer, which has presented an estimated incidence of 38,000 cases diagnosed annually worldwide (Paver et al., 2020). In recent years, a significant increase has been observed in cases of oropharyngeal cancer that is not related to smoking or alcohol consumption, considered as the main risk factors for this disease, but to the increase in risk sexual behaviors such as a high number of sexual partners and oral sex practices, which have led to the increase in sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including HPV. A direct causality between infection by this virus and the development of squamous cell carcinoma of head and neck (HNSCC, by its acronym patients, is found in the Colombian oropharynge.
