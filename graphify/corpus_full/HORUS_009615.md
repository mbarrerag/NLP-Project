# INTERCULTURED CHALLENGES OF THE GREAT CARIBBEAN

In Tunja, on September 18, 1797 (SC, 47, 10, D. 18, ff. 821r-853v), the body of merchants and pulpiers of the city denounced that on the night of the 17th of the current they attacked three of our shops trying to break the doors as they did, and to de-escalate the thresholds with damages and remarkable facts [causing] to our credits and our interests a remarkable loss with evident risk of life. (SC, 47, 10, D. 18, f. 822r.) To these criminal acts were added those that happened in the neighborhoods of Our Lady of the Snows and Santa Barbara, quite distant from the parish, where the locals faced daily several misfortunes of theft and deaths, like the one that occurred to Joaquín Ruiz, who in one of the carnivals of this city by force of blow, without giving him mortal wound left him deprived of life even before six o'clock in the night (SC, 47, 10, D. 18, f. 832v.)
