# OLEIFF ELAEIS (KUNTH) CORTE AND OXG INTER-SPECTIFIC HIBRITOR BILOGY AND BOTANIC

The species Elaeis oleifera, known as the American oil palm, has an important place in the programs of genetic improvement of oil palm, for its ability to generate interspecific hybrids (OxG) with the species Elaeis guineensis, the palm of African oil, and in this way to originate characteristics of agronomic interest for the agro-industrialization of the oil palm. For several decades different research centers in oil palm have carried out surveys and collections in South America, to access this species and to form banks of germplasm for the purposes of insertion in their respective programs of improvement. This has led to a certain knowledge of its geographical distribution in the American continent and, in turn, the morphoagrnomic differences according to their geographical origin are known. This last point is important in the generation of hybrids OxG, for the differences that the progeny can express when considering the phenotypic and genetic diversity that the maternal parent.
