# LETTER RE: UPSTAGE RATE OF COMPLEX SCLEROSING LESIONS/RADIAL SCARS

Dear Editor I have read the retrospective study by Jones et al.1 In their study, all their core needle biopsies were vacuumassisted (VAB). Some services opt for VAB, which provides more tissue, thus reducing the frequency of upgrade to in situ or invasive carcinoma, and they proceed as the next step after diagnosing a radial scar (RS) to its excision by vacuum-assisted excision to remove the entire lesion, as an alternative to the traditional open biopsy. However, the transition to this procedure has limited evidence in the medical literature. In the analysis of the RS, itmust be taken into account that it is not a premalignant lesion. This lesion is considered benign but can be accompanied by carcinoma that may be indistinguishable on images. The authors1 intend to determine if the entire lesion is resected with VAB. It is about this that I want to express a concept that maybe has not been well studied, the finding of Doyle et al2 who showed that of 25 malignant lesions in the excision biopsy, 4 were in the RS, 17 in the margin, and 4 separate (possible not resected by VAB). In this regard, I performed a systematic review in PubMed, with the terms breast radial scar and neoplasms, searching articles with a diagnosis of RS by percutaneous biopsy and subsequent open biopsy with an upgrade to malignant breast neoplasm, but separate from the site of the first biopsy, confirmed by measuring the distance between the two lesions. I found with these inclusion criteria only two articles. In Leong et al3 of 161 pure RS biopsies taken by stereotaxic biopsy (VAB with a 9g needle) and with surgical excision, only one had ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) of 2 mm focus located 5 mm away from the RS biopsy cavity, and in Li Z et al4 of 220 biopsies, 2 cases with carcinoma were found. The first was a 10 mm invasive ductal carcinoma with nuclear grade 2 and a distance of 8 mm from the biopsy site. The second was a focal DCIS of 5 mm with a cribriform growth pattern, nuclear grade 2, and a distance of 7 mm from the biopsy site. These few reports, due either to the low frequency of neoplasia in the vicinity of the RS biopsy or because this issue has not been studied, may reinforce the need for surgical excision. I consider the information presented in the article by Jones et al1 very important, as well as their interest in determining a ¿cutoff¿ size wherein a lesion is recommended for excision vs close mammographic follow-up. I wish them success in this project
