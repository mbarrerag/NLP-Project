# Parametric computational model of endochondral ossification

The ossification endochondral is the main mechanism of growth of the long bones, regulated by the interaction between mechanical forces, biochemical signals and cellular differentiation. In this thesis is proposed a growth approach based on a coupled model that integrates three fundamental components: (1) mechanical stimulus, through an elastic model in flat deformation to predict ossification patterns according to the geometry and applied load; (2) biochemical factors, by means of a diffusion formulation for the spatial dynamics of Ihhh and PTHrP; and (3) cellular activity, represented by ossification indexes based on the distribution of local stresses and signals. In addition, two joint geometries constructed by means of B-Splins non-uniform rationing (NURBS, by its acronyms in English), encompassing from concavas to convexa forms, as well as ossification fronts in different stages. In addition, two scenarios of load history are explored: one with single contact and another with double contact, representing configurations such as metacarpophalangic, tibiofemoral, humericubital and humerradial. Through a reaction–diffusion framework for spatial dynamics of Ihh and PTHrP; and (3) cellular response, via ossification indexes drive by stress distributions and signaling activity. Joint geometrics constructed using Non-Uniform Rational B-Splines (NURBS) parameters, ranking from concave to convex shapes, with varying degrees of ossification front progression. Two distinguish between historical modes are evaluated: a single contact and a dual contact scenario, resembling the mechanical configurations of joints such as the metacarpophalangeal, tibiofemoral, humeroulnar, and humeroradial.
