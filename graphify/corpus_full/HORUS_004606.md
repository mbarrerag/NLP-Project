# Intention prediction of cycling based on their orientation angles as vulnerable road users in autonomous driving environments

The deaths due to traffic accidents are currently the eighth cause of death according to the World Health Organisation (WHO). 71% of these deaths correspond to vulnerable users of the road (VRU), in particular 3% are cyclists. In an environment in which the autonomous vehicles (AV) are the non vulnerable road actors of the greatest presence, it will be of great importance that the VRUs are detected by those AVs effectively and in the shortest possible time. This task is still an open challenge, because the cyclists have patterns of movement highly complex and their detection is affected by occlusions and problems associated with lighting when they move on urban routes. In this doctoral thesis, it is proposed to predict the intention of the cyclists in urban ways from the estimation of both their orientation and their inclination during the movement in proximity to AVs. Due to the lack of datasets that contain images associated with angles of orientation of cyclists, a system was designed and a dataset was built for Cyclops.
