# MONITORY FOR COMPOSITION ASSESSMENT IN THE ISLAND OF SAN ANDRES

Floods represent one of the most frequent and threatening natural disasters at a global level. Caribbean island regions such as San Andrés, are exposed to cyclonic floods during hurricanes, storms, strong winds, low pressure and sea level rise. Several authors agree on the need to develop methodologies for the acquisition of quantitative data in situ in order to validate models representing the composite floods, these are characterized by being generated by multiple effects occurring simultaneously or in close succession. Characterizing and evaluating the composite floods in the island of San Andrés, using hydrodynamic, meteorological and in situ inspection data of the storm drainage system. To collect the data, field campaigns were carried out in November 2023 and in February 2024 in the north of San Andrés, in the planning of these campaigns the information collected from the literature, as well as the availability of the stormy and by local staff. The different flood prone areas were defined sites for the underflow of pressure sensors to obtain data from the oleage and tidal systems and the installation of the weather station. as Rey et al. (2021) have found that the level of flooding is underestimated when cyclonic tides are modelled without including the contribution of waves. So the measurements made represent a valuable contribution to the study of floods in San Andrés
