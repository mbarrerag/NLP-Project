# IMPACT OF HIGH ANDEAN CATTLE RANCHING ON THE AMOUNT OF SOIL ORGANIC CARBON IN AGROECOSYSTEMS UNDER TWO CONSERVATION FIGURES

Cattle ranching is the main economic activity in the Colombian countryside, present in all regions and thermal floors of the country, where it plays a determining role in the transformation of vegetation cover throughout the national territory. In some departments of the country, the relevance of this activity does not lie in the number of animals but in the sites where it is carried out, since it can be practiced in hillside areas on fragile ecosystems, with negative consequences on the ecosystem services they provide (water regulation, carbon capture and storage, etc.), which may be under different conservation figures with management plans specific to their objectives. For the above reasons, the objective of this work is to generate knowledge of the incidence of livestock agroecosystems on the amount of soil organic carbon (SOC) under two conservation figures over 12 years. The work was carried out in the La Nevera sector (Palmira), department of Valle del Cauca, central mountain range of the Andes. The farms were under two conservation categories: i) Las Hermosas National Natural Park ¿PNN¿ (5) and ii) Amaime National Protected Forest Reserve ¿RFPNA¿ (3). A first analysis was carried out with SOC information (year 2010) from eight cattle ranches, where the effects of farm, conservation figure (PNN or RFPNA), site (forest or pasture), height above sea level (1=2692 to 3100; 2=3101 to 3309 and 3=3310 to 3412 m.a.s.l) and sampling depth (0-5 and 5-10 cm) were evaluated. Tukey's test was used for the differentiation of means (p<0.05). The second analysis was made with information from SOC of the year 2022. The change in SOC levels was then studied (t-student for dependent samples with p<0.05). The highest SOC levels were found in forests at any altitude within the evaluated range with respect to paddocks (8.16 +/-0.24 Vs 5.37 +/- 0.14). The interactions between site*height, height*sampling depth and conservation figure*site*sampling depth influenced SOC concentrations (p<0.05). It was observed that between 2692 to 3309 m.a.s.l SOC was higher from 0-5 than from 5-10 cm depth (6.27 +/- 0.17 Vs 5.0 +/- 0.17 respectively). Finally, it was identified that regardless of the figure (PNN or RFPNA) SOC was higher in forest 0-5 cm (8.9 +/- 0.28), followed by forest 5-10 cm (7.63 +/- 0.28), paddocks 0-5 cm (5.7 +/- 0.16) and paddocks 5-10 cm (4.6 +/- 0.16). The second analysis identified that the SOC levels of the paddocks in some of the evaluated farms were different between 2010 and 2022 (p<0.05), increasing their concentration by 47.6%. two main conclusions emerged: i) a well-developed livestock activity can contribute to increase SOC levels, the main objective of regenerative agriculture currently promoted to reduce climate change, ii) ecosystems of such importance as those analyzed in this work, where human populations and anthropic activities have been present and carried out since before they were included in strict conservation figures, should have access to programs and projects associated with conservation (payments for environmental services and carbon markets) to achieve sustainability
