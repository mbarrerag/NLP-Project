# Evaluate two methodologies for the analysis of residues of mancozeb and propineb fungicides in fruits and vegetables as alternatives for analysis of their quality and safety.

Dithiocarbamates (DTC), specifically mancozeb and propineb, are the most widely sold fungicides in Colombia, these are applied in a large variety of crops due to their broad spectrum fungal activity, and have been reported in recent years with high frequency in monitoring pesticide residues carried out by the European Commission. The TLDs are analyzed by indirect methods due to their chemical instability and low solubility, which is why their analysis presents important challenges. In this work two methodologies were implemented and evaluated for analysis of dithiocarbamates residues, specifically for mancozeb and propineb in fruits and vegetables. This with the aim of providing a tool to evaluate the quality and safety of food in Colombia. The first methodology developed was proposed as an alternative and solution to difficulties in the official methodology of the European Commission. This resulted in the derivation of mancozeb and propineb in high-stage products Dimethyl-ethylenedithiocarbamate (dimethyl-EBDC) and Dimetyl-propyl diplendiditium-carbamate (dimethyl-BDC) and in the final solution (dimethyl-BDC). The test method was established on the basis of statistical evidence that the low temperature homogenization process prevents dithiocarbamate degradation. The two analysis methodologies proved to be equivalent for compliance with maximum residue limits in dithiocarbamates for fruits and vegetables, the official method presents challenges that can be overcome by the derivative method, allowing for more information on the origin of carbon disulfide, improving monitoring and reducing the probability of false positive reporting
