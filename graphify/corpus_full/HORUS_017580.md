# TRPV-1 ANTAGONIST ABROGATES THERMAL AND OSMOTIC-STIMULI RESPONSE

To establish transient potential receptors (TRPV-1) in human odontoblast-like cells (OLC) as a possible therapeutic target for dental pain mediated by noxious thermal and osmotic stimuli using a selective antagonist Methods: After ethical evaluation, OLC were differentiated from dental pulp mesenchymal cells using conditioned TGF-ß1 supplemented medium. The cellular phenotype was confirmed with the expression of DSPP and DMP-1 by real time PCR and immunocytochemistry. Activation of TRPV-1 was determined by evaluating changes in calcium concentration after stimulation with mannitol and xylitol hyperosmotic solutions (370 mOsm/L) or DMEM at 45°C, using the fluorescent calcium probe Fluo-4-AM. Changes in calcium flux ratio (F/F0) were evaluated by fluorescence and flow cytometry (FC). Simultaneously, cells were co-stimulated with the selective antagonist capsazepine (CZP) for 15 minutes and then treated with the solutions previously described. HEK cells were used as a positive control. The OLCs expressed DSPP and DMP-1 (transcript and protein). Calcium input measurements showed a F/F0 ratio of 4.51±0.41, 4.41±0.42, and 4.28±0.27 in xylitol-, mannitol- and high-temperature-stimulated OLCs, while the addition of CZP reduced the F/F0 ratio to 0.85, 0.84, and 0.87, respectively. Consistently, we observed similar F/F0 ratios using FC. Hyperosmotic and thermal stimuli increased calcium entry into the OLCs, which was reduced by CZP. This suggests a modulation in the activity of TRPV-1 in these cells and therefore, the use of an antagonist of this receptor could be implemented as a pharmacological strategy for the management of dental pain
