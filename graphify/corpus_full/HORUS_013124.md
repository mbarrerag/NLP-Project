# Automatic quantification of tumour metabolic load in 68Ga-PSMA positron emission tomography images in patients with prostate carcinoma

Prostate cancer is the second most frequent type of cancer in men worldwide, and its incidence varies considerably according to geographical regions. In Latin America, it is estimated that it is the third most common type of cancer and the most lethal among men. For effective treatment, it is crucial to determine precisely the extent of the disease, and PET/CT images with specific radiotracers of the prostate membrane antigen (PSMA) have proven to be useful for this purpose. These images also allow measuring the tumor metabolic load, an important indicator to evaluate the response to the treatment, since a higher metabolic load is related to a higher degree of malignancy and a possible advance of the disease. However, PET/CT images present challenges due to their low resolution and high noise, which makes it difficult to accurately delimit the tumour metabolic load and requires a considerable amount of time. Common methods for this delimitation are visual interpretation and use of thresholds in semiautomatic software, but both present limitations and variability depending on the selected threshold. In the case of metastatic cancer, manual delimitation is even more complex due to the presence of multiple metastasis and the use of the following. The main models include the creation of a model that can automatically quantify the metabolic burden of tumours in the PET/CT examinations of 68Ga-PSMA in patients diagnosed with prostate carcinoma in the pelvis region.
