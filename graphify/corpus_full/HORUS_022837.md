# ENGAGEMENT OF DARVINISM TO COLOMBIA

This paper investigates the reaction of Colombian society to the theory of evolution by natural selection from its publication and until the end of the 19th century. We reviewed various publications of the period and secondary sources. The publication of Darwin’s work in 1859 coincides with a period of political instability in Colombia in which the discussion of scientific advances does not seem to be a priority. The first reactions to Darwinism are recorded in the last two decades of the century, coinciding with relative social stability. The reaction was led by literate, poet, politician and clergy with remarkable ability to influence the opinion of the commons. The discussion in the academic environment was less impact. The connotation of Darwinism that generated greater reaction related to the origin of our species. The notion of an ancestor primate was considered offensive and denigrating. Darwin’s ideas were also confused with those of teleological and positivist dye of Lamarck and Spencer. Although the evolution was included in the programs of some university ideas, the explanations did not include the mechanism of natural selection and if it was framed in the creative, essential and catatrophic vision
