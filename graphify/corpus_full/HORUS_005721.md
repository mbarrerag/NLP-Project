# Beyond the promised land: institutional response to land restitution

The Law 1448 of 2011 created the Land Restitution Program (LRP) in Colombia, aimed at repairing the victims of the armed conflict in the country. The LRP has undergone various impact assessments with an equal number of variables of interest. In this study, the specific focus was on analyzing whether the program had an impact on the commission of crimes associated with land abandonment and dispossession. In other words, collateral effects of the LRP were examined based on institutional security support in municipalities that had at least one property registered in the Forced Displaced and Abandoned Land Registry (RTDAF). Through an event study and applying the Callaway & Sant'Anna (2021) methodology, it was found that the LRP had a negative effect of 6.162 percentage points on crimes associated with land dispossession and abandonment for the year 2015. The effect remained until 2019 but lost significance in 2020. This indicates that the dynamics of escalation in the armed conflict in the territories, combined with the absence of a clear territorial security policy, have resulted in the loss of some of the collateral benefits of the LRP. The main recommendations are to strengthen the implementation of the Peace Agreement in its Points 1 and 5, create a territorial security policy, and establish a mechanism to verify compliance with the obligations that sentences impose on national and territorial entities
