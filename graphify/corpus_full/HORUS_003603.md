# LOVE AND KILL A HISTORY OF CONJUGICIDE IN THE NEW GRANADA, 1780-1830

When asked about the influence of sexual differentiation in the application of justice and in the investigation of the intersectionality between gender, class and race, historiography on the crime produced in the last twenty years has allowed to emerge women from the low estates who did justice by their own hands in contexts of a cruel marital violence, but also to elite women whose files for murder of the husband were preserved in the archives as exemplifying and anecdotal, for which reason they had a wide display in the press and even reached the cinema and television, when the twentieth century is about. Today with Professor Elisa Speckman we will look to deepen in the common points and the differences in the administration of justice between the end of the eighteenth century and the beginning of the twentieth century when ending the life of the husband is treated. Maltrate, Vengeance, Int pain, Ideals feminine and masculine, Honor, Fidelity, Loyalty and Dignity will be some of the subjects that we will address in this dialogue
