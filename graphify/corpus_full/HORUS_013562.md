# From tropical dry forest to cities: responses from bird assembly to urban environments

The rapid urban growth has been drastically transforming ecosystems, replacing natural coverage with artificial, causing homogenization in biological communities. Much of the growing literature on bird responses to urban environments is restricted to temperate regions, and few studies are developed in the neotropics. Particularly, the dry tropical forest Colombian is the ecosystem in a most critical state, suffering a strong pressure from urbanization, negatively affecting biodiversity. The responses of birds to urban environments were determined by identifying and quantifying the changes in: 1) the taxonomic and functional diversity; and 2) the structural properties of the fruiting networks, given the variation in the degree of urbanization, structure and diversity of vegetation. The main findings show that 1) the diversity of Shannon, the richness, the equitability and functional dispersion of the vegetation assemblies are negatively affected by the urbanization and different aspects of the structure and diversity of the plants; 2) the structural properties of the frugivory networks did not show association with the urbanization nor with any particular variable of the structure and diversity of the vegetation.
