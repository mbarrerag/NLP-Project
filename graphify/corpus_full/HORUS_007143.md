# Estimation of speed-density, density-flow and speed-flow ratios of vehicle transit in the urban roads of Bogotá and Medellín based on inferential statistical techniques for multiple flow regimes

The functional relations between macroscopic traffic variables, including the average volume of transit (flow), density and speed, are essential components of the theory of the vehicular flow. Understanding these relations has numerous applications, including the development of intelligent transport systems, the development of micro and macro transit models, and their planning and management. However, the current state of research on this topic in the context of the urban routes of Bogotá and Medellín should be deepened by taking advantage of the massive information available. In this thesis, functions were empirically estimated to represent the relations between flow, speed and vehicle density, using average speed information and the volume of vehicles obtained by sensors and traffic chambers using nonlinear regression models, using data analytic techniques using the experimental method. Timed records were used for 200 street segments in Medellín and 50 in Bogotá. To ensure the robustness of the findings, models were estimated for cross-sectional error (interrupted, semi-interrupted and continuous) and eight functional forms were tested considering transit conditions with and without congestion in both cities.
