# Determination of toxic compounds in Barypthengus martii (Aves, Momotidae), Colombia

The study of toxic compounds in birds is a very recent field in which only 11 species with alkaloids used to deter predators and parasites have been identified, among other functions. Since Colombia is the country with the greatest diversity of birds on the planet, it is possible that it presents species that have developed different types of toxins obtained through food items. Some characteristics that have in common toxic birds are the presence of an aversive odor, striking colouring and ignorance of natural predators. One candidate species that meets these characteristics is the momoto rufo Barypthengus martii. To test the toxicity of this species, samples of different tissues (plumas, epithelium, musculature, heart, stomach, intestine and uropigial gland) will be collected in 10 individuals, as well as some of their possible food items with presence of toxic compounds (ranas of the species Oophaga sylvatica and insects of the families Meloidae and Oedemeridae). The presence of alkaloids will be determined through analysis of future chromography coupled to biometometry of masses.
