# Total residual thyroid tissue post-thyroidectomy, how common is it?

The total thyroid tissue has indications both benign and malignant pathology and is the pillar of treatment of differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) (1). In turn, the iodine 131 is the adjuvant treatment of the CDT (1), which is performed with the intention of detecting recurrence and improving the period free of disease by destroying the persistent thyroid tissue benign or malignant (2). The presence of residual thyroid tissue has been demonstrated by studies carried out in patients undergoing total thyroidectomy with or without central emptyness. The pre-ablative studies with 131I were performed in more than 90% of cases (3-8). The above has led to doubting in the possibility of performing a total “real” thyroid tissue and thyroidectomy (8), leading to developing ambiguous concepts such as “generally micro-thyroid tissue” or “residentially microscopic disease”. (9). In this context, this descriptive study was designed as the frequency of residual thyroid tissue post-thyroid biopsy, with total thyroidectomy, or without the mean. or with the technique described in the same surgical act and had histopathological report in the subsequent review of the clinical history. A system of capture type database was designed using the platform RedCap and the sociodemographic and clinical variables associated with its presence were analyzed, or not, by means of a bivariate statistical analysis performed using the Stata version 15 system. A total of 1923 total thyroidectomy with or without central ganglionic emptying, by benign or malignant pathology in the two hospital centers during the period evaluated. An 88% of the surgeries were performed in women, with a mean age for the sample of 46 years (DE 14.2).
