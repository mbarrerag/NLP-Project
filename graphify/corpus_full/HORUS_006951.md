# Magdalena

The Magdalena is the largest (1543km) South America river flowing into the Caribbean Sea, with a discharge of 7200m3/s; in the range of 4068-10,287m3/s monthly. The basin covers a wide range of climates due to the high elevation mountains (>5000ma.s.l.), from alpine tundra in the high mountains to tropical humid and semiarid in lowlands. It also contains several large swamps; the largest is La Mojana, which is an inland delta covering 6000km2 at the maximum flooded extension. Two of the world’s “hotspots” of biodiversity are in the Magdalena: Tropical Andes and Tumbes-Choco-Magdalena. The basin is home to 80% of the Colombian population, provides 80%-90% of the country crops (mainly coffee), and contributes to 85% of the country GDP. As a result of human activities, the Magdalena basin has suffered severe environmental damages. One is deforestation: Since 1990, 42% of the forests have been destroyed, mainly gallery forests and tropical dry forests. In 2010, less than 15% of the original forest cover remained. Deforestation resulted in increased erosion and river sediment load. Water pollution is also an important problem; nearly 62% of the country’s total organic load of contaminants is concentrated in the Magdalena basin.
