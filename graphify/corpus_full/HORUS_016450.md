# PERSPECTIVES FROM 17:30 GERPH BIOLOGICAL COLLECTION.

Knowing the diversityof parasites thataffect wildliferequires extensive samplingwhose resultingmaterial isa snapshotofparasiticinteractions,includingorganisms notintended to studyinitially.Colombiaisamegadiverseterritoryinecosystems andoccupies thetop places in the world in vertebrates¿ diversity, which favors the variety of parasites. Biological collection GERPH, Study Group Host-Parasite Relationship (by its acronym in Spanish), is the only one of its kind in the country and one of the few functional in the neotropics. This collection comprises more than 20,000 blood smears, 4700 blood samples/DNA extracted, or tissues from approximately 6500 individuals of amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals and their blood parasites. The most prevalent parasites correspond to Haemosporida, Adeleorina, Trypanosoma, and Nematodes. These samples have been collected since the beginning of the 1998 up to the present day, including several environments in protected and transformed areas such as flooded savannahs, Choco and Amazonian rain forests, Andean cloud forest, and paramo, Xerophytic environments, among others. This biological material has been the starting point for studies about genetic and morphological diversity, revealing several new species of Apicomplexa, geographic and host range expansions. Besides, the field information associated with the vertebrate species has allowed approaches to ecological aspects of these interactions and hematological studies on vertebrates. However, there is much to be done in terms of analysis, mainly in herpetofauna and mammals, so we greatly encourage collaboration between groups focused on studying host-hemoparasites interactions in the neotropics by joining efforts to train people, as well as facilities for the exchange of preserved material
