# Role of language-based surgery or lingual tonsillectomy in the management of obstructive sleep apnea in paediatric patients at the Hospital Foundation of Mercy

Obstructive sleep apnea (AOS) is a frequent entity in the paediatric population, with a prevalence of 1 to 5%. It affects the school and social development of paediatric patients who suffer it (it is associated with hyperactivity, aggression, low school performance and learning disorders). The first line surgical management of obstructive sleep apnea in children is based on palatin and/or adenoidectomy, with a success rate greater than 80%. Given the high success of adenoamygdalectomy as first line surgery, it is controversial the realization of linguistic amygdalectomy or basic language resection surgery in the same surgical time, so this has been proposed as rescue surgery. An observational study was carried out, in which the post-surgery improvement of patients taken to adenoamigdalectomy or adenoamigdalectomy was evaluated. a retrospective observational study was conducted to evaluate the improvement after surgery in patients undergoing adenotonsillectomy or adenotonsillectomy plus linguistic tonsillectomy or tongue base resection surgery, showing that there is not statistically significant difference between both groups. This support the approach of performing a stepwise surgical management in the pediatric population with a diagnosis of sleep apnea
