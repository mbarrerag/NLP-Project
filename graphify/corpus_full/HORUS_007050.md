# Epilepsy surgery in paediatric age: review of results of patients with drug-resistant epilepsy who were taken to surgery at HOMI Foundation Paediatric Hospital La Misericordia for 10 years of follow-up

Pharmaco-resistant epilepsy is defined as the failure of 2 anti-epileptic drugs tolerated and appropriately chosen (1). Epilepsy is a frequent paediatric neurological problem, its prevalence rate ranges from 4.3 to 9.3 cases per 1000 children, and affects 1.2% of the world population (2, 3). Despite the pharmacological treatment, 30 to 40% of patients do not achieve crisis control, and approximately half of these patients become candidates for epilepsy surgery (3). Once the diagnosis is confirmed, treatment options include medication, dietary regimens, immunomodulatory agents and surgery.
