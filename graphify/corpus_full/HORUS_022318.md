# Challenges of Using Remote Sensor Products in Colombian Regional Hydrological Models

Regional hydrologic modeling is a useful tool to assess hydrological parameters and water cycle monitoring in large areas throughout a country. But, in regional hydrologic modeling the spatial resolution of the Digital Elevation Model (DEM) becomes a key factor given that the higher the spatial resolution, the greater the hydrological terrain detail considered. A step back of a high spatial resolution model is the high computational time required in the calibration and validation process. Thus, resampling the DEM to a smaller spatial resolution is usually a common solution to this issue. The objective of this paper is to assess the challenges of resampling remote sensor products (DEM) for building regional hydrologic models in a study area of 30,000 km2 in the Magdalena - Cauca area in Colombia. A DEM is resampled for both the projection and the spatial resolution from 90 m to 2700 m through resampling techniques such as nearest neighbor, majority, cubic, and bilinear. The resampling process is assessed by means of changes in morphometric parameters (i.e., area, average length of the channel, basin average slope, and elevation accuracy). By reducing considerably the spatial resolution of a DEM a leveling effect of the terrain surface affects the basin average slope followed by the average elevation. Affected slope and elevations may impose misrepresentations in simulated hydrographs. Yet, there is a good representation of the drainage lines and the sub-basin area. Nearest-Neighbor and Cubic are better for projecting while Nearest-Neighbor and Bilinear are better for resampling spatial resolution of DEM.
