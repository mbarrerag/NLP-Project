# Isolation of ureolytic ryzo-acteries that induce the pre-meditation of carbonates with potential for the bio-remediation of cation in agricultural soils

The presence of toxic metals in agricultural soils has been evidenced in different plantations. Particularly, in cocoa crops is a problem because in cocoa beans (primary material for the manufacture of chocolate) it has been reported that cadmium (Cd) levels from different parts of the world exceed the critical limit (0.6 mg/kg) established for this fruit, where it is worth noting that the Cd reaches cocoa mainly by translocating the fraction available from the metal in the soil. A strategy to mitigate the presence of Cd in cocoa, is to use bacteria that induce the precipitation of carbonates (MICP) that react with the metal decreasing its availability and translocation from the soil to the fruit. In the first stage of this study a working route was established to isolate rizospheric bacteria (from cocoa crops) obtained with MICP activity, tolerant to the Cd and production of the indoltic acid phytohormone (the latter to reduce the stress generated by the toxic metal, thus encompassing a polyfunctional/integral strategy)
