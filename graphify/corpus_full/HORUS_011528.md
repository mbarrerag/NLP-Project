# EXCENTRIC TRAINING PROTOCOL WITH ISOINERCIAL TECHNOLOGY FOR PREVENTION OF MUSCULARY INFUTBOLISTS

Football presents greater risk of injury, compared to other team sports. In particular, lower limb muscle injuries are the most frequent injuries in the sports environment (Fousejis et al., 2011). Most of these injuries occur through overuse; and generate a significant loss of training time, which affects the quality of life of injured athletes. One of the main factors of injury risk is the deficit or imbalance of muscle strength (Daneshjoo et al., 2013) between the agonistic-antagonistic muscles and between the dominant and non-dominant leg, especially in the lower limb muscle segments. This can lead to inadequate control of body movements and therefore lead to injury (Lu et al., 2012). The greater development of muscle strength occurs when an external force is greater than that produced by the muscle; and this is extended, maintaining the contraction and, with it, a negative work. This process is understood as an eccentric contraction, usually used to slow down or to slow down the movement or to absorb energy (Vogt and Hoppeler, 2014). Therefore, this paper invites the advancement of research regarding the development of eccentric force and its implications in the prevention of sports injuries, so its aim is to present a proposal for an eccentric training protocol for the muscles of lower limbs in the Flywheel machine
