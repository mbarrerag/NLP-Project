# H-STEPS-AHEAD PROBABILISTIC FORECASTS: THEORY AND PRACTICE

Forecasting is a fundamental aspect of temporal and spatio-temporal statistical models. Point forecasts are useful to summarize the modeling effort in a single quantity that can be used to analyze action policies. However, in spite of coming from an optimal forecasting methodology, point forecasts can be misleading since they do not incorporate and report explicitly a measure of accuracy. Interval forecasts come in handy in that regard, but they do not fully grasp the uncertainty of the phenomenon under study, which motivates the development of probabilistic forecasts. Probabilistic forecasting is based on the characterization of the entire predictive distribution of a random outcome of interest. This talk will explore the importance of probabilistic forecasting in applied scenarios, the mechanisms that can be used to generate such forecasts and the existing and new tools proposed to assess their predictive accuracy
