# Sexual pheromone as a strategy for the integrated management of Opsifanes cassina Felder, 1862 in oil palm crops

One of the main insect-plague that currently affects the palmicultor sector of Colombia is Opsiphanes cassina Felder, 1862 (Lepidoptera: Nymphalidae).The foliar consumption of the larval stage of this lepidoptero, makes it an aggressive species of difficult management, causing significant economic losses.The bioecological characteristics of O. cassine in oil palm cultivation indicate that one of the key points for its integrated management is the monitoring and control of adult populations.In this sense, the modification of the behavior in response to the use of stimuli of chemical nature, such as pheromones, is a promising scenario.The aim of this work was to establish the conditions of extraction, chemical analysis and evaluation of the semiochemicals that modulate the sexual behavior of O. casssina. For this, the investigation was developed in three stages: in the first one the behavior of the species under laboratory conditions, the second one was extracted and identified the VOCs released by adults and finally, in the third, the electrophysiological and behavioral structures, and behavioral. Anthenography EAG showed that females are stimulated by compounds, suggesting their possible participation in intraspecific interaction of the species; however, under the conditions used in behavioral evaluation it was not possible to confirm their action attractive. Nevertheless, the identified semiochemicals may constitute an alternative to be incorporated into the MIP of this pest in oil palm cultures, so its evaluation is recommended in the field. .
