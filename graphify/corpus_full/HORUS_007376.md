# Application of innovation strategies in the development of sustainable technologies for surface water remediation and smart materials against COVID-19

The applied innovation strategies led the development of sustainable technologies for the challenges addressed in this thesis. In the first place, to remediate surface waters such as the Doce river and springs of Minas Gerais, Brazil, we examined the possibility of using natural serpentinite mining waste and Macauba endocarpium that underwent an alkaline treatment, as a sustainable alternative for the removal of aluminum(III), iron(III), lead(II) and manganese(II). The study used a Box-Behnken experimental design to examine how initial metal concentration, adsorption dose and adsorption time affect the effectiveness of metal adsorption. The results showed an important performance, with removal rates exceeding 80 % for Al(III), Fe(III) and Pb(II), and 60 % for Mn(II). This study delves into the mechanisms of adsortion, kinetics, isotermas de adsorción y caracterización. The results of the isotherms suggest that the chemisortion is the main mechanism of the chemically treated Macauba endocarp. For serpentinite, fisisortion and chemisortion mechanisms were identified in which the formation of complexes with OH-released groups and ionic exchange with Mg(II) of serpentinite emerged as key contributors to the adsorption process. In addition, the cycles of adsorption of metals and regeneration of the adsorbents were evaluated, showing for the serpentinite a constant adsorption capacity in each cycle and without losses of its adsorption capacity after regeneration. By contrast, the chemically treated Macauba endocarp loses 50% of its adsorption capacity after the first regeneration cycle. The average adsorption capacity of the Macauba serpentinite and endocarpal chemically treated was 0.71 and 0.23 mg g-1, respectively. The chemically treated Macauba showed a greater selectivity for the adsorption of Mg(II) and Pb(II). The combined application of both materials successfully reduced the metal content of the Doce river and water from the springs to potabilization standards. It proposes and evaluates a discontinuous and continuous process to scale the metal adsorption of materials. La evaluación de la tecnología con bases de datos de patentes y artículos mostró la novedad de la invención presentada. The evaluation finds the low cost of this technology. In general, this study shows the potential of both materials as a basis for sustainable and cost-effective methods to treat surface water contamination with Al(III), Fe(III), Pb(II) and Mn(II). The second challenge addressed was the development of intelligent materials against COVID-19. The confinement due to the pandemic began in Brazil in March 2020, coinciding with the start of this thesis’s activities. In March 2024, there were 774 million confirmed cases and 7 million deaths due to the COVID-19 pandemic caused by SARS-CoV-2. This work emphasizes the importance of multidisciplinary collaboration in the creation of monitoring strategies and new materials to control viral spread, especially through contaminated surfaces or phomites. The transmission of SARS-CoV-2 fossils is still not clear. Se propone un nuevo sistema virucida que utiliza complejos de inclusión de clorhexidina (CHX) con β-ciclodextrina (β CD) impregnadas en matrices textiles o de celulosa para abordar esta dificultad. The findings show that these matrices inactivate SARS-CoV-2 and reduce viral levels on surfaces for 30 days. For the preparation of the inclusion compounds, freeze-drying and spraying were used. The spectroscopic and microscopic analyses showed the impregnation of CHX-β CD inclusion compounds in the matrices. The scaling of production using spray drying allowed us to obtain virucidal matrices on an industrial scale. This innovative and long-lasting surface disinfection option produced in this thesis is the result of a translational approach in response to the pandemic .
