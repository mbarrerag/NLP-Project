# Re-Construction of resilient territories in the Caribbean: Socio-environmental conflicts, Recovery of cultural, architectural and agroecological heritage in Providencia and Santa Catalina

Understanding colonialism in the Caribbean means a way of understanding extractive development and nature on a planetary scale from the perspective of environmental history and Latin American political ecology, as a global centre for the extraction of nature and the dispossession of peoples and geostrategic control, in a relationship of production that is structured in the process of incessant accumulation of capital. Extractivism, in this sense, is not only a mode of production, but a form of participation in the development of capitalism corresponding to the period in which it operates as a world market, through a concentration and centralization of the progressive capital. In this context, Hurricane Iota destroyed much of the cultural (architectonic) and environmental (agro-ecological and fisheries) heritage in the islands of Providencia and Santa Catalina on November 16, 2020. This project seeks to support the processes of rebuilding the memory of its cultural heritage based on the diffusion of materials that strengthen the root culture from the agroecological practices and the reconstruction of a series of cartographic materials that strengthen both the knowledge and the memory of this Caribbean region for a Atlas of conflicts, was based on the construction of the communities
