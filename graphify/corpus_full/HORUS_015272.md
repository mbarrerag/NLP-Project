# TRANSITION TO OCCIDENT FOOD HABITAT: ITS IMPACT ON LOCAL AGRICULTURAL PRODUCTION AND ON THE NUTRITIONAL SITUATION OF COLOMBIAN POPULATION

The nutritional transition (adoption of Western food habits) is presented in different Latin American countries, due to socio-economic dynamics such as globalization, neoliberal policies and increasing exposure of the population to media related to these strategies. In Colombia, this transition generated changes in food production and consumption models, especially since the 1990s with the implementation of FTAs, decreasing local food production and increasing its imports, affecting biodiversity and altering productive techniques by increasing in monoculture areas and demand for greater competitiveness of farmers-producers in the market. This work analyses impacts of economic opening policies and exposure of the Colombian population to media that promote unhealthy eating habits. From Bourdieu’s perspective, it is discussed that these phenomena favor symbolic domination by expanding a dominant culture, causing the transfer of eating habits as cultural capital and the management of food as economic capital. Colombia has identified 54.2 per cent of food insecurity in households, increasing overweight and obesity in 56.4% of the population (ENSIN, 2015), due to the double nutritional burden caused by high caloric consumption but low in nutrients, resulting from the consumption of chronic diseases, generating an increase in the number of chronic diseases
