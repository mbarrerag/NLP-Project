# BIODEGRADABLE POLYMERS AND NATURAL ADDITIVES AS A REPLACEMENT FOR POLYETHYLENE IN THE PACKAGING OF COLOMBIAN AGRI-FOOD PRODUCTS

In response to the growing global concern and restrictions on the use of non-biodegradable plastics and toxic additives of petrochemical origin, developing countries such as Colombia must develop sustainable and efficient packaging alternatives for the conservation and marketing of their agri-food products since this is a sector of particular interest for their economy. Plastics such as low-density polyethylene (LDPE), in addition to its additives, are used in the packaging of crops and tropical fruits during post-harvest. Any material that aims to replace them sustainably must have a high degree of biodegradation but also suitable physicochemical and mechanical properties, very low food contact migration, high consumer acceptance, and scalability of its production. This work proposes an alternative biobased packaging development to replace LDPE for post-harvest handling of banana fruits. Initially, mixtures of polylactic acid (PLA), polyhydroxyalkanoates (from residual biomass from cocoa processing), and polybutylene adipate terephthalate (PBAT) were evaluated to obtain flexible packaging via blown extrusion. In the second stage, the development of a composite matrix with synthetically modified microcellulose (MMC) was evaluated to give the flexible packaging a higher barrier to water vapor permeation. For these tests, the ratios of polymer components and additives and the appropriate extrusion conditions were determined.
