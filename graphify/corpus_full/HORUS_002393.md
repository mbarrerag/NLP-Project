# The uses of soft and intelligent power in the transmission of the American Way of Life in the Middle East through the Egyptian elites 1952-1963

This paper starts from the hypothesis that the US and its power elites transmitted the American Way of Life in Egypt and the Middle East through the project of the Egyptian elites: nasseristism. Therefore, soft and intelligent power would be used by the US, the Egyptian elites and their allies in the Middle East to secure the American interests in the region. To verify this hypothesis, primaries and secondary sources were used in six languages and different historiographies. The categories to analyze are orientalism, as a discourse of racial superiority, realpolitik, as a policy of national interest, and elite, as a superior group of people who lead a country, which is complemented by “eleites of power” of Charles Wright Mills, superior positions of political, economic and military power, and “lobbies” of John Mearsheimer and Stephen Walt, as a group that influences Israel in the US foreign policy. It was concluded that the US propagated the American Way of Life in Egypt through its image of “anti-colonial power” from the 1820s, and the US-wide economy in the Netherlands and Stephen Walt, as a group that influences Israel in the US.. The analyzed categories are orientalism, as a speech of racial superiority, realpolitik, as policy of national interest, and elite, as a superior group of persons who rule the country. The last one complete “power elite” of Charles Wright Mills, higher circles who control the political, economic and military power. It is also associated to “lobbies” of John Mearsheimer and Stephen Walt, a coalition of individuals and institutions that influence the US-foreign policy in a Pro-Israel Direction. In conclusion, US has expanded the American Way of Life since the 1820s through its image as “anticolonial power” to differentiate itself from France, Britain and the Ottoman Empire. The American Way of life was adapted to the USSerist politics and expanded the Stance and regional power of its influence from France, Britain and the Ottoman Empire
