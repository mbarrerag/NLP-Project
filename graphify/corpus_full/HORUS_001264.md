# Seismic attenuation in the Middle Magdalena Valley, Colombia, and possible relation to fluid lubricated fault zones

The Middle Magdalena Valley (MMV) is an intermontane basin within the zone of convergence of the Caribbean, Nazca, and South American plates; a region of intense deformation and high seismic activity at several depths. Fault activity in the MMV can be influenced by migration of natural or anthropogenic fluids along faults. We explore this link as well as the effect of rock heterogeneity using intrinsic, scattering, and total seismic attenuation. Seismic attenuation from 1653 earthquakes between 2016 and 2022 is presented and related to structures on the Transandean seismic section. The results show a dominance of intrinsic over scattering seismic attenuation. Precipitation correlates with seismic attenuation, although there is a time offset of ∼1 year. The effect of water injection for enhanced oil recovery however is below the detection limit of our method. The Serranía de San Lucas west of the MMV, exhibits intrinsic seismic attenuation, suggesting infiltration of fluids along the Cimitarra fault. A similar but less pronounced attenuation is observed in the Bucaramanga and La Salina faults, east of the MMV. These results show a relationship between precipitation and active faulting via fluid fault lubrication, which could be important for assessing seismic hazards in the region
