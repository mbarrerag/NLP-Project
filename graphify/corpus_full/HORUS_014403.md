# Production of non-ester biodiesel by catalytic deoxygenation of palm oil with hydrogen generation in situ.

The development of advanced biofuels is an option that contributes to the energy transition in the short and medium term. Thus, in this research, the obtaining of biodiesel was verified experimentally by catalytic deoxygenation of palm oil with hydrogen generated in situ instead of feeding it. Two catalytic systems capable of generating it were defined, by catalytic transfer and/or reformed in aqueous phase, from donors as formic acid and ethanol, to then hydrotreat the triglycerides. They were selected as active phases platinum and paladium supported 5% carbon, as well as their equimatic mixture. To study primarily the yield of hydrocarbons and selectivity towards hydrodeoxygenation, the process with hydrogen excess of 50% and 100%, at 250 °C and 300 °C. The 5% Pt/C system with morphic acid was considered as the most efficient and the most efficient production of hydrocarbons was obtained
