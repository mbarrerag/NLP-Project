# Food sovereignty and public policies in Providencia Island and Santa Catalina

This research project analyses the scope and coherence of the food policies of the local government of the islands of Providencia and Santa Catalina in accordance with the ancestral knowledge of the root community after Hurricane Iota of 2020. As a perspective of analysis, political ecology is taken to understand the set of social relations, conceptions and power relations present in the practices of food sovereignty studied. As a horizon of analysis, food sovereignty is taken as a counterposition to food security, which only deals with the availability and access of products, while food sovereignty poses as fundamental the ability to decide on what, how and who produces, that is to say, establishes the importance of the self-determination of the population over the public food policies, in this way the peasant, in our case the root community becomes also producers of food, as well as carriers of valuable knowledge for society and agroecological managers. Based on a quantitative and qualitative analysis on the public food policies of the last 7 years in the region and on the social representations self-perceived by the root community, an analysis of the scope and coherence of public policy will be established with the root knowledge about food practices
