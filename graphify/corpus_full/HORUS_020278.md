# Entrepreneurship as a driver of economic growth in Colombia

This academic paper studies the effect of entrepreneurship on Colombian economic growth during the period 2011 to 2019. A model is proposed in which the determinants of entrepreneurship are incorporated in a systemic way and it is sought to find how this generates effects on the growth of the country's GDP. Using an augmented Cobb-Douglas production function and a linear econometric model, the results fail to demonstrate that in Colombia there is empirical evidence that entrepreneurship actually generates significant changes in GDP, contrasted with the increase in these types of businesses. This conclusion is consistent with the existing findings in the literature considering that it is not possible for the Colombian case to differentiate the new fast-growing companies (dynamic ventures) with the creation of new companies in general, additionally it is concluded that public policy and the different institutional elements that interact in the ecosystem have not been able to generate effective support for the development of high growth potential ventures
