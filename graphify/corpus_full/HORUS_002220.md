# The emerging role of nanotechnology in photoprotection

Ultraviolet (UV) radiation causes long-and short-term skin damage, such as erythema and skin cancer. Therefore, the use of sunscreens is essential. However, concerns about UV filter safety have prompted exploration into alternative solutions, with nanotechnology emerging as a promising possibility. This review searched studies utilizing nanocarriers to encapsulate sunscreens to enhance their efficacy and safety. Polymeric nanoparticles, solid lipid nanoparticles, liposomes, nanoemulsions, and nanocrystals are frequently employed to encapsulate organic and inorganic UV filters and natural antioxidants. Nanocarriers have demonstrated benefits including reduced active ingredient usage, increased sun protection factor (SPF), and mitigated photo instability. Notably, they also decreased the skin absorption of UV filters. In summary, nanocarriers represent a viable strategy for improving sunscreen formulations, offering enhanced physicochemical properties and improved photo protective effects, thereby addressing concerns regarding UV filter safety and efficacy in cosmetic applications
