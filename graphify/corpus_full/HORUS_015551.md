# ELECTROCOAGULATION PROCESSES APPLIED TO WASTEWATER FROM COSMETIC INDUSTRY

This work compiles the results and the applicability of the laboratory-scale electrocoagulation process in wastewater of the cosmetic industry. The experiments carried out focused on the analysis of chemical oxygen demand (COD), volatile organic compounds (VOC) and quality indicators such as turbidity (NTU). The influence of the electrode material, pH and retention time has been studied in the electrocoagulation process. The treatment allowed the elimination of the COD of 45.6%, 53.7% and 65.0%, at different pHs (3, 6 and 8 respectively) using Al electrodes (El-Al) as anode. The EC treatment was effective in removing turbidity, achieving a reduction from 77 to 98% for the El-Al anode and a reduction from 54 to 93% when using El-Fe as anode. The best results were obtained using Al electrodes as anodes at pH = 8, using a retention time of 20 and 30 min, with a current density of 18.5 A/m2. The HS-SPME-GC-MS analysis revealed the ability of the process to reduce the concentration of some toxic micropollutants present in cosmetic wastewater such as D-limonene, Hexyl acetate, 1-ethyl-2- methyl benzene and Mesitylene
