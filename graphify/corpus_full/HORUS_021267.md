# INVOLVEMENT OF A DURING POLINEZING INSTECTS MONITORING PROGRAMME IN THE UNIFIABLE SABAH OF THE NORTHERN ORINOCIA

Pollination is one of the essential services for the optimal functioning of terrestrial ecosystems. This study will document the community of pollinating day insects in the savannah ecosystem in the Arauca department with a view to generating a monitoring protocol that can be implemented periodically. Four tree species of known phenology (Vitex orinocensis, Handroanthus chrysanthus, Cordia collococca, Sapium glanulosum) will be used and are widely visited by multiple species of insects during the flowering phenophase (January-March, dry season). The frequency of visitation of insects and the richness of species associated with focal trees (6 individuals per species) will be quantified. The use of complementary techniques of direct observation and collection of specimens in the area surrounding flowers will be used. In addition, the reproductive system of the focal tree species will be described.
