# REGIONAL COMPETITIVENESS, EFFICIENCY AND PRODUCTIVITY

The recent attention to study some techniques to incorporate spatial analysis into economic models is due to two factors: the first, it is related to a theoretical motivation, that is, the interest within economic theory in models that involves interaction between heterogeneous agents.Involving neighborhood effects; and re-establishing spatial aspects of Marshallian externalities, agglomeration economies and other spinolovers that are fundamental to the new economic geography.The second factor, is related to the availability of regional databases and geographic information systems (GIS); that is, data containing the location of the observation units.The purpose of the paper is to estimate efficiency in six sectors of the Colombian manufacturing industry, through a stochastic frontier production model, and considering the influence of spatial dependence on such estimation.The results indicate that the industrial sectors that have a greater share in the total industrial aggregate value, are located in the most developed departments.The greatest efficiency in sectors such as textiles, metals, food and beverages, leads to the industry there to suggest that the spatial dependence in other regions, that is, that the empirical trend is not being presented in Colombia
