# Study of the potential effect of neonatal hypoglucemia on the brain astrocytes of ratas

The first 24 hours postpartum are fundamental for all newborns, due to the physiological process of self-regulation of the systemic glucose concentration to be exceeded. This period is called transient hypoglycaemia (Stanley CA et al.) (Mother breast less than 6 months old, 2019). pes (pediatric endocrine society) in 2015 (thomton ps et al. 2015), advised a series of recommendations on neonatal management among them: initiating immediate treatment if blood glucose levels are lower than normal <60 mg/dL or 3.3 mmol/L. Despite this, glucaemia is currently tolerated for healthy neonates, which vary between 45 and 50 mg/dL (Lezcan et al., 2021), the studies carried out may not specify whether the interval is safe for the neonatal brain. Considering this variability, it is important to clarify the conditions of hypoglycaemia (Shah R et al. 2019; Iklé JM et al. 2021). Research of the last decades, relate to the astrocytes with fundamental processes for the central nervous system (Oberheim-Va. One prolonged (Zhou, 2008), G3 Controls (Liu, 2013). After exposure to treatment, the immunoreactivity of three proteins characteristic of astrocytes will be evaluated: fibrilar fibrillal acid protein (GFAP), Glutamine Synthetic (GS) and glutamate transporter EAAT-1 (GLT-1 in rodents) (Vizuete et al. 2017; Muñoz Valencia et al. 2019), in 2 specific brain areas (Corteza parieto occipital and CA1 in the Hippocampo), using immunofluorescence for immunodetection (Establishment of immunotintion protocols for GFAP, NeuN and BrdU antigens in rat brain, 2016). Hoping to find significant differences in the location of proteins that allow to contribute to the understanding of the effects produced by hypoglycaemia in neonatal astrocytes
