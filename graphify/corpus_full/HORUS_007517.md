# OBTENTION OF THE CHLORIDE DIFFUSION COEFFICIENT OF FLY ASH BLENDED CONCRETE THROUGH RESISTIVITY MODELLING

Concrete structures face durability challenges due to corrosion of embedded reinforcement steel, mainly induced by chloride ions from de-icing salts or coastal environments. Assessing and predicting chloride ion penetration into concrete involves several methods, with electrical methods being the most used nowadays. This study explores the theoretical aspects of concrete resistivity, focusing on concrete-fly ash mixtures. A multispecies model is proposed to simulate concrete resistivity, allowing the optimization of the intrinsic chloride diffusion coefficient. The model considers the Nernst-Planck equation and statistical correlations to determine the ionic concentrations of the pore solution. Experimental results indicate that resistivity decreases with increasing fly ash content, a fact that influences the bulk conductivity of the material. The study also introduces the Formation Factor for calculating chloride diffusion coefficients and highlights the importance of considering ionic depletion in fly ash samples. The proposed approach provides a rapid and cost-effective alternative for evaluating concrete’s transport properties. Experimental data from mixtures with different fly ash content corroborate the model's effectiveness and reveal the interaction among resistivity, ionic concentrations, and diffusion coefficients
