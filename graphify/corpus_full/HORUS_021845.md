# Evaluation of the effect of centrifugal force on the efficiency of the transfection of umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells.

A human mesenchymal oestromal cell (hCEM) is an adult stem cell characterized by its capacity for self-renewal and differentiation. They also present several advantages such as immunomodulatory properties that make them promising source in regenerative medicine. Therefore, the use of these cells as gene carriers has been one of the main focus of several studies. In recent years, non-viral vectors still present some limitations, being the most important the low transfection efficiencies in the CMS. It has been theorized that this low efficiency is caused by multiple factors related to the properties of the nanoparticles, including the size, load, degradation, endosomal exhaust and transport of the cergo to the nucleus. In addition, it has been demonstrated that the type of cell and the proliferation of the cell cycle are an important impact on the delivery of genes. In summary, the transfection depends directly on the chemical nature of the material (vector) that covers the gene and its non-specific interaction with all the pathways within the cell.
