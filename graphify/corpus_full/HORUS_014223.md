# Flood X-ray: paradigm of water-sensitive cities in a case study in southern western Bogotá

This thesis problematizes several issues related to water management in the south-west of Bosa, Bogotá, and explores how the approach of cities sensitive to water can offer alternatives to address the urban and environmental challenges present in this area. The research is framed in a historical context in which the cities have emerged near rivers and bodies of water due to their advantages in agriculture and supply. However, since the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, the practice of channelling and burying rivers has prevailed as a response to pollution and to free space for urban development, especially in countries of the southern cone.This strategy, besides invisibilizing the bodies of water, has intensified environmental, social and aesthetic problems by not offering adequate sanitation solutions or recognizing water as a fundamental component of the territory (Novotny, 2010; Wantzen, 2019).The problems of flooding and loss of social services is another phenomenon that has affected the natural cycles of water.The extensive use of materials such as concrete and asphalt has diminished the ability to infiltrate the soil, increasing the superficial runoff and, with this, the problems of the urban areas and the economic cycle (the impact of the water, the dynamic). The lack of planning to address the infiltration and sustainable management of rainwater has accentuated the vulnerability of the area. The research focuses on understanding how these problems intertwine with living modes, conflicts in public management and the possibilities of community action to mitigate their effects. Despite the high rainfall in Bogotá, with an annual average of 1000 mm, the city faces a particular vulnerability to droughts due to its exclusive dependence on surface sources such as reservoirs, without taking advantage of underground aquifers. This situation highlights the urgency of adopting a water-sensitive approach that promotes the retention, use and reuse of rainwater. In urban design, this approach involves structuring public space in a way that optimizes ecosystem services, reduces runoff and encourages community participation in water management
