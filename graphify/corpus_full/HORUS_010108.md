# CURRENT SITUATION OF LONGOIFILA GAGNE PRODIPLOSE (DIPTERA: CECODOMYIIDAE) IN COLOMBIA CULTIVE AND PERSPECTIVE TO CONTROL

The mosquita Prodiplosis longigfila is a key pest in tomato crops in Colombia. Larvae is fed by foliage, flower and fruit which causes economic damage and losses up to 100% of the crop. This work proposes strategies for control of the insect in tomato based on the review of previously published research, some in co-authorship. Height and precipitation limit the population distribution of P. longifula and these are genetically homogeneous in tomato. The state of egg lasts 1 day on average and the time of development larva-adult is almost 14 days to 26.2 °C ± 1.34; 61.5 % ± 6.3 RM. The pupa develops in the soil from where adults emerge who are active and copulan during the scotophane. The main method of control of the insect is the spraying of insecticides in the calendar scheme and is less effective. Therefore, it is necessary to implement different strategies of control.
