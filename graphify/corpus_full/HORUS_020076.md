# Characterization and potential risk factors of low back pain and cervical crews of command of the National Police of Colombia,

Introduction The occurrence of musculoskeletal problems have been well-stablished as one of the most common disorders in rotary wing and fixed wing aviation pilots. Rate prevalence between 30% and 92% have been reported in the literature, being a condition that alters the quality of life, enhance episodes of fatigue, and affects the operational safety. This study aims to determine the prevalence of cervical and lumbar pain, in aircrew pilots, and unmanned aerial systems pilots, as well as to estimate the most likely factors related with the occurrence of both entities, based on their sociodemographic, occupational and operational characteristics in a population of personnel belonging to the aviation division of the Colombian National Police. Methods This is a retrospective cross-sectional study that enclosed 70.9% of the entire population universe to be studied (N=261), which included 81 (31.03%) fixed-wing crew, 145 (55.56%) rotary-wing and 35 (13.41%) unmanned aerial systems pilots (UAV). A three section questionnaire was applied, in order to assess demographic, low back/neck pain, and associated occupational factors. Results The general prevalence of low back pain found for the last 12 months is 31.42%, and 70.1% for acute pain in the last 3 months. A significant risk association was found with the fact of belonging to the crew of rotary-wing aircraft, with low back pain (OR: 2.18, C 1.26 – 3.77). Being a fixed-wing crew member was found as a protective factor (OR 0.34, CI 0.18 -0.66). The prevalence of neck pain in the last 12 months was 26.67%, on the other hand, the prevalence of neck pain in the last 3 months was 58.5%. The highest prevalence (37.14%) of neck pain in our study was reported by operators of unmanned aerial systems, followed by crew members of fixed and rotary-wing aircraft, with 23.46% and 24.14%, respectively. An association was found as a protective factor for cervical pain (OR 0.47, CI: 0.34 -0.66) with physical activity greater than 20 minutes, at least three times a week. No other significant demographic, operational or occupational risk associations was found. Conclusions The prevalence of low back pain is consistent with that reported in the literature in the military population. Aircraft type and age were established as independent risk factors for the development of low back pain. The prevalence of neck pain is lower than the one described in the available evidence in the general population, although it shares similar rates when compared with the military population not categorized as fighter pilots. Operators of unmanned aerial systems pilots have been established as a new target population for researches on ergonomics, prevalence of morbidity, and operational safety impact, given the predominant prevalence of neck pain showed. New follow-up studies, causality researches, and treatment effectiveness studies are recommended to strengthen the evidence for the findings described in this study
