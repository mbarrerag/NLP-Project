# AMONIACO TOPD UNDER CONDITIONS OF NON-READSORTION

The Temperature Programmed Desorption (TPD) technique is a very popular technique for quantifying acidity in acid solids, especially when using ammonia. To make the measurements the acid solid is saturated with NH3 which is then disorbed when heating the solid at a specific speed. The NH3 is quantified with the help of an appropriate detector. The most popular detectors, are mass spectrometers and thermal conductivity detector. The first detector is more selective than the second, but for the case of ammonia, when the solid releases water, both detectors have problems to avoid water interference. Recently the ammonia TPD technique was published with an ionic conductivity detector that does not present interference with water and whose calibration allows easy traceability to the SI [1]. The detection of the acid molecule is integral (accumulative) non-differentiation, which allows working at very low heating ramps. Independent from the traditional ammonia TPD detector is the possibility of readsorption.
