# TOWARDS A WORK WORLD STORY IN A PETROLERO KEY

This paper argues the need to rethink the history of the workers' movement by taking into account the historical specificities of the forms of work and the social configurations that derive from them. Although this idea is not novel in itself, it displays a great theoretical-methodological potential when it is attempted to put it into practice in a specific case, such as that of enclaves. There is an interesting theoretical production about this form of resource exploitation in Latin America, but, the enclave tends to be seen as the context (background) of the workers' movement, limiting its analytical potential.2 An understanding of its role as a shaper of relations of different order and its impact in the long term, widens and complements the understanding of what would be a workers' history, in benefit of what we have called the world of work. Its result, moreover, opens up a perspective of historical analysis for current problems, such as ethnic or socio-environmental conflicts
