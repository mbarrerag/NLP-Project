# Prisons for Women and Divorce: Sexual Differentiation of Sentences in the Viceroyalty of the New Kingdom of Granada, 17th to 19th centuries

The historiography of the administration of justice places the origin of the prison as a punitive device in industrially developed societies. In previous periods, such as the end of the middle age and during the modern age, it is postulated that this space was used to house criminals while their judicial processes were being carried out and not as a tool of correction and corporal punishment. Although this thesis is widely demonstrated for men offenders, since in modern age they paid sentences especially through the modality of imprisonment (penalty work) and not from imprisonment, in the case of women the prison had a unique trajectory. Heir to voluntary or compulsory gathering spaces, he sought to moralize those who threatened the social and family order. From a gender perspective, this article aims to demonstrate that the prisons of Women and the Divorce are part of a process of sexual differentiation of sentences, with which the punitive function that the jail spaces will subsequently have is initiated. The the thesis is demonstrated from reports of visits to prisons and sentences in judicial processes in the Vireinato of the New Kingdom of Granada
