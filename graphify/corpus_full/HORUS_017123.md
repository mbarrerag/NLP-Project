# Evaluation of an Exclusive Area of Artisanal Fishing-ZEPA, as a strategy for adaptation to climate change for artisanal fishermen in the Nariña Pacific.

The impacts of climate change are causing significant detriments in the welfare of coastal populations of artisanal fishermen; threatening their main source of income, marine fishing resource, affecting food security. In addition, there are other factors such as overfishing, poverty and low educational achievement, which increase their vulnerability. The creation of conditions that promote the capacity of fishermen to adapt to climate change will contribute to reducing their vulnerability to impacts. Therefore, the general objective was to determine an Exclusive Area of Fishing Artisanal-ZEPA potential (marin/stuarina) as a strategy of adaptation to climate change for the artisanal fishermen of the study area; using a decision-making tool (Marxan) and the opinion of the fishermen, to identify favorable areas considering ecological, social, cultural and economic factors. Three scenarios were modeled with the program and participatory mapping was carried out with a group of fishermen to obtain the fourth scenario. Subsequently, the areas in common between the four scenarios to obtain the SPA. In addition, surveys were conducted to validate the SPA as an alternative to reduce the pressure of anthropogenic and natural threats. The similarity between the sample of fishermen, the marxan and the software. identified that the implementation of a SPA in the territory is feasible as a strategy for adapting to climate change for artisanal fisheries, because it was possible to establish the area that met the objectives set and the acceptance of the fishing community was achieved in the face of the possible benefits that would bring it the implementation of a SPA, identifying that they believe it would benefit them to improve aspects associated with the development of their productive activity
