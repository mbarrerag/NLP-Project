# Study of fractional condensation of coffee cisco pyrolysis gases.

The condensation experiments of pyrolytic coffee cisco vapours were carried out in a two-stage fractional condensation system, with condensation temperatures of 80 and 0 °C respectively. Condensers of three materials with different surface energies were tested, in order to observe their effect on the yield and composition of the biooil, using the techniques of GC/MS, FTIR and Karl Fischer titration in the liquid fractions obtained. The biooil yields per unit area obtained were proportional to the energy of the condensation surface, which is attributed to the greater mojability of these surfaces that affects the critical size of the drops during condensation. In both stages, fractions with different characteristics were obtained: the condensed liquid at 80 °C had a lower humidity and was abundant in phenols and sugars, while the condensed at 0 °C was rich in lighter compounds such as acids and ketones, which gave it potential for the refining of biofuels.
