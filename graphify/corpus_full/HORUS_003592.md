# REDOX ENTITLING

Another type of reaction in which we can use the principles of titration that we have studied in the two previous practices is redox reactions. These are characterized by chemical systems in which, in addition to changes in the mass of the reacting substances, changes are also present in the loads. These types of reactions involve the presence of two types of substances: an oxidizing agent (which oxidizes the other substance) and a reducing agent (which reduces the other substance). In this practice we will use a redox titration to determine the concentration of an aqueous solution of hydrogen peroxide, commercially known as oxygenated water. This latter is a substance that we know very well, since it is used daily for several purposes, such as discoloring the hair, removing stains or cleaning wounds. It is well known that this must be stored in opaque containers, since, by the action of light, hydrogen peroxide tends to break down easily forming water and gaseous oxygen, which is why it is always convenient to titrate hydrogen peroxide solutions to know exactly its concentration.
