# CRIMINAL RESPONSIBILITY OF INDUSTRIALS IN NUREMBERG’S POST-TERRITORIAL JURICES AND THEIR TEACHINGS FOR THE COLOMBIAN CASE

Summary The twelve subsequent trials of Nuremberg were personal, advanced against National Socialist criminals by the American National Military Tribunal. The trials were divided into professions and guilds within the Nazi Government. In the Flick, I. G. Farben and Krupp cases, large German industrialists were convicted for their participation in the commission of war crimes and crimes against humanity during the Second World War, as the relationship between industry and the regime was revealed. In addition, standards were established to investigate and prosecute this type of macro-criminality and to determine the criminal responsibility of directors of signatures, in accordance with international criminal law. These criteria may be important for studying cases such as Colombian, which are investigated by thousands of civilians who belonged to corporations and companies involved in the execution of such crimes during the armed conflict
