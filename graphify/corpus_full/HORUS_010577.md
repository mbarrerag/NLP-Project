# Modeling of development and building of peace in the substitution of coal-fired cultivating substances. Analysis of Community participation in the cases of Bolivia and Colombia.

This article makes a comparative analysis of the policies of control and substitution of coca crops in Bolivia and Colombia, respectively, based on the contrast between two approaches of peace and development, post-neoliberal versus neoliberal, and the place occupied by the variable of community participation in relations between the State, institutions and communities. It is concluded that, although in both cases community participation is presented as the fundamental axis to confront the phenomenon of coca crops, it is evident that the State-community relationship in the case of Bolivia has been successful because of the importance of linking grassroots associations in the design and implementation of policies against bottom-up cultivation through the Cato Agreement, achieving its effective control and containment. In the case of Colombia, on the contrary, the State continues to reproduce technocratic and focal measures top-down in which social participation is reduced to a legitimizing function in a context of permanent presence of crops, conflict and violence in various rural areas of the country
