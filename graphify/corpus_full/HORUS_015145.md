# INTRODUCTION TO THE FUNDAMENTAL CONCEPTS OF CHEMICAL: A PHYSICAL CHEMICAL APPROXIMATION

If we analyze the history of science in the last 300 years we see that scientific knowledge advances much faster than its diffusion and assimilation in a coherent way by the scientific community and, worse still, by society, in general. Sample of this is, for example, that in the xxi century there are many people who do not accept the scientific fact of evolution, the majority of scientists have a misconception of the laws of thermodynamics and very few understand the laws of physics. In chemistry we continue to use concepts without a solid support in experimentation or in the laws of nature like that of orbital, electronic par or resonance. In addition, we use models of chemical bonding based on myths and misinterpretations, we relate the temperature to the average kinetic energy of particles, the heat is assimilated as a form of energy, the entropy is related to disorder, we think, from the point of view of ontological, that matter and energy are at different levels, etc. This book aims to introduce the basic concepts necessary to understand the chemical processes from the point of view of the laws of nature that we know to the beginnings of the xxi century. In this book we understand the law, we do not understand the fact, we do This book has eight chapters. It is recommended that chapters from one to three dealing with what is science?, the kinetic-molecular theory of matter and thermodynamics are read in the order in which they are written. Once read these, the last chapters, which deal with liquids, solids, chemical kinetics, units and models of the atom based on physics, can be read or studied in any order, although it is recommended that this book be read in the order in which it was written
