# A SPATIAL RANDOMNESS TEST BASED ON THE BOX-COUNTING DIMENSION FOR POINT PROCESS ON LINEAR NETWORKS

Statistical modelling of a spatial point pattern often begins by testing the hypothesis of spatial randomness. In this work, we consider the same problem for point process on linear networks which is a more complex geometric space and where the classical properties of a point process change. Over the last two decades, many works in the literature have tried to extend different methodologies for the analysis of point patterns in the plane to the context of the linear networks, encountering great challenges due to the data visualization is not intuitive, the lack of geometric inhomogeneity and the high computational cost. For the purely spatial case, Caballero et al. (2022) propose a statistical test of spatial randomness based on the fractal dimension (Stoyan and Stoyan, 1994; Falconer, 2004), calculated through the box-counting method. We extend this methodology with the aim to obtain a suitable method for testing if a point process on linear network is completely random and also to discriminate between aggregation or inhibition behaviour of the point patterns. Box-counting dimension estimates the Hausdorff dimension of the spatial point pattern seen as a geometric structure called fractal and has the advantage that it is invariant to translations or rotations of the fractal. Furthermos, box-counting dimension provide the advantage of not having to assume stationarity or pseudostationarity of the underlying point process on the linear networks
