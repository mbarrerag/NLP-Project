# INDIGENOUS LAITICE: POLITICAL VISITABILITY AND TERRITORIAL BUILDING OF URBAN INDIGENOUS PEOPLE.

This article presents information on the urban indigenous population in Leticia (Colombian Amazon) in relation to authors who have claimed that indigenous urbanization corresponds to a model of high mobility between the city and the extra-urban territories, where urban housing is part of a system of multi-situated housing. (multi-sied dwellings) Our data reveal an urban indigenous population that has long established in the city, without active links with the territories of origin and little access to land in the peri-urban area. The oldest Tikuna and cocama populations and the early immigration of downtown people form the peri-urban indigenous population of Leticia, which has political and territorial visibility since the 1980s with legal recognition of their territories and authorities.The urban indigenous population is more than twice the peri-urban indigenous population, but the first have barely gained political visibility since the 2010s, with the creation of an indigenous lobby called CAPIUL.
