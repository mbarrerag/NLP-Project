# Adsorption of the emerging contaminant caffeine in aqueous medium using modified clay

In this research, we studied the adsorption of caffeine on a bentonite modified by thermal treatment.The raw material of the adsorbent was a clay type bentonite, coming from a deposit located in Armero-Guayabal (Tolima), zone that is characterized by the presence of volcanic clay rocks, with influence of the volcano Nevado del Ruiz. The purified clay was subjected to thermal treatment at 200, 300, 400 and 500 °C, obtaining different adsorbents. To each of the materials obtained and to purified clay dried at 60 °C, the initial analysis, structural, textured using different analysis techniques (FRX, DRX, N2 adsorption to 77 K), in order to identify changes associated with the thermal treatment and its effect on the adsorption process was considered.The initial studies of adsorbents were used to use the thermally treated 400. The analysis of the total rate of adsorption of caffeine over benzonite thermally modified at 400 °C, is controlled in the kinetic models determined by the Langmuir model was 80.33 mg/g. It was also found that the kinetic models of best fit were those of Elovich and pseudo second order, suggesting that the active sites of the adsorbente are favourable and that the adsorption process is controlled by the transfer of external mass.
