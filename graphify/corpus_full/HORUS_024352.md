# Female migration in the Sierra Nevada from the trajectories of two indigenous women linked to care work

From the trajectories of two indigenous women of the Arhuaco and Kankuamo people, their migration and the way in which they are associated with care work are analyzed. To achieve this, migration is presented in two moments: inward and outward. Migration ‘intoward’ has to do with the whole process of ethnicization and gender relations within their communities during their childhood. And outward migration allows us to understand the moment in which they migrate towards cities and their relationship with domestic work. The reconstruction of their stories allows us to understand the care from their voices, and the way in which they overcome all situations that put their rights at risk. But at the same time, their stories reveal a powerful agency through which they succeed in subverting the relations of power, from their ancestral knowledge. Their stories help us to understand the phenomenon of women's migration in the Sierra Nevada of Santa Marta and domestic work as a reality that deserves to be studied. .
