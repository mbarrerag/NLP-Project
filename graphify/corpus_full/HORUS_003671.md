# THE USE OF RELIGIOUS TEXTS IN CONJUgal VIOLENCE PROCESS, NEW GRANADA KINGDOM, 1780-1830

This paper analyses the usual use of moralist texts, exemplary lives and which refer to the Sacred Scriptures in legal proceedings for marital violence in New Granada between 1780 and 1830, employed by defence lawyers and accusers to argue the male duty-right of punishment of women in the context of complaints for abuse and requests for separation of bed and table. The practice of correction of women is analyzed through punishment, its legal limits and discursive transformations at a time of transition to the Republic, inspired by Enlightenment and Liberalism
