# Evaluation of angiogenesis in canine breast cancer and its relationship to histopathological criteria of malignancy

Breast cancer is one of the most frequent neoplasms in bitches not sterilized worldwide, and has epidemiological, clinical and molecular similarities with human breast cancer, which makes the canine species a relevant model for comparative studies in oncology. This study aims to evaluate the angiogenesis through the immunoexpression of VEGF and CD31 in normal canine mammary glands, malignant tumors (simple carcinomas) and non-neoplastic lesions (adenomas and hyperplasias), and to assess whether there is a relationship between these and histopathological criteria of malignancy, such as histological grade, tumor size, mitotic index, nuclear pleomorphism, angiolymphatic invasion, necrosis, the extracapsular extent of ganglonar treatment and the presence of metastasis.
