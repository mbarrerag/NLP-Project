# Spatial distribution model of the Earth ' s radiation imbalance measured by radiometers placed in orbit

This research focused on the implementation of a spatial distribution model based on the application of geostatistical interpolation techniques. This procedure allowed a more accurate analysis of the variables that influence the spatial patterns of the imbalance of the terrestrial radiation in the center of Colombia. The input data used in this model are called FlashFlux TISA, and come from the observations of the Terra and Aqua satellites of the CERES projects (Clouds and the Earths Radiant Energy System) and MODIS (Moderate-Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer) (NASA, 2020). In summary, the way in which this research was carried out was initially with an assessment of the image quality, to analyze the potential characteristics and statistics of the data of FlashFlux TISA; Subsequently, a method of spatial interpolation Kriging that enabled the regional data to be identified by enabling the regional imbalance to be analysed and the patterns of terrestrial radiation imbalance in the center of the country, trying to explore variables that influence these spatial dynamics was implemented
