# RURALITY, SAFETY AND HEALTH

The transformations in the world of work in the twenty-first century, both at the urban and rural levels, demand the adoption of measures and policies that respond to the challenges in health and safety at work that are taking place, especially in the rural Latin American and Colombian territory, thus guaranteeing better working conditions. Rurality as a social construction must be addressed from a critical perspective that goes beyond a reductionist economic productionist approach, typical of the dominant development paradigm. The present chapter seeks to present the relations between the global and national contexts in which rurality has been thought in Colombia, the rural labour market and working conditions, as well as the implications that it has had in the ways of living, sickening and dying of rural workers and in this sense contribute to the debate of public labour policies
