# EXPLORING THE DOMESTICATION OF COCOA AND ITS INFLUENCE IN THE DIVERSITY OF MICROBIAN PENHOTIC PENHOTES TRANSMITTED BY SEMIL

The study of plant-microorganism interactions is a field of research in rapid growth, with an increasing attention to the role of the microbial endophytes transmitted by the seeds in the protection of the plant during its development involving the abiotic and biotic stress. Recent evidences suggest that the microbiodolence of the seeds is crucial for the establishment of the plant's microbial community, affecting its composition and structure, and influencing the plant's physiology and ecology. In Theobroma cocoa L., the diversity and composition of the vertically transmitted microorganisms has not yet been addressed in detail. We explore the composition and diversity of endophytes transmitted by seeds in commercial genotymal macorcas (ICS95, IMC67), genotypes recently released by AGROSAVIA (TCS01, TCS19) and varieties of Pmaco (Colombia) (AC9, ROS1, ROS2), with the aim of evaluating the vertical transmission of microorganisms and their establishment in various tissues during the development of the plant.
