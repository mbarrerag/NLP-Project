# Measurement and evaluation of digital alphabetization: a review of literary and biblio-ethical analysis

Information and communication technologies (ICT) have transformed virtually all sectors of society because of the benefits these tools bring. The education sector has been one of the most impacting with these digital transformation processes, and teachers and students are required to improve their levels of digital literacy. However, it has been identified that there are different ways of carrying out the digital literacy measurement process, but no literary review has been built on this topic. Based on the above, this article reviews literature and bibliographic analysis on digital literacy measurement and evaluation. For this purpose, a systematic search for bibliographic material was made in the Web of Science database and an analysis through the Biblioshiny tool. The results of this process show that, although this topic is discussed from 2004, only until 2012 is relevant research on digital literacy measurement, it was also possible to identify that what is sought is to measure the skills and competencies of teachers and students for the use and use of ICTs. In practical terms, this document is expected to enable people interested in measuring and evaluating digital literacy, to identify the relevant areas of digital literacy
