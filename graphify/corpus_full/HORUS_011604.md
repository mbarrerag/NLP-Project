# A new framework for training a CNN with a hardware-software architecture.

The facial expression recognition systems (FER) classify emotions using geometric strategies or Machine Learning algorithms (ML) as convolutional neural networks (CNNs).However, the design of these systems is a complex task that depends on the quality of the data set and the designer's experience. In addition, the inference of CNNs requires memory and computation resources that make the use of low cost embedded systems unviable. Likewise, although the GPUs are expensive and have a high power consumption, they are frequently used because they reduce the execution time compared to the CPUs. On-chip (SoCs) systems implemented in FPGAs employ less power and support computation in parallel. However, numerical representations as a floating point can result in complex work only suitable for high-end FPGAs. For this reason, the methodology includes the pre-employment, the higher-level and the higher-level architectures (the higher-level) and the higher-level model. In this case, as the hardware records support entire operations, the accelerator results are similar to the software counterpart. The RES SoC was implemented in the Zybo-Z7 development system, which employs 1 GB RAM, and the FPGA Zynq XCZ7020-CLG400. In addition, it was observed that the architecture obtained the same accuracy as a laptop with a 16 threads AMD CPU, 16 GB of RAM, and the GPU of Nvidia GTX1660Ti, but it was 20% slower. So it is recommended to evaluate yes the exchange between the quantitativeization and the execution time is sufficient for the target application. Finally, another contribution of the work is the Resiliency framework, which allows training and inference of single channel CNN models.
