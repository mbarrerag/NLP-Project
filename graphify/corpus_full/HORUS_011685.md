# CATEGORATE OF URBANA AVES USING FREQUENCES AND ABONDANCES: A PROPOSAL FOR PRIORIZING CONSERVATION EFFORTS

Assessing bird tolerance to urbanization can be a useful tool to focus conservation efforts on urban landscapes, especially in sites with high biodiversity and endemism, where red lists do not always highlight local conservation needs. Between 2014-2019 we performed bird counts at 141 points in the Aburra Valley (Colombia). Based on presence and frequency data we proposed a method to categorize species according to their response to urbanization, using four spatial scales: urban-periurban, and three decreasing scales around each point (1000, 500 and 200 m), assigning each site to high urban density (67100%), moderate (34-66%) or low (0-33%), based on satellite images. We made 6444 records of 156 species of birds, 129 of which we managed to categorize; according to our method 37 eluden, 55 are adapted and 37 exploit urban ecosystems.
