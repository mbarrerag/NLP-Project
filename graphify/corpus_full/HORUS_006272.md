# From Carbon Extraction to Blue and Green Extractivism Demands for Radical Socio-environmental Transformations in La Guajira, Colombia

In Colombia, coal mining and the extraction of hydrocarbons through offshore drilling, and more recent calls for fracking, coexist with alternative energies such as wind and solar energy. This chapter analyzes the Guajira, the northernmost region of Colombia's Caribbean coast, a place where all the structural inequalities related to extractivism, and alternative energies make evident that energy transitions have become "green pretexts" to impose different development projects that give continuity to the accumulation of capital. The chapter proposes the concept of radical socio-environmental transformations to argue in favor of a profound change in capitalist structural relations—in terms of control of and access to nature as economic resources, and the territorial and environmental appropriation of such resources—in the struggle to position other ways of living in pursuit of relational environmental justice. In the case of the Guajira, these radical socio-environmental transformations call to reposition Wayuu people's ways of life to the center stage and to consider human-nonhuman relations under other ontologies, based on local practices and knowledges for the defense and continuity of life
