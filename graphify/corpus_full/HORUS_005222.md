# Modeling social-ecological systems: influence of governance structure on land cover and land use costumes

This thesis shows a methodological route to analyze the effect of multilevel governance on the spatial-temporal path of land uses and coverage (LUC). For this, a case study was used in an area of a strategic watershed in the Colombian Andes: the balance of the Grande and Chico rivers. The first study emphasizes the importance of considering various spatial extensions and governance characteristics when analyzing the patterns of change LUC in the basin. There is challenged the conventional approach of using generic analysis units as a basin, highlighting the attractiveness of dairy livestock in an area that becomes the center of the the the thesis. From this point, the analysis is based on the structure of the model of causes of change of LUC: underlying force, mediator agent and immediate cause. The underlying force of the changes of LUC is described in the second study, where the governance structure according to three functions is defined, and its coherence to achieve objectives. The third analysis of geo-localized social networks, revealed cycles of unconnected feedback with production and conservation objectives, and advocates for a governance driven by the community to promote the active participation and mitigate the conflicts. The first study study highlights the importance of a differential analysis, and governance characteristics when analyzing the impact of the analysis of the impact on the economic situation of the economy. third study, which explores the impact of governance structure on farmers' LUC decisions. The variables of each governance function are defined and their effect on the decision is evaluated by estimating a Seemingly Unrelated Regression model. The study finds that actors promoting production projects have the great impact on the farmer and proposals to explore various governance settings to understand their territorial implications on LUC challenges. The four studies focus on the proposed cause of LUC change, introducing a simulation model of the social-ecological system (SES) at the risk of reducing the risk of harm and harm, and emphasizing the need for robust measurement and integration of social variables
