# ENHANCING LEVELS OF ENGAGEMENT IN CITIZEN SCIENCE PROJECTS INVOLVING LAKE WATER LEVEL MONITORING

Citizen science projects have gained more attention in hydrology in the last decade. The benefits they provide include high effectiveness of community participation in collecting high accuracy data collection, positive impacts on people's lives, improving awareness of water resource issues, informed decision-making in water governance, and related development of educational programs associated with water. We will present our experiences implementing participatory action research within the Lake Observations by Citizen Scientist and Satellites(LOCSS) project. LOCSS seeks to involve citizen scientists in monitoring lake water level and combine the data collected with satellite data to better understand lake water dynamics. LOCSS is currently present in nine countries, each of which has distinct connections with local partners and communities; in this talk, we particularly focus on the experience in Colombia. By collaborating with the community, we build a characterization of relevant ecosystem benefits of lentic water bodies. We use social cartography and semi structured interviews, which allow mapping the relationships the community has with the water body in the past, present, and future. This characterization helps to identify the relationship between the community and the wetland, risks associated with water level changes in the lake or wetland, current strategies in place to minimize the risk, and expectations of risk mitigation. In addition, we explore tools combining ground data collected by the community with available satellite data to help answer questions such as, how are changes in the water levels related to human activities? The strategies generated with these experiences are integrated to LOCSS tools in community engagement and contribute to documentation of best practices for this type of project
