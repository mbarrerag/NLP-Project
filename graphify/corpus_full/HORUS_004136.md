# THE GIRI: MEASURING INFRASTRUCTURE DISASTER RISK RESILIENCE AND MONITORING AT THE GLOBAL LEVEL

Measuring infrastructure resilience is pivotal in the pursuit of enhancing it. Integrating risk into resilience analysis is crucial for effective disaster risk management. The proposed Global Infrastructure Resilience Index (GIRI) is built on a multi-hazard probabilistic risk assessment, influenced by various social, economic, environmental, and political indicators. The GIRI acknowledges the relevance of all dimensions of infrastructure resilience, holding a great importance on physical risk, which captures a significant aspect of the resilience challenge, and usually is a determinant of service disruption and sustainability. It also acknowledges the impact of the infrastructure gap on a country's disaster risk profiles. Outdated infrastructure escalates vulnerability, while insufficient infrastructure leads to non-diversified systems, hindering post-event recovery The GIRI's results provide insights into a country's performance across diverse capacities and the shape of its resilience performance curve, as well as a numerical value that facilitates the ranking of countries based on their resilience. The GIRI can be decomposed to examine the contribution of risk and indicators for each capacity, revealing which metrics significantly impact a country's capacities. The composite indicator enables the tracking of capacity changes over time, while analyzing individual indicators provides a detailed understanding of the dynamics within each country, positioning resilience as a performance outcome. Developed for the International Coalition for Disaster Resilience Infrastructure (CDRI), the GIRI has been applied to analyze and rank 171 countries, utilizing the best available global information
