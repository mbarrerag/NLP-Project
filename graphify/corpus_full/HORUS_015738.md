# TERRITORIAL ORDER MODELS: PAST, QUIBDO, VALLEDUPAR AND MEDELLIN STORY

The population of Colombia, urban experience that spans five centuries, distances itself from the processes carried out in Latin America because we do not share with other countries like Brazil and Argentina, the tendency towards urban macrocephaly, that is, the population and productive concentration in a great city. When we look closely at the Colombian urban horizon we find that, while there is a unit represented in the State, its symbols and its national rites, it is undeniable the presence of a varied network of cities inhabited by multi-ethnic societies. This text offers the reader a narrative about how in the organization of the Colombian urban network, different models or systems of territorial planning have been succeeded, while the urban primacys have been changing when determined by dynamic factors such as the paces of integration of the country to the world markets and even the particular characteristics of the environmental supply in each region, which has made us a biodiverse power at the global level that has been causing deep fragmentations.
