# PROPERTIES OF DISOLUTIONS: ELECTROLYTICAL DRIVING

The presence of ions in an aqueous solution can be detected by measuring the capacity of the dissolution to conduct electric current. This ability depends on the ions that exist in the dissolution, from the solute. Water, by itself, is not conductive. Dissolutions that do not lead to electric current correspond to non-electrolyte solutes; if the solutions lead to electrical current, giving evidence of high ionization of the solute, it is classified as electrolyte solutes, and if they lead relatively little, they correspond to weak electrolyte solutes. In this practice, the measurement of the conductivity of various solutions is proposed to understand the implications of dissociation of solutes in a dissolution
