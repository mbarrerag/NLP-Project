# Antimicrobial activity of synthetic peptides derived from LL-37 versus Mycobacterium tuberculosis, mechanism of action and interaction with some genes involved in pathogenicity, as an approximation to an anti-tuberculosis alternative

Tuberculosis (TB) is an infectious disease caused by the bacillus Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB), which in turn is recognized as a very serious problem of public health worldwide. The emergence of multi-resistant MTB strains (MDR) and extremely resistant (XDR) to the anti-TB drugs traditionally used, has prompted the search for compounds with novel mechanisms of action as well as therapeutic alternatives. Different studies have identified the antimicrobial peptides (ADPs) as interesting molecules with broad spectrum antimicrobial activity, due to their particular mode of action, given their ability to bind to the cell membrane, without requiring specific receptors. The LL-37 peptide, has demonstrated antimicrobial, antifungal, antiviral and immunomodulatory activity of the host, including against resistant strains. Otherwise, we also want to show whether a subletal concentration of PAM modulates the P-type ATPase profile related to virulence. The results of this project will contribute greatly in the fields of research on the pathogenesis of MTB and in the pharmacological field in an attempt to promote PAMs in anti-TB therapies. Likewise, further research will be possible to understand how MTB would respond to modified synthetic PAMs, which would lead to a better understanding of the mechanism of action of these molecules on bacterial virulence
