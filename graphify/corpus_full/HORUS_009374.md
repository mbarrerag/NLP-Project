# A COMPUTATION MODEL EFFECTIVE FOR THE PROBLEM OF MINIMUM PARTICIPATION INFORMATION IN THE CONTEXT OF INTEGRATED INFORMATION THEORY (IIT)

One of the questions that has disturbed humanity is the reason why we are conscious. How does the mind work? How can the relationship between body and mind (mind-body problem) be rationally understood? These are questions that from Plato and Aristotle remain in force.The great advances achieved thanks to the scientific study of the brain have allowed to know many aspects of the mental phenomena by finding their neurophysiological bases.The problem mind-body is closely related to consciousness.The search for how the phenomenon of consciousness occurs by neurobiological processes of the brain, has recently had an active work, although little progress. Churchland (2013), Crick (1995), Penrose (1996), Dennet (1991), among others, have treated the subject without reaching a widely accepted theory.The The The Theory of Integrated Information (Integrated Information theory or IIT) posed by the neuroscientific Giulio Tononi (2004) is one of the most accepted theories on consciousness.The IIT is an effort to give meaning to the philosophy of consciousness, the conscious experiences and the important neurology of consciousness, however it allows to generate information in a theoretical framework and determine whether consciousness is a more. This is a numerically intractable problem due to the combinatorial explosion generated by the number of resulting partitions (Oizumi et al., 2014). Given the importance of this problem for neuroscience, cognitive sciences and artificial intelligence, this doctoral work raises the search for alternatives that facilitate finding MIP. The solution of this problem would be very useful for applications in the fields mentioned above. An application would be in decision support systems based on artificial cognitive systems as organizations can be seen as organisms of a competitive ecosystem and that these organisms have a behavior like that of an intelligent and conscious being (Arrabales, 2016)
