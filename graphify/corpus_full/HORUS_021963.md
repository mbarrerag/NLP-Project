# Heuristic methods for teaching-learning concepts associated with the development of spatial reasoning with students of architecture.

In this research, we work around the heuristic methods that use students of Architecture in problem situations involving notions of perimeter, area, volume, and isometric and isomorphic transformations. This research, under the methodology based on design, sought to relate part of the compositional design processes with respect to the notions of geometry, seeking to strengthen the argumentation in these processes, from the interpretation of Architecture as form and function. In the results, we show the notions of geometry inherent in the processes of composition in design, the forms in which students solve problem situations through the use of tangible material such as pentomino and papyroflexia, and finally, the contribution in spatial reasoning. Thus, a relationship is established between the elements that integrate this research: mathematics and architecture from their own disciplines, tangible material as the means to propose problem situations and heuristic methods as the approach that enhances spatial reasoning
