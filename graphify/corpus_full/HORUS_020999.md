# Electrochemical study of the PTEO polymer and its behavior against organic molecules such as glyphosate.

The analysis of chemical compounds is carried out mainly in laboratories with high-tech instruments and equipment such as chromatographs and spectrophotometers, which allow the detection and quantification of compounds in a high variety of samples. However, the use of these equipment represents a high-cost and difficult to access analysis; therefore, paper-based electrochemical devices (PAD) are proposed as an alternative for economic and effective analysis, which can be done in situ, with the aim of carrying out control and monitoring of compounds harmful to the environment and human health. Using modified carbon electrodes with conductive polymers such as Poly(4-glycidyloxy-2,2,6,6-tetramethylpiperidin-1-oxyl) (PTEO) based on chemical structures with the presence of radicals, thus allowing the evaluation of the latter's ability to react with the analyte and to improve the interaction of the latter with the working electrode. This project will be carried out by the undergraduate student in Chemistry, Edward Zamudio, with the tutoring of Professor Bryan Boudouris, of the University of Purdue, and Professor Andrea Sando-Val, of Bogotá, Universidad-Unián
