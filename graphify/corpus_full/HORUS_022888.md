# EVALUATION OF THE EMISSION OF H2S, NH3 AND VOLATILE ORGANIC COMPOUNDS IN THE PRETRATTING AREA OF A PTAR

The wastewater treatment plants are an important source of emission of offensive odours [1] generated mainly by volatile inorganic compounds, such as hydrogen sulfide (H2S) and ammonia (NH3), and volatile organic compounds (VOCs).These compounds, have harmful effects on the health of workers or surrounding populations such as nose irritation, throat, nausea or stress [2].For this reason, in Colombia, standards have been implemented such as resolution 1541 of 2013 to be regulated by the concentrations [3].However, to implement technologies that treat direct emissions of the offensive odours, mitigate their impact and comply with the standards, it is necessary to identify and quantify those compounds. It is therefore important to monitor the emission of these gases, located in the Rio Bogotá Via Lisboa-Arrive Intersection in the city of Bogotá, where the chemically assisted gases of the residual water are performed by both the concentrations of HS2 and the PATR. of July 6 (fig. 1a), the rainfall precipitation of the previous day was 0,00 mm of water, while for the monitoring of August 24 (fig. 1b), the precipitation of the day was 9.75 mm of water. On the other hand, according to the chromatographic analysis, it was identified that the most abundant volatile organic compounds are xylene, toluene, decane and limonene. It is noteworthy that in the PTAR -The Salitre of Bogotá is identified toluene, which is the most common compound to be found in the emissions of other PTARs [5]. Consequently, it can be concluded that the concentrations of H2S, NH3 and COVs are higher to lower precipitation. Thus, considering the concentration ranges emitted in the PTAR-El Salitre, which are from 5 to 86 ppm for H2S, 1.5 to 10 ppm for NH3, and 2 to 20 ppm COVs, can be explored alternatives that allow to choose suitable technologies to treat this type of current as for example the biofiltracing, Cf, PEC.
