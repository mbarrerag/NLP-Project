# Cutting connectors for sections consisting of concrete and cold-formed steel profiles

Since the end of the 50’s, there is evidence of the use of composite sections in buildings, being Switzerland the pioneer country in applying this constructive alternative. Given the advantages of combining metallic profiles and concrete elements efficiently, its implementation was quickly disseminated in Europe and the United States, being the main industrial powers of steel production.The proper performance of the composite systems depends, in part, on the mechanism of transfer of effort in the interface of the components, mainly attributed to the cutter connectors.According to the current standard, the only devices endorsed for this purpose are the spurs, channels, screws and perforated plates, using welded fixation in hot laminated profile (HRS), without this being an efficient option for thin sheet profiles (CFS), since the application of the welding can affect the profile with burns and/or perforations, reducing its structural capacity. However, to date, there is no reference within the regulations, which specifies the use of a type of cuter connector in composite sections where CFS profiles are included. The CFS profiles, coming from a bending system. was evaluated analytically by means of numerical simulations of the tests performed by the method of finite elements (MEF), with which complementary information was obtained on the behavior of the component elements of the system, and their fault mechanisms. Finally, by statistical analysis, design formulations are presented that allow to estimate both the nominal resistance of the CSC type cutting connectors, and the degree of action composed of the system, conditions to be taken into account in normative areas for the design interpositions in section composed of concrete slabs and profiler CFS. .
