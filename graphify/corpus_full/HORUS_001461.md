# UNDERSTANDING THE FREIGHT (TRIP) ATTRACTION OF FOOD SERVICES IN URBAN AREAS OF EMERGING ECONOMIES: A STUDY IN URBAN PLANNING AND FOOD SERVICES

Efficient freight transportation systems facilitate the exchange of goods and services and generate market growth in urban areas. The study aims to characterize the freight trip attraction (FTA) of Food Services (FS) in an urban area of the Medellin Metropolitan Area (MMA). ISCI defines the FS as preparing and selling food and drinks, mainly complete meals, for immediate consumption on the premises. It may or may not offer home delivery, alcoholic beverages, or some entertainment. A database from the Freight Transportation Study conducted in the case study city was used. This classification contains data for 266 establishments. To estimate FTA, the study will develop linear and non-linear regression models with the ordinary least squares (OLS) regression method. Three final equations were obtained to model the relationship between weekly freight trips and variables such as hours opened, a new variable that significantly contributes to the academic literature, and total area. These independent variables were found to describe the FTA better. They mean the weekly freight trips obtained range between 1 and 15. The hours opened vary from 4 to 24 hours, and the total area ranges from 4 to 670 square meters. These results offer valuable information to decision-makers and business owners of logistics companies or food service establishments, highlighting the potential benefits of suggesting accurate land use and freight flow policies and adapting business strategies for better resource management in urban areas
