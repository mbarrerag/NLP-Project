# EFFECT OF MOTHER, GENOTY AND GEOGRAPHICAL LOCALIZATION OF CACCOA ON THE PHYSICAL CHEMICAL CHARACTERISTICS OF THE GRAND DURING FERMENTATION

It is estimated that the global production of cocoa for the years 2019/2020 is 4,824 thousand tons with an increase of 1.7 % regarding the years 2018/2019. The consumption of chocolate products in 2017 was 102 billion dollars and is projected to grow by 8 % in 2022. The consumption preferences are given by its pleasant taste and the benefits to the health that is attributed to it as a source of antioxidants, with anticancer potential and cardiovascular protector. The quality of the cocoa grain is fundamental to compete in the world market, by grouping characteristics such as the nutritional composition, content of polyphenols and fermenting quality. The genotype of the fruit and state of maturity determine the chemical composition of the grain, specifically the content of storage proteins, polysaccharides and polyphenols. This establishes the quantities and type of precursors formed during the fermentation and drying processes that lead to the formation of the flavor, therefore, it influences both the type and intensity of the taste. The maturation has been defined as the transition between growth, development and the senescence and involves changes of appearance, flavor and texture that are genetically regulated; therefore, it is not an influence of the fruit. longitudinal analysis, given that measures are taken over time and individuals can be self-correlation over time, which allows the results to be contrasted by another analysis. Under the two methods of statistical analysis used, the corresponding validation of model assumptions that include normality and homocedasticity on the model residues was performed. The most influential factor on the physicochemical characteristics evaluated was the genotype, which affects the pH of the grain and pulp and the fermentation index. This allows establishing a solution to the quality heterogeneity of the grain, the main problem of international marketing, making homogeneous fermentations of the same genetic material
