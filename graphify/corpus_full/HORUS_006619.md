# Electromagnetic field applicator system for melanoma treatment using local hyperthermia

The aim of this study was to design, simulate and characterize an appropriate radiant system for the treatment of melanoma with hyperthermia at 2.45 GHz. Melanoma is skin cancer with the highest mortality, and its main treatment is surgery, however, in cases where it cannot be performed, it is necessary to turn to other alternatives such as hyperthermia. Hyperthermia together with other treatments such as chemotherapy or radiation therapy has shown to improve their effectiveness. First, the procedure to validate the function of the applicator using the distribution of the specific absorption rate (SAR) in a fantoma was verified in international standards. Subsequently, an experimental assembly was carried out following the indications and procedures of the standard IEC/IEEE 62209-1528:20, in addition, a dipole type antenna was designed and constructed, to finally compare the results of the experimental measurements.
