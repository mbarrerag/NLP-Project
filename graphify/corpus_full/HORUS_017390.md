# Probiotic viability and inhibition capacity of L. gasseri microencapsulated in L. monocytogenes and S. aureus

ABSTRACT Lactobacillus gasseri is recognized as an important comensal microorganism, capable of inhibiting multiple pathogens by producing antimicrobial substances and by its influence on the mechanisms of the immune system at the intestinal level. Listeria monocytogenes and Staphylococcus aureus, on the other hand, are potentially harmful zoonotic microorganisms that affect animal and human health, and in turn, are contaminants of the food chain. In this context, the effect of L. gasseri microencapsulada on L. monocytogenes and S. aureus was evaluated. The bacterial growth of the lactic strain in two means of cultivation (MRS-PRO), was taken to microencapsular in a matrix of inulin-maltodextrin at 15 %. The viability was assessed in time and at in vitro gastrogenic conditions at 8,15, 20, 30 and 45 days with maximum values of 3.3 x 10o8313; UFC/mL.
