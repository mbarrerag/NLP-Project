# Phenotypic foliar variation in natural populations of Quillaja saponaria and its relationship with climate variation

Quillaja saponaria is an evergreen sclerophyll species that is distributed from 30° to 38° S in Central Chile. In this wide range of distribution, it is expected that the species exhibits phenotypic plasticity in its foliar morphology associated with climate variation; however, the information on this subject is scarce. The morphological variation of the leaf in 85 Q. saponaria stands was studied along the natural distribution of the species and its relation to temperature, precipitation and aridity. The results show that the basal diameter of the petiole, the number of secondary veins and the basal diameter of the main vein increase with temperature and decrease with aridity. On the other hand, the length, basal diameter and relative length of the petiole decrease with the increase in temperature. This confirms that the phenotypic variation of the foliar characters of Q. saponaria is related to climatic variables indicating a capacity of adaptation of the species to the contrasting climatic conditions in which it develops
