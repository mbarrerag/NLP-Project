# Validity and predictive values of the Critical Care Pain Observation Tool (CPOT) scale

The International Association for the Study of Pain defines pain as a sensory and emotional experience unpleasant associated with real or potential tissue damage. Different studies have indicated that between 10% and 50% of adult patients hospitalized in ICU experience moderate to intense pain. Patients in hospital can self-inform pain. However, in intensive care units there are factors that make it impossible to self-report patients, factors such as orotracheal intubation, levels of analgesia silk, state of consciousness and the medications given to the patient.Reference framework Scales have been designed to allow the evaluation of pain objectively. In 2013, the American College of Critical Care Medicine recommended the use of these two tools, Behavioral Pain Scale (BPS) and Critical Care Observation Tool (CPOT) for the evaluation of pain in unconscious patients and tracheal intubation. These scales should be used periodically every 4 to 6 hours in rest and also during procedures or other mobility events. In this study, they performed content and construct validity, the confirmatory factorial analysis for construct validity with a value of 0.78, allowed to establish that there is a strong relationship between the BPS scale and the Campbell Scale. In addition, in the study they carried out a Bartlett Sphericity Test obtaining a value p <0.0001, which is quite significant; also they performed the validity of criteria in which they measured pain in parallel with the Campbell Scale in Spanish and the BPS scale as a reference standard during the 60 painful procedures with a good correlation where the Spearman coefficient was 0.755. Based on the information obtained regarding validity, sensitivity and specificity the validity and adaptation of the CPOT scale was taken into consideration. General objective Adapt and validate the Spanish version of the Critical Care Pain Observation Tool as a clinical tool for the detection of pain in the intensive care unit.
