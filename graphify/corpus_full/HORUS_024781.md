# P88 FREEZING OF BOVINE SEMEN IN A EXTENDER WITH SODIUM CASEINATE

Products such as skim milk and egg yolk have been conventionally used for cryopreservation of bovine semen. However, alternatives are being sought to reduce the risks that these could generate, such as the microbiological alteration of semen and the transmission of diseases. Although some additives of vegetable origin have been a valuable alternative to this problem, in many cases they do not achieve the level of sperm protection of additives of animal origin. The aim of this study was to evaluate the sodium caseinate (SC) in the extending and freezing of bovine semen. Fifteen ejaculates from five bulls were extended and then, individually frozen in an extender composed of trisglycerol- citric acid with 8% v/v of lowdensity lipoprotein (LDL), and supplemented with SC in four treatments: SC 3% w/v (SC3), SC 4% w/v (SC4), SC 5% w/v (SC5) and without SC (Control). Motility and kinetics (SCA® System), and functional integrity of the membrane (HOS test) of fresh, extended and thawed semen were evaluated. Also, morphology and vitality (eosin-nigrosin) of fresh and extended semen, and structural integrity of the membrane (SYBR14/IP), acrosome integrity (FITC-PNA) and in vitro fertilizing capacity (IVF) of thawed semen were assessed. Statistical analysis of sperm quality parameters was performed using linear models and the comparison of means by the Tukey test. Statistical analysis of IVF was performed with the Kruskal- Wallis test. SC3 increased progressive motility and beat cross frequency of thawed semen (p 0.05). In conclusion, supplementation of freezing extender with 3% sodium caseinate can improve the post-thaw quality of cryopreserved bovine semen
