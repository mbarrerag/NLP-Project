# FREIGHT TRIP GENERATION ANALYSES BASED ON ONLINE SALES IN MEDELLIN, COLOMBIA

All around the world, due to the improvement of technology accessibility, market necessities, and all the pandemic externalities, e-commerce rates have increased [1]. For instance, Colombia, a developing country, during 2020 got a raise of 53% in online purchases, and it is forecasted that by 2025 the growing rate is going to be about 74% [2]. The constant and abundant growth of sales is directly related to the different business models (e.g., B2B, B2C, G2B), in which freight transportation plays an important role in the supply chain management and the fulfillment of customer's expectations. The present research focuses on the Business to Consumer (B2C) model in the Medellin Metropolitan Area (MMA) where government has strongly supported online purchase initiatives while reducing taxes and educating enterprises on technologies and financial management. These industries and companies sell directly goods to customers by online means or subsidiaries, and frequently send parcels with the products directly to the customer's preferred address that, depending on the population density, order's frequency, amount of companies selling products, vehicle size and capacity, city parking conditions, and traffic speed, could generate problems not only in delivery logistics but also in traffic characteristics such as congestion, accidental rate, parking, etc. With this in mind, it is important to understand and analyze this constantly growing commercial behavior which is just recently being monitored and tracked by Colombian governmental agencies, and the implications caused by it in urban transportation and logistics. In doing so, authors use data obtained from the characterization and analysis of metropolitan freight patterns in the MMA executed in 2018 [3], and other measures obtained from secondary information. Although both information shows the e-commerce performance in the region, it is key to mention that it could represent pre and post-pandemic scenarios from the consumer's and seller's point of view that all together can elaborate on characteristics and important factors in freight transportation and last mile operation. Drawn from the information, econometric models are posed to estimate freight trip generation (e.g., freight trips produced and attracted) in the MMA owed to the B2C e-commerce model. The Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) technique is used to generate both linear and non-linear models based on similar studies [4], [5], allowing to identify critical freight and mobility patterns for supporting the regulation and planning policy processes in order to minimize freight transportation negative externalities in the urban areas (congestion, noise, pollution, etc.), and improving home delivery logistics
