# Genomic evaluation and positivity to Chlamydia psittaci for the management of parrots and macaws recovered from traffic in three areas of Colombia

Illegal trade in wildlife is considered one of the largest traffickings in the world, which generates a considerable decrease in populations of different species and directly and indirectly affects the ecosystem and its surroundings. Colombia, as one of the countries with the greatest biodiversity, is a country with a tendency to develop illegal practices. In the national territory, the presence of the Centers for the Care and Assessment of Wild Fauna (CAV) contributes to the process of assessment, treatment, rehabilitation and relocation of the fauna that has been displaced from their habitats. Loros and macadayyas are among the most trafficked species in Colombia and other tropical countries, and when one of these birds is recovered from illegal trafficking, genetic studies should be carried out to determine their origin and also studies of infection with some pathogens within which Chlamydia pisttaci is found, with the results of these studies, the environmental authorities of each area will determine the feasibility of being resocialized and released, kept in captivity or ultimately if they should be sacrificed because they are unviable to be resocialized
