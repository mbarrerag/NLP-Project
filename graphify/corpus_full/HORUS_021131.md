# MANUFACTURING OF METAL/ELASTOMMER HIBRED MATERIALS, BUILDING AS METAL A CELL STRUCTURE BASED ON INTERCONNECTED PORUS, FOR THEIR POSSIBLE USE IN THE ABSORTION OF IMPACT-BALISTIC ENERGY.

The purpose of this research is to obtain hybrid materials Metal/Elastomer. The metal corresponds to a cellular metal (MC) aluminium base with interconnected pore, which will be manufactured by two different routes: The first route consists of the manufacture of MCs using the technique of infiltration of removable fills and using as a material of filling generating pores of rounded salt grains. The above, in such a way that a relatively homogeneous porous structure can be obtained where, although it is not possible to control completely the distribution of pores within the MC and its size, if it can be controlled to a large extent the form of the poro from the rounding of the salt particles (sea salt salt). Once the salt particles are rounded, they are classified by ranges of size through their sifting. With the use of the rounded grains of salt it is intended to improve the mechanical properties, mainly the absorption of mechanical energy of the MC. The rounded granules of salt will be classified in the following ranges: Rango I, from 12.7 to 11.1 mm (1/2" - 7/16") Rango II, from 11.5 mm) from 11.5 to 9.5 mm. The high energy absorption will be used for the obtaining of the hybrid metal/elastomeric materials, through the inlaying of an elastomer within the porous network using a device designed for that purpose. To carry out this research it is intended to link the seedbed "obtaining porous materials" and four undergraduate students to develop grade work who will be responsible for the manufacture of the MCs necessary to determine their mechanical properties and MCs necessary to produce the hybrid material and determine its structural and mechanical properties. All MCs will be manufactured with interconnected pore and the most suitable manufacturing conditions will be established for each pore size. The determination of the properties under ballistics of the hybrid materials will be made by the project director in the ballistic laboratories in INDUMIL. With this study will have the first data to determine the behavior of the cellular metal and the hybrid material (energy absorption) will be determined as a function of the pore size, in a wide range of pore sizes.
