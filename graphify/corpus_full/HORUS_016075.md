# Influence of tree-level and species-level factors on the mortality of canopy trees in an Amazon forest: linking remote sensing with ground-based data

The mortality of trees is a fundamental ecological process that determines the structure and functioning of forests. In this study, we link data of remote sensors and terrestrial monitoring to evaluate the influence of the exposure of the tree top to light (in relation to the total area of the crown), the individual deviation of the growth rates, the size of the tree (DBH), and the density of the wood of the species, on the mortality of 984 canopy trees in the Permanent Park Amacayacu, North West Amazon, between 2013 and 2019. We adjusted Generalized Linear Models of Mixed Effects to investigate variables or combination of variables that best explained the probability of death during this period.
