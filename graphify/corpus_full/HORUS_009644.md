# THE TRAVELS OF INTERSECTIONALITY IN LATIN AMERICA: BRINGING THE DESKS OUT ONTO THE STREETS

In this chapter I will trace how intersectionality ¿ as a theoretical, methodological, and political feminist approach ¿ has travelled and been appropriated in Latin America. To this end, I will examine, first, its origins which sprang up simultaneously in the United States and in Brazil in the theorising of Afro-Brazilian thinkers and activists of the same period, such as Thereza Santos, Lélia Gonzalez, and Sueli Carneiro. Second, I will explore how intersectionality has been appropriated in Latin America and how it has been useful for exposing Latin American feminism's blind spot regarding race and racism. Finally, I demonstrate the contrast between intersectionality's application in feminist academia in the Global North, where it has become a standard academic concept, and what has happened in Latin America over the last decade, where intersectionality has been redefined, reinvented, and re-politicised outside of universities: in the streets and on social networks; in the activism of social movements and in its relationship with community and decolonial thinking and practice. I will conclude by observing that this shift that has seen intersectionality move from the centre to the margins is also a shift in focus from the intersectionality of identities to the intersectionality of social struggles ¿ which may usher in a new politics of feminist solidarity, as is already starting to occur within and among Latin American feminist movements
