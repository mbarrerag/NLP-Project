# RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN ALZHEIMER AND PERIODONTAL DISEASE THROUGH ENTITICAL TREPOME.

Clinical and scientific evidence supports that Periodontal disease is linked one or two-way with multiple systemic conditions through different mechanisms that have been extensively studied. In this topic, evidence has been increased of the possible relationship between the bacteria involved in Periodontal disease and neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's. One of the bacteria possibly involved is Denticola Treponema, on which, recently, some studies have been developed that attempt to elucidate its possible relationship and determine whether it could initiate or enhance Alzheimer's. Explore the relationship between Alzheimer's disease and Periodontal disease through Treponema denticola research through a systematic exploratory review of the scientific literature, evaluating the possible ways and mechanisms of the relationship. For the systematic exploratory review of literature, the methodology suggested by the Joanna Briggs Institute was used. The search strategy covered articles in full text, in any language and without a certain time range. Studies of systematic reviews and narratives, studies of cohorte, cases and controls and clinical trials were included. 373 publications were obtained, 346 later reading of the title and summary were excluded, 24 after reading the full text; finally, 3 articles met the inclusion criteria and the objectives of the research. The results of these articles point to that, although there is a relationship between both diseases through Treponema denticola through different pathways and mechanisms, it has not yet been established if this could initiate Alzheimer's disease.
