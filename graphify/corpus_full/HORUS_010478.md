# HEALTH REGULATIONS ARE POLITICAL: WHEN HEALTH STANDARDS BLOCK COMPETITION

If someone lets himself tempt by bureaucracy and agrees to be Minister of Health of any country, besides dealing with local health problems, he has to know that his field of action will actually be limited and defined globally. Being an independent, activist or academic officeman – especially in the midst of the pandemic by COVID-19does resemble a little what Malcolm Gladwell describes in the book of David and Goliat (Gladwell, 2013), that is, what happens when someone normal faces powerful opponents. Gladwell, through real stories about people who have faced decommunal challenges, tries to sustain two ideas. The first is that much of the valuable in the world comes from that kind of challenges, because fighting when you have everything against, generates greatness and beauty. The second is that systematically they make readings or misinterpretations about these situations, and the fifth book that leads to losing them in principle can open doors, create opportunities and make feasible what in other circumstances would be unthinkable. It is political: when health standards block competition; 4) The market does not work and the state is weak: confidentiality, secrecy and distortions of the pharmaceutical sector; and 5) The epidemic of consumption: pharmaceuticalization that is sick and contaminated. All chapters develop an uncomfortable cross-sectional truth: the disjunctive State/market is a false dichotomy. This book is presented in electronic and interactive format and is the product of the Manuel Ancízar Chair that was dictated the first semester of 2019 by the Center of Thought Medications, Information and Power of the National University of Colombia, with the collaboration of the Center for Sustainable Development Goals of the University of the Andes, so it is also presented as a pedagogical resource to support public health courses, global health, health economy of health or other disciplines. Being the product of a professor, the text, on some occasions, comes from transcriptions and is written in the first person. The book is organized in interactive sections arranged in a web environment that includes novel cases, videos, presentations, bibliographic references and selected material.
