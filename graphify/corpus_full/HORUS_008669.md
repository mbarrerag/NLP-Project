# EFFECT OF THE FORM AND SIZE OF PORUS ON ENERGY ABSORTION CAPACITY IN AL-SI12 METAL PUMPS

Cell metals or metallic foams are artificial three-dimensional cellular solids, formed by a metallic matrix, and by a set of small polyhedral-shaped compartments called cells or pores [1,2]. Thanks to their porous structure, which comprises different parameters such as shape, size, distribution, orientation and type of pores, these materials have a combination of unusual properties in many conventional materials, for example, low density, high rigidity and specific resistance, excellent sound absorption capacity, good vibration buffer capacity and excellent energy absorption capacity, which is why they have become in recent years the most advanced materials for structural and functional applications in many sectors of the economy [3,4,5]. For the manufacture of cellular metals, various techniques have been developed, which are usually classified according to the initial state of the base metal to be processed12, i.e. from molten metal, metal to powder, metal to metal or metal ions [6,7,8]. One of the most common techniques used to manufacture cellular metals from molten metal is the infiltration of removable fillers, given that they can be manufactured by good quality and at low cost.
