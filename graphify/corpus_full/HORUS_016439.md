# ESISTORICAL INJURY AND INCULATION: ARMANDING THE CASE OF STRUCTURAL VIOLENCE AGAINST THE EXCOMBATIENTS OF FARC

On the basis of Fricker’s conceptualisation of epistemical injustice and the practice of perdom as moral justice, an analysis is made of the relationship between epistemic injustice and redundantincrimination. It is argued that, in the context of the post-conflict Colombian case, negative identity prejudices applied to ex-combatants produce a case of epistemous injustice and redundantincrimination that has generated a type of structural violence. It is suggested that an appropriate understanding of the role of the Special Court for Peace (JEP) and the Commission of Truth, as well as the meaning of transitional justice, should provide interpretive resources that would promote the witness credibility of ex-combatants.
