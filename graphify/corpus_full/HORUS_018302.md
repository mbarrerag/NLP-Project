# EFFECT OF THE ADDITIONAL APPROACH OF PHOSPHATE IN GLYPHOSAT DEORTION AND RADICULATE TRANSLATION TO PLANTS

Effect of the addition of phosphate in the dissorption of glyphosate and the transfer via root to plants Mónica Yadira Dotor Robayo, (mydotorr@unal.edu.co) Department of Agrarian Sciences. Universidad Nacional de Colombia sede Bogotá, (Colombia) Maria José Martínez Cordón (mjmartinezc@unal.edu.co). Department of Chemistry. Universidad Nacional de Colombia sede Bogotá, (Colombia) To evaluate the effect of the bioavailability of glyphosate in soil, the metabolism and production of biomass of rice plants was proposed a test with different doses of herbicide applied in soil in the presence and absence of mono amonic phosphate (MAP). For this purpose, a repeated measurement experiment was designed over time, which included three soils (CS3, CS2, CS1), four treatments (T1-T4), six doses of herbicide (including zero dose) and five repeats per experimental unit, with seven measurements at intervals of eight days. The implemented treatments were: glyphosate; T2: Dfosate, D2 = joint dose, Ds, 8 days, and 5 days. The addition of this favors the accumulation of shikimic acid and the decrease in the potential production of biomass of exposed plants. With respect to the process of transfer from the soil, the limited bioavailability of the herbicide is evidenced, being this the reason why the results do not imply lethal effects on plants
