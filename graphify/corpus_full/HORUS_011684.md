# Bioherbicide potential of the aqueous extract of Artemisia absinthium L. on the germination of Taraxacum officinale L.

Although the use of herbicides constitutes a tool for the control of weeds, the uses of substances of plant origin becomes an alternative that would favor the transition to production processes with lower environmental impact. Artemisia absinthium L. is a plant with a large number of secondary metabolites, some of them with alleopathic potential, such as artemisin, which has been shown to inhibit germination by altering the permeability of the cellular membranes of the seeds, affecting the water and nutrients, also affecting processes of cell division or generating radical oxygen species in excess (Radhakrishnan, et al., 2018; Pouresmaeil, et al., 2020; Bharati, et al., 2014 ). On the other hand, Taraxacum officinale L. cosmopolitan plant, of rapid germination and competition with other species, make it a species of interest in the management of plants accompanying crops.
