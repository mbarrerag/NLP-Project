# Post-Transplant Hematopoietic Progenitor Chimerism. Experience from an Internationally Accredited Reference Center

Post-transplant chimerism analysis is used to assess the effectiveness of grafts after hematopoietic progenitor transplantation. This analysis involves pre-transplant donor and recipient studies and post-transplant monitoring at defined time intervals. Currently, the most widely used methodology in chimerism studies is the analysis of STR markers. With prior informed consent, 17,493 samples have been processed for chimerism testing from 2004 to the present date, including pre-transplant donor/recipient profiles and post-transplant recipient analyses. The analysis platforms used have been STR markers from commercial companies Applied Biosystems and Promega, using capillary electrophoresis on ABI 3130XLS and ABI 3500 equipment. From May 2017 to the present date, 7,305 reports have been generated, including 2,359 recipient or donor profiles and 4,946 post-transplant analyses with the following results: 122 reports with the absence of chimerism (graft failure), corresponding to 2.5%, at various post-transplant times; 1,174 reports of mixed chimerism, corresponding to 23.7%, and 3,650 reports of total chimerism, corresponding to 73.8%. Given the track record and experience of our group, post-transplant chimerism studies have been accredited by the European Federation for Immunogenetics (EFI) since 2019. Intercomparison tests are conducted with the American Association for Histocompatibility and Immunogenetics (ASHI) with 100% concordance in the intercomparison reports
