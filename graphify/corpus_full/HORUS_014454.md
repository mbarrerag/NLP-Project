# Evaluation of fluid-fluid-rock interaction in modified salinity water injection processes (IASM) and its impact on oil recovery in sandstone systems.

The improved recovery processes are a family of technologies that seek to obtain the greatest benefit from the hydrocarbon deposits; however, each one of them presents difficulties of various kinds; techniques, economic and environmental. The injection of controlled salinity water, or injection of low salinity water as it is also known, has highlighted in the industry because of its low environmental impacts and economic benefits; however, there is no scientific agreement in the phenomena that govern it. This study analyzes the effects on specific systems of rock-crude-salmurre when injecting low salinity water with different compositions, separating the fluid-fluid and rock-fluid interactions to identify the phenomena so far proposed in the literature. The results obtained also show very clear evidence on the importance of the fluid-fluid mechanisms. Desalado (salting in / out) and possible generation of microemulsion being important compared to other mechanisms proposed in the literature, in particular the fluid-roque mechanisms. The results show that the mechanisms present in the process of recovery improved under study go beyond the reduction of the salinity of the injection water, and obey the specific management of the technique
