# Production of iron oxide-based nanoheterostructures for applications in magnetic hyperthermia therapy

Magnetic hyperthermia is an antitumor therapy based on the increase in local temperature in cancer cells, by means of the use of magnetic nanoparticles (NPs) under the action of an altern magnetic field. In recent years, the possibility has been studied that the NPs used in this therapy are heterostructured materials, that is to say, NPs formed by multiple components such as Ag, Au, Zn, Mn and Co, among others, with the aim of optimizing their structural, morphological, optical and magnetic properties that allow for greater efficiency in treatment. In view of the above, this work presents the synthesis and characterization of magnetic nanostructures (NEs) of ZnFe2O4 and Zn0.41Mn0.50Fe1.83O4, as well as its superficial modification. The synthesis of the NEs was completed by means of thermal decomposition and its superficial modification was performed by the use of polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) . as their surface modification.
