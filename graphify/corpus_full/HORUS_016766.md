# Study of hydrolysis catalyzed by lipases of residual oleochemical currents.

The vegetable oils and residual fats have been captured worldwide as they typically are incorrectly available generating a complex cascade of environmental and health problems. However, because these residues are composed mainly of triglycerides, these can be used as a second generation raw material for the oil-chemical industry. In particular, the fatty acids that are an intermediate for the manufacture of surfactants, plasticizers, biofuels among others, can be obtained by the hydrolysis of residual triglycerides. However, the current industrial process (i.e. Emery-Colgate) for the production of fatty acids has increased the efficiency of the fatty acids has given rise to new disadvantages such as their energy intensity and the generation of residues. For this reason enzymatic processes have been explored as a viable alternative to conventional ones, since these operate in conditions of temperature and pressure more benevolent. In the same way, the interest to develop effective enzymes at industrial level has been promoted such as immobilisation of enzymes in suitable and modifiable solid supports to increase their stability and facilitate their reusability. Likewise, the intensification of processes has been employed to improve the efficiency of the reaction and reduce the generation of waste
