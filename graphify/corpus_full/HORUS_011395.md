# Molecular connections and their involvement in psoriasis: Variations in gene and protein expression at the keratinocyte level in patients with moderate to severe psoriasis who were treated with monoclonal antibodies.

Due to the pleiotroptropic effect of some cytokins involved in the development of the immune inflammatory response, the potential deletetereal effect in cell physiology induced by chronic inflammation and the moderate success of existing conventional therapies (allopatic therapy), the understanding of the immune mechanisms in psoriasis and their physiopathological effect have become the cornerstone for the development of new and better medicines. With the introduction of monoclonal antibodies and fusion proteins, together with existing chemical and physical therapies, the therapeutic arsenal available has been increased. As a consequence of the immunological complexity of the disease and its association with other diseases linked to the immune inflammatory response, there is a need to broaden knowledge about the physiopathology of disease and the immunoclonal and molecular mechanisms of action of the medicinal products used in order to provide the best care for patients.
