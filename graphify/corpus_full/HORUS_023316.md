# OMEGA COEFFICIENT: SOME METHODOLOGICAL AND PRACTICAL CONSIDERATIONS

The reliability is an important psychometric property of the measuring instruments in Human and social sciences, and indicates the accuracy of the measurement and quantify the error.There are several coefficients to estimate the reliability of an instrument, the best known is the Cronbach alpha, however its use has been questioned under conditions in which there is no tau-equivalence, normality, non-correlating errors or unidimensionality (Trizano, 2017; Frias, 2019) and as an alternative has been proposed the Omega coefficient, arguing that it is a more precise estimate of the internal consistency before the mentioned conditions (Dunn, Baguley & Brunsden, 2013).The omega coefficient is estimated from the factor charges with which it guarantees more stable calculations, greater certainty about the real value of the index and independent factor of the number of items of the instrument (Ventura & Leon, and the sum of the sum of the total)., highlighting the advantage of having confidence intervals when using this coefficient.
