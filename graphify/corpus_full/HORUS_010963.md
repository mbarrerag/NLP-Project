# OMI SCIENCES IN PLANTS AND ITS APPLICATION IN THE CONSIDERATION OF PATOGINE PLANT INTERACTIONS

Plants are fundamental to humans, as they provide us with food, medicines, wood, fuel, flowers and fibers. In addition, they provide shelter to a multitude of other living beings, produce the oxygen we breathe, maintain the soil, regulate moisture and contribute to the stability of the climate. Thus, their presence on the planet is fundamental, allowing to attack hunger, reduce poverty, protect the environment and boost economic development. These constitute about 80 percent of the food we eat and efficiently produce a high percentage of the oxygen we breathe. However, they can be affected by different biotic or abiotic factors that can have an impact on their life cycle and therefore, in the case of crops of commercial interest, significant economic implications. Different environmental abiotic factors such as drought, increase of temperature or radiation, as well as biotic factors, such as the presence of potential pathogens, can have an impact on the production of certain crops, reaching 100 % of losses in some cases. This undoubtedly has an adverse effect on food security, the employment of many people and the poverty of the countries. It is therefore essential to understand the external factors involved
