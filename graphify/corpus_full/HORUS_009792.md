# EARLY DEVELOPMENT OF THREAT-TEMPORATE CORAL ACROPORA CERVICORNIS UNDER CONTROLD CONDITIONS

Acropora cervicornis was one of the most abundant coral species in the Caribbean Sea reefs. From 1980 the species began to present massive mortalitys in all its distribution scope, which were attributed mainly to the disease of the white band.. Currently in Colombia the species is in critical danger.. This work collects a series of experiments to reproduce A. cervicornis in laboratory, induce its settlement with Costrosas Coral Algas (ACC) and evaluate the post-settlement survival. In addition the effect of physical factors such as the incidence of solar light and the correciphal sounds in the larval behavior, and the microtopography and color of the substrate in the settlement. It was possible to reproduce the species in laboratory and was followed from fertilization to the acquisition of zoovasal. The greater percentages of settlement were induced by the Titanoderma prototypum and Hydrolithon boergesenii. However, the settlement was low ( & 40 %) compared to the species of the Acropora genus.
