# AN ANALYTICAL REMEMBER OF THE PHILOSOPHYSICAL FUNDS OF NORTE-AMERIAN PRINCIPIALIS

The article presents a historical and conceptual journey through the foundations of bioethical principialism in its most widespread version, namely the one proposed by T.L. Beauchamp and J. Childress as a deontological proposal, analyzing philosophically the way in which one conceives what is a principle and the epistemological, anthropological and social consequences of adopting such a conception. First, the historical and cultural background leading to the formulation of North American principialism, starting from the Declaration of Helsinki and the Belmont Report, are presented. Secondly, the anthropological, epistemological and philosophical elements are analyzed at the base of the principles of biomedical ethics. Subsequently, the methodological elements and the bets of application of the principialist theory are analyzed
