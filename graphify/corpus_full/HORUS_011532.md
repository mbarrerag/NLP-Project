# EFFECT OF THE ELECTRIC FIELD ON THE CONSTRUCTION OF FUNCTIONAL MUSCLE TISSUES

Electrical stimulation aims to mimic the electrical environment of electroactive muscle cells by helping to regulate cell-cell and cell-extracellular matrix interactions; however, to date, there is no consensus on the basic parameters of electrical stimulation (frequency, voltage, pulse time, and duration); on the contrary, published studies have used a wide range of pulse widths and electric fields, which has generated great discrepancy in the results reported in the literature. In this context, the aim of this work is to determine the set of parameters that produce regions of uniform electric field within a muscle-on-a-chip device used to stimulate muscle cells encapsulated in a three-dimensional construct. In this sense, immortalized mouse C2C12 cell line myoblasts were encapsulated at a density of 5×106cell/mL in a cell/hydrogel mixture composed of 30% v/v Matrigel basement membrane matrix (Corning). The mixture was pipetted into each PDMS mold using cold tips to prevent premature polymerization. The cell/hydrogel mixture was polymerized, then incubated with growth medium for 2 days and then changed to differentiation medium for 7 days. Finally, the tissues were electrically stimulated (ES) on day 7 after the induction of myogenic differentiation of the skeletal muscle tissues. The muscle tissues were subjected to a square pulse stimulation regimen with a 10% duty cycle, an electric field intensity of 1V/mm2, and frequencies of 1, 5, 10, 25, and 50 Hz to assess the response. Partial results allowed to evidence through videos and image analysis that the electric fields promote the contraction of the C2C12 cultivated in the scaffolds. An overview of different electrical stimulation protocols and their applications were highlighted, evidencing that this biophysical stimulus may be used to study and simulate the effects of exercise. Additionally, this kind biological tissues are a promising tool for muscle regenerative techniques, where electric fields are a useful mechanism to treat muscle defects
