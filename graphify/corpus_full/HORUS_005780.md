# MULTIMODAL SOLID WASTE MANAGEMENT NETWORK WITH TRANSFER STATIONS: A CASE STUDY OF MEDELLIN, COLOMBIA

The rapid growth of population in increasingly large urban areas has made Municipal Solid Waste (MSW) management a huge challenge for urban planning. Planning, implementing and operating these networks require careful considerations of candidate locations, transportation and logistics, and potentially the incorporation of transfer stations (TS). TSs are facilities where MSW can be compacted and transferred to higher capacity transportation modes. Although this concept is not new in Operations Research and Urban Planning, in the form of hubs or consolidation centers respectively, there has been little research and case studies in the context of MSW. In this paper, we propose a methodology to locate TSs in a multi-modal MSW network. The methodology uses a Mixed Integer Linear Programming (MILP) facility location model and incorporates considerations of capacity in both TSs and landfills. We applied this methodology to the Medellin Metropolitan Area (MMA) in Colombia. Our work shows the potential advantages of multi-modal transportation networks to deal with MSW; poses research needs and questions related to feasibility in urban planning; and highlights the potential for public-private-partnerships
