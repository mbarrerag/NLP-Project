# EFFECT OF INAPPROPRIATE TREATMENT IN HOSPITALISED ADULT PATIENTS WITH PYELONEPHRITIS IN EMPIRICAL MANAGEMENT WITH CEFUROXIME, A COHORT STUDY. P0347

Effect of inappropriate Treatment in hospitalized Adult Patients with Pyelonephritis in empirical Management with Cefuroxime, a Cohort Study Background There is no clear information on the effect of antimicrobial resistance on the use of cefuroxime in patients with community pyelonephritis. The aim of this study is to evaluate the effect of inappropriate empirical therapy with cefuroxime in adult patients with pyelonephritis who were hospitalized over the hospital stay and readmission. Retrospective cohort study of patients with Escherichia coli pyelonephritis in a tertiary hospital who had been hospitalized and received empirical management with cefuroxime. The effect of inappropriate therapy (patients whose urine culture result was resistant or intermediate in the in vitro susceptibility test) on length of hospital stay and readmission for pyelonephritis was evaluated. A parametric multivariate analysis of time to event was performed using inverse probability weighting of the probability of inappropriate therapy, calculate with a propensity score based on covariates that predicted non-susceptibility and possible confounders. Logistic regression models were used with the pseudo-population adjusted for the weights for the readmission outcome. Between 2013 and 2020, 817 patients with community-acquired E. coli pyelonephritis were included, of which 113 (13.8%) patients received inappropriate therapy. Women were 66% and 75% of them were menopausal. The mean age was 64.2 years. 481 (56.1%) had some comorbidity and the most frequent were diabetes (18.5%), immunosuppression due to oncological disease or use of medications (18.4%), urolithiasis or previous urinary procedures (17.0%). Compared with appropriate therapy, in the adjusted analysis with IPTW, inappropriate therapy was associated with a shorter stay (HR 0.41, 95% CI: 0.31-0.54). Other variables that were associated to length-of-stay include the availability of oral treatment (HR 0.41, 95% CI: 0.31 -0.54), change of antibiotic during hospitalization (HR 2.24, 95% CI: 1.56 - 3.22) and admission to ICU during hospitalization (HR 2.59, 95% CI: 1.84 - 3.66). Compared with appropriate therapy, inappropriate therapy was not found to be related to readmission for pyelonephritis (OR 1.31, 95% CI: 0.54 - 3.15). Inappropriate therapy is not associated with a longer hospital stay, as is the case of ICU admission. Having oral medications available reduces hospital stay. Inappropriate therapy was also not related to readmission for pyelonephritis
