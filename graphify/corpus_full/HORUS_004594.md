# PERIODONTAL PATHOGENS IDENTIFIED IN AORTIC VALVE SAMPLES USING METAGENOMICS

To compare the bacterial richness and diversity between calcified aortic (CAVD) and healthy heart valves (non-CAVD). Five RNA sequencing datasets were downloaded from GEO (IDs: SE138531, GSE76718, GSE171208, GSE148219, GSE153555). The RNA was isolated from 28 severe calcified aortic valve samples (CAVS) and 24 healthy heart valves (non-CAVS). RNA was sequenced on Illumina HiSeq 2500 platforms. Single and paired-end reads from 20 to 300 bp were available. The quality of the fastq files was evaluated using FastQC and Trimmomatic. The Kraken2/Bracken (a taxonomic sequence classification system) protocol was followed to assign bacterial taxonomic labels to the reads and per-taxa abundance estimates based on the Kraken Standard database and Greengenes 13_5 databases. The diversity and abundance of oral bacteria was analized using Vegan. The CAVS consisted of 18 men and 2 women (mean age 68.4±7.4) and the non-CAVS group were 16 men and 5 women (mean age 47.5±18.0). The sex was not available for 11 samples. Based on the Standard database 4391 species were identified in CAVD and 4208 in non-CAVD, with 2825 species in common. Based on the Greengene database, 85 species were unique to CAVD, 35 to non-CAVD, and 27 were shared. Twenty-six species from the Oral Human Microbiome database were identified in CAVD and 9 in non-CAVD; seven of them were in common. In CAVD, 22 bacteria from the subgingival plaque complex were identified, while 30 periodontal bacteria were found in non-CAVD. Among the red complex bacteria, Porphyromonas gingivalis and Tannerella forsythia, were in common between the groups while Treponema denticola was found only in non-CAVD. The periodontal pathogen Propionibacterium acnes was twice more frequent in non-CAVD. The green subgingival plaque complex, Capnocytophaga ochracea, was detected only in CAVD. Based on RNA sequencing data, this research shows that pathogenic and non-pathogenic oral bacteria get established on the aortic valves (possibly travelling into the blood). Finally, these results reinforced the importance of maintaining good oral health to prevent the development or worsening of cardiovascular diseases and to maintain a healthy heart
