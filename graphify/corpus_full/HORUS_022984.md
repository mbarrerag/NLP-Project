# Spectral analysis of the temporal and spatial distribution of Milankovitch cycles in -18O ocean floor records over the last 5 million years.

The influence that the orbital movements of the earth (Milankovitch cycles) have on the terrestrial climate is currently an important topic of debate. In this paper a new methodology is proposed and used to quantify the influence that the insolation has on the +18Benthic +51 registers from different regions of the world. Additionally, the spectral energy content of each registry is studied, as well as the relation with its position and the range of time covered. These analyses were carried out by constructing and processing power spectra, coherence and dephase; using the method of multitaper spectral estimation with windows type Slepian. It was estimated that, on average, 38.3% of the variations of +18 have a causal relationship with the insolation. It is found that such orbital influence does not have a significant relation to the geographical position of the registry, but if it has a significant temporal dependence.
