# Social determination of inequality in access to biotechnology medicines in cognitive capitalism: the case of recombinant insulin in Colombia.

This research aims to contribute to the construction of an alternative theoretical interpretive framework based on the classical liberal hegemonic vision of inequity in access to health technologies, in particular insulin, from the perspective of the critical political economy, based on the approach of cognitive capitalism. It begins by exploring inequity in access to insulin in Colombia. It then analyzes the production, distribution, consumption and capitalist accumulation of insulin during the stage of industrial capitalism, from its discovery to its mass production, to enter later, in equal analysis in the stage of cognitive capitalism, addressing the transition from the pharmacochemical paradigm to biotechnological, to approach the understanding of the dynamics of knowledge approximation through the strengthening of intellectual property, and to acts of resistance, including biohacking, which understand knowledge as a common good. The emergence of biotechnology and biotechnological medicines among them, insulin is the result of the profound transformations of the contemporary capitalist accumulation regime in the last five decades, where knowledge, the expansion of capitalism in the proposed field of life, intellectual property, and technological change, have played a key role
