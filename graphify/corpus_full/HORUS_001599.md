# Risk factors for self-harm before, during and after COVID-19 in Colombian adolescents [self-harm in adolescents through COVID-19]

There are interactions between the factors associated with suicidal behavior, which health crises can influence. The objective was to create an explanatory model of self-harm in adolescents before, during and after the COVID-19. A sample of 560 adolescents from Colombia was followed (2019–2023), allowing cross-sectional measurements to be made at three points in time: before, during and after the COVID-19 pandemic, assessed using validated scales to determine affective symptoms, resilience, parental adjustment, which were applied before, during and after the pandemic. Binary logistic regression models identified key risk factors for suicidal behavior that varied across the three time periods, such as affective/behavioral symptoms, family factors, and Indigenous ethnicity in the post-pandemic period. The findings align with theoretical models emphasizing the interplay of individual, social, and environmental factors in suicide risk, while highlighting the dynamic nature of these factors and their potential exacerbation during health crises like the COVID-19 pandemic. During the pandemic, the most significant risk factors for suicidal behavior in adolescents were caregiver relationship problems. After the pandemic, there was a significant increase in the influence of symptoms such as sadness, anxiety, and behavioral problems. This research underscores the need for tailored interventions and public policies that address the unique risk factors and vulnerabilities faced by adolescents during and after major societal disruptions
