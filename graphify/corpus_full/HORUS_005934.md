# Crop physiology of Physalis peruviana

The goldenberry adapts well to altitudes between 1500 and 3000m a.s.l., with optimum average temperatures in Colombia between 13°C and 16°C. 1500-2000 hours/year of direct sunshine promote the size, quality, and ripening of fruits. Light use efficiency is much higher in the reproductive stage. The roots and the basal vegetative stem are the largest sinks for starch and sucrose. A constant water supply is necessary for the indeterminate growth habit but heavy rains after a dry season cause fruit cracking. This crop does not tolerate more than 4 days of waterlogging. A deficiency of nitrogen and boron affects the development of plants the most. Depending on climatic conditions and crop management, flowering begins 2-3 months after planting, while fruit development takes between 60 and 80 days. Fruit weight is between 5 and 10g, depending on the variety or ecotype, providing yields between 10 and 25t/ha/year
