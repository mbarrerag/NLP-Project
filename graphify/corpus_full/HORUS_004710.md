# Modeling and simulation of the plasma response of finite arrangements of metal nanospheres for biosensors design

This thesis is developed within the framework of the alliance between the National University of Colombia, the Distrital University Francisco José de Caldas and the Leibniz Institute of Fotonic Technology (Jena, Germany), for the development of an ultrasensitive platform of plastic detection. This work consists of carrying out a theoretical-computing analysis of the plastic response of different geometric configurations of gold nanospheres, in order to evaluate the most viable configuration for its application in the biodetection platform. The method of Finitas differences in the Time Domain (FDTD) is used to study the optical properties of different configurations such as isolated nanosphere, dimer, hexagon and heptamer. Special attention is paid to the influence of the dielectric environment, the amount of nanospheres in each system and the separation between them. The results revealed distinctive patterns in the plasma response of the different systems, showing significant changes in function of the dielectric environment and the disposition of the nanosphes.
