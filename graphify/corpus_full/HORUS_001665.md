# Relational Indigenous spatialities

This chapter focuses on Indigenous peoples’ political and territorial demands in addition to indigenous people who are academically trained in geography and their demands for recognition of their territories and territorialities under their own conceptualizations, and practices. It examines how Indigenous proposals are redefining geographical categories from their ontologies and epistemologies and positioning a relationality between humans and non-humans as living beings and across the diverse territorialities among humans and non-humans. Indigenous proposals also position a notion of territory as a living being and as a being with a power of agency in permanent interaction with humans. Indigenous territorialities oscillate and intertwine time and space, memories, and processes of conviviality among living beings, which respond to a relational and spiral worldview that connects the whole territory. This chapter will also discuss Indigenous women’s demands of recognition of a body-territory relationship from relational dynamics, implying a rethinking of political, collective, and spatial practices. These geographies are associated with ways of being, existing, and feeling in and with the territory and allow a recognition of their rights to continue living in their territories. The chapter calls these perspectives relational Indigenous spatialities
