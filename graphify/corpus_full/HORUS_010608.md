# INFLUENCE OF RAICE RAGES ON SOIL PROPERTIES: INSUMS FOR THE RECOVERY OF DEGRADED ECOSYSTEMS

Livestock is a frequent activity in tropical dry forests with negative impacts on their diversity and ecosystem processes. Under scenarios of high ecosystem degradation, identifying species that promote the recovery of soil properties is one of the main challenges of ecological restoration. In this study we measured six root traits and six physical properties of the soil in eleven tree species planted six years ago in degraded pastures in Magdalena's BST (Huila). We found that the root traits were related to three soil properties: pH, water content and apparent soil density. The pH was higher in species with branched roots, with high amount of fine roots, high content of dry matter, and thin roots. The water content and density of the soil were higher in species with branched roots and high amounts of fine roots respectively. Sorpressively, neither the specific length of the root nor the density of the thick root were related to soil properties. Our results confirm the wide diversity of functional strategies of tree species in the dry forest and are a key input for the prioritization of species in restoration projects
