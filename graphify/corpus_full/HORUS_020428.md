# Coffee yield and nutritional status (Coffea arabica L.) in response to biocarbon application

Coffee from Colombia is recognized worldwide for its taste and mild aroma. Its cultivation generates a large amount of residues such as fresh pulp, which leads to environmental, health and economic problems. The obtaining of biocarbon (BC) by pyrolysis of the coffee pulp and its incorporation into the soil can be a complement to the mineral nutrition of the crop. The objective was to evaluate the effect of the application of biocarb obtained from coffee pulp on the physiology and agronomic behavior of the coffee cultivation variety Castillo el Tambo (Coffea arabica L.). The research has been developed in two stages: i) vegetative density, using plants of three months of age, carried out in greenhouse conditions; and ii) production, using a commercial coffee cultivation of three years of age, carried out in Chaparral Tolima. Four doses of BC (0, 4, 8 and 16 t has-1) and four plants of chemical fertilization (FQ) and coffee (0%, 33%, 66% and 100% of nutritional requirements) were evaluated.
