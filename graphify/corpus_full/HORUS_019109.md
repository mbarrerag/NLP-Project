# A CASE SERIES EXAMINATION OF WIM HOF BREATHING ON ATTENTION AND EXECUTIVE FUNCTION

Wim Hof Breathing (WHB) has been described as power breathing during which three or more sets of 30 full inhalations and exhalations are followed by varied periods of breath holding. The proposed benefits of WHB include stress reduction, greater focus and mental clarity, and improvements in cognition, cardiorespiratory performance, and metabolic/physical performance. The purpose of this case series report is to describe the effects of 34 days of WHB on the cognitive domains of attention and executive function. Case Description: Five healthy college students (3 female and 2 male) with a mean SD age, height, and weight of 27.6±3.13 years, 169.9±7.2 cm, and 76.8± 13.8 kg respectively, performed the coding subtest of the Repeatable Battery for the Assessment of Neuropsychological Status (RBANS) and Stroop Color and Word Test (SCWT) before and after 5 weeks of WHB. WHB was performed by each student at home 7 days/week using the Wim Hof Method Guided Breathing for Beginners (3 Rounds Slow Paced) video on YouTube, which consisted of 3 sets of 30 full inhalations and exhalations followed by breath holding for 30, 60, and 90 seconds after sets 1, 2, and 3 respectively or until breath could no longer be held. Oxygen saturation (SaO2) and HR were monitored via oximetry (FS20F Finger Oximeter) before, during, and 2 minutes after the session. Results were recorded via the ViHealth mobile app, providing acute WHB effects and adherence. Outcomes: Adherence to WHB was 79% and elicited an average decrease in SaO2 of 24%. WHB produced a near significant (p=.06) decrease in SCWT times from 23.0±5.9 seconds at baseline to 19.2±2.9 seconds post Wim Hof and a significant (p=.04) decrease in RBANS times from 159.2±22.2 seconds at baseline to 148.0±18.6 post Wim Hof. While the effects of chronic hypoxia have been proven to be cognitively detrimental, the effects of intermittent hypoxia (IH) seems to provide neuroprotective benefits at lower doses. Previous studies suggest a possible neuroprotective effect on memory and concentration in subjects exposed to IH protocols. The results of this current case study demonstrates that the low grade intermittent hypoxic nature of WHB may also offer some cognitive benefit in the domains of attention and executive function. While SCWT only yielded results near significance, the power of this study created a major limitation in the results of the study. Due to its high feasibility, more research should also be done to explore the potential role of WHB as a non-invasive method of ischemic preconditioning known as cross preconditioning (CPC). In CPC, the use of stimuli other than ischemia can evoke structural changes to help develop ischemic tolerance, which can be practically be stimulated using WHB. The wide potential application for WHB as not only a preventative measure for stroke, but also as a therapeutic agent in cognitive impairments warrants further understanding of its mechanisms
