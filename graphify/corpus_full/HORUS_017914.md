# Impact of socioeconomic zonal factors, land use and mobility on the frequency of traffic accidents in Bogotá.

Traffic accidents are generally involuntary and random events generated by at least one vehicle in motion that causes damage to people and goods involved in it. In Bogotá, these are the second most important cause of violent death, cost on average an equivalent to 2.9% of the Gross Domestic Product of the city and affect the mobility of approximately 7 million inhabitants, so they constitute a cross-sectional problem of health, economy and mobility. The urban structure of the city established by the inhabitants, the uses of the soil and the mobility represent an environmental factor of interest that is related to the traffic accidents. One method to analyze the relationship of these variables with traffic accidents is through area data. The present study was aimed at evaluating the impact of socioeconomic variables, the use of the soil and mobility in the frequency of traffic accidents in the urban soil of Bogotá by analyzing the spatial units: Transport Analysis Zones and Territorial Units of Mobility Analysis and to analyze the spatial units. For this, the performance of spatial regression models and vector support in the prediction of two indices of vehicle accident that relate the total of transit accidents and the deaths caused by them in the spatial perimeters and spatial unit, was assessed
