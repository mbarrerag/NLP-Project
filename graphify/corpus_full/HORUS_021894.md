# The President's legislative agenda in the public policy process: the case of Colombia (1994-2018).

This thesis addresses the study of the role of presidents in the process of public policies in Colombia. For this we concentrate, for the first time in Colombia, on the characterization and analysis of the presidential legislative agendas and their fate in the Congress. We just start from the question about the relationship between agendas and legislative success. Our hypothesis is that there are some types of agendas that favor the success of the president in the Congress. This work builds a typology of the agendas based on its detailed characterization according to three variables: size, diversity and “ambicioity”. Thanks to this effort we find that effectively the type of agendas that share the characteristics of small, less diversity or more focalization, and that are more ambitious, relate particularly strikingly to the best rates of presidential legislative success, among the six governments analyzed. This empirical work is based on several original data bases for Colombia, constructed using the codebook of the Project of Comparative Agendas created by professors Brian D. Jones and Frank R. Baumgartner. The measurements of diversity and ambitiosity are equally innovative in the country. The first one is supported by the literature, statistically and the index. This thesis shows the centrality of the presidential government in the process of public policies in Colombia, in line with previous findings, but with the extra demonstration that allows us to variable of ambiciosity. Finally, this concrete thesis is his contributions in five components, 1) new databases that can be further expanded and explored for various purposes, while at the same time constituting the starting point of the Draft of Comparative Agendas in Colombia. 2) the first detailed characterization of the Presidential Legislative Agendas in Colombia in a period of 24 years that group all the complete governments between 1994 and 2018. 3) the proposal of types of agendas, perhaps the first of this type in the public policy research. 4) in relation to the above, the novel relation of the types of agendas with the presidential legislative success, finding that it can effectively be a new explanatory factor for the literature. 5 the theoretical enlargement, which implies that the theories may begin to consider that the configuration of agendas can be an explanatory factor, together with others, of the presidential success in the Congress.
