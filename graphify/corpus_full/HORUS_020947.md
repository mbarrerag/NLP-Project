# Study of the behaviour of a fuel cell made up of a new protonic conduction system of Polyvinyl Difluoride (PVDF) + phosphoric acid (H3PO4) working up to 60 C.

This project will aim to synthesize and characterize (thermal, electrical and structurally) new polymer systems based on polyvinyl difluoride (PVDF) and phosphoric acid (H3PO4) to apply them in fuel cells which are described as a device that converts the chemical energy produced by means of the reaction between hydrogen (H2) and oxygen (O2) with a catalyst generally of platinum, in electricity and heat. Following this to carry out a study of the operation of the cell in medium temperature environments between 20 and 60 C, as a future technological application given the limiting of the current density of the cells based on Nafion working at more than 35 C. We will concentrate on the phase transitions that experience the polymeric system in high temperatures since these enrich the spatial mobility of the load carriers, in this case the hydraulics of the acid, and this favors to some degree the electrode reactions that happen in the catalysts of the fuel cell. On the other hand, the polymeric superionic systems show a great technological expectation, especially in the development of the mobile devices. a better concentration of the merica to be applied in the fuel cell and to perform the measurement processes of the fuel cell in environments of average temperature between 20 and 60 C, and to analyze the results that will be evaluated in scientific journals of a national or international character
