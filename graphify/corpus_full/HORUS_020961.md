# Implementation of new RNAi-based technologies for controlling Sigatoka black in bananas

The black sigatoka, caused by the fungus Pseudocercospora fijiensis, is a disease of great importance that affects the production of bananas. Conventional control of the disease today is still dependent on the application of chemical fungicides. These fungicides are potentially dangerous to the environment and human health and induce the emergence of resistant strains, forcing the exploration of more effective alternatives for the management of the disease. In this project it is proposed to evaluate the feasibility of the use of genetic RNA-mediated silencing (RNAi) as a mechanism for the control of P. fijiensis. For this, sequences of double-chain RNA (dsRNA) homologous to the genes PfCYP51 and PfFus3 that were previously identified as important genes for the survival of the fungus and will be tested their protective activity against the Black Sigatoka in banana plants. Using this strategy, they are intended to develop tests of silence in controlled environment that can be scaled for the management of the disease in the field
