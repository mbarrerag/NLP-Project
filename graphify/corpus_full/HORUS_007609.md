# Ecological and evolutionary diversity of birds of the biogeographical ecotones of Guaviare

With this project, the student Natalia Pérez will be able to execute the plan outlined for a chapter of her doctoral dissertation, and supplement data for a second, which tries to understand the biological heterogeneity of the avifauna in zones of biogeographical transition that converge in the Guaviare. The immense gaps of knowledge about the biological diversity in regions with a megadiverse biota, such as the ecotone zones in the north of South America, represents a scientific and conservation challenge. The geographical distribution of the species, the diversity and distribution patterns are known in an insufficient way, but even more unknown are the evolutionary processes that originated them and the ecological processes that maintain it. In this project, we address two fundamental focal points in the investigation of the biodiversity of a zone of biogeographic ecotones in the department of Guaviare, Colombia, where they converge at least three biogeographic zones in transition: Orinoquía, Amazonia and Escudo Guayaño. First, we explore the evolutionary processes that have given place to isolated and separate populations in the basin orinocense. This region will focus on aspects of the composition and structure of regional bird communities in order to determine the biogeographic affinities of the avifauna in the region and its phylogenetic diversity. We propose to better understand the geographical distribution of the birds that converge in this region through field work and ecological and biogeographic analysis. To this end, we will not only base our efforts on the data and records of the literature and biodiversity databases, but we will also carry out field work in the less documented ecotone sites. Through this complementary and detailed approach, we will contribute decisively to the understanding and conservation of the birds of Guaviare. The funding requested in this call will be used mainly for the execution of a field exit and infrastructure and inputs that allow analysis of collected samples
