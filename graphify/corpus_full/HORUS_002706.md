# The drivers of fire in the Caatinga Biome in Brazil

The goal of this research was to analyze the drivers of fire occurrence in the Caatinga Biome, located in the northeastern part of Brazil. Thirty-four explanatory variables of fire occurrence were selected and extracted from divers sources for the period between 1985 and 2022, including one atemporal variable (elevation) and the 2022 population data of municipalities. Environmental variables included rainfall during dry season, temperature, evapotranspiration, relief features, and land use land cover (Forest, Savanna, Open grasslands, Rocky Outcrop, Urban Area, and other Forest formations). Economic variables included agricultural crops and animal husbandry, while social variables referred to demographic data. The variables were fitted in the regression model using the Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) method. Geographically Weighted Regression (GWR) was also used to explain the non-stationary spatial relationship between the independent and dependent variables. The OLS model explained 58.4 % of the variation in fire occurrence. However, an analysis of the residuals with the Global Moran Index revealed significant positive spatial autocorrelation (0.318), indicating a violation of the independence assumption in OLS regression. To address this issue, the GWR model that includes 12 variables explained 69 % of the variations in fire occurrence in the Caatinga Biome. In addition, fire drivers in the Caatinga were predominantly related to environmental features such as Savanna Formation, Rocky Outcrop, Forest Formation, Temperature, and Precipitation during dry season. Finally, this study demonstrated that the GWR model can be an effective tool for capturing the spatial heterogeneity of fire drivers, providing a more complete understanding of the complex dynamics influencing fire occurrence in this semi-arid region
