# Study of Nicotinamide Mononucleotide Adenyl Transferases (NMNATs) of protozoa parasites: Determination of its bifunctionality as molecular chaperones and implementation of a methodology for its detection

Certain proteins have the ability to exercise more than one function, property known as moonlighting. The proteins that present this characteristic are enzymes that have as canonical activity pathways of the basal energetic metabolism. In order for the proteins to perform their function or functions, it is necessary that they have a native conformation. To achieve this structure within the cell, it is necessary that the proteins be assisted by the molecular chaperones. The Nicotinamide Mononucleotide Adenil Transferases (NMNATs), in addition to their role as final step in the synthesis of the nucleotide of nicomide and adenin (NAD+), has been described a function moonlight as molecular chaperones in several organisms. Given the high importance of the NMNATs, these represent a pharmacological target relevant for the diagnosis and treatment of protozoan parasites (Lishmania braziliensis, Trypanosoma cruzero, and Giardia lambilia).
