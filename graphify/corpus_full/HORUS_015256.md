# ¿HURDLE LOW-COST TECHNOLOGIES EVALUATION TOWARDS SHELF-LIFE EXTENSION OF FRESH PURPLE YAMPEE (DIOSCOREA TRIFIDA L. F.) TUBERS¿

The food industry has made important efforts to optimize resources and processes towards a greater sustainability, increasing exponentially on past decades. A variety of solutions have emerged to approach the issue depending on the disciplines, by designing new products, taking advantage of by-products or improving critical points. Productive systems in Colombia for example, have followed recent trends to promote agroecological practices that favor cultural and biological dynamics through the utilization of local, diverse, underutilized species that promote the natural resilience of the ecosystems. Such is the case of purple cush cush yam, an American yam grown over the country that, as other species of Dioscorea genus, may face high postharvest losses due to the lack of knowledge of its physiology and postharvest technologies, thus having a great environmental and economical repercussion on the chain. Considering the high vulnerability of many of the producing communities, the combination of scalable, low-cost technologies, including GRAS ingredients were assessed in the preservation of the postharvest quality of the tuber, through a block design using a combination of hot water, acid-calcium chloride dip and edible lipid-based coating. As expected, weight loss evolution showed a pronounced slope at the beginning of the measurements, that decreased along the time. According to Hotelling's statistic, parallelism was not ruled out, being greater at the end stages, however a great disparity in the biological material initial weight may be responsible for the effect, and not the treatments. Differences were found in total color change ¿E, dry matter content, soluble solids, firmness, respiratory rate and browning index along the storage progression as further described. An accelerated essay on the tubers however, showed a greater percentage of surface visible microbial contamination that may be a disadvantage of the lipid coating application. The effect of potential low-cost technologies to decrease postharvest losses and prolong shelf life of purple yampee underutilized species of tubers was assessed as a foundation for future research that may be easily transferred to yam producers
