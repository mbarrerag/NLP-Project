# THE STANDARDITY OF LOGIC PHILOSOPHICUS TRACTATUS: THE LOGIC CONSISTENCE IN CONTINENTAL LEGAL PHILOSOPHY

This article does not seek to establish direct relations between the Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus and the tradition of continental law, rather it tries to show how legal theory can benefit from the logical conception of language presented in Wittgenstein's first work. In this sense, it will be presented as necessarily akin to continental tradition: this perspective understands law not as an empirical discipline that regulates from previous cases, but a social science that involves a particular epistemic treatment of the world. To this end, a brief outline of the theory of law is presented that has been articulated with Wittgenstein's ideas to show how, based on the conceptual tools of the Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus, a logical theory of law can be formulated that serves contemporary constitutional systems
