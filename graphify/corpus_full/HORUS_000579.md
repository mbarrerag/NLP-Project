# Manufacture and characterization of graduated thin layers and multilayered InxAl1-xN for potential use in photovoltaic power generation.

The doctoral thesis focuses on the study of the InAlN semiconductor, a versatile material with applications in optoelectronic devices such as LEDs, lasers, solar cells and transistors of high mobility of electrons. Various methodologies were investigated for the synthesis and characterization of layers of InAlN, highlighting the technique of co-sputering DC at ambient temperature, which allows to reduce production costs. This innovative approach facilitates the manufacture of more affordable and efficient devices. In addition, the deposit conditions were analyzed by means of single-chamber sputtering, observing how variables such as the flow of gases and the applied power affect the structural and optical properties of the material. An alternative methodology was proposed to the traditional Tauc method to determine the optical properties, providing a more complete characterization when considering all possible electronic transitions.
