# Evaluation of milling efficiency and classification of raw materials: case study of a cement plant

This study evaluated the efficiency of the grinding and sorting system in a closed circuit for a cement plant. The energy requirements determined by the Bond model were compared with the energy consumption calculated in plant using the mobile average model. The causes of differences between theoretical calculations and actual values were identified considering the classification of particles by partition curves and the Fish-Hook effect. It was found that the energy requirements of milling in plant exceed the Bond model by 125.9-229.3% in terms of energy consumption, due to a fairly low classification efficiency caused by the high percentage of fine particles in the rejection current. This was evidenced in the classification curves through short circuit, the clarity index and the Fish-Hook effect. This provides information to take measures and improve the efficiency of the process
