# EFFECTS OF THE SPEED OF ADVANCE AND POTENCE OF LASER IN GEOMETRIC QUALITY OF SHOULTIFIED PIECES BY SELECTIVE LASER FUSION (SLF)

The manufacturing techniques of Additive Manufacturing are widely used technologies that allow to create metal parts of complex geometries with less time and cost of manufacture, compared to classical manufacturing techniques such as machining. Laser Selective Melting (SLM) and Laser Selective Fusion (SLF) are some of the different additive manufacturing techniques that use high-energy lasers to construct metal parts from a 3D model. Although manufacturing with this type of techniques is more economical and fast than with other additive manufacturing techniques, these techniques present some geometric and surface quality problems. Some of these problems are high roughness, porosities and nominal radios as used in the CAD model. Lack of precision in the nominal radios generates problems in areas whose applications require high dimensional precision, such as manufacturing of dental and orthopedic implants, or precision parts in mechanical applications. This is because the lack of dimensional precision affects the tribology and functionality of the parts.
