# TOPOLOGY OPTIMIZED DESIGN OF FUNCTIONALLY GRADED POROUS STRUCTURES FOR LOW-ENERGY IMPACT APPLICATIONS

Several natural materials such as bones, teeth, horns, bird beaks and wood are graded in order to comply with one or various functionalities depending on the specific application. Moreover, not only these biological structures are graded, but many of them are also porous, which helps nature to efficiently use material and be lightweight. Typically, the aforementioned living materials work under impact loadings, which is a fairly critical situation compared to the static case. Due to the well-performance of porous natural materials for dynamic loads, humans have been studying their behavior and trying to replicate their design to use their advantages for engineering applications in the biomimicry research area. However, translating biological concepts into manufacturable prototypes have its own challenges: the bioinspired design process of engineering structures has many parameters to be selected and configured. Therefore, a structural optimization technique is more suitable for the formidable task of trying to mimic a natural structure. Herein, the topology optimization (TO) is used to design porous plates at the macroscopic level in order to obtain the stiffest structures. Hence, to achieve the proposed objective, we use a local per-element constraints by specifying upper bounds on the localized material volume in the proximity of each element, which is treated as a p-norm to form an equivalent global constraint to facilitate an efficient optimization process. This constraint is explored in several ways to produce functionally graded porous structures (FGPSs) in 2D and 3D. The traditional SIMP (solid isotropic material with penalization) model is used to interpolate the material properties, while the design variables (relative element density) are filtered, and the projection method is used to obtain 0-1 design. The optimization problem is solved with a gradient-based algorithm (MMA: method of moving asymptotes), where the sensitivities are computed with the adjoint variable method (AVM). The impact is modeled as a linear elastodynamic phenomenon solved with the transient finite element analysis (FEA) and the implicit Newmark method. Thereafter, the optimized topology is postprocessed to obtain a CAD based model. Next, the complex geometry obtained by TO is additively manufactured (AM). Several test specimens are fabricated in order to explore the gradation function and the different topology parameters that results in different designs. Finally, the impact behavior of the topology optimized porous plates is verified in a low-energy impact drop test facility using a high-speed camera to capture some of the phenomena. Results show that the techniques proposed herein (TO + AM) are promising approaches obtaining energy absorbing structures
