# Progress in the research of intestinal bacterial populations of Cavia porcellus

The cuy (Cavia porcellus) is a small mammal present in several countries of South America where it has gained recognition for its aptitude as a species of promimiary livestock production.In Colombia, the breeding of this species is mainly in the department of Nariño, place where it forms an important part of the culture, gastronomic diversity and is the economic sustenance of many families in the urban and rural sector.The majority of the research efforts of the C. porcellus have focused on analyzing zootechnical parameters (weight gain, food conversion, number of births, among others) and the effect of different diets on the productive performance.As well as the genetic improvement of the populations, to fix attributes such as the quality of the meat and the expression of desirable characteristics that improve the reproductive performance. Physiologically, C. porcellus is classified as a single-gastric herbivore of the microbial fermentation in the blind (a tissue modified of the thick intestine) therefore, the most of its nutritional requirements depend on the fermentation process in this segment. In spite of its importance, there are few studies that address the composition of this micro-cacaltane composition. will be molecularly detected by end-point PCR using as a target for amplification pathogenicity genes of relevant agents (e.g. Salmonella typhimurium invA gene, Yersinia pseudotuberculosis Ail gene).This analysis will allow to determine whether the conditions of management influence the presence of these pathogens in the context of animal health and epidemiological surveillance. Finally, this proposal is expected to constitute a baseline in the context of basic and applied science that enriches microbiological knowledge in the species, improves the understanding of the country's alternative productive systems and the biotechnological potential of the microorganisms present in the C. porcellus microbiome
