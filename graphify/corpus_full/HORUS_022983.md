# The Colombian hyper-presidentialism in the face of the democratic challenges of the 20th century: the presidential figure after the 1991 Constitution.

In Colombia, political letters and constitutional reforms have been characterized by an organic tendency to political paternalism, to embody power in a man within the “myth of the protective ruler”, to personalize power and to give confidence to an individual rather than an institution. This phenomenon has produced a general weakness in the mechanisms of political representation and has led to the establishment of authoritarian regimes in the country throughout its constitutional history. The main purpose of this paper is to analyze the Colombian presidential figure through a constitutional study that starts from the historical individualization of the two main Colombian political letters and their reforms (Constitutions of 1886 and 1991), taking into account the functioning of the executive figure, its gestation, influence and importance within a political system proposed as presidential democracy. Likewise, it seeks to identify basic theoretical and conceptual notions that guide the analysis of the configuration and evolution of the presidential figure in Colombia, in order to finally dilucate the concept of “hyperpresilisentialism” as a phenomenon present in the history of this figure
