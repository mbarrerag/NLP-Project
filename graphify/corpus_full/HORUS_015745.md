# METAVIROME CHARACTERIZATION IN ANOPHELES DARLINGI FROM THREE DIFFERENT GEOGRAPHICAL REGIONS OF COLOMBIA

Next-generation sequencing technologies have increased knowledge about the diversity and ubiquity of viruses in arthropod hosts; yet, there is lack of knowledge in the composition of the neotropical anopheline metavirome. Thus, this work aimed to characterize the metavirome of Anopheles darlingi from Colombia. Mosquitoes were collected in the Bajo-Cauca (BAC) region-NW, the Pacific (PAC), W and the Amazonas (AM), SE Colombia. Mosquitoes were grouped into pools and total RNA was extracted and sequenced by RNA-Seq Illumina (30M PE-reads). The An. darlingi reference genome was used to map the reads to exclude mosquito transcripts. Viral family read counts were determined by a sensitive taxonomic classification to find maximum exact matches against the Kaiju NCBI-nr+euk database. RNA virus-derived sequences were retrieved by contig de-novo assembly with SPAdes and a BLAST search against the Reference Viral Database was performed. Finally, Bray-Curtis (BC) was estimated from normalized datasets. The Illumina runs generated 357M PE-reads; 101,700 were classified as of viral origin, distributed in 16 viral families and two unclassified groups. A high proportion of reads was assigned to unclassified-Ortervirales (52%), Chuviridae (10%) and Baculoviridae (8%). A similar viral composition was found in An. darlingi from all regions. Regarding the distribution of viral read counts by family, a high similarity was observed at the intra-region level (BC>0.84); and among regions, a greater similarity was observed between PAC and BAC (BC=0.74) as compared to AM (BC=0.41 and 0.43, respectively). Furthermore, seven RNA virus families were recovered from virus-derived sequences. The similarity found in the An. darlingi virome between BAC and PAC and its difference with AM may be due to the Andes; this mountain range acts as a geographical barrier separating An. darlingi population from the northwest and southeast. Also, the differences may be due to environmental specific conditions in each region. This work contributes knowledge on the Anopheles virome composition and on the ubiquity and abundance of insect-specific viruses that could be used as biological control agents
