# Justice and understanding. Hermeneutic dimensions of transitional justice and its application in the Colombian case.

The project in mention is presented by the Research Group _x0093_The hermeneutic in the contemporary philosophical discussion_x0094_ (Minciencias A) of the Department of Philosophy of the National University of Colombia. The project is, in the first instance, a philosophical research, that is conceptual and theoretical, that seeks to contribute to the discussion on the notion of transitional justice from contributions from contemporary hermeneutic philosophy, while maintaining a constant dialogue with the classical philosophical paradigms of justice. In the second place, beyond a purely speculative reflection, the proposed research aims to rely on concrete experiences of application of transitional justice in Colombia, seeking to give empirical content to the set of notions that, according to the proposal, are typical of a hermeneutic constellation of transitional justice. For this second aspect, the Research Group counts with the support of a task of the sovereign justice and its arbitrariness to the special jurisdiction for peace (JEP). To make this description more clear it is necessary to present briefly the theoretical context that underpins the project. This is the first step in the area of victim redress and the recomposition of the social fabric through, for example, the demand for truth and the recognition of fault on the part of the victim. Moreover, beyond this conceptual innovation, restorative justice seems to have practical advantages with respect to the remuneration model: it not only decongests the administration of justice and lightens the processes, but also seems more efficient than the punitive model to reconstitute an injured sense of community life and social harmony. This project is therefore intended to carry out this foundation on the basis of only theoretical elements of contemporary hermeneutic philosophy, especially in the notion of understanding. The ultimate objective is to outline a pragmatic advantage of this innovative concept of justice, which is to be based on a philosophical basis. This project is intended to be based on the theoretical elements of contemporary hermeneutic philosophy, not only as a theoretical guide, but also as a theoretical one to be developed as a central concept of restorative or transitional justice
