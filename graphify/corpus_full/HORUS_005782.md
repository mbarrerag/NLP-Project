# FORMAL AND INFORMAL FREIGHT TRANSPORTATION AT MARKETPLACES IN URBAN AREAS: EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE FROM MEDELLIN, COLOMBIA

This paper evaluates the importance of freight transportation and its relationship with marketplaces in urban areas. For this purpose, the authors conducted semi-structured surveys in the most important marketplaces and large trade corridors in Medellín, Colombia considering both the formal and informal vendors for cargo transportation in the retail and wholesale food supply sector. The results show that these marketplaces are essential generators of large cargo trips. They attract trips from various regions of Colombia and produce others to supply traders in different metropolitan area and municipalities. The marketplaces play a vital role in the supply of fresh food to Medellin, generating 1.3% of the daily cargo trips in the metro area, which translates to about 3,178 trips or about 15,749 tons/day of freight movement. Unstructured interviews revealed that people engaged in informal economies face difficult social and sanitary conditions, in addition to reductions in their income due to the payment of rent and parking fees. The ever-growing informal or grey economy in Colombia leads to an increase in the occupation of public spaces, contributing to urban congestion and, as this research reveals, large load generators and traffic situations associated with such informality
