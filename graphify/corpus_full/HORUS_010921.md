# Constitutional model evaluation for predicting the mechanical behavior of a residual igneous soil in the south of the Aburrá Valley.

The dynamic growth of cities has generated a reduction of useful areas, making it necessary to optimize space, which implies the execution of works with special conditions such as large-scale excavations, often close to existing infrastructure prone to suffering damage. In these cases it is necessary to evaluate the performance effort-deformation of the soil. The numerical modeling has become the main resource of engineers to predict the behavior of the soil. Although the advanced constitutive models are able to reproduce aspects of the behavior of the soil such as its load history, anisotropy, among others, the state of the local practice is still developed under theories of limit equilibrium or very simple constituent models, whose application results in untrue predictions. Another important fault identified is the lack of studies focused on the mechanical behavior of residual soils, and the development and validation of constitutive models applied to these, which in most cases are focused on soils of sedimentary origin. In this work, the ability of three constitutive models to reproduce the mechanical behavior of a soil of residual ignea of soil of the southern valley of the Seburranian response is evaluated.
