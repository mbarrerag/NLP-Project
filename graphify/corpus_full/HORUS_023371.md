# Development of absorbents from fly ash through acidic and basic activation processes through conventional and intensified microwave heating.

The properties of some microparticles make them possible to be used as adsorbents of water pollutants. Its low cost and ease of application demonstrate that it has the potential to adsorb contaminants in water treatment. In this study, traditional techniques of synthesis of iron oxides and activation of fly ash (an industrial waste) were evaluated for adsorbing arsenic, asphaltene and phenol, with the aim of identifying the procedures that generate the best adsorbents. It was found that the best adsorbent of arsenic among the materials studied is iron oxide with 5 mL of NaOH synthesized in microwave, with a maximum adsorption of 35.46 mg of As/g of solid. For adsorption of asphaltenes, the solid of higher adsorption capacity is the fly ash activated with NaOH activated in microwave, the lowest capacity with 49.5 mg of asphaltene/g of solid.
