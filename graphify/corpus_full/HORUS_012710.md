# Microscale evaluation of air quality in kindergartens in Bogotá using low-cost sensors

Air pollution is one of the most important environmental concerns in recent times due to the impact on mortality, morbidity, and costs to the health system it represents. Cities invest significant resources in implementing and maintaining an infrastructure focused on diagnosing air quality conditions to formulate and evaluate public policies. However, microscale and neighborhood scale dynamics are not easily identifiable with these systems. The use of low-cost devices has had exponential growth for the indicative measurement of pollutants that allows responding to this micro-scale information gap. This research work evaluates the air quality in the microenvironments surrounding six public kindergartens in Bogotá using low-cost sensors for particulate matter, carbon dioxide, temperature, and relative humidity donated by the “Horizonte Ciudadano” Foundation from Chile, which promotes the “Aires Nuevos para la Primera Infancia” project in Latin America. Using the information from the city's official air quality monitoring network and the data supplied by the sensors allowed us to identify that the air quality in microenvironments is strongly affected by the dynamics of the neighborhoods, in addition to the influence of the synoptic behavior of the city. The difference in average concentrations between the sensors and the closest stations of the official network was between 29% and 70%. Besides, intramural measurements of concentrations of carbon dioxide were used as an indicator of the quality of ventilation in kindergartens. According to the measurements, the studied kindergartens have good ventilation, but this has the undesired effect of introducing pollutants from outside. Lastly, through visits to the surrounding areas and surveys among the collaborators of the gardens, some factors that affect the air quality on a microscale were identified
