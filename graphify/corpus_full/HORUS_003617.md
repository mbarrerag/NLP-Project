# SUSTAINABILITY STANDARDS IN BUILDINGS: IMPACT ON THE BUSINESS MODEL OF THE REAL ESTATE INDUSTRY

This article examines the different stages of a building ́s lifecycle, with a particular emphasis on its use and maintenance. The aim is to create a model based on International Management Systems Standards (SG) that will enable a systematic approach to sustainability during this critical phase. The current discussion highlights the importance of implementing effective policies to govern the use and maintenance of buildings. This research utilizes a qualitative study employing Grounded Theory methodology and surveys administered to technicians to gather insights from maintenance specialists. The objective is to formulate a strategy for implementing standards. A systematic search methodology is devised to investigate standard-writing bodies, their application within relevant agencies, and establish interrelations. An analysis was conducted on the implementation levels in organizations, resulting in a classification system with varying levels that aim to improve management practices. The findings highlight the importance of high levels of control and tacit knowledge in maintenance activities, which in turn require a greater time commitment to comply with existing regulations. According to the study, companies have an explicit knowledge level of 64.25%, which is higher than that of maintenance technicians. The study also found that sustainability and maintenance actions, including prevention, maintenance, and correction, stand at 52.35%. These results indicate that there is potential for improvement in the building lifecycle through the implementation of these measures. These findings suggest that structured approaches and improved knowledge dissemination could be beneficial in enhancing sustainability practices during the use and maintenance phases of buildings
