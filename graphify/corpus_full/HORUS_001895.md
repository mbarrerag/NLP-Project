# Morphometric and molecular characterization of Onchocercidae specimens present in the blood of amphibians, reptiles and wild birds of Colombian territory between 1999 and 2016

The microfilarias are blood stages of micro-motives parasites of the Onchocercidae family, which infect different groups of amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals. They are characterized by having a heteroxene cycle with arthropod vectors, having tropisms by various tissues of host and having a blood stage, microfilaria, which is used for diagnosis. Microfilaria infections in birds and herpets of Colombia have been characterized mainly up to the level of presence absence; so this work aimed to provide new morphometric, molecular and association knowledge of the Onchocercidae based on the microfilar stage. We analyzed by morphology the material deposited in the Biological Collection Parasite Relation Hospedero for 3820 birds with 138 microfilary infections (infection frequency 3.6%), 354 amphibians with 20 infections (5.6%) and 646 reptiles with an infection (0.6%). Based on qualitative morphological characteristics such as tail type, vaina and morphometry of ten standard characters were assigned 11 morphs and analyses. amphibians and wild reptiles in Colombia . Microfilariae are the blood stages of parasitic nematodes of the Onchocercidae family, which infected a range of amphiban, reptilian, avian, and mammalian species. They are characterized by a heteroxenous life cycle with arthropod vectors, tropes for various host tissue, and a blood stage microfilaria that is used for diagnosis. In Colombia, microfilariae infections in birds and reptiles has been characterized by the majority as absent or present. Therefore, this study aim to provide new knowledge regarding the morphology, molecular lines, and associations with the Onchocercidae using the microfilaria stage. microfilariae were identified, named Aproctella, Splendidofilaria (Splendophilarinae), Eufilaria (Lendaniinae), Ochoterenella (Waltoneliinae) and Oswaldofilaria (Oswaldofilariinae). The similarities of the morphotypes with those described for species of filariae from wild bird and herpets in America, as well as the coherence of the molecular analysis, are discussed. In the case of microfilariae infection in birds, it was found an association with the variables altitude, mass, and ninest type. Include, this study presents the first morphological and molecular classification of Onchocercida parasites infecting wild birds, amphibas, and reptiles in Colombia
