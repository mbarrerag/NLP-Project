# Social differentiation and living conditions in the Great Near Sanctuaries (2350-450 A.P.), a political and legal centre in the highland of Rio and Colombia

This article explores the archaeological manifestations of power and social differentiation in the muisque societies that inhabited the north of the Cundiboyacense Altiplano, center of Colombia, and that were described by the Spanish of the sixteenth century as deeply hierarchical and inequitable groups. Here we analyze the relationship between the social differentiation and the living conditions of the people buried in the Great Encircle of the Sanctuaryes, an important political and pre-Hispanic ritual center and is addressed through indicators of social differentiation. The latter are expressed in the ajuar (presence or absence of vessels, composition), in the artificial cranial modification, and by bone and dental indicators of physiological stress (porotic hyperostosis, cribra orbitalia, hypoplasia of the dental enamel, periostic reaction) and physical activity (enterial changes and degenerative joint disease). The results suggest that the population did not exhibit important differences in their living conditions. According to the indicators analyzed, if there was social differentiation, this was not accompanied by a distinction in the tasks specific to everyday life that led to a dominant group
