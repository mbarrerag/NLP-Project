# From planting to forest: recruitment of woody species in a restoration project in the Andes

The increasing deforestation of tropical forests makes it necessary to implement restoration actions capable of assisting in the recovery of biodiversity and functioning of these ecosystems. This research studies the recruitment of woody species in a project of restoration of high Andean forest with native species in Medellín (Colombia), in order to identify the environmental factors that most influence the abundance and diversity of the recruitment. 22 plots of 200 m2 were sampled, in which all individuals greater than 80 cm of height were measured and identified. The selected environmental factors were the edaphic characteristics, the structure of the plantations, slope, elevation, previous use of the soil and forest cover at landscape level. A generalised linear model was constructed for the density of the recruitment, and mixed linear models for the wealth, diversity and dominance of the species recruited.
