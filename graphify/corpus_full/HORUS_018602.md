# Assessment of the conditions of growth and production of hydrogen from Aphifella marina using industrial waste as a substrate by photofermentation.

In this work, the production of hydrogen from the Aphylella marine bacterium was evaluated using a synthetic medium with composition similar to a dark fermentation residue of wheat straw with a total acid concentration of 112 mM. This medium contained organic acids such as lactic acid and acetic acid which were used by the bacterium as the main source of carbon in a photofermentation process. To establish the optimal conditions of growth of the bacterium and hydrogen production, some variables significant for the process were studied in synthetic media: concentration and relation of organic acids, nitrogen source, luminic intensity, agitation, salt concentration and buffer solution in 120 mL bottles. The verification of the presence of hydrogen in the biogas produced was performed by means of a gas chromatographer of the 5890 series of Hewlett Packard (series A), with the best relationship of the thermal conductivity detector (TCD) and obtaining 95% of hydrogen (H2) in the processed samples, the remaining 5% was characterized as carbon dioxide (CO2). Initially, a single-leasing mixture design was used (series A), with the maximum relationship of the combined and maximum production of the combined effect of the combined with the total production of the total production of were: a total acid concentration of 76 mM, a glutamate concentration of 0.26 mM and a light intensity of 12,000 lux with a yield of 19.08% with respect to lactic acid and acetic acid. For the LGB buffer solution the conditions that presented the best biogas production were: a total acid concentration of 76 mM, a glutamate concentration of 8.25 mM and a light intensity of 12,000 lux with a yield of 44.13 % with respect to lactic acid and acetic acid. Finally, an escalation of the experiments with the two LGK and LGB buffer solutions in 1 L reactors was carried out.
