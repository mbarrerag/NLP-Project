# Quantification of the effect of climate on population isolation of Neotropical bird species (Spisriform Order)

This research focuses on identifying the determinants of the genetic divergence in the neotropical birds of the passuriform order. The main objective is to analyze how the geographical distance and the climatic dissimilarity influence the genetic differentiation of these populations. Contrary to the previous belief on the predominance of ecological factors, the findings underline the crucial relevance of the geographical isolation in the populations studied. Despite considering the geographical distance, the persistence of the environmental isolation is observed, indicating possible local adaptations, probably attributable to the divergent natural selection or reproductive barriers. The influence of the climatic resistance on the genetic differentiation is examined in detail, highlighting the cost of the movement between populations as a possible determinant of these genetic differences. The complex interaction between geographical and environmental factors highlights the need for a holistic approach in the understanding of these processes.
