# The pond and Narcissus. Exercises to disarm the anthropic axis of the gaze

In this doctoral thesis, a review of certain specific components of the representational system, forged during the Italian Renaissance through the works and thought of Filippo Brunelleschi and Leon Battista Alberti, then extended to all Europe and the Western world. Such analysis seeks to make manifest certain ideological purposes proper to Renaissance humanism, manifested in various products and cultural constructions conscious or unconscious heirs of it (among them, the majority of the art recognized by the Western canon of the last five centuries), which have been reproduced and perpetuated, reaching even contemporary artistic and visual practices, invisible, however, by custom, conventions and discursive automatisms that naturalize and universalize values that precisely are not universal, but, on the contrary, they are the basis and perpetuating various forms of discrimination, distinction, exclusion, privilege and disempowerment. Thus, following various strategies of study of the image, the present the dissertation reviews how certain mechanisms for the construction and perception of visual productions have been formed, the constructions of power and privilege that underlie in these practices and the way of art are present, and active in the Colombian art
