# RESISTANCE ASSESSMENT OF THE TEPHOS AND DELTAMETRIN IN FLORENCE, CAQUETA AND INTESTINAL MICROBIOTA A NEW STUDIO WHITE (92-O-EMVF)

The mosquito Aedes aegypti, the main vector of diseases such as dengue, zika, chikungunya and yellow fever, adapts very well to urban environments and represents a great threat to public health worldwide, being the use of insecticides one of the most used strategies in vector control. In the department of Caquetá, resistance of Ae. aegypti to the larvicide Temefos and to the adulticide Deltamethrin has been reported. Previous research suggests that the intestinal microbiota plays a decisive role in this resistance to insecticides. This study aimed to describe the state of resistance of Ae. Aegypti to Temefos and Deltamethrin, as well as characterizing the bacterial isolates from the intestine of larvae and resistant females in a wild population of Florence, Caqueta, exposed to bioassanos of susceptibility in controlled laboratory conditions.
