# Optical design of perovskite solar cells

Scant attention has been paid to the investigation of optical losses that affect the performance of perovskite solar cells. Therefore, in this chapter, an optical matrix calculation methodology for planar thin perovskite solar cells with both normal and inverted structures is explained. The method is based on the Fresnel equations (and coefficients), which are explained first, so that a general (optical) matrix equation can be established for the multilayer systems. Subsequently, this method is used to determine the spectral reflectance (and transmittance) from planar perovskite cells. Reported refractive index and extinction coefficients for each of the materials that constitute these devices are used for this purpose. The results show that for typical perovskite thickness above 500nm, the total average reflectance in the solar spectrum can be more than 10%. It is also shown that a conventional n-i-p planar structure with titanium dioxide as the electron selective layer has a much better optical performance than an inverted p-i-n structure with PEDOT:PSS, as the hole selective layer, due to the absorption at this layer. In the latter case, an optimum PEDOT:PSS layer thickness should be around 200nm
