# Mechanicalbiological approach for skeleton muscle adaptation

The skeletal muscle is the most adaptable tissue of the human body. Its adaptation includes changes in the form and size, changes in the function and distribution of the organelles within the muscle cells and changes at the molecular scale. The macroscopic characteristics, such as size and strength, are related to the cellular scale through the content of proteins in the myofibrillas, which are the serial arrangement of the units that generate strength (sarcomeros); at the scale of the organelles, adaptation occurs in the content of the mitochondrials, as well as in the function of the reticule sarcoplasmic. The microscopic characteristics at the molecular scale are related to the type of molecules of proteins that contain the myofibrillas. The type of molecules includes a variety of heavy and light chains of myosin.
