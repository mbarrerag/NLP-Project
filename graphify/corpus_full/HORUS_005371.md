# Nutraceutical potential of fruits of wild species of blackberry and raspberry

Summary The frutils such as zarzammar, rambise, cran and strawberries are rich in phenolic compounds, secondary metabolites biosynthetised as a result of the evolution of terrestrial plants to meet environmental challenges; in particular, wild species tend to have characteristics that allow them to adapt to adverse environments. The aim of this study was to assess the nutruustic potential of wild genotypes of the genus Rubus by determining their antioxidant properties for inclusion in genetic improvement programmes. The content of phenols, flavonoids and anthoctoctoctoctivins total, as well as the antioxidant capacity, by the ABST and FRAP tests, were quantified in the polar fruit extracts corresponding to six zars: Rubus glaucus, the Tupy variety, a commercial and three wild (Hidal 2.3, Hidalgo 2.4 and Chiapas 1) and a rambous (Rubus niveus).
