# RELATIONS BETWEEN WAR, MENTAL HEALTH AND DISABILITY: BETWEEN CROSSED FORCES RELATIONSHIPS BETWEEN WAR, MENTAL HEALTH, AND DISABILITY: CAUGHT IN THE CROSSFIR

This article contrasts from the published academic literature on the topic two thesis contrasted around the possible interactions between mental health and psychosocial/mental disability in war contexts. It follows from the theoretical explanations of both theses in relation to their historical developments, from the ancient period to the present. From this contrast, the implications of this relationship in public health are analyzed, the effects of these conceptual categories and their treatment, recognizing a focus on psychosocial disability as a consequence of war. It is concluded that historical correlations of intrapsychic mental affectation due to war are not exhausted at present, coexist and conflict with the accounts related to the mental affectation caused by war in relational matters. The interaction between war, mental health and disability is between crossfires, this does not undermine individual and community dignity, but rather provides opportunities to make visible and empower historically marginalized people
