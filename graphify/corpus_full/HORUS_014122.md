# Study of the potential of chemical constituents from Piper Cumanense Kunth to regulate Quorum Sensing (QS), biofilm formation and the production of virulence factors in Pseudomonas. aeruginosa

Pseudomonas aeruginosa is a microorganism associated with resistance to antimicrobials (RAM), with characteristics of pathogen opportunistic, omnipresent and highly resistant to environmental conditions.This microorganism is responsible for infections of nosocomial type, which due to its characteristics can develop infection in the first 72 hours of admission to hospital units or intensive care units (UCIs), triggering diseases such as bacteremia, respiratory tract infections and urinary tract infections, affecting in most cases the efficiency of the health system, increase of hospital expenditure for long stays in the patient's ICUs, and finally the death thereof. Various researches have established that one of the main mechanisms related to the development of resistance in P. aeruginosa is the quarum sensing (QS), a mechanism of cellular communication that controls the factors of virulence and production of biofilms. This is what favours the survival of bacteria in the host. The importance of controlling the QS is to control the associated virulence factors and to reduce the evolutionary pressure that are generated by the new. The first stage of this research includes the phytochemical study directed at P. Cumanense to isolate and identify chemical constituents with inhibitory potential for QS. In the second stage, the effect of bioactive molecules on the formation of biofilm, production of virulence factors and/or concentration of autoinductive molecules in P. aeruginosa will be determined. In the last stage, potential improved QS inhibitors on P. aeruginosa will be designed and synthesized from chemoinformatics methods on chemical constituents from P. Cumanense
