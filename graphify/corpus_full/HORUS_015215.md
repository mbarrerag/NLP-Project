# ASSESSMENT OF THE TRIPANOCID ACTIVITY OF POMOLIC ACID AND INDICIAL AFFINITY BY SINTASS CYSTEIN, STEROL-14-A-DEMETYLase AND REDUCT TRIPANOSIS TRIPANOSOMIC CRuzI

Chagas disease, caused by Trypanosoma cruzi, is endemic to Central America and South America. The toxicity and limited effectiveness of conventional treatment in chronic phase make it necessary to develop new medicines. Pomolic acid has shown antiparasitic activity. However, its activity on T. cruzi, the mechanisms of action and the targets on which it could act are unknown. The prediction and validation of the tertiary structure of enzymes were made in the Master software and the Swiss-Model server. The molecular coupling calculations were performed in AutoDock Vina and, as control, the cocrystalized inhibitor of each enzyme was used. The enzyme-ligating interactions were characterized in the PLP platform. The tripanocide activity on the stages tripomastigote and mastigote of T. cruzi, and its cytotoxic activity on VERO cells. Pomolic acid presented greater affinity for the three enzymes than the controls, and the active acid was coupled to the site and recognised by the cofactors of the enzymes CS and CYP51
