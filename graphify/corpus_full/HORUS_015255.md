# ZONES OF SIMILITUD AND TERM DIFFERENCE IN THE URBAN AREA OF VALLE DE ABURRÁ, COLOMBIA

The climatic diversity of the Andean tropics in Colombia is remarkable. It is expressed in its biological biodiversity, but also in a thermal diversity that the local architecture has just begun to address. For example, the national energy efficiency standard still does not recognize the microclimate scale and classifies the country in just four climates, inconvenient simplification to make better design decisions, construction and occupation of architectural spaces. In the Valle de Aburrá, which is located between 1,300 and 2,800 m above sea level, it could be expected that its buildings will respond to the thermal variability that characterizes the cities and neighborhoods that make it, but this does not happen. Developing methodologies to study this microclimate variability is an outstanding task. This research describes the thermal variability of the Valle de Aburrá in a cartographic support that groups urban areas due to thermal similarity, taking advantage of an extensive environmental monitoring network and historical meteorological records to consider the variations derived from the phenomenon of children/children. The result is maps that reveal thermal trends by city sectors, which describe the displacement of these strips of thermal similarity throughout the year and which provide evidence to avoid using the temperatures of the valley as the only representative temperature
