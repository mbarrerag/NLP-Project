# SILENT VALANCH: HOUSE DEFITATION AND DESVASTING PROBLEMS IN MOCOA (2017-2019)

The indebtedness of Colombian households has grown in recent years: it has gone from representing 9.6% of GDP in 2000 to 27% in 2018, and since 2020 it remains above 31%. The debts, moreover, are increasingly destined to consumption and this even before the Covid-19 pandemic: the consumption credits have gone from 31% to more than 67% of the credit portfolio in the period 2000-2018. With the increasing exposure of banks to credit portfolios to households, the concern of institutions like the Bank of the Republic and the Financial Superintendence has grown in view of the perspective of a crisis linked to the repudiation of credits and bank failures, as the happened in 1999. It is devoted much less attention, however, to the effects -positive and also negative - of the indebtedness not only in the stability of the financial system but in the households. The universe of affects to take into account not only scenarios of repudium of credits, but the effects -and problems that arise from continuing to pay them, and that are not limited to the transfer of assets.
