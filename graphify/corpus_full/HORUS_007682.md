# Evaluation of the capacity of biogenic limestone in the activation of clays for the production of LC3 cement.

In the production of Portland cement there are two factors that generate a carbon footprint of great magnitude, the first is the energy necessary to calcin the limestone at 1400°C and the second is a chemical process called descarbonation in which the calcium carbonate (CaCO3) is broken down giving origin to the calcium oxide (CaO) and releasing carbon dioxide (CO2). The construction industry over the last decades has sought to replace the Clinker by means of supplementary cementing materials, reason why the proposal of cements LC3 _x0096_ Limestone Calcised Clay Cement, which is composed of 30% of calcined clay, 15% of lime, 5% of plaster and 50% of Clinker. The use of limestone generates a decrease of emissions since this is not calcined and allows to activate the cementing properties of the calculated clay, using the high content of calcium carbonate. Calcinated clay also represents a decrease of emissions since this is calcinated between 700 and 800°C, therefore, does not have a process of carbonation and uses less energy in the process, and generates the calcose. potential benefits from the use of biogenic limestone include: reducing carbon emissions, using organic waste to generate high-value products, reducing polluting waste generated from exploitation and production of limestone that affects air quality and human health
