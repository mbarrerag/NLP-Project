# Synthesis and characterization of thin films of double perovskit Rb3Bi2I9 for photovoltaic applications

Currently, new PV materials based on organic-inorganic hybrid compounds are researched intensively. They are highly functional and highly functional. They are based on the functional structure of the product.The main objective of this project was to identify the optimal conditions for the growth of thin films of the compound Rb3Bi2I9 with a perovskit structure, free of secondary phases.It is a completely inorganic material, proposed as an active layer in solar cells with high stability.It is important to note that this compound is free of lead (Pb), which represents a significant advantage, since the Pb is toxic.The perovskits that have shown the greatest efficiencies in photovoltaic devices usually contain Pb in their composition, being the most representative compound of the CH3NH3PbI3 (MAPbI3), commonly used as an active layer in this type of cells.In the framework of this work, contributions were made related to: Optimization of the optical, structural and morphological properties of the films. . New photovoltaic materials based on organic-inorganic hybrid compounds that cryptalize in a perovskite-like structure are currently under intensive research due to their exceptional optical and electrical transport goods, including an optimal energy gap, high photo absorb absorption capacity, and long wheel wheel diffusion longer. The main objective of this project was to identify the optimal conditions for the growth of thin films of the Rb3Bi2I9 compound with a perovskite-like structure, free of dry experimental characterisation using techniques such as X-ray diffration (XRD), Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) and Spectral Transmission. In particular, it was found that through an an ananealing process, carried out in a high-pressure nitrogen atmosphere, it is possible to significantly improve the morphology and crystalline quality of Rb3Bi2I9, thin films, as well as a remarkable reduction in the density of intrinsic and structural defects.
