# RELATING LABOUR STABILITY AND COVID-19 IN COLOMBIA

The stability of labour in Colombia has been a constitutional right established since 1991, and a wide-ranging case law analysis has been developed in line with the international treaties and the International Labour Organization (ILO) in Colombia, which has made it possible to ensure continuity of work for many people in a vulnerable condition. Currently, the extent and severity of the pandemic of the COVID-19 has put both employers and workers in a difficult position. The first concerns the need to suspend and terminate some employment contracts; in the case of the second, mainly due to serious contagion, compulsory isolation due to the situation of health worldwide and with direct national involvement, as well as the consequent discovery or diagnosis of diseases as a result of the contagion. This prevented the normal continuity of working relationships, presenting situations of legal uncertainty at a time of economic crisis, with the result that companies were in need of adjustments or changes in organizational matters to face the crisis to face the unforeseen situation by employers, employers or governments. However, it cannot be ignored that this situation has triggered a series of administrative and legal complaints on the part of the actors involved
