# From borders, livelihoods and life. An anthropological look at the productive and ecological transformations of peasants-colonos of Guaviare

This research work takes place among peasants-colonos of the department of Guaviare (Colombia), in the ecosystem of Amazonia-Orinoquía. During the first generation of peasant colonization, the conflict and economic limitations perpetuated the displacement of jungle settlers within. The trend in recent decades has been marked by coca crops, monocultures and extensive livestock farming, by the hand of land grabbing, causing the destruction of the ecosystems of life through deforestation. Currently, this second and third generation of peasants-colonos continues to face difficulties to support the local economy and the social reproduction. Faced with this, some inhabitants of the so-called agricultural frontier are carrying out environmentally sustainable productive practices that contrast with these trends of traditional cut, and that account for transformations in socio-economic practices and ecological relations. Under eco-social restrictions and immersed in the paradigm of development and unlimited growth, these social actors account for their agency, focus of this work that examines their perspectives, experiences and feelings. to sustain local economy and social reproduction. Considering these circumstances, some of these dilemmas of the border are currently performing more ecologically sustainable practices which differ from the traditional ones, then giving evidence of socioeconomic and ecological transformations. Constructed under tight ecological relationships and the paradigm of development and limited growth, these social actors provide their agency, on which this research approaches by analytical their perspectives, experiences and feelings.
