# PHYSIOLOGICAL COMPORTMENT OF THE CURUBA USED LONGITUDITAL ANALYSIS BASED ON DISTANCE

A new methodology for longitudinal data analysis based on distances between observations associated with explanatory variables used to adjust continuous response variables is proposed. The maximum generalized verisimilitude method is used to estimate the parameters of the model and to perform the hypothesis and inference tests in large samples. In addition, the distance-based method was compared with the classical multivariate variance analysis using the AR(1) and composite symmetric correlation structures. For this purpose, the AIC and BIC criteria were used. In small samples, the adjusted proposed model shows a gain from the classic model. The results are similar for both methods for large samples, regardless of variance, the number of time measurements, correlation and the autocorrelation structure. Finally, an experiment was conducted to characterize the physiological behavior of curuba plants under two environmental conditions where chlorophyll fluorescence was measured. In addition, the temperature in the culture was monitored under different phenological states and it is found that the proposal presents a better adjustment by adding at least one main co-ordinate additional to the number of explanatory variables, obtaining significant predictions
