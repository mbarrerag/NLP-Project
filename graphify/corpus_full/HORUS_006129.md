# Development of carbonous materials obtained by thermochemical transformation of industrial waste for freshwater production from air dehumidification

Drinking water is an indispensable resource for humanity. However, in areas of high water stress it is difficult to find it in conditions suitable for consumption and use. Therefore, the main objective of this study is the production of fresh water from the dehumidification of the air using activated carbons obtained from industrial residues to cope with this situation. activated carbons were sintered from the thermochemical transformation of polyethylene terephthalate residues (PET) and their performance was compared with commercial activated carbons synthesized from agroindustrial residues of coffee and coconut.In addition, carbons were modified superficially with nitric acid to improve their affinity for polar compounds. The materials were characterized deeply through electron microscopy of sweeping (SEM), transverse spectroscopy of photoemission of rays (XPS), Raman spectroscopy and adsorption of nitrogen. The results showed materials with surface area of up to 1313 m2 Toldg-1 for those without surface acidification. The field test, the water sorption/capture was performed during the night, while the release and production of water occurred during the day with exclusive use of solar energy. The average relative humidity during the adsorption stage was 80%, while the wind speed was 2.3 m=1 and the temperature was 20.7 °C. During the desorption stage the average temperature was 26.2 °C. The productivity of the water was equal to 0.9 g of water condensed by g of dry material. These results indicate that the synthesized materials can produce water efficiently in challenging areas within a circular economy, contributing to sustainability and social well-being in areas with high water shortages. .
