# Black Upward Mobility, Neoliberal Multiculturalism and Social Whitening in Colombia

In the new millennium, neoliberal multiculturalism took on the function of governing difference that the discourse of mestizaje had fulfilled in the 19th and 20th centuries in Colombia. Although the state has given greater recognition to the multiculturality and multiethnicity of the country's social formation, this reorientation has not reduced the effects of neoliberal policies or the impacts of the armed conflict that disproportionately affect racialized populations. Using a biographical method and based on interviews on the emergence of the "Black" middle classes in Colombia conducted with Afro-descendant men and women, this chapter examines the relationship between upward mobility and social whitening, arguing that in the current context of multiculturalist policies, there is no contradiction between upward mobility and embracing a Black identity. It also explores forms of resistance to white cultural domination and micro-agencies that seek to reverse the long-lasting structural trends that have constituted the Colombian socio-racial order, concluding that the balance of the achievements of the age of neoliberal multiculturalism against racism is ambivalent
