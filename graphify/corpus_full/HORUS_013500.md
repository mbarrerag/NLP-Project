# Translinguism and (self)translation in the work of Jhumpa Lahiri: the search for a third space

This research explores Jhumpa Lahiri's work and proposes the creation of a third space from which one can understand his literary project beyond the labels “writer of the Indian diaspora” or “universal writer” and where it is possible to see the connections between his writing in English and Italian. The proposal of a third space is an extension of Karen Cardozo's approach in “Mediating the Particular and the General: Ethnicity and Intertextuality in Jhumpa Lahiri’s Oeuvre” (2012). The work consists of three moments: first, the identification of Lahiri's interest in interpretation, translinguism and intertextuality in his work written in English (seeds and preparation of the floor); second, the analysis of Dove mi appvo, his first novel written in Italian, in order to identify how his narrative is transformed after his transling process (the opening of the third space) and finally, the analysis of how the translation and self-translation make Lahiri a hybrid (the consolidation of the third space). This reflection is based on the study of the translation and the translation of the first, the identification of Lahiri's interest in interpretation, translingualism and intertextuality in her work written in English (sees and soil preparation); second, the analysis of Dove mi trovo, her first novel written in Italian, in order to identify how her narrative is transformed after the traditional process (the opening of the third space) and finally, the analysis of how translation and self-translation turn Lahiri into a hybrid individual (the consolidation of the third space). This reflection is part of the studies on literacy translation proposed by Steven G. Kellman, and it is a step towards denaturalizing the monolingual paradigm in which a language/homeland is the only source of a identity
