# Methodology for the identification of mass graves from multispectral images

In Colombia, the identification of mass graves has been carried out manually in most cases, i.e. to find burial sites is used as input the information given by the victims or by the accounts of the relatives of the victims; these methods, besides vague, are extremely slow and expensive. To increase the chances of success, search methods based on geophysical resistivity, magnetometry or radar of penetration of the soil have been addressed; these methods can only be used in a area delimited often small and that in many cases can be of difficult access or can be found in areas in conflict. Thus, it has been seen the need to implement methods that do not involve direct access to the ground and that can cover major areas, to speed up the search, reducing the cost in time and in money. In this work it is suggested that the common pits can be identified by means of NDVI, GNDVI and GCI septal indexes with the content of organic matter buried in the common graves, understanding how it affects the organic matter of the common graves and the health of the plants that are found in the experimental area. ting radar, have been explored in an attempt to increase the chances of success. However, the tested methods have drewbacks, notably the necessity to cover a restricted range of areas to yield results which would render these techniques impracticable, especially in conflict zones. Hence, there is a need to deploy new methods that circumvent the need for direct access to the field and enabler branch coverage, with the aim of facilitating the search processes, which will save time and reduce the financial investment required.
