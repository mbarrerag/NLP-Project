# IN VITRO ANTIBIOFILM AND IMMUNE-MODULATORY ACTIVITY OF CANNABIDIOL AND CANNABIGEROL

to evaluate the in vitro antibiofilm and immune-modulatory activity of cannabidiol and cannabigerol. cytotoxicity of CBD and CBG on periodontal ligament-derived fibroblasts (PLF) was measured by flow cytometry with propidium iodide and their cytokines production (IL-4, IL-2, CXCL10, IL-1ß, TNF-a, CCL2, IL-17 A, IL-6, IL-10, IFN-¿, IL-12p70, CXCL8, and TGF- ß) was quantified by immunoassay using a LEGENDplex kit (BioLegend, USA). The antimicrobial effect of CBD and CBG at different concentrations was tested on microorganisms in a planktonic state (S. mutans and C. albicans) and on multispecies biofilms, on which the metabolic activity of the bacteria was also measured. for CBD in concentrations from 0.5 μM - 20 μM, cell viability at 3, 6, and 12 hours was > 95%, while for CBG was > 88%. MIC for S. mutans was 20 μM for CBD, and for CBG, it was 10 μM. For C. albicans, no inhibitory effect was observed. The ability of bacteria to form multispecies biofilms was inhibited by CBD and CBG. The metabolic activity of bacteria in biofilms was reduced by 50% with CBD at 125 μg/mL and 60% with CBG at 62 μg/mL. The production of the anti-inflammatory mediators TGF-ß and IL-4 increased significantly at 6 and 12 hours with the addition of 10 μM CBG. The greatest IFN-¿ production by PLF occurred with activation with CBD at 10 μM to 20 μM. CBD and CBG at the measured concentrations do not compromise the cellular viability of PLF and could inhibit the formation of multispecies biofilms as well as to reduce metabolic activity in pre-established biofilms. CBG could have an anti-inflammatory effect by increasing the production of TGF-b and IL-4. Both derivatives of cannabis constitute safe prospects for producing oral health and oral regeneration bioproducts
