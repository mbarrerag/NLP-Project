# Local-scale changes in topography influence tree growth and mortality in a terra firma forest in the Northwest Amazon

It is believed that spatial variation in diversity and distribution of tree species is influenced by environmental variation, including topography, but underlying processes are not well understood. Wet habitats, such as valleys, should bear higher growth and survival than drier habitats, such as hills. However, deviations from this pattern may occur due to species habitat associations, which should be aligned with the ecological strategy of species along the inter-specific purchasing-conservative spectrum: rapid growth at the expense of lower survival and higher survival at the cost of slower growth. Here, we assess the influence of topography on the growth and mortality of trees between topographic habitats (valves, slopes and hills) in the Spanamic Forest Park of 25 ha, in the north-west of the Amazon.
