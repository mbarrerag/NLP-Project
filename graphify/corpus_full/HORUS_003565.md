# Military expenditure and economic growth for Latin America, 2000-2019

This document examines the relationship of military spending and economic growth for 16 Latin American countries, during the period 2000-2019, in the framework of the Solow model expanded with human capital. With the methodology of Generalized Method of System Moments (MGM-SIS), a dynamic panel was estimated for the annual per capita growth rate. Moreover, a fixed effect was estimated for the per capita GDP of stable state. Two common results were observed: (i) the estimated coefficient of military expenditure is negative and significant in the presence of internal armed conflict, and (ii) when controlling by the level of income, military expenditure presents a positive net effect in the countries of average-low income. It is highlighted that the estimates of military expenditure do not have a well defined magnitude, nor do they give decisive evidence that, in general, military expenditure is a relevant variable in the economic growth of Latin America. . evidence that, in general, military hanging is a relevant variable in Latin America’s economic growth
