# Territorialities of democracy in the Anti-Oqueño Urabá: between peasant resistance and the solidarity fabric of women

In the anti-Oqueño Urabá, social actors have configured, in connection with the war, organizational practices and collective action that seek to claim rights, survive the armed conflict and maintain in a dignified way in their territory through civilist positions. The purpose of this research was to trace little visible or unknown aspects that arise precisely within these practices, under the assumption that these processes territorialize democracy in its framework and representative sense, generating social and cultural relations and grammars from civil resistance, solidarity and public construction. Methodologically it is a qualitative type of research, with a focus placed on case studies, analyzing two experiences that arose between 1991 and 2006: the Community of Peace of San José de Apartadó and organizational processes of Women of the Urbá region. Within the framework of the social theory were founded democracy, collective actions, territory and internal armed conflict. The primary information was generated through semi-structured interviews and discussion groups with social and political actors of the region; press tracing on the context, the cases addressed and the categories of research; and observation not involved in events where the central issues were addressed. civil resistance.
