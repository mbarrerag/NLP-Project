# GENERAL MEASURES FOR INFECTION CONTROL OF TUBBERCLESIS

One of the most important objectives for the control of tuberculosis infection is to prevent the transmission of the disease in workers who carry out care activities in health services [1]. It is also undeniable that the current pandemic has had a negative impact on the achievements that had been achieved for tuberculosis control and in this regard the implementation of infection control measures to protect health workers in their care process is an unposterable priority in their work environments. Nosocomial infection by Mycobacterium tuberculosis is a reality in high-burden, middle- and low-income countries, because the number of cases of tuberculosis in health workers is twice the incidence rate compared to the general population, due to the high risk of transmission of the disease in these scenarios [2]. In this sense, the rules or guidelines for the control of tuberculosis infections is an imperative for the current epidemiological context. The initial rules for the control of tuberculosis in health institutions in the Americas emerged since 1982, 1990 (in the form of declarations) and in 1994 guidelines for the control of tuberculosis infections were already developed by the Centre for the control and prevention of diseases (cccc) [3] (more than 39 years ago in the context of tuberculosis in the United States)
