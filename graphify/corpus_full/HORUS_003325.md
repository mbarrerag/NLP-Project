# FORRAJEO, CONSTANCIA FLORAL AND LEARNING IN POLINIZERS

The forage behavior corresponds to the set of processes with which an organism acquires nutrients and energy of the system in which it inhabits. Such definition should include technically the process with which plants feed: photosynthesis and absorption of nutrients.However, the term has traditionally been used to refer only to the animal behavior in which there is an active search for resources, which involves the detection of resources, and the obtaining and consumption of food. Considering that all organisms of the animal kingdom constantly require food, it is possible to easily imagine the variations of strategy, which depend on whether for example the food is consumed directly or immediately, or transported elsewhere, either to supply a nest, feed the breeding, or store it for times of scarcity. In this context, the behavior of forage affects directly and immediately the survival of the individual, its pollinators, and, in the medium and long term, the reproductive rate of the populations of plants with flowers and pollinators: therefore, such animal behavior media the processes of interaction, adaptation and coevolution given between plants with flowers and their pollinators, and, in the medium and long term, the mode of production of the population. In addition, flora or floral fidelity is a behavior exhibited by various types of pollinators; especially by bees. Such conduct consists in that the individual restricts visits all, or most of them, to a single type of flower, for each forage path. Although angiosperms clearly obtain a benefit from that type of behavior, that is less obvious for pollinators. Although numerous hypotheses have been raised about this, to date, there is no explanation of this conduct of the pollinator (see the review of the theme in Amaya-Márquez, 2009).
