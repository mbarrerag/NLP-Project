# THE THREE SCHEMES OF THE OPIO IN ASIA IN THE XIXth

In East Asia there is an old tradition of rejection of opium, for religious and political reasons, and which was translated into prohibitions, which, according to some sources, date back to the 14th century, but can only be dated with certainty from the 18th century. Since the end of that last century, Dutch and British have forced the opening of markets for opium in South East Asia and China. The only country that remained on the sidelines was Japan. Based on these experiences, it is proposed to identify three different opium regimes in the region: full legalization in South East Asia, partial legalization in China and prohibition in Japan. The article ends with some suggestions about the influence of these three models on the global prohibition of drugs in the 20th century
