# Quantitative analysis of freight trip attraction due to the agriculture sector in urban areas of developing countries.

This research focuses on finding the most influential variables in the attraction of cargo travel from commercial establishments selling food and located in urban areas of developing countries, where the food retail market is commonly dominated by small family shops located in the neighborhoods, rather than supermarkets. This analysis was carried out with data from the Metropolitan Area of Medellín (Colombia) obtained from the two cargo surveys carried out in the city (2012 and 2018). The data were used to estimate models using the technique of minimum ordinary squares and logarithmic transformation of the data to obtain a better fit. The total area of the establishment and the equivalent number of full-time employees were the most influential variables. Practitioners will benefit from these models because they will be able to include the generation of cargo travel in residential neighborhoods and commercial areas to better plan transportation in the city
