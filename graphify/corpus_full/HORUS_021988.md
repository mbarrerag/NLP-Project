# Influence of the nature of modified starch in its performance as an emulsifier of capryl/capric triglyceride systems – water.

The search for new emulsification alternatives is a relevant topic in pharmaceutical research given the safety restrictions for some materials and the interest in taking advantage of the natural resources available in countries such as Colombia. In this regard, this research studies the performance as emulsifiers of five Cerile starches modified with succinic octane anhydride (Hi-Cap® 100, Purity Gum® 2000, Capsul®, N-Cremer® 46 and Purity Gum® Ultra), using capryl/capric and water-based triglycerides as oleous and aqueous phases, respectively. The methodological development includes the morphological, physiochemical, structural and reological characterization of the starches, the preparation of emulsions at different modified starch concentrations and their characterization, the conduct of stability studies and the microstructure study (0,0 % – 97,60 %), the degree of substitution (DS: 0.017, 0,03), the degree of branching (DB: 2.27 – 2.93 %), the average length of the linear chains (H alfa - (1– 1– 10– 10,7 – 0,7 %), the mean level of the microstructure (+, the Mw, Rz and dispersed molecular density. The study of the microstructure of emulsions shows the formation of a three-dimensional network, suggesting that the main stabilizing mechanism using these biopolymers is the seroic effect, which was corroborated by the evaluation of the zeta potential and the electrolyte-induced aggregation test. Together, these results provide fundamental information about the characterization of hydrophobic starches, useful for the development of novel emulsified systems in the pharmaceutical and cosmetic fields
