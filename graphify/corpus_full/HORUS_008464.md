# Early social exposure, learning and development of sexual behavior of Japanese quail (coturnix japonica)

The present study evaluated how early social exposure influences the development of sexual behavior and learning a task of sexual conditioning. Ninety-seven male quads of 20, 30 and 40 days post-closion (DPE) were assigned to different exposure groups (physical, visual and non-exposure) with the aim of assessing the influence of early experiences in the development of sexual behavior. The results showed that a high degree of exposure to females caused a delay in the development of sexual behavior in younger subjects (20 EPE). On the contrary, subjects of 30 and 40 days showed an improvement in the performance of sexual behavior under the same conditions, compared to males who had less exposure. Subsequently, all subjects went through a learning procedure in which they presented a conditioned stimulus followed by copulatory access to females. The results showed that regardless of age, subjects were able to perform sexual behaviors against undisposed stimulation. However, only the group of 40 days showed clear evidence of learning, as they had implemented sexual behavior responses against the stimulated sex in a consistent way.
