# TRPV1 ANTAGONISM IN HUMAN ODONTOBLAST LIKE CELLS: A POSSIBLE DRUG TARGET FOR DENTAL PAIN TREATMENT

To establish the modulation of transient potential receptors (TRPV-1) in human odontoblast-like cells (OLC) using a selective antagonist, as a possible therapeutic target for dental pain management. After ethical evaluation, OLC were differentiated from dental pulp mesenchymal cells using conditioned and TGF-ß1 supplemented medium. The cellular phenotype was confirmed by the expression of DSPP and DMP-1 by real time PCR and immunocytochemistry. The cytotoxic concentration (CC50) of the antagonist capsazepine (CZP) was determined by the resazurin test. Activation of TRPV-1 was determined by evaluating changes in calcium concentration after stimulation with the agonist capsaicin (CAP), using the fluorescent calcium probe Fluo-4-AM. The cells were stimulated pharmacologically with CZP 40 μM, CAP 200 μM or co-stimulated with CZP+CAP for 15 minutes and the changes in fluorescence were evaluated at 1, 10, 20 and 30 minutes in a microplate fluorimeter and by flow cytometry (FC) using the 530/30 (FITC) filter. HEK cells were used as a positive control. The OLC phenotype was confirmed by the detection of transcripts and proteins for DSPP and DMP-1. The CC50 for CZP was 45.3 μM at 24 hours post-treatment. Regarding calcium influx, CAP-stimulated OLCs showed a [Ca2+]i ratio of 2.7±0.11 that was maintained through the time and 0.5±0.03 in the CZP/CAP cells treated. The CAP-treated OLC presented a F/F0 ratio 3.66 in the flow cytometer and 1.1 for the CZP/CAP co-stimulated cells. The OLC responded to CAP by increasing calcium influx and the antagonism with CZP significantly decreased the response, modulating the TRPV-1 activity, demonstrating its participation in the odontoblast-nerve fiber feedback. The TRPV-1 antagonism mediated by CZP could be a pharmacological target for the dental pain management
