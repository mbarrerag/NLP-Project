# A MODIFICATION OF BIC IN LINEAL MODELS

The choice of the best model is a fundamental task in the modelling of statistical data. Parsimony is one of the principles that should guide this choice. Despite its wide use in the selection of models, the basics of the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) are generally little known. AIC, AICc and BIC criminalize the likelihood function to select the simplest model. These criteria are based on information and entropy concepts. In statistics, the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) or the Schwarz information criterion (also SIC, SBC, SBIC) is a criterion for the selection of models from a finite set of models; models with a lower BIC are preferred. By adjusting models, it is possible to increase verisimilitude by adding parameters, but doing so can lead to an overadjustment. Both BIC and AIC try to solve this problem by introducing a penalty term for the number of parameters in the model; the term of penalty is greater in the BIC than in the AIC
