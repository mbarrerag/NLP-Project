# Study of the transesterification of used cooking oil using butanol and isoamylic alcohol

The following are the most relevant aspects related to the reactive system, which must be taken into account for the implementation of the project.The overall reaction is the result of three consecutive reactions and each leads to a state of chemical equilibrium, so that excess alcohol must be added, and, if high conversion and performance are required, at least two stages of reaction.The reaction system is heterogeneous due to the thermodynamic incompatibility of reactants and products.This decrease as the molecular weight of alcohol increases.In any case, the inmiscibility of the reaction system affects the speed of the reaction due to the contact between the raw materials being made difficult.The most used catalysts are the high content of free fatty acids (AGCUL) and water; this is the case of the sodium and potassium methoxides used by the oil. The results of this characterization will define the treatment process to make it a useful raw material for transesterification. The properties with which this process will be controlled are the acid value, water content, iodine index and saponification index. For the transesterification stage, the effect of the temperature variables (2 levels), molar ratio (3 levels) and catalyst percentage (2 levels) will be analyzed for a total of 12 experimental tests per alcohol. The response variables will be the conversion of ACU and yield to alkyl esters, which will be calculated from the concentrations determined by gas chromatography. This method is standardized for the determination of methyl esters, but will be adapted, taking into account that the times of eluciation of the alquil esters will be greater. Since reaction products will not be used as fuels, conversion and performance need not have the values required for this application and, in principle, it will require a single reaction stage
