# SELF-COMPATION IN DISEASE PROFESSIONS IN BOGOTÁ, COLOMBIA

Self-compassion is understood as a state of personal well-being in which the suffering itself is recognized and managed, previous studies report that it positively impacts the mental health of nursing professionals and contributes to their ability to provide compassionate care, which ultimately improves the perception of people with respect to the quality of care.1 The model of cultural and compassionate competence of Papadopoulos mentions self-compassion as part of cultural and compassionate consciousness, the basis for the development of this competence.2 Self-compassion is a phenomenon of relatively recent approach, and in Colombia there are few studies on this subject.The aim of the study is to describe the level of self-compassion in nursing professionals in Bogotá. Cross-sectional descriptive study. The sample corresponds to 93 nursing professionals from three university hospitals in Bogotá, who participated voluntarily, after explaining the study and signing of informed consent (5.4%) With support from the platform RedCap, the results of socio-demographic, professional, as well as the Self-Compasion Scale (SCS) were analyzed in Spanish version (a. 0.77 and coefficient of variable-retrest of 0.92).3. These results are similar to those of other studies, which reflect moderate levels of self-pity in nurses and other professionals, who also associated with Burnout.4 An investigation with students of specialization in community nursing showed low levels of self-pity, although with a high level of compassion and confirms the association between self-pity and Burnout.5 Self-pity can protect the health and personal well-being of professionals. It is necessary to continue to study the relationships between self-pity and other variables to understand how it can help the professional in the care relationship and contribute to the quality of care. Limitations: The results are descriptive, should be further elaborated in the analysis and establish correlations that allow to establish more precise conclusions regarding the association of other variables with self-pity
