# Pd-based nanoparticles stabilized by ligands Carben N-Heterocyclic for H/D exchange reactions

The activation of C-H bonds through the coordination of sigma bonds has grown in the last thirty years as an important tool in the functionalization of hydrocarbons.[1] Specifically, the deuteration of organic molecules through the activation of C-H bonds is attractive[the use of the active C-H bonds,] because it is a direct process that does not require intermediate steps that can lead to the detriment of the reaction.[2] In addition, the compounds marked isotopically with deuterium are of great interest in life sciences[3] since the kinetic effects of the active C-H bonds can improve the pharmacokinetic properties and thus develop new drugs.[4] This process has been normally achieved by the use of homogeneous or heterogeneous catalysts. However, the use of nanoparticles (NPs) of small metal (1-3 nm) has led the scientific community to be interested in them, and thus to have better selectivity in exchange reactions. However, in several cases, good selectivitys are not achieved in the reaction, which leads to a systematic study to have control in the reactivity of the NPs and thus to have better selectivitys. In addition, and with the aim of carrying out more selective deuteration reactions in aromatic compounds, we report the use of bimetallic NPs of Ru/Ir[16] stabilized by NHC ligands, finding a synergistic effect of each metal on catalytic performance. These NPs deuterated several 2-phenylpyridine positions without presenting the reduction of rings catalyzed by Ru, usually. However, when applying this system in aromatic substrates of biological interest such as amino acids, no good results were obtained. Thus, the need arises to explore other metals that allow to catalyze the exchange H/D selectively in aromatic amino acids.
