# DISEASES TRAVEL WITH HEAT: A CLIMATE CHANGE RISK

The Lancet Countdown 2024 report reveals alarming figures about the health risks associated with climate change. Heat-related mortality reached a record high among people over 65, with a 167% increase'102 percentage points more than expected without the rise in temperatures. This phenomenon, coupled with increased rainfall, has facilitated the spread of diseases in areas previously unaffected. The report notes that over the past decade, 61.3% of global regions have experienced increases in average daily rainfall compared to 1961'1999, while nearly 40% faced extreme droughts during 2023. Events such as the Valencia disaster in Spain and flooding in the desert areas of the Province of La Guajira, Colombia, illustrate this issue. Floods pose a public health risk by fostering the breeding of disease-carrying insects like dengue-transmitting mosquitoes or bacteria such as Leptospira and Vibrio. For the latter, 692,000 cases were reported in 2023 (www.lancetcountdown.org)
