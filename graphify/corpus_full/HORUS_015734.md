# CHAPTER VI - SIEMBRA SYSTEMS

The planting density is the most important parameter for deciding the extent of competition affecting growth and yield in trees, since competition between rubber trees is an effect of the size, number, proximity and spatial disposition of rubber trees (Wei et al., 2005; Hein et al., 2008; Naji & Sahri, 2012). The standard density for planting rubber in Colombia has been 500 trees per hectare, regardless of the genotype or rubber clone, its growth and morphological characteristics, or environmental factors in producing regions. It is assumed that this density provides the optimal space required by the mature rubber tree for all useful life and it is expected that competition within the species is at its most favourable level, thus producing the highest yield of dry rubber per hectare (Pathriratna, 2006). However, it is necessary to consider that, in addition to the seed density, planting systems modify competition within the species, due to the arrangement or distribution of trees in the field.
