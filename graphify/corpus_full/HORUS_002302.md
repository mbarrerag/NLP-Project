# --The art of living: ancient perspectives for a contemporary world

One of the most important aspects of the reception of old thought in the contemporary world was its conception of philosophy as _x0093_forma de vida_x0094_, as _x0093_art de viva_x0094_, as _x0093_terapia_x0094_. The central question of ancient ethics, _x0093_how should I live?_x0094_, has its correlation in the urgent need of the contemporary world to reflect on the challenges that it presents to human life. These challenges can be demarcated into three axes. First, the human being is a technical being, a being that breaks into nature adapting it to its needs and purposes and that, moreover, it can make his life itself, individually and collectively, an object of technical intervention. The possibility of articulating a life art arises precisely from this finding, because, for Greek thought, what we would today consider aesthetic expressions are essentially technical or, in other words, what we would consider today purely technical is also an artistic expression. it was assumed that philosophical practice was a necessary condition to achieve what some Hellenistic schools called the _x0093_art of living_x0094_. This project will address this complex and ambitious notion, which gives meaning to another much more worked within the recent historiography, namely that of philosophy as a way of life. We will seek, first, to articulate it in its original context, working classic texts of thinkers who explicitly coined the idea of the _x0093_art of living_x0094_, but also of others who constitute direct background of them or who were their explicit critics. We will also seek, second, to determine what degree of relevance that idea may have in light of certain contemporary philosophical conceptions. There is indeed a surprising revitalization of that ancient conception of philosophy in feminist or ecological proposals. All of them, moreover, seem to assume a perspective as normative as it could be to speak of a _x0093_art of living_x0094_.
