# The time of chicharras: cosmology, technology, and ecology, in the malacas of the People of the Center and the People of Jaguares of Yurupari

In this work I perform an analysis of the different perspectives of conceiving and doing maloca, from two experiences of construction by two indigenous architects (Ocaina village, and Yucuna village) based in the city of Leticia, capital of the Department of the Colombian Amazon. Through a methodology that combines ethnography with the participatory work and the graphic record of architecture, I intend to extend my description of the constructive practices towards the definition of traditional architectural typologies for the case of these malocas. The work concludes with the description of some qualities of this construction as a collective and ritual fact, in which it highlights the cosmetic representation and ecological management, reflecting an extensive network of knowledge that are put into practice during the construction and which allow to infer similarities between the ways of doing maloca and those of making the chagra; I underline the symmetry of its practices and stress of the capital that entails dialogue with the urbanism and constructive techniques of modernity. between the ways of making the maloca and those of making the chagra; I emhasize the symmetry of their practices and high light some possibilities and risks involved in the dialogue with the urbanisms and constructive technologies of modernity
