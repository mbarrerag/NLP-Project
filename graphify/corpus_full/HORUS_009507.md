# Theorical evaluation of the impact of viscosity on the dissipation of potence in MAGNETIC hyperthermia applied to ferrofluoride WATER/FE3O4

The heating by magnetic induction is widely used in the industry for various processes, such as heating of fluids and casting, forging and application of thermal treatments to metals. In recent years, a new field of research has emerged related to heating of ferrofluid by magnetic induction, driven by the development of nano-materials. One of the main applications of this technique is hyperthermia, which is used as complementary treatment to chemotherapy/radiotherapy for certain types of cancer. Hyperthermia consists in raising the body temperature to cause the death of malignant cells, since they are sensitive to the thermal conditions. While there are other techniques for the application of hyperthermia, heating of ferrofluid by magnetic induction has shown a more uniform distributive temperature, which partially solves the existing limitations. However, due to the multiple factors involved in the process disfluencedness, such as the properties of the magnetic field, the characteristics of ferrofluid and the conditions of application, it is difficult to pre-deduce how the power is dissipated as a function of time.
