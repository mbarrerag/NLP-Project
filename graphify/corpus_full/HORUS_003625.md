# INACTIVE TEST OF THE ANDROGEN RECEPTOR OF CHROMOSOME X (HUMARA) FOR COMPLAINT ASSESSMENT IN HUMAN TEJIDES

Clonality is the characteristic that has defined neoplasm for decades; however, it is now known that carcinogenicity involves competition between multiple dynamic and changing subclones, living in a single tumor environment [1,2,3,4]. One of the most novel methods to define clonality is based on the phenomenon of inactivation by methylation in 46XX individuals. We present a protocol for clonality analysis in women using PCR in the locus humara and methylation-sensitive restriction enzymes. In this pilot study we use tumor and non-tumour DNA from human tissues to evaluate the different patterns of inactivation of chromosome X and controls of tumor samples in fresh and blood
