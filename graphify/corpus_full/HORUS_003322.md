# REPRODUCTIVE PLANT BIOLOGY AND DEPENDENCE OF POLINIZERS

From the evolutionary perspective, the interaction between the plant and the pollinator is based on the gain of biological fitness derived from the two parts. In this sense, for the sesil organism (plant) and the biological vector of pollen (pollinizer), when they interact in a biological function (i.e., foraging, feeding, sexual reproduction) and ecological (pollinization), such gain is greater than the performance achieved by the organism in the absence of the other part. Such mutualism leads to the evolutionary and ecological specialization reciprocal; therefore, from the perspective of conservation, the loss of one component, its deterioration, or the decrease of its populations, anticipates negative consequences in the other component, and in the ecological function of pollination.
