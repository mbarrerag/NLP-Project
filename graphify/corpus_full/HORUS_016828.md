# Myricetin improves collagen structure and stability in demineralized dentin

OBJETIVES To evaluate biochemical and biomechanical changes in demineralized dentin induced by treatment with myricetin (MYR) as a dentin biomodification agent. METHODS After approval of the ethics committee, medium coronal dentin slabs were obtained from human molars (n=15), conditioned with 10% phosphoric acid for 5 h. They were randomly distributed and treated with 100% ethanol (EtOH); 0.2% chlorhexidine (CHX), 1% glutaraldehyde (GA); 6.5% proanthocyanidin (PAC), and 600 μM MYR. Untreated mineralized dentin (DM) and demineralized dentin (DD) slabs were used as control groups. Biomodification potential was analyzed by micro-Raman spectroscopy and hydroxyproline assay. Dentin micro-bars (n=90; 1x1x8 mm) were obtained and subjected to the micro tensile strength test (μTS). Data were analyzed using the Shapiro-Wilk test, ANOVA, and Tukey's post hoc (a=0.05). RESULTS The increase in the Raman peaks of Amide II was evident, concerning Amide I; the Amide I/Amide II ratio contains that MYR, PAC, and GA have a cross-linking effect on DD. MYR highlights a decrease in collagen solubility (10.47±2.82 mg/mL) as well as GA (8.84±4.60 mg/mL) and PAC (12.47±4.57 mg/mL) (p=0.073). MYR-treated dentin showed the highest values of μTS (30.58±1.78 MPa) and elastic modulus (E) (123.9±25.04 MPa). CONCLUSIONS MYR is presented as a novel natural dentinal biomodification agent since it demonstrated a crosslinking effect, decreased collagen solubilization, and improved mechanical properties of the dentinal extracellular matrix. KEY WORDS Myricetin, biomodification, dentin bonding, collagen cross-link, flavonoids
