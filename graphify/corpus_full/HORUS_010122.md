# BOAR SEMEN COOLING IN AN EXTENDER WITH LDL, QUERCETIN, OR RESVERATROL

Low-density lipoproteins (LDL), quercetin (Q) and resveratrol (R) have been used for sperm preservation to improve sperm quality in different species; however, little has been studied for cooling of porcine semen. This study aimed to evaluate the cooling of boar semen in an extender with LDL, Q or R. Fifteen boar semen samples were extended in a BTS extender supplemented with the treatments: LDL at 6%, Q at 10 μM (Q10), 30 μM (Q30) and 50 μM (Q50), or R at 10 μM (R10), 30 μM (R30) and 50 μM. A control without supplementation was included. Semen was stored by cooling at 16°C for 96 h. Every 24 hours, sperm motility and kinetics were evaluated using a computer-assisted sperm analyzer (IVOS). At 24 and 96 h of cooling, functional membrane integrity and total antioxidant capacity (TAC) of semen were evaluated using hypoosmotic test and FRAP assay, respectively. At the same times, reactive oxygen species (ROS), mitochondrial membrane potential (¿¥M), lipid peroxidation, and vitality of sperm were evaluated by flow cytometry with DHR123, JC-1, C11-BODIPY(581/591) and SYBR14/PI probes, respectively. Statistical analysis was performed using linear models and the Tukey's test. LDL improved total and progressive motility at 24 and 48 h of cooling. Q10 and Q50 decreased ROS at 24 h. LDL, Q10, Q30, and Q50 increased TAC at 24 and 96 h. Q10 and R10 increased the live non-peroxidized spermatozoa at 24 and 96 h, respectively. LDL, Q10, Q30, and Q50 modulated mitochondrial activity by reducing high-¿¥M sperm and low-¿¥M sperm, at 24 and 96 h of cooling. It is concluded that LDL and quercetin can improve the motility, vitality, and antioxidant capacity of boar semen, through the reduction of oxidative stress and the modulation of mitochondrial activity during cooling
