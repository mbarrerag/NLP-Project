# EFFECT OF PROCESSING VARIABLES IN THE MANUFACTURE OF BIOCOMPOSITES SUBJECT TO LINEAR BUCKLING

The use of vegetable fibers as reinforcement of composite materials requires the optimization of some manufacturing process parameters, such as fibers type, length, orientation, dosage, and its adherence with the matrix. In this work, the influence of design parameters on biocomposites mechanical properties was evaluated. The effect of three design parameters was considered: fiber type, surface treatment and fiber volumetric fraction. Two types of vegetable fibers were used: Bamboo and Rattan fibers. A composite reinforcement was arranged with fibers as a fabric with an orientation of 0°/90°. To improve the adherence of the fibers with the composite matrix, the fabrics were treated using two types of treatment: cold methane plasma and a mercerization process. For the elaboration of the panels, the method of manual molding and compression compaction was adopted. The standard dimensions of the manufactured panels were 30 cm long by 30 cm wide and an average thickness of 0.7 cm, varying the fibers type and dosage, according to the conditions established in the experimental design. The pressure conditions, compaction time and processing temperature were kept constant. To evaluate the effect of the design parameters, the physical (density and absorption capacity), chemical (crystallinity index), morphological (roughness), and mechanical properties (linear buckling critical load) were determined. An experimental model based on a central composite design was implemented. The obtaining of response surfaces via commercial software allowed the optimization of the manufacturing process of the biocomposites. As a result, it was possible to find equations that allowed predicting the properties of the biocomposite based on the design variables
