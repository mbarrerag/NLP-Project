# Evolution of the metabolism of steroid glycoalkaloids in species of the genus Solanum from a multi-omic perspective

Plants produce different types of specialized metabolites that mediate the biotic and abiotic interactions associated with the defense, reproduction and survival of individuals.In the family Solanaceae, different species produce metabolites with medicinal, nutritional and economic importance.Among the metabolites of greater interest for their medicinal and ecological characteristics are the steroidal glycoalkaloids (SGAs), which are mainly found in species of the genus Solanum.These compounds, facilitate the defense of plants against pathogens and herbivores and have been recorded to generate toxicity in humans and other herbivorous organisms.Genomic analysis and co-expression of genes, have identified that many genes of the metabolic route of the SGAs are grouped in different chromosomal regions.Because of their toxicity, humans have selected the potential metabolites of plants, the varieties that produce less GHS content.These processes of dometication have allowed to identify structural genomic changes in the sequence and organisation of the genes of the SGAs, which are associated with the genes of the species of the diversity and concentration of the GMOTs between species. The aim of the study is to identify the potential pathogens and herbivores and have been reported to cause toxicity in humans and other herbivorous organizations. s species concerning the evolution of SGA metabolism and consider the phylogenetic and metabolic distance among species
