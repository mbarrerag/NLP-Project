# THE AGROINDUSTRIAL DIVERSIFIED AND SUSTAINABLE FARM AS SOCIAL INNOVATION MECHANISM

The Boyacá Region in Colombia, is characterised by small rural farms that offer several raw materials and food products necessary for the good health of the community. Some small farmers have developed their own entrepreneurship projects with ecological design and notable success, such as the wild roses cultivation, the ¿sagu¿ species process that offers the ¿achiras¿ meal, extensive laurel leaves crops for exportation as well as a recent new greenhouse with agroindustrial cultivation of tomatoes. All of these production processes are clean and organic. The businesses are growing and therefore need a greater amount of supplies for the production to cover the growing demand by the community. Therefore, the entrepreneurs encouraged nearby small farmers to developed sustainable production of raw materials. A first step was to create a coordination centre, a kind of regional cooperative association that had emerge from a training programme. At de beginning after the initial promotion of this project, the objectives were to improve soils of the participating farms by adding volcanic rock, to change the common fertilizers, to compost organic matter and to avoid the application of wide spectra synthetic pesticides. Instead of those pesticides, natural control agents were used. In addition, the Biophysical Treatment of Soils, for controlling of non-ionizing electromagnetic radiation, was introduced. As result of the implementations of a number of farm tools and laboratory tests, the selected materials such as fruits, flowers, leaves and roots gained added value. One central intervention area of this programme is the Laurasia Farm. On this farm roses have been grown for more than 14 years. The rose petals get transformed into rosewater and rose hydrolate products through vapour distillation. Those products are then bottled immediately for their distribution. A great number of roses are required for continuous production. We are also stablishing a system to fabricate and register a permission and then a patent, for a nourishment drink with rose hydrolate due to its numerous antioxidants and minerals with medicinal properties. This is the result of research done in our investigation group. Similarly, for processing the ¿sagu¿ roots, the necessary equipment for its preparation had been installed. For increasing the production of the ¿achiras¿ meal is urgent to enlarge the cultivation of ¿sagu¿ roots because they take a year to be ready for cutting. This market grows continually with prices steadily rising. So far we have had to invest in the basic infra-structure such the laboratory for rose water production and packing in controlled conditions, a water purifying plant, a big storeroom for drying of petals and other fresh products, such as laurel leaves, ¿sagu¿ meal and some herbs and weeds which are considered beneficial for health and control of pests such as ¿sage¿, ¿ageratum¿, ¿chipaca¿ and ¿geranium¿ among others, each one during its own flourishing time. We will present a report about the results of the work of the collaborative association. These report will focus on the changes we have observed in the production and the progress we have made due to social innovation
