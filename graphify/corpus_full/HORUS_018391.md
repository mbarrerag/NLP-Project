# ASSESSING OF COMPLICATIONS BY ESTEOINTEGRANE ASSISTANCE WITH TECHNICALS WITHOUT SUB-CONTINUE REDUCTION OF SUB-CONTINUE TESTING

Introduction The surgical procedure of osteointegrated percutaneous implant in some cases involves reduction of subcutaneous tissue. This reduction of tissue has been associated with skin complications that may require intensive medical management or revisional surgery. Methodology A descriptive observational study of patients with percutaneous osteointegrated implant, with techniques without reduction of subcutaneous tissue. Demographic and clinical variables were recorded and analyzed, especially skin complications using the Holgers scale. Results In total 18 patients were included, male predominance (72%), the average age of the procedure was 28 years. The main etiology was microtia with CEA atresia (44%), followed by WTO and acoustic trauma (16%). Patients were included in those who did not resection of subcutaneous tissue. The most frequently used implant was 4 mm (78%) and the length of the procedure was 9 mm (55%). There were no intra-operative complications.
