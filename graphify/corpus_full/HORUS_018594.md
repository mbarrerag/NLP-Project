# Design-based inference using divided questionnaire designs.

When using a sampling strategy (design combination and estimator) any, the rule of collecting all variables in all respondents is implicitly assumed in the literature. Currently, surveys tend to have lower response rates, sometimes due to the length of questionnaires; a greater amount of information is required to be collected; there is the availability of cheaper secondary sources such as administrative records or big data that act as substitutes for survey data. Therefore, it is necessary to look for analyses that combine efficiency and flexibility as the technique of divided questionnaire designs that replace the assumption of collecting all variables for all respondents by collecting a subset of the information required for each unit in the sample. However, under this type of estimating strategies of the Horvitz–Thomson or Hansen–Hurwitz type for the population total have not been described for complex sample designs (unequal, stratified, conglomerate and several stages)
