# DEVELOPMENT OF A CATALYTIC MATERIAL FROM MUNICIPAL and INDUSTRIAL WASTES FOR THE OXIDATION OF ALCOHOLS TO PRODUCTS OF HIGH ADDED VALUE

The oxidation of alcohols to aldehydes represents one of the reactions of organic synthesis at the industrial level; among the reactions proposed in different investigations, the synthesis of benzaldehyde from benzyl alcohol is detailed. Benzaldehyde is one of the important raw materials in various industries (medicine, dyes, resin additives, cosmetics, perfumery, and pharmaceuticals). Traditional processes towards the synthesis of benzaldehyde involve the use of corrosive chemicals such as sulfur, iodine, and manganese, and require the use of high stoichiometric quantities, generating pollutants and residues in the environment. In order to replace the use of these toxic substances, research has focused on the development of alternatives applying the concept of green chemistry; for example, the use of noble metals (Au, Pt, and Pd) to obtain heterogeneous catalysts is reported, achieving good yields; however, they are usually expensive. An economical alternative for the acquisition of raw materials is the implementation of catalysts based on transition metals such as Cu, Co, Ni, and Fe; elements such as Co and Fe play an important role in the catalyst as active phase, obtaining conversions and selectivities between 38% - 98% and higher than 95%, respectively. However, most of the metallic precursors used are obtained from petrochemical processes, commercial salts, or oxides, for which it is necessary to replace them with sources that favor the reduction of the use of natural resources. In the search for more economical alternatives, the use of renewable sources, and the application of environmentally friendly processes, metal catalysts are being developed from some industrial and urban wastes with different applications for the degradation of dyes and pharmaceutical compounds, production of hydrocarbons and value-added products of amine character, since it is estimated that more than 50 million tons of waste are produced in the world with a high content of heavy metals; Among the urban and industrial wastes that will be studied are lithium-ion batteries, which are found in numerous portable devices that after their useful life generate waste of up to 3. Although lithium-ion batteries are an important source of cobalt and other metals, only 5% p/p are recycled, so there is an opportunity for the valorization of this type of waste. Accordingly, industries such as electroplating represent 37% of the total metal finishing market, where elements such as Cr (III and VI), Ni (II), Zn (II), and Fe (II) are used to produce surface coatings on plates and protect them from corrosion. These processes generate waste sludge that is not treated correctly and is discharged directly into water sources or sanitary landfills. In this sense, in the literature, there are no studies on the synthesis of benzaldehyde from aromatic alcohols through the valorization of lithium-ion battery waste and from the electroplating sector for the synthesis of heterogeneous catalysts based on Co and Fe, and therefore it becomes the objective of studies. Therefore, it is expected to generate scientific knowledge that allows looking at new perspectives for the development of catalytic materials with an environmental vision, since it is desired to valorize this type of waste and at the same time generate a commercial product with industrial applications
