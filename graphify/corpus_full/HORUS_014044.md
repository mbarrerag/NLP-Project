# Assessment of the water supply of the Páramo Chingaza and its implications for the water supply for Bogota region in the face of the effects of global change in the Orinoquía basin

The supply of water from Bogotá is supplied by more than 80% of the Páramo de Chingaza, through a transfer from the Orinoquia basin. Given the expansion of the city and surrounding municipalities, the demand for water has been increasing exponentially, however, the availability of the resource, on the contrary, is being affected by the effects of global change, which includes both climate change and the availability of coverage and uses of the soil, and anthropic activities. In this context, knowing where the water supplied by Páramo Chingaza is coming from will allow the establishment of policies for its conservation. Recent studies at the country level have highlighted how a decrease of about 40% of the sources of moisture is expected, that is, the availability of precipitation in the Colombian Andes. These figures support the need to make a study in Bogotá, which includes the assessment of the changes in the Orinoquia basin, since it is the main source of humidity of precipitation and, consequently, of supply of the Páramo Chingaza.
