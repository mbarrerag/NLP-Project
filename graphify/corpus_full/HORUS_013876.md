# Community monitoring and citizen science project for Manizales Gorge to strengthen the early warning system for floods and increasing

The basin of the Manizales gorge is at high risk hydrogeomorphological by exposure of vulnerable elements (housing, industry and infrastructure) in areas with high probability of being affected by flood events, mass movements and torrential avenues. During the years 2000-2013, 3,480 people and 415 affected families were reported, 89 houses destroyed and 27 deaths, which were the result of hydrometeorological disasters (Alcaldía de Manizales, 2014). The event of flooding occurred on March 21, 2011 on the middle-low part of the Manizales gorge basin left 160 people affected, 20 homes destroyed and significant material losses on the industrial area of the city, and the Malteria and Verdum neighborhoods, showing the risk of flooding to which the riperen communities are exposed (Castillo et al., 2020; Serna, 2012; Quintero, 2013). At the root of these events of rising sudden and torrential avenues that have historically affected the gorge, the inhabitants of the Malteria neighborhood saw the need for a system of early alerts of increasing people that would activate them. This participatory action research for a project of community environmental monitoring and citizen science to improve Early Warning Systems due to sudden increases and to promote and innovate with social and community strategies in the territory for the appropriation, creation and analysis of knowledge by communities and students of basic and secondary education of the territory. Citizen science is presented as an innovative approach that could be remarkably beneficial to validate and complement the measurement of SATC variables and obtain objective information through the implementation of low cost sensors. The participation of citizens in the flood management cycle, helps to improve decision-making processes, support the resilience of the community, capacity development and improve the effectiveness of alerts issued by a system of early warning to floods and sudden rising. With this research, it is hoped to enhance the role and facilitate the incorporation of community SATs based on citizen science in the management of flood risk and in mountain river SATs, such as the case of Manizales Quebrada, which can help to highlight the potential of citizen participation that would benefit research providing key information and play an active role in the factors leading to the implementation of the integrated development of the 11th and the 7th century
