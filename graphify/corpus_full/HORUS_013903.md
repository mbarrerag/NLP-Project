# Formulation of comprehensive gender diversity and inclusion policies in the CEF: Conceptual and methodological framework

In recent years, there has been an increase in the visibility of problems related to inequality derived from the diversity of the people participating in society (Leal and Osuna, 2022). Therefore, it is of great importance to work on reducing barriers for all people who are part of different spaces of interaction. For the present project, it is sought to develop theoretical and methodological inputs in the Faculty of Economic Sciences of the National University (FCE) with the objective of promoting the inclusion of all people, regardless of the differences of gender, ethnicity, sexual orientation, sexual identity, and other categories that are in the intersectionality of the heterogeneity of the population (Chamorro, et al., 2022; Pinzón-Fuchs, 2018; 2021). It is important to take into account that in the field of economic sciences there are multiple difficulties and inequalities due to gender and gender roles that are strongly rooted and perpetuated in society (Villaveces and Alvarado, 2021; Valero, et al., 2020; Chamorro et al., 2020). The aim of the project is to address two fundamental aspects of inclusion in the CEF. First, a precise diagnosis will be made of the current status of inclusion in the faculty. This will include an analysis of the challenges and problems that exist in terms of exclusion, violence and discrimination that some members of the faculty may be experiencing. This diagnosis will allow the CEF to have a clear vision of the areas where improvements should be made, while setting the basis for future actions. Secondly, the project aims to create lasting and robust theoretical and methodological tools that are flexible so that they can respond to the changing needs of the CEF population. It is important to note that the CEF has taken institutional measures to address exclusion problems, such as the implementation of gender and diversity policy. However, tensions and complaints of harassment and violence persist, demonstrating the need for a more comprehensive and transformative approach
