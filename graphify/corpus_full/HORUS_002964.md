# ESSENTIAL OILS COMPOSITION AND ACETYLCHOLINESTERASE INHIBITION CHANGES AFTER OVEN DRYING OF PLANT MATERIAL

Drying process of plant material has proven to enhance the extraction of essential oils due to the weakening of cell structures, and several methods have been reported within which stands out sun/shade drying, oven drying, and freeze-drying among others [1,2]. The objective of this investigation was to study the effect of oven drying at 50 °C on the composition of the essential oils and its acetylcholinesterase inhibition activity of three plants obtained from the Lamiaceae family: marjoram (Origanum majorana), mint (Mentha sp.), and rosemary (Rosmarinus officinalis) and two plants of the genus Citrus: Orange (Citrus sinensis) and Tahiti lime (Citrus latifolia). The essential oils were extracted using steam distillation at different stages of the drying process and from the fresh plant material. The compounds present in the oils were identified through GC-MS while the variations in composition were quantified using GC-FID. The yields were calculated and compared on a dry basis in all the cases. The acetylcholinesterase inhibition of the oils was evaluated at 1 mg/mL in the colorimetric Ellman assay. The essential oil yield was significantly increased in the citrus oils after oven drying, while in the case of the herbs, its yield wasn't affected by the treatment. The variations in composition were small enough to maintain the in vitro acetylcholinesterase inhibition activity. Nonetheless, the biggest variations per plant were as follows: orange: reduction in linalool, octanal, decanal and nonanal contents; Tahiti lime: reduction in p-cymene geranial, neral, geranyl acetate and neryl acetate contents; marjoram: reduction in sabinene hydrate and p-cymene contents; mint: reduction in isomenthone content; rosemary: reduction in p-cymene content. The results allowed us to conclude that oven drying at 50 °C is an efficient technique to optimize the essential oil's distillation process with little variations in the oils composition, without negative effect on the bioactivity investigated
