# COVID-19, HEALTH AND WORK RIGHTS IMPACTS IN TWO BARRIES OF BOGOTA

Previous: Historically, pandemics have had a negative impact on the population, widening the gaps and challenging those responsible for formulating public policies in their objective of mitigating inequalities and seeking protection for the most vulnerable people. The pandemics arise due to a break in the balanced interaction between society and nature and are determined by global productive, commercial and work processes. The pandemic of COVID-19 took the form of a comprehensive health and humanitarian crisis that affected especially the populations in the worst socio-economic conditions of the developing countries. Analyzing the short-term impact of COVID-19 on the exercise of the right to health and work in two neighborhoods of the city of Bogotá. A qualitative study with an ethnographic approach, carried out in two neighborhoods of Bogotá: Santa Isabel and Villa del Río. The participants were the participants in the phenomenon of the health and work. In the districts, the participants took part in the participatory, daily and daily observation of persons and 6-semi-structure., there are reported labor challenges, reduced opportunities, extended days, lack of subsidies or aid and economic difficulties. Regarding the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic on the exercise of the right to health, barriers were found to access quality services, limitations in the monitoring of the health-disease process and neglect in mental health care. As regards the right to work, informal workers faced labour challenges, such as atypical or extensive working days, decreased income associated with work, economic difficulties and indebtedness
