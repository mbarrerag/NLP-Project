# THE TORTURE OF COLOMBIA CHOICES BY STUDENTS AND THEIR RELATIONSHIP TO ACADEMIC DESIGN: EVIDENCE FOR COLOMBIA

This research assesses the effect of student school intakes (TCPEs) on academic performance. It takes advantage of the temporal and geographical variation of the SSPEs, in addition to the impossibility that households and teachers have to anticipate these actions of students, to estimate a model of regression of data panel with fixed effects of year and school. Estimates use information on standardized tests of the SCIs, information on schools and an unexplored and rich source of information about shots for the years 2012 to 2019. Estimates show that SCIs were associated with a decrease in scores in standardized tests of mathematics (-0.009 DE), reading (-0.012 DE) and citizen skills (-0.011 DE). This is consistent with literature suggesting that class reduction is related to lower learning. Furthermore, analyses of heterogeneous effects indicate that men, compared to women, and students of lower economic strata, compared to higher strata, have an additional negative effect due to SCTs
