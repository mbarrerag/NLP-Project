# HUMIDITY OF THE SWEALS OF COLOMBIA PARARES AND THEIR SPACE-TEMPORARY DINENAMICS

Soil moisture is a variable that controls physiological, edaphic and biological processes in terrestrial ecosystems, including evapotranspiration of vegetation and complex processes of nutrient exchange between soil and plants, insofar as it contains and transports the nutrients that are solubilized, both from the parent material, and from the organic matter. At the scale of the hydrographic basin, the state of soil moisture determines the non-linear behavior of the hydrological response to precipitation events, in the measure that controls the infiltration of precipitation and therefore the spatial and temporal humidity of the soil in the selected moors. The absence of information on this, of strategic ecosystems, such as moors, hinders the understanding required for the use and management of these ecosystems and their hydrological services. Consequently, the objective of the study was to characterize the spatial and temporal dynamics of soil humidity in the selected moors, at different depths and in different positions of the landscape, and to evaluate their contribution to the hydro regime of the moors of Colombia. With this purpose, between the years 2006 and 2019, the study was carried out a broad monitoring of soil based on the moist, The average of the lower averages in the Belmira and Guerrero moorings and the larger ones in the Chili-Barragán moorland. Overall, the soils of these moors maintained a 540 mm water layer at its first meter of depth, which varied between 4894 and 577 mm, the largest in the Chili-Barragán moor, in agreement with the highest humidity values. The information available allows us to understand the way these ecosystems work, and especially the dynamics of the flow and its response to precipitation events
