# Influence of the synthesis route on the structural and magnetic properties of nanoparticles of La0.7Sr0.3MnO3

Cancer is one of the leading causes of death worldwide and has a significant impact on public health, with current treatment options that often generate side effects in patients. In this context, magnetic hyperthermia emerges as a non-invasive alternative that uses magnetic nanoparticles to generate heat and destroy cancer cells. Given this, this research sought to synthesize magnetic nanoparticles from La0.7Sr0.3MnO3 (LSMO) to study the effect of the synthesis pathway on the production of nanoparticles with optimal properties for biomedical applications. LSMO samples were synthesized by the Sol-gel, Ceramic and Pechini methods. These samples were characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD), X-ray dispersion to low angle (SAXS), thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and magnetic field vibration (VSM) samples were used to study their structure, morphology and magnetic behavior. The nanoparticles obtained by Pechini method presented the best crystalline structure, the smallest size and reduced magnetic properties.
