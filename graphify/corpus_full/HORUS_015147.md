# DETERMINATION OF PHYSICAL AND MICROBIOLOGICAL QUALITY OF COLOMBIAN NATIVE CORN DURING STORAGE

Corn is the second most produced and consumed cereal in the world. Several native species of corn are cultivated throughout the Americas, which despite having cultural and social importance, are relatively unknown if compared to widely spread commercial varieties. Native corn usually has fewer post-harvest losses due to its innate resistance to pests and diseases based on better environmental adaptability. Some difficulties are associated with native corn handling and storage, particularly related to the formation of aflatoxins, classified as carcinogens, thus the natural resistance to the growth of fungi that generate mycotoxins can be evaluated under microbiological growth conditions as well as some physical post-harvest parameters. Therefore, the objective of this work was to assess the stability of a Colombian native corn Negrito variety under storage in terms of physical and microbiological quality. The experimental trial started with uncontrolled laboratory conditions for one week. Subsequently, two groups with humidity levels of 15% and 16,5% respectively were generated, by adding water and mixing the corn constantly in Ziploc bags, for 30 and 60 days in a refrigerated chamber (4°C), performing the following determinations: proximal analysis, amylose/amylopectin ratio, count of fungi and aerobic microorganisms, and aflatoxins content. Preliminary results showed no significant differences between the protein and fat content over time and qualitative differences were evident when comparing microbiological growth since the product stored at higher humidity had a visible formation of microorganisms. This project allowed determining the best storage conditions of native corn as a necessary strategy to improve handling and marketing as well as to generate value based on the quality of traditional products
