# Characterization of compaction caused by a mechanical harvesting system of sugar cane in two types of soils of the valle del cauca

A process of characterization of the spatial variability in the compaction caused during the mechanical harvest of sugarcane was carried out in two representative soils of the Manuelita mill, corresponding to agroecological zones 11H0, 6H1, significant effects of traffic intensity on the compaction, with the highest value in the sites where it was carried out with two passes of the harvester and the tractor - self-dumping wagon assembly (478.79 Mg*km/ha) higher by 39% than the sites where the tractor - wagon traveled once of self-turning, the apparent density determined in the 11H0 soil (loamy texture) did not present significant differences in the different levels of traffic intensity presented in the mechanical harvest of sugarcane, although there was an increase in the magnitude of the apparent wet density after the transit of harvesting machinery, observing a maximum value of 1.64 g/cm3. The penetration resistance for the two types of soils with loam and clay textures presented significant differences resulting from the number of passes of the equipment, reaching values of 2.33 MPa for the loam texture at 30 cm depth and in the clay soil it reached 1.68 MPa. at depths of 50 cm. Knowledge of the spatial distribution of compaction is of vital importance to plan and determine the best sequence of operations with the cutting and chaining equipment that performs the work of mechanical sugarcane harvesting, as well as determining the appropriate patterns in the operation that guarantee the least impact on the ground
