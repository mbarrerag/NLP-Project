# The Preeminence of Reference and Other Presumptions of Meaning Theory

In this thesis it is argued that the project of a theory of meaning, as was proposed by Donald Davidson in his influential essay “Truth and Meaning”, is based on at least two questionable assumptions about language: on the one hand, the principle of compositionality (articulated with arguments about the learning character of language and the productivity of our linguistic use) and, on the other hand, the idea (of presumed Fregean rootness) according to which, when stating the references of the terms and sentences of a language, they are indirectly shown their meanings. This second budget is called “preeminence of reference”. The thesis then presents a critical approach to the principle of compositionality and, above all, to the budget of the preeminence of the reference adopted by the advocates of the veritative-conditional theories that Michael Dummett characterized as “modests”.
