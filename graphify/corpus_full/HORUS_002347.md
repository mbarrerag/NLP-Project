# IMPACT OF DIRECT FOREIGN INVESTMENT ON CLIMATE CHANGE AND EMPLOYMENT IN COLOMBIA: REFLECTIONS IN THE FRAMEWORK OF SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT

This article assesses the impact of foreign direct investment (FDI) on CO2 emissions in Colombia, in the context of discussions on sustainable development and the potential impact of FDI on employment. Initially, a reflection is made on the concept of sustainable development, to understand it in the context of the contemporary debate and its relationship with economic growth. A second part describes the type of FDI that has entered Colombia, showing that one of the main recipient sectors has been the mining-energy sector and the acquisition of companies already existing in sectors such as services and manufactures. Then, the sectoral impact of employment in Colombia and the type of FDI is analysed. On the other hand, the environmental impact evidenced by greenhouse gas emissions is assessed. For this last, the relationship between economic growth and environmental degradation is analysed, with the aim of determining whether FDI has had any direct or indirect effect on the increase in emissions of gases into the atmosphere. On the other hand, a debate is proposed that analyzes the type of international integration of countries such as Colombia, and, like this, because it is highly extractivist and rerentist, concentrates on highly polluting industries, and generates improvements in terms of human capital
