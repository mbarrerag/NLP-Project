# EXPERIMENTAL DETERMINATION AND MODELING OF THE SOLUBILITY OF CANNABIDIOL (CBD) IN PURE SOLVENTS

Increasing medical evidence supports the idea of a beneficial effect of cannabinoid compounds from the cannabis plant for the treatment of chronic diseases and pathologies. However, the pharmaceutical industry uses the non-psychotropic compound, isolated cannabidiol (CBD), to ensure the absence of psychoactivity induced by tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) and to comply with regulatory requirements in different countries [1]. To isolate the CBD, a crystallization process is carried out where solvent is added to the CBD extract and the mixture is subjected to thermal shocks and low temperatures. In this way, isolated extracts of pure cannabidiol (CBD > 99.9%) are obtained, with high stability and without organoleptic or psychotropic properties that affect the formulation of pharmaceutical grade products [1]. Studying the behavior of the solubility curve allows establishing conditions such as: the type of crystallization, solvent:CBD extract ratio and the different cooling ramps that could be used [2]. The aim of this work was to measure and model the solubility of cannabidiol (CBD) in four pure solvents: pentane, hexane, heptane, and isooctane, under local pressure of 74.66 kPa. Experiments were performed by an isothermal method using an in-situ filter to sample only the homogeneous liquid phase. The samples were analyzed by gravimetry and gas chromatography coupled to a flame ionization detector (GC-FID). A thermal analysis of the CBD by Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) enabled to verify its melting point, enthalpy of fusion and the temperature dependence of the liquid and solid specific heat capacity. According to results, the corresponding melting point and enthalpy of fusion for CBD were 74.40 ¿ 0.02 °C and 28.40 ¿ 0.02 J mol-1, respectively. The heat capacity of the solid and liquid did not show a significant variation with the temperature. As expected, CBD solubility increased with temperature in all the solvents. At a temperature of 30°C and 4°C, the solubility of CBD was 0.890 and 0.017 ¿ 0.005 g/g and 0.440 and 0.013 ¿ 0.005 g/g in hexane and heptane, respectively. Hexane and Heptane are the most suitable solvents for the purification and isolation process of CBD due to its lower hazard and better solubility behavior that suggests a lower amount of CBD remaining in the mother liquor due to its lower solubility at low temperatures. Using the obtained data, a solubility model was proposed to obtain the activity coefficients of dissolved CBD. These data were used to regress the binary parameters for a WILSON, NRTL, UNIQUAC and UNIFAC equation. Regressed models fit reasonably well the experimental data and can be further used for process design
