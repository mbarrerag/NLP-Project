# Methodological proposal to identify determinants that affect the coordination of last mile logistics actors.

With the growth of e-commerce, urbanization and population growth, the complexity of the last mile in the supply chain has increased, forcing companies to evaluate new distribution strategies and face new logistical challenges. Coordination mechanisms appear as a solution to meet the challenges of the last mile. Studies related to coordination mechanisms in the last mile of the supply chain have been influenced by the absence of practical applications, reflecting a low consideration of all actors in the supply chain, their roles and the needs of the chain in order to avoid inefficient diagnoses in the application of coordination mechanisms. A three-phase methodology is proposed to identify the determinants that affect the coordination mechanisms in the last mile logistics. In the first phase, the determinants that affect the coordination mechanisms are identified in a review of the literature and subsequently are compared by the industry and the academy to select those that have the most weight in the implementation of coordination mechanisms. In the second phase, the level of impact of each determinant is measured to finally, in the third phase, to propose a coordination mechanism to improve the performance of the company
