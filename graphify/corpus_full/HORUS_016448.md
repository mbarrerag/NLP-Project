# DIVERSITY OF BLOOD PARASITES INFECTING HERPETOFAUNA IN CASANARE, COLOMBIA

Colombia is a megadiverse country with the second-highest richness in amphibian species and third place for reptiles richness in the world. However, little is known about the blood parasites they harbor. In order to explore host parasite relationships in the highly diverse region of Orinoco basin, a field trip was carried out in March 2021 in the flooded savannas of the department of Casanare, Colombia. One hundred and twenty-seven individuals were sampled, 79 reptiles and 48 amphibians, where a total of 519 microscope slides (peripheral and organ blood) were taken. The diagnosis was made with light microscopy and several taxa of Apicomplexa, Nematoda and Trypanosomatidae were identified; the overall prevalence for herpetofauna in these savannas was 63%. The respective prevalences for the different groups of hemoparasites are as follows: Trypanosoma 41.7%, Hepatozoon 19.7%, Lankesterella 15.0%, Plasmodium 3.9%, and microfilariae, Dactylosoma and Haemogregarina with 2.4% each. It should be noted that 21 individuals were found with coinfection, usually Trypanosoma with Hepatozoon or Lankesterella. While little is known about non-haemosporida parasite found in the region, and all those resoult are new, the Plasmodium finded are morphologically similar to which had been found in Brazil, and Oricono-Amazonia ecotone in Colombia. Further studies using molecular markers will complement those findings
