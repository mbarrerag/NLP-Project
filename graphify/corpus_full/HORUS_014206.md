# Urban ruins: case study in contemporary photography in Latin America between 1990 and 2010

During the late 20th century and the beginning of the 21st century, several artists in Latin America were interested in cities and their decay. In their works, these artists used photography to highlight processes of deterioration that transform cities, such as the alteration of the original functions of urban structures, the abandonment of these or even their ruins. The proposal of this thesis consists in studying a set of photographs that address the topic of urban ruins in Latin American cities between 1990 _x0093_y 2010. This research does not in any way attempt to ignore the heterogeneity of what seeks to encompass the Latin American sign; instead, it will articulate a concept that acts as an axis and delimiter of the proposal. The concept of allegory, developed by the philosopher Walter Benjamin in his early work, implies for this research to reject an aesthetic approach that relates photographic images to schools, authors and individual works. The approach points out a series of allegorical operations that open the sense of each image to refer to the transitiveness of the cities and man himself. This opening of meaning is inscribed in a historical moment of Latin American photography
