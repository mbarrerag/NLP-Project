# STAMP: Between the MARK OF A HISTORY AND THE STORY OF A MARK. A REMEMBER OF STREETS AND MYSTERIES OF THE HAND OF PAMUK

Istanbul exists as a concrete historical reality, but also as a great imaginary, in both multiple and practically uncompromising cases. Although the narratives and tourism have reduced it to certain archetypes, what is there beyond the obvious and archetypal? As a tourist and academic traveler I wonder what I can visualize in a brief stay in such a large, dense city and with an urban accumulation of more than 8,000 years. A few days at a frantic pace put me in front of how to overcome the condition of an indolent traveler and that is where the dialogue between the short experience of the interested visitor with that of the reader of the book Istanbul, by Orhan Pamuk, is put in relation to shortening the distance in the imagined city, the narrated and the constructed, while finding the traces of a better delineated and less ephemeral landscape. Pamuk’s work serves as a reference to us to enter into the authors who barely preceded it in the construction of an imaginary and a landscape, to confront it with the imaginarys that have been built to the service of the tourism industry.
