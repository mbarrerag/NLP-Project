# EXTRACTION AND CHARACTERIZATION OF LIGNIN FROM CANNABIS HEMP WOOD

The pollution of water sources is a problem that society has faced for the last decades, the growing effort in the development of alternatives for the purification of water bodies has a wide repertoire and bibliographic review in the use of materials capable of selectively extracting typical pollutants such as metal ions (cadmium, copper, lead, etc.). Therefore, the use of lignocellulosic biomass residues is proposed as a cost-effective alternative in the detoxification of water with metal ions, being in this case the lignin derived from cannabis hemp wood. In the present study, the characterization of the raw material is presented, obtaining the lumber in a mechanical way and its subsequent treatment to obtain pure lignin. The raw material has a moisture content ranging between 9-11%, an ash content of 2.3% and an extractable content of 14%, among others. Afterwards the Kraft method was used to obtain lignin from the wood, which involves chemical modifications such as submerging the raw material in a sodium hydroxide solution and allowing sufficient reaction time for the desired extraction. Once the extraction process is completed, a basic solution known as "black liquor" is obtained, containing the lignin extracted from the lumber. For the recovery of the lignin, the medium was acidified with 37% hydrochloric acid up to three different pH values: 1, 3 and 7, for which the lignin precipitation was different. Once the lignin was recovered and washed for each of the pH evaluated, a calibration curve was made using the UV-VIS spectrophotometer to provide a tool for monitoring the Kraft reaction and the corresponding verification of the composition of the lignin obtained performing a TGA analysis
