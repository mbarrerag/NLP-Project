# Effects of biofertilizers and bio-stimulants on root development and the yield of Solanum tuberosum in contrasting soils with organic matter

The use of plant growth promoting bacteria is an alternative to the use of chemical fertilizers that favors the care of the environment and the production of crops. This research aims to evaluate the effect on the root growth of Solanum tuberosum plants, the inoculation of previously isolated and characterized plant growth promoting bacteria, and to compare it with commercial bio-stimulants designed for root stimulation, in soils with high and low organic matter content. Bacteria belonging to the Bench of strains and genes of the Institute of Biotechnology of the National University of Colombia (IBUN), which according to previous works have presented attributes of interest in their growth promotion capacity. Bacteria that promote root growth, will be characterized in terms of their capacity to solubilize phosphore and produce regulatory substances of plant growth as indol-3-acetic acid (IAA), the bioassays will be performed with an experimental design in blocks at random to evaluate the root response parameters as they are, the area of the root surface of the product (RSA), the average diameter of the plant (ARDL) and/A.
