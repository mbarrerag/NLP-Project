# Role of microtubule motor proteins in the spindle anchoring and positioning during meiosis I in mouse oocytes

In the meiosis I phase of the oocitus, errors frequently occur during the process of chromosomal segregation, resulting in aneuploidias that are the main cause of spontaneous abortion and poor congenital formations. After the rupture of the motor, the oocitus becomes the outermost part of the oocitus, the formation of the meiotic spindle begins.The initial position is indispensable for the prevention of aneuploidias. Subsequently, in the metaphase I, the meiotic spindle migrates towards the cortex to allow a marked asymmetric division.In the mouse oocytes, the positioning and migration of the meiotic spindle is regulated by two opposing forces, the F-actin and the organizing centers of cytoplasmic microtubules in metaphase (mcMTOCs, by their acronyms in English).It is thus the iopulsation of the mote body (MTs) nucleated by the mcMT. The dynamic of the gyno protein Eg5 shows as a result a failed migration of the spindle. Finally, the disturbance of the gyno around the mcMTOCs results in an abnormal positioning and migration of the meiotic spindle. These findings suggest that the dynamics of the lyso protein Eg5 regulates the positioning and migration of the gyno protein. The dynoin and the kynesine Eg5 drive two forces of the gyno to the gyno (the gyno) and the gyno Eg5 drive two forces of the gyno to refine the traction force of the nucleated MTs by mcMTOCs.
