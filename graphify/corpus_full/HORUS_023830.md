# From the margins to the center: emergency and consolidation of the movement for human rights in Colombia, 1979 – 1991

In recent years, the global emergence of the human rights movement has sparked wide debates in the social sciences, especially in history. This research is a contribution to this discussion starting from the Colombian case, since it focuses mainly on the social, international and ideological phenomena that caused the discourse and practice of human rights to cease to be marginal to occupy a central place since the late 1970s. For this, in the first chapter, a review of the main discussions on the social origin of the movement is made and some criticisms are posed to the more widespread temporalities with the aim of introducing an analytical framework that allows to establish temporary differences with the dominant analyses. Subsequently, in the second chapter, we analyze how the emergence of the movement for human rights was a contingent result in which two political trends that understood internationalism and solidarity in a different way came together. The third chapter is dedicated to the movement of the department of Antioquia and here we study in detail its main activities, as well as its relations with the State and transnational organizations, highlighting the specificity of the movement in this part of the country. framework that allows the establishment of temporary differences with the dominant analysis.
