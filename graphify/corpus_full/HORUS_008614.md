# Bogotá and trade in imports into Colombia (1830-1930)

This work proposes a journey through the History of the goods of import consumption that were offered in Bogotá during the 19th century and until 1930, as well as some practices of traders in the main Colombian cities. It explains the role that fulfilled outstanding traders like the literate Soledad Acosta de Samper, Mamerto García Montoya and the prestigious house of Francisco Vargas & Hermanos, selling so many products from Europe and the United States through links with national and foreign businessmen. It defends the the thesis about the importance of Bogotá as epicenter of imports, interacting with other centers of trade like Rionegro and Panama City, from there Colombian importers had to make their payments by means of gold and through the production of coffee, tobacco, quinca or cocoa. To a good extent, the traders managed to beat those who defended the local production of paper (protectivists), hence Colombia was a country that did not succeed industrially in a decisive way, giving more to the influences of an urban elite that made wealth by joining to the mercantiism and fixing remarkable forms of social exclusion by means of possession of foreign goods and goods.
