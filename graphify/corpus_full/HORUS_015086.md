# Entire cow milk consumption and fat mass loss in outpatient patients with obesity or overweight in the state of Mexico

Abstract Introduction: Fat is one of the most controversial macronutrients of whole cow's milk (LEV) due to its role in the development of obesity and overweight. However, there is sufficient evidence to contradict the fact that whole cow's milk has responsibility for the development of obesity or overweight. The aim of this study was to evaluate the effect of LEV within a dietary intervention for fat loss in obese or overweight adults. Semi-experimental, non-randomized, parallel-designed study of 40 patients who were prescribed the same diet therapy scheme, differentiated by the type of milk they usually consumed; 10 per each type of milk: whole (LE), semi-decremated (LS), defatted (LD), without milk (SL). Each subject was evaluated for three months. The loss of kg of fat among the 4 groups was compared. The probability of losing 4 kg of fat for LE was 20 times greater than for SL (OR=21), five times greater than for LD (OR=6), and eight times greater than for LS (OR=9)
