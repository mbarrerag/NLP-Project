# Degradation of dyes present in wastewater using a natural mineral based on iron oxides as a catalyst.

One of the main problems of the textile industry is the high amount of dyes deposited in the bodies of water, which can have carcinogenic effects, cause eutrophication and disturbances in aquatic life. The Fenton process, is an advanced oxidation process that uses iron salts (Fe2+) soluble as catalysts in the presence of H2O2 at acidic pH. In the present research, with the aim of valorizing a natural material based on iron oxides from Acerías de Paz del Río in the department of Boyacá, Colombia, this material was taken to reaction as catalyst in the Fenton type process against two dyes used as a model in degradation reactions: Violet Crystal (CV) and Fucsina Ácida (FA). The material was characterized through different techniques, finding a low surface area (27 m2/g), different phases of iron, within which the majority of the siderite, goethite and haematite and an elemental majority composition for iron and oxygen.
