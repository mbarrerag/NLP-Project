# Re-incorporation into the production cycle of an industrial steel waste in the manufacture of additive filaments.

The 3D printing technique based on extrusion (MEX) has proven to be reliable and low cost for obtaining metal parts. Therefore, in this work, its suitability to obtain parts of a material composed of iron/iron oxide from an industrial waste was studied. Initially the influence of grinding time and chemical reduction temperature was evaluated. Once obtained the dust were manufactured filaments with polymer matrix of TPU/PP/SA and TPE/PP/SA in a proportion for both cases of 87-13 %wt., (metal dust – polymeric mixture, respectively). The influence of the temperature and the print speed on the geometry of the printed parts was evaluated, and the probetas that most closely approximated the nominal value of design were chosen. Finally, the influence of the synterization cycle was evaluated on the mechanical and microstructural properties of the same. The results showed that the printing temperature did not have a significant impact on the printing geometry of the pieces as it did the selection of the thermal polymelt and the sinterization phenomena was generated
