# USE OF SEISMIC ISOLATION ON LIMITED DUCTILITY REINFORCED CONCRETE SHEAR WALL BUILDINGS: A CASE STUDY

In Colombia, the use of reinforced concrete walls as a combined structural system for the construction of high-rise housing has become a popular solution due to its low cost, fast construction, and better use of built areas; however, the current implementation of this structural system is characterized by a low ductility capacity due to the large rigidity of the walls combined with poor steel reinforcement requirements, compromising the building's performance during a seismic event. To address these problems, new regulations increased the minimum quantities of steel reinforcements and cross-section dimensions, as well as forced the use of boundary elements, leading to an increment of the structural cost without knowing what the expected seismic performance would be. The implementation of seismic isolation could reduce the seismic demand on the structural elements, avoiding the need for newer and more strict requirements, while keeping the advantages of the current application of the combined structural system based on limited ductility reinforced concrete shear walls. To corroborate this assumption, a 12-story reinforced concrete shear wall building was taken as a case study to evaluate the effects of the use of seismic isolation in such structural systems. The case study building was assumed to be located at a high seismic area and was designed and detailed following the Colombian building code NSR-10, considering both the fixed-base and isolated-base configurations. In addition, three-dimensional nonlinear time-history analyses were carried out to compute the seismic response of the case study building with both base configurations. The use of seismic base isolation allows for a reduction of up to 59% of the reinforcement steel necessary for the structural system compared to that obtained for the fixed-base configuration, mainly caused by the reduction of the use of boundary elements. In addition, the nonlinear time history analyses showed that the seismic response of the isolated-base case study building is lower compared to that of the fixed-base case study building in terms of peak floor shear forces, peak story drifts, and peak floor absolute accelerations, also reducing the high modes effects. These results corroborate the assumption that the current application of reinforced concrete shear wall buildings can still be used by isolating the structural system, thus reducing building costs and increasing the overall seismic performance
