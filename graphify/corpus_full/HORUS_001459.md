# MODELING FREIGHT TRIP GENERATION FOR LARGE HOSPITALS IN MEDELLIN, COLOMBIA

This study investigates freight trip generation (FTG) in large hospitals within the Medellín Metropolitan Area. We've identified and quantified key variables influencing hospital trip freight transport patterns through a comprehensive methodology, including detailed data collection and applying linear and non-linear regression models. Our analysis, which focused on five hospitals with more than 300 beds, represents the entire population of major freight generators in the area. Linear regression models, particularly those incorporating "Beds" and "Permanent Employees" with an intercept, stood out as the most robust, explaining a significant portion of the variation in freight trips. These models indicate that hospital size and staffing levels are crucial for generating freight trips. Additionally, non-linear models, such as linear-logarithmic, logarithmic-linear, and logarithmic-logarithmic, provided further insights, capturing diminishing returns and proportional relationships between resources and freight trips. The study also highlighted significant logistical challenges, such as restricting freight trips to daytime hours, which contributes to urban congestion, and the need for dedicated infrastructure for loading and unloading. However, these findings also present exciting opportunities for logistical reorganization. By considering the potential for nighttime deliveries and infrastructure improvements, we could significantly improve urban freight transportation efficiency, offering a hopeful outlook for the future
