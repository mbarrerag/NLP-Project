# THE TRANSPARENCY CENTER FOR CARRYING OUT INTEGRAL RESEARCH PILOT PROJECTS, A COMMITMENT TO TRANSFER KNOWLEDGE AND UNDERSTAND THE TERRITORY

Colombia occupies a territory that is highly biodiverse, but also vulnerable to Climate Change, as it is located in the torrid zone. Current national polices have carried out an Energy Transition plan that includes the use of its natural gas resources to guarantee the country's self-sufficiency and in the same time a proper strategy of environmental care. One of the possible sources is obtained by means of hydraulic fracturing of source rocks in the Middle Magdalena Valley basin. In an exercise without precedent in Colombia, Integral Research Pilot Projects (PPII) were designed to get deep knowledge of environmental variables (biotic, physical and social) of the territory, and through governance exercises, with an informed society, make decisions about the possibility of the commercial deployment of the exploitation of the oil and gas from the La Luna source rock. The PPII have three phases: the first in which the institutions of the Colombian State were strengthened and carried out the construction of the regional environmental baseline of the territory, the second in which controlled and monitored tests of hydraulic fracturing will be carried out in real time, and the third phase includes the benefit/cost evaluation of the exercise. As a fundamental axis for the development of the PPII, the Transparency Center was structured, a digital platform in which the datasets, information and knowledge generated had a repository for the scope and handing of civil society. The Transparency Center is managed by the National University of Colombia and its mission is to build content in the thematic areas identified as having the greatest impact of hydraulic fracturing activity: water, air, ecosystems, induced seismicity, public health, technical issues and socio-economic aspects. Owing to the policy debate of this industrial practice, the Transparency Center may represent a reference repository and a community pedagogical strategy for a proper understanding of potential impacts of the fracking development. Along this talk, we present the conceptual bases and the first results of the Transparency Center.
