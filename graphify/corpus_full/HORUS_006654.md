# Evaluation of public policy on State participations since maximization of value in the Colombian electricity sector (2010 – 2020)

This thesis evaluates the implementation of the public policy of state participations (CONPES 3851 of 2015 and 3927 of 2018) from the approach of maximization of value in the Companies of State Participation (EPE) of the Colombian electricity sector (EPE) during the period 2010-2020. The general objective is to evaluate how these policies have influenced the management and financial and social performance of the EPE, contrasting its declared objective of Maximization of Value for Shareholders (EPE) with the theory of Management for Public Value. The methodological design adopts an interpretative qualitative approach, using the case study strategy to deepen two public companies generating electricity: URRA S.A. E.P. and GECELCA S.A. E.P. The empirical work is structured in two phases: a documentary review of the official figures and a study of cases that uses a strong analysis of content and financial. The analysis of content explores the practices around corporate governance and the creation of economic and public value in the EPEs. The financial analysis evaluates the impact of these practices in the MVA of the EPEs for the shareholder state. The results show a transformation of the results and practices in the public sector. policy of state participations (CONPES 3851 from 2015 and 3927 from 2018) from the value maximisation approach in the State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs) of the Colombian electricity sector during the period 2010-2020. The overall objective is to evaluate how these policies have influenced the management and financial and social performance of SOE, contracting their objective of Maximising Shareholder Value (MVA) with the theory of Management for Public Value. The methodological design adopted an interpretative qualitative approach, using the case study strategy to delve into two electricity generation companies: URRAS S.A. E.S.P. and GECELCA S.A. E.P. The empirical work is structured in two phases: a documentarity review and analysis of the effectiveness finds reveal that, though state ownership policy has made progress in implementing corporate governance practices, its focus on maximising economic-financial value has limited its capacity to generate genuine public value. It is observed that corporate governance practices, throughout aligned with international recommendations, do not needarily translate into improvements in the provision of public services or the satisfaction of social and environmental needs. Futuremore, the these highlights the need to reconsider the corporateization model to more effectively integrate public objectives, proposing management that balances financial returns with the social and environmental responsibility of state-owned enterprises
