# DEPREDATION IN ARTIFICIAL NETWORK OF AVES IN A GRADENT OF ANTROPICAL INTERVENTION IN COLOMBIAN AMAZON

The coverage of natural forests is changing due to the advance of the urban, agricultural and livestock frontier, affecting the availability of shelters and resources for the reproduction of some birds. Despite the progress in the knowledge of the effect of anthropic interventions on biodiversity, research that shows the anthropic effect on the life history of birds, including reproduction, is limited. To describe the anthropic effect on nest predation, 108 artificial nests were established in an an anthropic intervention gradient in the jurisdiction of Leticia, Amazonas, Colombia. Of the 38 nests that were predated, 6 were in the site with less anthropic intervention, 15 at the intermediate intervention site and 17 at the site with greater intervention.
