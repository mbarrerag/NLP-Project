# SOCIAL DISIGURES RELATING TO MORTALITY BY TRAFFIC ACCIDENTS

Objective To synthesize the social inequalities related to mortality from traffic accidents reported in scientific publications. Method An exploratory review of the literature was carried out following the guide PRISMA-ScR. Using the vocabulary MesH, a systematic search was made of articles published in English, Portuguese and Spanish in the databases EBSCO, Scielo, Scopus, Ovid and PubMed. Results 47,790 records were identified in the initial search, of which 35 articles met the selection criteria. Nine out of ten publications are from high-income countries and there is a greater interest in the analysis of mortality in occupants and motor drivers. Half of the publications use race, ethnicity and geolocation as socioeconomic position variables. The articles included in this review indicate that groups of people with low socio-economic status present higher mortality from traffic accidents. Conclusions The greatest mortality from traffic accidents is presented in people with low socio-economic position, suggesting the development of road safety actions that are linked to other political agendas in order to reduce their incidence by 2030.
