# Use of white rot fungi for enzymatic production and their potential application in the paper industry

The growing economic development of the last decades has become evident through the strengthening of the industry, which has undoubtedly brought great benefits and prosperity to humanity, but also great challenges to overcome within its margin of process. In this sense, the mitigation of the generated toxic byproducts is perhaps one of the greatest challenges. In the case of the paper industry, a quantity of aggregates is generated that can be highly polluting due to the use of different chemicals and dyes that, not to be fully exploited in the transformation stage of the pulp to paper, end up being discarded in the watery effluents, causing a serious affectation and deterioration of the aquatic microsystems and all their biodiversity. [1][2] [3] This environmental problem has been addressed by some classical methodologies such as filtration and flocculation, however, biotechnology, with its large arsenal and variety of potential enzymatic, has become in recent years a highly favorable alternative for the replacement of the traditional chemical processes that can affect not only the health of the human being, but also impact negatively the environment [4][5]. This is why the use of rice is one of the main lines in the national economy, and the use of FES as a novel methodology for the use of agricultural waste with the generation of value added products, such as ligninolytic enzymes, will be evaluated in addition to its potential application in terms of both biopulping and bleaching, as well as the treatment of coloured effluents generated by the paper industry, thus allowing it to contribute to the solution of an environmental problem of regional and national interest
