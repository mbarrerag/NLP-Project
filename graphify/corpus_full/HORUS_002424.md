# Hernán Rodríguez Vargas. Seven Myths of the Independence of New Granada (1810-1819). Images, Imaginaries and politicization of history. Bogotá: Pontificia Universidad Javeriana, 2023. 276 pages.

The book by Hernán Rodríguez Vargas is based, as its title warns, on Matthew Randall's work.1 The text analyzes some processes through which imaginary ideas were built, since the nineteenth century, on the independence of the country, but which, with the help of iconographic and media dissemination, became myths both in a heroic history and in an “official history” (p. 29). In this case the myth means something fictitious that is accepted as true, totally or partially (p. 32), in contrast to what was understood as myth in its origins, that is, a credible explanation to phenomena of nature. Thus, Rodríguez Vargas tries to disassemble the explanatory myths of independence, showing them as fictitious, but that Colombian historiography, since the nineteenth century, has wanted to show as true explanations. The seven myths treated by the author are: that of the heroes of the homeland; that of the lack of participation of women; that of the homogeneity and completeness of independence, that of the false equivalences, that of the national edade, that of the Church, and that of the consummation, was even to be built
