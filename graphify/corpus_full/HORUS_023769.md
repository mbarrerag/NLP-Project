# Multivariate characterization of brain dynamics for disorders of consciousness.

After a severe brain injury some patients may present serious alterations of consciousness. These lesions greatly limit the quality of life of these patients and consequently that of their family. The last two decades have generated special interest the understanding, description and characterization of the brain dynamics in this group of patients. The immediate consequence of this understanding is the construction of tools that allow to improve the decision making for the doctors who attend or intervene these patients. Multiple strategies have been explored for this, from the construction of rigorous behavioral tests to the use of novel neuroimaging techniques. These latter have shown a promising future for the context of study, reaching transcendental findings in relation to the understanding of the emergence of consciousness, its alteration in comatose states and have even derived in potential diagnostic tools. However and much in spite of these important achievements, there are still multiple challenges to solve in it. One of these fundamental challenges is the definition of an adequate experimental framework to model the brain dynamics given its overwhelming complexity. Various strategies have been proposed, however, the doctor's thesis remains unable to unify in a single representation of the most important agents for the emergency of the brain.
