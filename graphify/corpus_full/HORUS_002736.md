# EFFECTS OF EXOPOLYSACCHARIDES ON THE SENSORY QUALITY OF FERMENTED DAIRY PRODUCTS

Currently there are new trends aimed at the food industry which focus on the consumption of products with less inclusion of additives in order to meet consumer demand versus the consumption of products that retain their properties without additives to replace their own food characteristics. Therefore, the aim of this work is to perform a systematic review of literature to know the effects of EPS on the sensory quality of fermented dairy products as an alternative for the replacement of some additives in this type of food matrix. In the present work, Scopus databases were used for the systematic review of literature to identify studies associated with the effects of EPS on the sensory quality of fermented dairy products. Based on a literature review, it was shown that the microorganisms used in fermented dairy products are Lactococcus, Lactobacillus, Leuconostoc, Pediococcus, and Streptococcus thermophilus, responsible for synthesizing the monomers that make up EPS. These monomers are of great importance for the formation of the protein-EPS complex according to their configuration and molecular weight, they allow to form hydrogen bonds between the polymer and the protein, generating a three-dimensional network that allows compacting the tertiary structure of proteins, promoting the water retention capacity due to high porosity, decreasing particle size, obtaining more homogeneous dispersion and therefore, reducing phase separation, increasing the cohesiveness and firmness of the product. Therefore, the incidence of EPS in the properties mentioned above lead to better sensory properties such as texture, sensation in the mouth, appearance, allowing also to enhance the characteristic flavors of these products. The functionality of EPS strongly depends on the type of LAB, the monomers that compose them and the fermentation conditions, as this will limit the formation of the Protein-EPS complex and therefore the effects on the physico-chemical properties described in fermented dairy products, which have an impact on the sensory perception of the consumer. However, several authors suggest the combination of strains (commercial and modified) increase the efficiency of EPS production, suggesting these polymers as substitutes for thickeners and emulsifiers
