# CASE MANAGEMENT FOR PATIENTS WITH MULTIMORBIDITY FROM THE USERS' QUALITATIVE PERSPECTIVE

People with multiple morbidities have greater limitations to perform activities of daily living. Consequently, these patients usually require the support of a caregiver, who must take on the task of not only accompanying them on a daily basis but also of making decisions (Carreño Moreno & Chaparro-Díaz, 2017) and engaging in the administrative processes necessary to give continuity to the treatment of the patient, such as consultations with various medical specialties and simultaneous specialized treatments. Evidence suggests that case management allows for comprehensive care of chronically ill patients and their relatives because it articulates the resources required by complex patients and generates optimal clinical outcomes (Joo & Huber, 2017). In this context, this study aims to understand user experience after a case management intervention in patients with multimorbidity and their caregivers in the city of Bogotá, Colombia. A qualitative descriptive study was considered appropriate to address the main study's qualitative phase (Hernández-Zambrano et al., 2019). It included users who received the home case management intervention by nurses with advanced training. Seventeen semi-structured interviews were conducted, either in person or by telephone, with 33 users of the case management intervention (17 patients with multimorbidity and 16 caregivers). Data were analyzed using the open and focused coding proposed by Charmaz (Kapriskie, 2019). Eleven themes emerged, which were organized in 3 dimensions: 1) characteristics of the actors involved in the model and factors that facilitate the connection between professionals and the dyad (person in multi-morbidity - family caregiver). 2) The care encounter and having sufficient encounters, although some issues remain unresolved, relationship with other health professionals during care transitions. 3) Results achieved by navigating the health system; autonomy and availability considering the health system's shortcomings; memory of the case management process. The results reveal that case management facilitates trust between users and nursing staff (Askerud & Conder, 2017), which is fostered by timely administrative management. This is an opportunity for nursing therapeutics and negotiation in health self-management processes. Caregivers identified real benefits and saw in this intervention an alternative for the Colombian health system to recognize their role in maintaining the life and health of chronically ill patients. The present study is the first in Colombia to outline the role of advanced practice in nursing, which has been recognized by health authorities and insurers and will provide guidelines for playing a role that had not been considered in national nursing regulations
