# Maturity Reflects children in the process of restoring rights in a boarding school in the city of Bogotá.

This research recognizes maturity as part of motor development and significant factor in the occupational participation of boys and girls; through processes of permanent, continuous and dynamic interaction with the environment. Consequently, in the face of adverse interactions, such as physical, psychological or neglect violence, they can result in effects on neurodevelopment, both in neuroanatomical structures (Rufo, 2006), as in neurophysiological conductivity (Mesa-Gresa & Moya-Albiol, 2011).
