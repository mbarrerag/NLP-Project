# PHYSIOLOGICAL REACTION TO BLOOD PARASITIONS IN ANPHIBIOS

Amphibian populations are declining worldwide due to well-known causes such as climate change and infection with Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis. However, there are other common agents, such as hemoparasites, whose consequences have been poorly studied. This study sought to analyze the frequency of erythroblasts and erythrocytic mitosis with haemoparasites infection. They were captured and sampled by puncture of the brachial vein or cardiac puncture, 116 amphibians (31 species), in six locations of Colombia. They were performed spread of peripheral blood and dyed with Giemsa. Subsequently, blood parasites were detected, parasitemia was quantified and, also, the infected amphibians had the greatest number of erythroblasts and erythrocyte mitosis. The parasites found were also Trypanosoma, Hepatozoon, Dactylosoma, Karyosus-like and microfilarias.
