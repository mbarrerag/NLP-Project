# Voices around the hill: understanding the cultural landscape of Portachuelo from its inhabitants, knowledge and everyday artifacts

The indigenous community of Portachuelo is located in the northwest of the department of Caldas, in the middle of a complex area, inhabited by various cultural groups and in constant territorial dispute. The aim of this research is to understand how a cultural landscape is constituted in the midst of these overlapping territories. The landscape is understood here as a spatial unit socially produced by a web of multiscale relationships, prone to be read from its elements and interactions (habitat); from there the conceptual device house-inhabitant-artifact (C-H-A) is constructed to sharpen the gaze on the house, which is the epicenter and daily container of habits, modes and routines, which are the same things that make life possible and create the landscape. This research methodologically proposes the constitution of the landscape as a horizontal dialogue between the inhabitants and the researcher, as the drawing of the relationships to which value and vitality are given from the domestic-community, and concludes that the narrated system of relationships, with its nodes, actions, artifacts and meanings, ends up constituting the cultural landscape of Portachuelo, constantly disputed with external pressures and defended by its inhabitants by enunciating and reproducing itself in everyday life. Thus, this research seeks to add to the narratives of the institutional landscape other heterogeneous, daily, local and small-scale voices, whose dissemination has been overshadowed by the great discourses of heritage. .
