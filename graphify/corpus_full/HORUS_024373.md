# Spatial-temporal variability of the Atmospheric Limit Layer in the Aborra Valley: characterization, processes, multi-scale interactions and impacts.

The Meteorology of the Limit Layer refers to the theoretical, numerical and experimental framework that focuses on studying the interactions between the terrestrial surface and the low troposphere. These interactions are fundamental for many applications in various spatial-temporal scales. A better understanding of these interactions involves the study of the structure and variability of the Atmospheric Limit Layer as a result of processes that are best described in the field of a combination of fluid and thermodynamic dynamics. By definition, the Limit Layer is turbulent, which means that the processes of mixing and exchange of energy, momentum and scalar, from the Superficial Layer to the rest of the atmosphere, depend on the turbulence, both thermal and mechanical. In this sense, the physical processes of small scale described by the terms in the equation of balance of the turbulent energy modulates the image of the turbulent energy, to a wide variety of phenomena on a larger scale.
