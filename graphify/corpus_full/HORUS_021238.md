# The Exotic Positron Link

Multiple experimental techniques developed over the last 30 years have allowed production and handling antiparticle as positrons to extremely low energies [1-5]. Experimental advances related to the interaction of these particles with atoms and molecules have created a new science that involves physics and chemistry [3-6]. Some relevant results show the formation of the electron-positron pair, also known as positronium (Ps), the Ps2 molecule, and the union of positrons to atoms and molecules[6].One of the most fascinating aspects of the interaction matter-antimatter for chemistry is the formation of positronic compounds, i.e., of compounds that trap positrons energy-stable species. It is noteworthy that the compounds formed are kinetically unstable due to the annihilation of pairs, which makes it difficult to manipulate and detect two positronic molecules that have been detected with average life times inferior to nanoseconds [6]. The energy stability of these compounds can be predicted with precise computational methods. This year we report the energy stability of the molecule of 2e+[H33-] in which three hydrides are stablely linked with two positrons by means of the formation of a link of two positrons and three centers [14]. There are many questions that arise from this unusual union. These have to do with the nature of the link, in particular if it has a similar counterpart in the world of electronic links. On the other hand, the question arises of how many more anionic systems can be joined by means of positronic links. The computational study of electronic-positronic systems can be performed with quantum chemistry computational packages that implement multicomponent methods, that is, that they can solve the Schrödinger equation independent of time for systems that contain electrons and positrons [13]. In addition, these packages must implement levels of higher theory to the Hartree-Fock, since it is necessary to give an adequate description to the electron-positron correlation.
