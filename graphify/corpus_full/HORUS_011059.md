# Analysis of the influence of precipitation and the conditions of humidity prior to formation of the hydrogram during storm events in the Valley of Aburra.

The hydrologic response (HR) of the channels to precipitation events, is reported as a process of important variability due to the multiple and complex controlling factors that influence it. This work explores such variability in the sub-basins of the Aburra valley (AV) with a low humidity, focusing on the two factors with a higher prevalence of the HR at an event scale: detonating precipitation and the humidity conditions background. For this purpose, hydrological information of the level and precipitation of the radar was compared, with a short temporal resolution, at 58 sub-basins within the study area. In turn, this information was contrasted with the main physical characteristics of the sub-basins. We also analyzed the records of the first humidity network of the soil (SM) in-situ of the area, which included the 7 sub-basins of the AV, compared with the information of precipitation and increasing events.The analysis of the events in the AV was carried out, exposed a growing relationship between the first magnitude of the accumulated precipitation and the maximum level reached by the increasing ones.The study suggests that the manifestation of this relationship is susceptible to the size of the sample of events and increasing. controlling factors for HR, precipitation and previous humidity conditions in the sub-basins of the Abura Valley. These suggest that monitoring both factors is potentially useful for understanding and early warning of growing events in the study area
