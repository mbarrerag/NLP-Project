# Institutional response to the emergence of neo-paramilitarism and armed groups organized since 2002 based on the study of national public security and defence policy

To analyze the neo-paramilitarism and the response that the Colombian institutionality has given to this phenomenon turns out to be a rather complex challenge, since it implies referring to the origins of paramilitarism in Colombia and to the definition of the characteristics that give account of this new generation, a reconfiguration based on its founding principles but oriented on a criminal logic based on illicit economies and on the correlation of questionable political dynamics in the territories.The present analysis seeks to address those origins and current characteristics, as well as the way in which the governments from Álvaro Uribe to Iván Duque responded in their political actions, this under the magnifying of an institutionalist and neo-institutionalist reading of public policies, demonstrating the role of formal, informal and those that have been called “illegal” institutions, to account not only for the adaptation and normative relevance to the phenomenon, the transformation and institutional reform, the action from the public forces, and the capacity of governance through the analysis of interinstitutional cooperation and confrontation at the different levels of public or territorial administration, but going further as the approach of social, cultural relations and in the emergence of other phenomena (the first-party and institutional phenomenon). highlights the role of formal, informal, and so-called "illegal" institutions to account not only for the adaptation and normative relief to the phenomenon, institutional transformation and reform, the action of the Public Force, and governance capacity through the analysis of cooperation and inter-institutional confrontation at various levels of public or territorial administration but also going beyond, addressing social and cultural relations and the emergence of other phenomena inherent to the notion of neo-paramilitarism
