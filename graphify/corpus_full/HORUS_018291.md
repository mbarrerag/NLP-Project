# Uncertainty of outcome hypothesis in professional football: A scouting review

This article reviews the empirical studies that tested the hypothesis of uncertainty in the outcome proposed by Rottenberg (1956) in the European professional football attendees. First, the theoretical aspects of the uncertainty hypothesis in the result, the uncertainty measures of match and championship results, and their precedents in European football are reviewed. In addition, eight empirical articles selected by the scope review model are examined and compared according to the variables and shared results. This paper analyzes the research carried out in different leagues of English football, the German Bundesliga, the Spanish league, the Serie A (Italian league) and the Swiss and Austrian leagues. The analysis of this selection concluded that most studies reject the hypothesis of uncertainty of the results of the matches and accept the uncertainty of the championship results. However, it does not represent a generalizable conclusion due to the sample size
