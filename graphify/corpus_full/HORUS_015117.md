# REPEANING ACCOUNTABILITY; THE EMONCIPATORY PERSPECTIVE

Accounting was seriously questioned in the 1980s and 1990s of the 20th century (Tinker, 1984, 1988; Lehman and Tinker, 1987; Richardson, 1987; Gallofer and Haslam, 1991), because discipline was isolated from a political conception. Tinker (1984, 1985) proposed emancipatory accounting. Currently, a consolidated movement of accounting researchers postulates a change in accounting and society. Accounting can improve social condition (Gallofer, 2003). With counteraccounts (Boiral, 2013, 2016; Gallhofer, Haslam, Monk, & Roberts, 2006) or expressions such as external accounting, new accounts, shadow accounts or silent accounts, enabling accounting, dialogical accounting, attempts are made to reposition accounting (Apostol, 2010; Brown & Dillard, 2015; sonja gallhofer & haslam, 2003; R. Gray et al., 2014; Lehman et al., 2016; Thomson et al., 2010; Vinnari & Laine, 2017). We explore in Rafael Franco an emancipatory perspective of Latin American accounting, afore critical thought
