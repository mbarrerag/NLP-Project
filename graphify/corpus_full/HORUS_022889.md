# A SPLINE MODEL FOR EXPLANING THE EVOLUTION OF THE MANDIBULAR PLANNING ANGULE IN CHILDREN AND CHILDREN OF THE COHORTE CELPH-DAMASS

Studying craniofacial growth in individuals is essential in many areas of knowledge and human work such as anthropology, biology and orthodontics, among others. Generally, measurements with craniofacial characteristics are recorded longitudinally at certain time intervals. Predictors such as direction, speed, and acceleration are important to understand the nature of craniofacial growth according to the gender and age of each individual. When data are longitudinal type, mixed linear models are suggested since they are useful and precise tools to generate quality knowledge. This knowledge is significant for decision making, especially in the fields of orthodontics and dental occlusion where excellent results are sought during a treatment. Polynomials with random coefficients have demonstrated good performance in modelling of cranial growth data in population with normal facial traits without history of orthodontic treatment. For this reason, the purpose of this work is to model and justify the implementation of two models.
