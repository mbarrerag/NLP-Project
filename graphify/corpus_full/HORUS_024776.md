# TRANSCRIPTIONAL CONTROL OF RESIN MONOMER-INDUCED MATRIX METALLOPROTEINASES EXPRESSION BY POLYPHENOLS

The degradation of the dentinal organic matrix in the resin-dentin interface has been associated with MMP-2 and MMP-9 in situ activity. The role of dental pulp cells in the secretion of MMPs as response to oxidative stress produced by contact with resin monomers has not yet been elucidated. Polyphenols have been demonstrated to have antioxidant, crosslinking, and MMP inhibition properties, useful for preventing extracellular matrix degradation. To evaluate the effect of two polyphenolic flavonoids on the reactive oxygen species (ROS)-induced MMP expression by exposure to triethylene-glycol-dimethacrylate (TEGDMA) in human odontoblast-like cells (hOLCs). Differentiated hOLCs from mesenchymal stem cells of dental pulp were exposed to TEGDMA for 18, 24, and 36 h. TEGDMA-induced intracellular oxidation and flavonoids¿ antioxidative potential were evaluated using the 2',7'-dichlorofluorescein probe, and RT-qPCR was used to determine the effect of 6.5% proanthocyanidin (PAC) and 150 μM Myricetin (MYR) on the MMP-2 and MMP-9 expression. The extracellular MMP concentration was quantified using ELISA, and their activation by means of fluorometric assay. The results were analyzed by one-way ANOVA and post-hoc Fisher's Least Significant Difference Test, p<0.05. An increase of intracellular ROS and upregulation of MMP-2 and MMP-9 expression were demonstrated in hOLCs exposed to TEGDMA (p<0.001). MMP zymogens and active forms increased significantly after a 24-h TEGDMA exposure (p<0.005). These secreted proteases showed a high activation ability. Flavonoids modulated MMP expression at the transcriptional level. Additionally, flavonoids were able to decrease the activity of secreted MMPs. A relationship between TEGDMA-induced ROS increase and expression, secretion, and early activation of MMPs was observed. However, PAC and MYR were able to perform a transcriptional control on MMPs expression. Therefore, the use of PAC and MYR is proposed as a therapeutic strategy for improving the adhesive interface stability
