# Plants and humans in Plaza Samper Mendoza in Bogotá, a geography of meetings

The Plaza Samper Mendoza de Bogotá is a night market dedicated exclusively to the trade of plants. The relations between the humans and the plants of the Plaza demand a type of understanding that defies the limits of what conventionally we understand by nature and culture and another series of modern bifurcations such as that separating the body from the mind, the theory of practice, the subjects and the objects of knowledge, and the science of politics. Inspired by the geographies interested in the discussions of the most-that-human in close dialogue with the Studies of Science and Technology, I propose a geography in which the concept of encounter allows me to follow the relations between humans and plants of the Plaza Samper Mendoza as the space of excess. Through an ethnography of the affections, understood as a knowledge located, this work analyzes the practices in which human and plant are interwoven in a constitutive relationship. The body of the top-plantation and the plants takes the form of tactile encounters of bodies more than human beings that, as connecting events, make the Plaza a top-plantation space. Ween human and plants at the Plaza unfold. Through an ethnography of effects, defined as situationed knowledge, this research examines the practices through which humans and plants intertwine in a shared becoming. I argue that effective processes between humans and plants create tactics accounts that give rise to more-than-human bodies. These human-plant bodies are events of connection that make the Plaza a topological space of changing borders. The body, as the topological space of human-plant computers, is a relationship shaped by difference. Ultimately, these elements contribute to making the Plaza a space for multipleity where knowledge is a spatial practice, giving risk to a political of contact
