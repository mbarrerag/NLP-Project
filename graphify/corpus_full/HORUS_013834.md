# Characterization of a material composed of epoxy matrix with residues of vegetable ivory from the palm nut of Tagua (Phytelephas macrocarpa)

In recent years significant advances are being made in the development of new materials, and research into composite materials is taking force through the use of recycled products, this in order to improve the performance in mechanical and thermal properties, by means of homogeneous dispersion of particles in a polar, non-polar, thermofix or thermoplastic matrix, or polymer mixture. Many of these studies have been developed through polymers mixing processes with lignocellulosic fibers or materials, with the aim of obtaining new materials with modified mechanical properties that are desirable and that improve the cost-benefit ratio, besides seeking to contribute to the environment, using residues from other processes to give them an added value. Among the most studied mixtures with lignocellulosic materials are the matrices of the thermoplastic systems (polyethylene and polypropylene), where different fibers have been used as hemp, yute, linen, among others, and particles as the a sawdust that come from different trees and have had an important acceptance as new products in different applications. On the other hand, the epoxy systems have a wide trajectories as a product of engineering in multiple applications. 88% by weight of waste compared to the initial seed. These residues are not currently used for a specific purpose, and are mostly discarded, increasing the amount of organic waste that are thrown into the sanitary fillers. The main chemical characteristics of Tagua as mature seed are associated with the composition: cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin. The main component of the cell wall of the endosperm of the seed is manane (mannan), polysaccharide of the mannose with 70% (hemocellulose), 7.5% cellulose and the remaining percentage of unknown nature (may be petticolic or lignin material). This polysaccharide is insoluble in water and very dense, associated with its crystallinity, which makes this product known as vegetable ivory.
