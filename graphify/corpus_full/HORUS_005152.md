# British and Russian Nervism: A Comparison Between Two Trends in the History of Medicine Focused on the Nervous System

Abstract The history of physiology experienced a division in the nineteenth century into two currents that were little known: the analytical current and synthetic or nervism, whose origin is in the West and Russia, respectively. These maintained a contemporaneously marked conceptual fence around the nervous system despite its different origins. The synthetic current was also called nervism due to its conceptual axis that held that the nervous system was the coordinator of all the physiopathological states of the organism.
