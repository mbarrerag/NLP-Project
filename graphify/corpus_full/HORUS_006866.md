# Characterization and valuation of the construction technique in wood, by studying housing in the San Juan, Chocó, to promote its conservation

The Colombian Pacific Region has been the object of generalized state abandonment, promoting complex living conditions that affect the well-being and coverage of the basic needs of its inhabitants. The impossibility of accessing drinking water, the precarious health and safety of the constructions, the risk of flooding and pollution of the rivers and ecosystems, put at risk the habitability, permanence and pervivence of the communities. Despite this, the region shows a great cultural and environmental richness that is maintained through music, dance, gastronomy, architecture and inherited knowledge. In particular, the architecture of the Pacific Region of wood possesses a great value due to its balanced relationship with the territory, the use of the material resources of the place and the transmission of knowledge that guarantee the conservation of traditional constructive techniques. In addition, the use of wood cultivated in a responsible way can mitigate the forest effects through a concrete plan of reforestation or payment for environmental services (PSA).
