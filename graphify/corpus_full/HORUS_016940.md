# Obtaining a chemometric tool for sensory classification of a fragrance.

The cosmetic and fragrance industry employs sensory and instrumental analyses to declare the conformity of a product or to describe it sensoryly. In this industry, sensory analysis is an indispensable tool for decision making. This analysis constitutes a multidisciplinary science in which different areas of knowledge are involved such as chemistry, physics, mathematics, statistics, among others. The purpose of sensory analysis is to be able to translate the perceptions evoked by products through the use of the senses. On the other hand, the sensory analysis requires panelists, adequate facilities, established evaluation processes, of human resource capable, among others. The high number of variables makes defining the sensory profile of a fragrance costly in terms of time, money and human resources. Then, it is necessary to reduce, replace or refine the sensory analysis of fragrances, using the instrumental techniques that facilitate the prediction of the sensory profile of a fragrance. To achieve this objective, ten fragrances of the FLORAL family of the active portfolio of SYMRISE Ltda were selected. and were evaluated sensoryly by a trained panel. After this analysis was applied statistical data obtained by the sensory panel and the functional analysis with the objective of the objective of the functional data., corroborating the fact that each fragrance is unique sensoryly. Because the olfactory active compounds identified by GC-O were not common among the study fragrances, it was not possible to correlate the data. However, these results cannot be used to generate a new matrix of sensory and instrumental correlation in a multivariate analysis. Another important aspect is the fact that the sensory notes of the olfactory active compounds identified by GC-O do not have direct correlation with the sensory profiles of each fragrance. This is because chemical compounds alone do not define the sensory profile of the fragrance. On the contrary, the defined mixtures of some of these compounds could be those who define the specific sensory profiles of each fragrance. Although the GC-O analysis does not predict sensory behavior, it could be used to declare conformity in the quality control area by using these olfactorically active compounds as sensory markers
