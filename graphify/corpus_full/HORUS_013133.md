# Use of soil as an indicator of landscape sensitivity in the Doña Juana Volcanic Complex (CVDJ), Colombia

The coupled soil-landscape system is complex and usually the approaches for its study are based on reductionist models that, while they are effective, do not address the interrelations between components of the system that regulate their behavior. This proposal complements the concept of threat, and seeks to describe the development of soil as a result of the interaction of erosion and deposition processes that compete to shape the landscape. This is achieved by the construction of a conceptual pedological model that explains the functions between components and a dynamic feedback model, carried out in Vensim ® software, which allows the simulation of dynamic systems. In the first chapter, the conception of the pedological model is described and responds to the objective of relating the properties of the soil to the position of the landscape. In the second chapter, the relationships of components and dynamic simulation are presented to assess sensitivity. It is identified that the behaviour of soil development, versus the position of the landscape, is asymptotically unstable and potentially chaotic, which explains the variability of the taxonomic subgroups of soil described in the pilot area, the Quebrada Humad. ® software, that allows the simulation of dynamic systems.
