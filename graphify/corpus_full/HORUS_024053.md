# CHALLENGES FOR CONVECTION-PERMITTING MODELS OVER SOUTH AMERICA

South America constitutes a fascinating hydro-climatic laboratory given its location, extent and its extraordinary suite of geographical features, life zones and ecosystems¿ diversity, which include the rainiest (Chocó, Colombia) and the driest (Atacama, Chile) regions on Earth, as well as the Amazon, Orinoco, and La Plata river basins, and singular regions such as Patagonia, Gran Chaco, northeast Brazil, and South-Eastern South America, surrounded by the Pacific and the Atlantic Oceans, amid the presence of the Andes, the longest mountain range in the world. The first part of this presentation will focus on the main controlling mechanisms of hydro-climatic variability of South America at annual, interannual and intra-annual timescales including the Intertropical Convergence Zone, the South Atlantic Convergence Zone, the role of diverse atmospheric low-level jets (Caribbean, Chocó, Orinoco, and South American), aerial and atmospheric rivers, and the dynamics of Mesoscale Convective Systems, all of which are affected by both phases of El Niño-Southern Oscillation and the Intra-Seasonal Oscillation. The second part will focus on the (mostly overlooked) important role of land surface-atmosphere interactions on South America's hydroclimate variability, with emphasis in tropical South America and the Andes, at a wide range of temporal scales from the diurnal cycle to the interannual. Observational evidences will be presented from diverse hydroclimatic variables including 3D rainfall, evapotranspiration, soil moisture and river flows, all of which constitute fundamental ground-truth to test the skills and capabilities of high-resolution and convection-permitting hydroclimatic models over the continent
