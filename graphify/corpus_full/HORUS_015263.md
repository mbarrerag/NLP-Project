# NATURAL COLORANT FROM COLOMBIAN BEE POLLEN: STABILITY AND SHELF LIFE

The development of ingredients with functional and sustainable qualities is an important stage in the evolution of new products; consumer trends are changing rapidly according to changes in society and the planet. Therefore, during the product development process, it is essential to evaluate stability and shelf life, which will allow determining its quality characteristics to be included in different food matrices. Furthermore, new alternatives to natural colorants are needed due to the growing concern about the adverse effects of synthetic products. Bee pollen, a product of the hive and innovative matrix, can be used as a source of natural pigments to obtain an acceptable replacement for artificial colorants and increase the added value of this natural coloring food. In this work, the evaluation of the stability and the determination of the useful life of a coloring extract rich in carotenoids (ERC) in an oil base was carried out. For stability, the content of total carotenoids (TCC) was evaluated by spectrophotometry, lipid oxidation indices (peroxides, p-anisidine, Totox), and color by tristimulus colorimetry for 90 days at (30°C ± 1°C), (45°C ± 1°C), and (60°C ± 1°C). Furthermore, the valuable shelf life was determined by defining the reaction order and using the Arrhenius relation. The results show that the color remains stable during the 90 days of evaluation while the CCT decreases, observing a trend of greater loss as the temperature increases; and the peroxide index increases, according to the food deterioration process. Furthermore, it was observed that despite presenting the same degradation trends as other enriched oils, it was carried out to a lesser extent, which confirms the antioxidant potential of the compounds extracted from bee pollen. The kinetics of degradation of the ERC tone had a zero-reaction order. It was considered an average temperature of Bogotá (20°C± 5°C) and a critical value for the tone of 70, the shelf life of 37 months was found, which is given for storage conditions in amber glass bottles. These results demonstrated the efficiency of bee pollen carotenoids in combination with oil lipids in color preservation
