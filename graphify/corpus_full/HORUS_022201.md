# Evaluation of caffeine permeation from commercial cosmetic products with a lipolitical action using in vitro tests in Franz diffusion cells.

In the Colombian market cosmetics are marketed with lipolitic proclamations that present in their caffeine ingredients, a compound widely used by its action on the mechanisms of fat accumulation in the adipocite. To comply with the promised cosmetic effect, the ingredient should be able to permeate to the hypodermis where the adipose cells are found, however, this would also imply that caffeine present in these products could reach the systemic fluid, causing contravention with the definition of cosmetics according to national legislation. In this sense, seven products of the Colombian market with lipolitical proclamations containing caffeine were selected, with the aim of collecting information on the concentration of caffeine that could reach the hypodermis and/or the systemic fluid when applied to the skin. The methodological development included, the realization of in vitro tests in Franz diffusion cells with two different thicknesses of membrane, one with hypodermic tissue and one without it, determining the permeative concentration under a dose application under conditions of use. Likewise, the physical and chemical characterization of the products chosen in terms of pH, reologic parameters, microstructure, sensory perception and formula were established. formulate a product that allows the delivery of the functional ingredient in the target layer, and the value of implementing this type of in vitro tools to contribute to information related to safety and efficacy, with a view to strengthening cosmethigincia programs
