# BUILDING THE SOUND-ATMOSFERA-VEGETATION MESS IN THE COELL RIVER FULL

The soil-atmosphere-vegetation models (SVATs), by allowing to pair atmospheric and surface water and energy flows, are an excellent tool to analyze impacts for example of changes in climate and soil coverage on water resources in a basin. This study describes the rigorous implementation of the Canadian SVAT MESH in the Coello river basin, seeking to understand why the model has a limited performance simulating daily flows in high mountain tropical Andean basins, as reported in previous studies. To this end, a review of the components of the model's water balance was carried out, comparing their magnitude and distribution with observed data. Then five different scenarios were implemented in which these components of the model were adjusted. The results were compared with reanalysis products of the variables evaporation and precipitation and the flows were evaluated by means of the model of the water flow metric and the curve of daily flow flow. Among the main findings, it was found that the model could improve the flow simulation by means of adjustments in the magnitude of the precipitation and in the evapotranspiration.
