# Study of the influence of silica/alumimin ratio on some traditional ceramic properties

The fracture mechanics in ceramics is a field widely studied due to their inherent fragility. Ceramics, characterized by their high hardness, wear resistance and thermal stability, present significant challenges in terms of their behavior under tension, due to their tendency to fracture in a catastrophic way. Factors such as microstructure, size and distribution of grains, the presence of defects such as porosity and load conditions influence the resistance to fracture of these materials. In this work, mechanical tremo properties such as resistance to thermal shock and tenacity to fracture were evaluated, as a function of the silica/aluminous relationship, determined from the change in the dosage of raw materials in the formulation of the paste. Chemical and mineralogical characterization techniques (FRX and DRX) and nanoindentation tests to measure the tenacity to the fracture, as well as cycles of heating and cooling to evaluate resistance to thermal shock were better in both properties. resistance and fracture depth were evaluated based on the silica/alumin ratio, determined by adjusting the raw material composition in the past formulation. Chemical and mineralological characterization techniques (XRF and XRD) were used, through with nanoindentation tests to measure fracture depth, and thermal cycling to assist the thermal shock resistance. The past with the best response in both properties had a stichiometric ratio of 3.01, evidenceing mechanisms of strength enhancement due to the formation of microcracks around the grain borders in the matrix, which can be caused by the introduction of second phases with different thermal expansion coefficients, understanding the propagation of macrocracks by creating their path length
