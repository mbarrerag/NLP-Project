# ASSESSMENT OF HORIZONTAL TRANSFER CAPACITY OF STAFF RESISTANCE DERMINANT FACTORS TO ß-LACTAMIC TWO CLASS 1 INTEGRATIONS IN CLINICAL ISILENCES OF SALMONELLA TYPHIMURIUM IN COLOMBIA

Salmonella enterica, serovar Typhimurium, is a common cause of gastroenteritis transmitted by food, associated with resistance to multiple antibiotics. In Colombia, strains of S. Typhimurium of clinical origin contain class 1 integrations such as IntI1-Col1 and IntI1-Col4, composed of ß-lactam resistance genes and other antibiotics, of which the genetic element that carries them is unknown. Evaluating the horizontal transferability of the IntI1-Col1 and IntI1-Col4 integrators in clinical isolations of S. Typhimurium in Colombia. A representative isolation of the IntI1-Col1 and IntI1-Col4 integrative integrators was selected, and the results were evaluated with a minimum inhibitive concentration of ampicilán greater than 64 μg/ml and greater than 512 μg/ml, respectively. By means of complete sequencing of the genome, an assembly of the novo of the accessory genome with Geneious Prime and BLASTn results were obtained
