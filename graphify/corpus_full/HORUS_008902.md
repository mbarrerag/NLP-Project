# AUTOMATIC LEARNING OF BIOACUSTIC SIGNAL CHARACTERISTICS FOR THE IDENTIFICATION OF SPECIES

The traditional methods of representation of bioacoustic signals for taxonomic studies use descriptors taken from time series or spectral transformation based on Fourier. Such representations require the expertness of researchers when choosing characteristics. Modern systems for automated recognition of audio signals in different applications use data-guided models where characteristics are automatically learned. Character learning is desirable because it requires less a priori knowledge of the phenomenon studied and opens the possibility of encoding important aspects of the signal that are hitherto unknown. However, it presents some difficulties, such as the need for a large amount of data and labels, over-training, and lack of interpretability. This study contrasts some traditional acoustic descriptors with representations obtained through models of neural networks applied to recordings of birdsongs of the Environmental Sounds Collection of the Institute for Research of Biological Resources Alexander von Humboldt, and proposes alternatives of semi-supervised learning and care mechanisms for training of robust systems of extraction of characteristics and with generalization and interpretation capabilities
