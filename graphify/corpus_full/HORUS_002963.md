# DIVERSITY AND QUANTITATIVE RELATIONS BETWEEN THE CASCO FACE OF MULA AND OTHER POBLIES OF CRIULLS

The population of Creole pigs is constituted by local races distributed throughout the American continent and share their ancestral origin with Iberian populations. The phenotypic characterization and comparative studies between Creole breeds are still scarce although the residues are widely used as a food resource.The objective was to determine the degree of phenotypic differentiation existing among some of these races, particularly in the mule hull, based on the qualitative and quantitative analysis of the information generated by its morphological characters.A total of 17 breeds (or populations) were submitted for comparative study by Creole porcines: Altoandino (Peru), Casco de Mula, Cauca, Chocó, Nariño, Sabanero araucano and Sampedreño (Colombia), Ch'ortí (Guatemala), Chino (Cuba), Nueva Guinea (Nicaragua), Pampa Rocha (Uruguay), Pillano, and Zapotillo-Puyango (Ecuador), La Maná and Venezolano (Venezuela).The results of the Iberian races were not included, since the fecotical changes were considered 66±0.18, the extreme values of 0.909 for the Sabanero araucano-Pampa Rocha pair, and 0.03 for the Valencia-Riobamba pair. Ward's dendrogram presented a cophenetic correlation of 0.927, and reflected two large clouds: one that grouped the Pampa-Rocha and the Sampedreño (races of clear hypermetric format), and the rest, in a cloud with various small conglomerates. The Mula Casco is located in a conglomerate together with the Venezuelan and Chinese taxonomy methods. Phenotypic differences found can contribute to the conservation of these criollas races. As a conclusion, this work only attempts to bring new points of view to existing morphological relationships between some breeds of Creole porcines analyzed by means of numerical taxonomy.
