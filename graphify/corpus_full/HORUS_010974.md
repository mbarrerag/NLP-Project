# Identification of mutations in genes associated with hereditary canalopathy by exomic analysis.

The study of sudden cardiac death is very important, given the high incidence worldwide, and in industrialized countries it has become a public health problem. However, the approach of this type of death is not easy, due to the genetic component of predisposition and especially in deaths caused by mutations in genes that encode for proteins that constitute the ion channels (channelopathy). With the discovery in recent years of genes associated with this type of death, molecular studies have been recommended that allow to know what are the molecular bases and the genetic risk factors of sudden cardiac death, with the aim of reaching preventive medicine with an appropriate genetic advice. In Colombia the study of sudden cardiac death has focused on conventional studies of forensic pathology, however, in some deaths it is not easy to diagnose the cause of death and remain in study. This leads to a molecular study, to know the genes and mutations of these genes that help explain the probable cause of sudden death by cardiac canalopathy. However, in some deaths it is not easy to diagnose the cause of cardiac ionic channels and remain in study. This leads to perform a molecular study, to determine the genes and mutations of new generation (NGS)
