# The concept of "Labour Relationship" as an international custom of ILO members in the face of the "unstandardized forms of work" of the Collaborative Economy.

With the aim of identifying the events in which the regulation of labour law should be applied, the International Labour Organization has established the labour relationship as the defining institution to define the scope of application of International Labour Standards in the economic interactions between capital and labour. Labour recruitment models emerging in collaborative economies are constituted as a paradigmatic case that shows the problems of applying International Labour Standards in national States that base the concept of labour relationship on the criterion of subordination. In order to solve this implementation problem, in 2006 the International Labour Conference issued recommendation 198, which defines various indications for establishing the existence of an employment relationship. In the absence of this instrument of a binding character, the principle of economic unit has been used to establish non-subordinate labour relations. Bearing in mind the above, this document aims to study whether the principle of economic unit can be constituted as a general principle of international labour law.
