# Exploration of the intestinal microbiome of Zophobas larvae morio (Coleoptera: Tenebrionidae) associated with an expanded polystyrene diet

Expanded polystyrene (EPS) is a synthetic polymer of common use in different industries and possesses molecular stability that allows it to remain unmodifiable for hundreds of years generating a negative impact on the environment. It has been reported that coleopter larvae of the Tenebrianidae family can biodegradate it thanks to enzymes produced by its intestinal microbiota. In this research, the intestinal microbiome of the larvae of Zophobas morio fed with polystyrene (PS) and oats, using metabarcoding, by means of sequence Illumina Miseq of the 16s region of the RNAr gene was preliminary characterized. With the results of sequencing, the taxonomic composition and relative abundance of bacteria were analyzed, from which it was obtained that the genus Spiroplama was the most abundant in the diets of PS and oats, and no differences were found in the analysis of alpha diversity.
