# MICROMORPHOLOGY AS AN INDICATOR OF SOIL RESTORATION.

Changes in land use are influenced by physical factors and socio-economic dynamics over time, which can sometimes lead to the degradation of natural resources. In Andean regions, the relationship between land use, land degradation, and conservation can be understood through soil indicators. The study was conducted in the La Montaña Mágica - El Poleo Nature Reserve, located near the Serranía de los Yariguíes National Natural Park, located in the municipality of Zapatoca (Santander, Colombia) within the Eastern Cordillera of the Andes. The biome corresponds to a premontane to low montane rainforest life zone with an udic soil moisture regime. The soils are Typic Dystropepts, coarse-loamy, kaolinitic, subactive, and isothermic; they are moderately to poorly drained, ranging from shallow to deep, with high aluminum saturation and low fertility. These soils developed from the Giron geological formation, which is composed of red conglomerates, sandstones, and siltstone layers of Middle Jurassic sedimentation, deposited in an alluvial environment during the break-up and fragmentation of Pangea. Five common land uses in the Colombian Andean zone; natural forest, active restoration, coffee plantations, badlands, and passive restoration, were evaluated to understand the relationship between land uses and their soil characteristics. Morphological changes and increased biological activity were identified in soils under sustainable management systems compared to traditional uses. These changes in soil micromorphology, including porosity, structure, and biological activity patterns, are considered effective indicators for evaluating restoration and management strategies
