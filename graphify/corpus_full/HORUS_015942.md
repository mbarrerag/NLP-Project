# PROGRAMMING OF THE FINITE ELEMENTS APPLICABLE TO STATISTICAL METATIC ANALYSIS OF SOLIDES

Since the decade of the sixties, the method of finite elements has been used to represent the mechanical behavior of solids. Its approach has gradually improved by different constituent models of materials, relations between deformation and displacement and formulations of elements, among other factors. On the other hand, the ability to calculate and the ease of programming of computers has evolved rapidly since then, and currently provide the possibility of performing complex analyses with a low computational cost. The method of finite elements requires undoubtedly the implementation of a computer program to solve problems with an acceptable level of precision. Therefore, the job of programming is important to understand this numerical technique. The programming languages interpreted or high level facilitate that task sacrificing a little efficiency. GNU-Octave and Matlab are languages of this type of graphical computation, prepared with useful numerical calculation functions for analysis with matrices. The generation of a finite elements mesh associated with the border conditions, the external actions and mechanical properties of the materials is a process that requires knowledge in graphical computation. After the process of calculation, the results and the objective is also necessary to represent the application of the grid of the terminal. In addition, the relationship between the components of deformation and displacement is defined by the theory of infinitesimal deformation. The problem is formulated and implemented according to the method of finite elements on a three-dimensional space. Likewise, it is solved in a simplified way in one-dimensional and two-dimensional domains. This book is aimed at undergraduate and postgraduate students in civil engineering and mechanical engineering, interested in the field of computational mechanics of solids. Its understanding requires a basic knowledge in solid mechanics and can be supported by texts on the method of finite elements to deepen their concepts. If the reader is familiar with structured computer programming and the interpreted language of GNU-Octave or Matlab, he will be facilitated with respect to numerical implementation of the method. In some chapters of the book, the reader could be trained in the generation of finite element meshes for two-dimensional and three-dimensional problems through the GMSH program. He could also prepare to represent the graphical results of each simulation used by the reader
