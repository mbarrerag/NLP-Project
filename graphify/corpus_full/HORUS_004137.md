# UPDATE OF STRUCTURAL DESIGN SEISMIC COEFFICIENTS FOR THE COLOMBIAN BUILDING CODE (NSR)

In the past, the association of the design coefficients to an established return period has led to multiple misinterpretations regarding the level of safety provided in structural design to future buildings. Design earthquakes do not have an associated return period. This misconception has led to associating the security level with a certain return period, which is incorrect and inconsistent with engineering practice. A new proposal has been made by the Colombian Association of Earthquake Engineering (AIS) for seismic design coefficients developed by the AIS-300 committee for updating the Colombian Seismic-Resistant Construction Norm (NSR). This proposal addresses the concepts of optimal design coefficients, a methodological basis for design movements, that has been considered by AIS as an alternative to the current methodology used in the United States, known as Risk Targeted Ground Motion (RTGM). Although the RTGM methodology is based on performance engineering concepts, which is always desirable, in practice the procedure used to determine it is highly arbitrary and difficult to justify, as is the attempt to meet a previous explicit performance objective for the building design itself. In contrast, the model of optimal design coefficients, devised by Luis Esteva to be used in Mexico, almost at the same time that he devised the probabilistic seismic hazard analysis, provides a way to improve the best design coefficients, allowing to consider aspects as relevant as rates of exceedance of accelerations, the initial investment (approximately), or the socioeconomic context in which the earthquakes occur. The criteria used in the application of the total cost optimization model are herein detailed to determine design spectrums unrelated to any return period. This innovative proposal of design spectra allows the NSR to move towards more accurate details of the design movement and also, with a greater approximation, to the realities of the country. The article includes a comparison between the new spectral shapes and the existing ones specified then in the NSR, providing valuable insights into the proposed enhancements
