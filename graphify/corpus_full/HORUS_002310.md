# High Andean corn climate variability scenarios

The climate variability represents a considerable risk for food security, since it can affect both yield and quality of crops. The main abiotic factors that limit crop productivity and food security due to climate variability are drought, negative and thermal stress. These conditions can also modify the relationship between plants and herbivorous arthropods. The objective of this project is to investigate the effect of drought stress, flooding and high daytime temperatures on the physiology, biochemistry and agronomic identification of the genotypes of maize (Zea mays L.) high-andean soil and its interaction with the arthropods crop pest. In this project three interdisciplinary experiments will be carried out: the first experiment will evaluate the interaction of stress due to drought and high daytime temperatures and its effect on the physiology of the genotypes of high-andine maize.
