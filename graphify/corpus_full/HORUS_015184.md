# Biomass use as an additive for the production of polymeric lactic acid (PLA) and polyethylene (PE) base film for fruit conservation.

For decades the fruit packaging industry has been led by the use of high density polyethylene (HDPE) due to its high mechanical strength, chemical resistance, thermal resistance and hydrophobicity.High global requirements in terms of food conservation and transport, as well as an environmental medium, have led this industry to be developed in two areas of research: the first in the improvement of current materials in order to generate packaging for food with new capacities such as intelligent packaging or with modified atmosphere; and on the other hand the development and transition of non-biodegradable materials to renewable polymeric matrices such as lactic polyacid (PLA). Taking into account the above, this project presents the development of two promising materials for use as fruit packaging, one of HDPE base with antifungal properties, and another corresponds to a PLA matrix reinforced with acetylated cellulose. In both cases, the polymeric matrices include naturally-resided biomass. Due to the recognized feature of the biodegradable polymers, the PLA films developed include the cellulose and its acetyls (cell-degraded) as well as a derivative. The latter, a well-known antimicrobial agent against various plant protection problems of tropical fruits, especially those belonging to Colletatrichum tamarilloi, and which in our previous work (using pure commercial lauric acid) has shown that it lengthens the shelf life of tree tomatoes in two weeks. This HCO product, without any further purification, was used as an additive in HDPE during the extrusion process, obtaining 84 μm thick films whose thermal, mechanical and permeability properties were analyzed. In vitro and in vivo studies showed that HDPE film with HCO product at 5 %w/w has antifungal properties before the fungus C. tamarilloi and this packaging increases the useful life of tree tomatoes by up to three weeks
