# VALORIZATION OF USED VEGETABLE OILS BY CATALYTIC EPOXIDATION USING FUNCTIONALIZED HETEROPOLYACIDS

In recent years, the chemical industry has managed to incorporate different renewable raw materials generally obtained from cropping or forestry activities [1,2]. However, the agricultural activity required to harvest such industrial raw materials involves a large use of arable land, a displacement of food crops, and a large consumption of water and fossil resources. In particular, the oleochemical industry is highly affected by this phenomenon because it mainly relies on first-generation oils and fats that are diverted from food/feed applications. For this reason, there is an urgent need to incorporate waste and second-generation lipids as oleochemical feedstocks. Currently, different residual lipids are wasted and their potential value is lost, including used vegetables oils (UVOs), yellow and brown greases, rendering fats, tall oil, acidified soap stocks, mill effluents, and high-fat content food wastes, among others. The exploitation of such waste lipids via circular economy approaches might help developing more sustainable oleochemical products. In particular, UVOs are of special interest because they are generated in large volumes and in different sectors. In 2020 [3], the global generation of UVOs was estimated in at least 42 Mt/yr, and this amount is almost equivalent to the entire consumption of edible oils used in the oleochemical industry. UVOs have similar properties to crude vegetable oils, but as their nutritional value was already exploited, they can be considered a second-generation feedstock [4]. Currently, recovered UVOs are mainly used in the production of biofuels, mostly in Europe, USA and China. However, this valorization route has limited competitiveness since the economic margin is very low, and it is only justified under public subsidies and promotion policies (e.g., double counting mechanism). In this regard, there is need to develop high value-added oleochemical derivatives that can be self-sustained without subsidized schemes, and that can be incorporated in large scale markets thus ensuring a long-term sustainability of the valorization schemes. Among the different alternatives, the production of polymer additives and monomers would generate high value-added opportunities for the exploitation of UVOs. In particular, biobased epoxides are among the biopolymers of major consumption growth worldwide [5]. Epoxidation of vegetable oils is normally carried out via percarboxylic route with sulfuric acid as catalyst, allowing high conversions but also promoting oxirane ring degradation and the polymerization of the product, thus leading to low selectivity [6,7]. Industrially, in-situ formation of the percarboxylic acid and homogeneous catalysts are preferred to avoid handling of unstable and flammable peroxyacids and to reduce production costs. However, this route induces equipment corrosion and requires a downstream neutralization stage that causes additional waste streams. Thereby, highly selective and more stable heterogeneous catalysts would be preferable to facilitate effective epoxidation of UVOs. In this context, functionalized heteropolyacids (HPA) of the Keggin type were evaluated as catalysts for epoxidation of UVOs. These materials exhibit high thermal and oxidative stability, and have shown good activity and selectivity in the epoxidation of olefins [8]. Advantageously, the epoxidation reaction is carried out without intermediate percarboxylic acid, because HPA fulfills the role of acid catalyst and of a reversible oxidant. For our study, phosphotungstic acid was neutralized with long chain surfactants, namely (CTA)3PW12O40, (TBA)3PW12O40 and (C12mim)3PW12O40 with the aim to adjust its hydrophobicity. These materials were characterized by elemental analysis, XRD, RAMAN and TGA, and subsequently evaluated as catalysts in epoxidation reaction.
