# MODERN USES OF VOLCANIC IMAGE COMPANY WITH ICONOGRAPHY IN ART RUPESTRE, AND THEIR RELATIONSHIP TO THE MITOS IN NARIC, COLOMBIA.

Studying the uses of the volcano image, in modern and ancient times, helps us to understand the appropriation of the volcanic concept, mutualism, and the perception of threats by communities. Hundreds of modern volcanic figurative representations, including works of art, advertising images, decorations, and shields, were analyzed and contrasted with iconography identified in 36 rock art sites, including mainly petroglyphs and some pictographers. Additionally, several direct and indirect allusions to the volcanic phenomenon were identified in 15 mythical narratives. Modern representations can be reduced to a few traces or lines that, although simple, convey the idea of volcano. On the other hand, in the cave art documented to date in Nariño the figurative representations are almost null, so the identification of the idea of volcano in these ancient pieces must be made from the symbolic. Although the pictographers and petroglyphs contain iconographies similar to the modern simplified forms, demonstrating the correspondence between some and others is an image of the Figures that make up the images.
