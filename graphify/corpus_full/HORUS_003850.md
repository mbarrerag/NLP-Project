# EXAMINING GLOBAL PERSPECTIVES ON PLANT-MOTH BIOTIC INTERACTIONS

Plant-moth biotic interactions play a fundamental role in shaping pollination dynamics and supportingecosystem services across terrestrial environments worldwide. These intricate relationships have profound implicationsfor ecosystem functioning, highlighting the need for comprehensive approaches, robust methodologies, and deepenedunderstanding. In this study, I explore plant-moth biotic interactions from a global scale. I conductedan integrative review of literature on plant-moth biotic interactions, delineating five overarching categories of research: (a)the contributions of moths to plant pollination networks, (b) spatial and temporal variations influencing these interactions,(c) biotic and abiotic factors as covariates, (d) coevolutionary dynamics shaping plant-moth relationships, and (e)international collaborative efforts. Current studies on plant-moth pollination networks often lack comprehensiveniche dimensions, since only a few of them are usually included (e.g., proboscid length). Spatial (e.g., altitudinal andlatitudinal scales) and temporal (e.g., long-term studies) dimensions, as well as their interaction, are frequentlyoverlooked. While ecological perspectives are progressively integrated, the incorporation of conservation andmanagement perspectives, particularly in the context of climate change, remains scarce. Direct measurements for abetter understanding of the coevolutionary dynamics shaping plant-moth relationships are needed. Despite theacknowledged significance of these interactions, international endeavors, such as methodological standardization andcollaborative initiatives, are underexplored. Implications: A conceptual framework is proposed herein to advance theholistic study of plant-moth interactions, along with identification of emerging opportunities in this field. This approachseeks to bridge existing knowledge gaps and foster a more integrated understanding of plant-moth dynamics in changingecosystems
