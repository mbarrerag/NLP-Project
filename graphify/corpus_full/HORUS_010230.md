# Physical processes influence on the dynamics of the main greenhouse gases in mountain tropical reserves.

Tropical reservoirs are recognized as sources of greenhouse gases (GHGs) of global importance. Mountain tropical areas of high hydropower development have been poorly studied. The aim of this study is to understand the dynamics of GHGs in tropical mountain reservoirs. Data were collected on seasonal and daytime GHG dynamics during six field campaigns in the Porce III reservoir in the Colombian Andes, where the importance of CH4 oxy production was demonstrated in the variability of surface dissolved gas, as well as the variation of water levels as an incident factor in seasonally scale GHG flows. The CO2 flow was monitored in the water-atmosphere interface of the reservoir with a high resolution technique over several weeks, where the importance of primary productivity was in the diurn cycle of CO2 flows, showing alternating as a submersor-source, and pulses of the hydrologic flows of the reservoir were observed as a consequence of simultaneous occurrence of superficial concentrations and high wind speed.
