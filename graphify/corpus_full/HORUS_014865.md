# TOWARDS A BARROCO ANALYSIS APPROACH TO PUBLIC POLICIES IN LATIN AMERICA

The introduction of public policy analysis (PPP) in Latin America began in the 1990s in the context of neoliberal reforms. It was made instrumental, uncritical and decontextualized, through neo-rationalist perspectives widely inspired by the economy and marginally by political science. These approaches in Latin America are criticized for their marked cultural or institutional postulates of their places of origin. It is considered urgent to develop, from a critical perspective, new approaches of PPPs that recognize and identify local specificities. We propose here to lay the basis for the development of an APP approach for the Latin American context based on the recognition of a peculiar way of entry to modernity dominated by Baroque culture (XVII-XVIII) We postulate that individual, social, political and institutional behavior is still linked to a Baroque ethos that differs from the rational ethos approach to the dominant weberian context (B. Echeverría) in the countries of the north from where do the dominant theories and concepts in that field come from.
