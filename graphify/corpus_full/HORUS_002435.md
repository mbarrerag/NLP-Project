# POLITICAL IMPLICATIONS OF THE SUDDEN INCREASE IN NEWS COVERAGE OF EARTHQUAKES IN COLOMBIA AND COMMUNITY PERCEPTION OF INSECURITY

The analysis of the community's perception of insecurity in Colombia regarding the sensation that seismic events are currently occurring more frequently is presented. A historical compilation from 2010 was carried out to determine the number of news articles related to seismic events, mainly in El Tiempo and El Espectador, to identify the dynamics of news publication related to such events. A quantitative analysis was conducted on the number of news articles related to seismic events in the print media El Tiempo and El Espectador over the past 10 years. News databases and content analysis tools were employed to gather and analyze the information. Additionally, surveys were conducted to assess the current perception of insecurity among the population and to determine their sources of information. The analysis reveals a notable increase in the number of news articles about seismic events in El Tiempo, but not in El Espectador, since the start of the new presidential term. However, when these data were compared with official historical records of seismic activity from the Colombian Geological Service's seismic catalog, no increase in actual seismic activity was observed. This suggests that people's perception of insecurity might be influenced by factors such as the rapid dissemination of information through social media, the increased volume of related news, and heightened public awareness of seismic risks. The discrepancy between perceived insecurity and actual seismic activity raises questions about the underlying causes of this phenomenon. It is possible that the excessive dissemination of news about seismic events exacerbates public anxiety, even in the absence of an increase in actual seismic activity. Addressing people's perception of insecurity and their belief that seismic events are occurring more frequently is crucial. Both the government and academia have vital roles in educating the public about seismic risks and developing strategies for disaster preparedness and response. Further research is needed to better understand the underlying causes of this perception and its impact on society
