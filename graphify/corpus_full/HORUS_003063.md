# CHARACTERIZATION OF EXTREME TORMENTS IN COLOMBIA. PART II

This study, which constitutes Part II of a research on extreme storms in Colombia, focuses on the characterization of the day cycle of extreme precipitation at the sub-day level, complementing previous work on extreme events in the country. The research uses digitized rainfall data for 70 stations distributed in various Colombian regions. It carried out a spatialization of characteristics such as the time of onset, duration and amount of precipitation, using the Angular Distance Weighting (ADW) method in the SAGA software. The monthly daytime cycle of extreme precipitation was analyzed by calculating the percentage of precipitation per hour and generating Hovmoller graphics to visualize the patterns throughout the year. In addition, the monthly occurrence of extreme storms was examined and the time of occurrence of the maximum precipitation peak with the altitude of the stations was correlated, observing orographic influences in the Andean area. The study also evaluated the impact of the ENSO phenomenon on the daytime precipitation cycle, classifying events in categories Normal, Niño and Niña to identify changes in the magnitudes and patterns of rainfall, finding important effects in the cycle before the occurrence of the under-s, offering of the under-design of the sub-statedial data
