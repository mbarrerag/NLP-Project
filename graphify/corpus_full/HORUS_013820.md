# Agroecology for food change

The National Nutritional Situation Survey (ENSIN) of 2015 reveals that 54.2% of households in Colombia have food insecurity, while 56.4% of the population suffers from excess weight.Although food insecurity has decreased 3.5% since 2010, overweight has increased by an accelerated 5%.These data demonstrate a connection between the two problems.Food security is defined in the Survey as "sufficient and stable availability of food, timely and permanent access and consumption of food in quantity, quality and safety by people, under conditions that allow their adequate biological use to lead a healthy and active life."This suggests that the most accessible foods tend to have low nutritional levels and high caloric values.Agriecological systems integrate both ecological concepts and social innovation.These systems regenerate the ecosystems and intensively use their biodiversity to replace inputs, and rescue ancestral knowledge to research new forms of sustainable production.The application of agroecology increases the socio-economic and environmental resilience of agricultural production processes.The Intergovernmental Committee of Experts on Climate Change has defined the proposed agroecology as a tool for the adaptation of agricultural production, agriculture and food production
