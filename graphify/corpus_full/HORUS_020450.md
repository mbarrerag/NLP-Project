# Use of oleogels as fat substitutes in meat products

The substitution of saturated fat (natural and hydrogenated) and some trans fatty acids in food is a subject of interest due to the demand for healthier foods to avoid the disease related to the consumption of these macromolecules in the long term. This paper presents a review of the literature about the use of oleogels as substitutes for saturated fat in meat products, specifically identifying the materials (oleogellators and oils) used to form them, as well as their physical and chemical and functional characteristics. The oleogels are three-dimensional structures that trap oils and convert them into a solid behavioral structure, are a good alternative as a substitute for saturated fat in food. These structures consist of the dispersion of a structural agent (oil gelator) in a continuous oleo-gelous phase (a liquid oil), forming a three-dimensional network without altering that oil and forming a solid gel material (colloid).
