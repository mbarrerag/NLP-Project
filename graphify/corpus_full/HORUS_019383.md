# Genetic diversity, acoustic signal and geographic distribution of a colourful rain frog of the genus Pristimantis (Anura: Strabomantidae)

Characterising genetic divergence, and documenting phenotypic attributes in species are essential to understanding how evolution works in the context of geographic patterns. Here, we established the phylogenetic relationships of new specimens, estimated the genetic variation and documented vocalisations of Pristimantis jaguensis, a colourful species with little known attributes of its life history to date. We extend its distribution to new localities and add new genetic data. We found high intra-specific genetic divergence reaching a variability up to 7.51%, even within the same population, but without a marked geographic structure at the phylogenetic level, which might suggest recent flow of individuals between populations. Also, from 13 vocalising males, we analyse and describe 231 calls, consisting of a very short note (sometimes two notes), with a duration of 0.068 ± 0.039 s (0.018–0.127 s) and the dominant frequency of the call was 3.647±0.459 kHz (2.483–4.145 kHz), and with notable variation in several acoustic characteristics between populations. In the two northernmost populations, P. jaguensis is the dominant species in the open areas unlike the population from the type locality where the species inhabits the forest interior. The high intra-and inter-population variation found in P. jaguensis emphasises the need for a thorough molecular and acoustical sampling when delimiting new entities and to better understand evolutionary processes across widespread species
