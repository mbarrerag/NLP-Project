# Peasant territoriality: absent in political projects for the East of Antioquia.

This article examines the functionalities of the rural area of the East of Antioquia in the political projects of territorial intervention deployed over the last 20 years, based on a theoretical-methodological approach to territorial studies. To this end, an approach is made to political bets on a supramunicipal scale, to their approaches and to the functionalities assigned to fragments of this geographical space; finally, it emphasizes the changes and tensions produced. In this subregion, which was highly affected by the armed conflict in the 1980s and 1990s, a series of long-term bets are implemented today, which weave multiple forms of territorial cut-off, divisions that do not recognize peasant territoriality or its links with the territory. As a result, overcoming the armed conflict has meant a deepening of a model centered on agro-industry, energy extractivism, ecosystem services and what the subregion offers. This has resulted in fragmentation based on economic functionality, which has the effect of direct or indirect denial of a peasant subject, its practices and knowledge, as well as its ways of intervening in the territory
