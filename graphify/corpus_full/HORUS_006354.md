# Contribution to the study of the role of elastases in the degradation of elastin isolated from human skin

Elastin is a protein of the extracellular matrix that provides elasticity and resilience to tissues. During its half-life (74 years), it is continuously degraded mainly by enzymes called elastases, which is associated with the appearance of signs of skin aging, development of diseases such as aneurysms and atherosclerosis, among others. It is necessary to understand the factors that influence the enzymatic proteolysis of elastin fibers for in the future, to be able to delay or control this process. Twelve elastas belonging to three different families of proteases have been identified in the skin: cystein, serin and metalroproteases. The tissue of origin of elastin and the integrity of the fiber can condition the action of the enzymes; few reports have evaluated the effect of these factors on the elastic potency, and some have found dissimilar behaviors between them.
