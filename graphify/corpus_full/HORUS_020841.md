# Spatial Syntax II: a strategy of social innovation since the diagnosis of educational infrastructure in the Antioquian Urabá.

This proposal arises as effect and scalability of the winning project of the National Call for Solidarity Extension 2021 called _x0093_Syntaxes spatial: methodology of organization, design and spatial signage for the prevention of COVID-19 in educational institutions of the Urabá Antioqueño_x0094_.The aim of this is to implement strategies for the mitigation of risk of contagion in schools and schools from an innovative methodology derived from the theory of spatial syntax.This concept was conceived by Bernard Hillier and Julianne Hanson as a tool for the architects to simulate the possible socio-cultural effects of their designs and make decisions based on behavioral predictions of the population. Taking its meaning as analogy of the linguistic syntax, which studied the relationships of ordering, hierarchy and relationships of the words in sentences or discourses, the spatial syntax is also allowed to analyze the possibilities, restrictions or transformations (the spatial). The analysis carried out in the educational institutions in the municipality of Apartadó, as well as the dissemination processes of the proposal and the recommendations delivered, allowed not only the fulfilment of the objectives of the project, but also the strengthening of relations with the communities and entities that endorsed the initiative, as well as the positioning of the National University of Colombia in the territory. Therefore, this project aims to continue the spatial syntax initiative, but with a goal aimed at the realization of an integral diagnosis of the educational infrastructure, taking advantage of the spatial analyses previously carried out. This diagnosis would be a fundamental complement, since it would not only allow to address the urgent problem of the risk of contagion, but also to think of the postpandemia scenario, which seems to imply an increase in the dropout rates and, therefore, to deluding into the problem of inequality and difficulty of social mobility that has been recurrent in the Antioqueño Urabá. The diagnosis then proposes a multidimensional analysis that allows to think in schools and schools as spaces of social innovation that, beyond the fulfilment of indicators, can be transformed into spaces of inclusion and opportunities for communities
