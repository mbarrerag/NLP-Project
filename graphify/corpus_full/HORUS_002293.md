# Tour and Re-acquaint

This photographic project arises from a constant and sensitive observation of the streets of Bogotá, where, in the middle of the accelerated pace of the city, my gaze frequently rested on people who would travel the roads pushing carts loaded with recycled material.These figures, apparently peripheral to the urban landscape, became for me a key to think of the city from another perspective: the invisible work, the journey as a form of existence and the relationship between body, object and environment. I began to wonder about the experience of those who recycle: what routes do they travel?, how do they choose the materials?, how do they live their day-to-day in the middle of a city that often ignores them? What was initially a look from the distance, we transformed in a deep interest to understand the human dimension, material and symbolic images of their work. In one of the first approaches, I accompanied Don Gilberto, a waste picker who always calls itself, ́ The Good People ́ ́ ́s way. For more than six hours we walk from the 52 near to where I live, and then we will go to the center until the 22th, and always with Caracas, ́s. This technique visually reflects how the past and present are intertwined in each bag collected, in each repeated journey, in each gesture that is repeated but never equal. The image layers work as a metaphor for urban memory and silent work that sustains part of the ecological functioning of the city. In addition, I am interested in reflecting on recycling not only as an environmental practice, but as a way of life: a system of knowledge, relationships and resistance that is woven daily between the hands of those who travel through the city collecting. This visual research aims to contribute to a more conscious, critical and empathetic look at the urban space and its dynamics, visualizing the crucial role of waste pickers as active agents of transformation and care. This work is a photographic installation printed in tul, a translucent material that allows the layers of image to overlap, generating a complex and changing visual reading. Each look reveals something different, as it happens in each journey through the city, which invites the viewer to walk and discover it step by step. Ultimately, this photographic project proposes an exercise of listening and observation. Each look reveals something different, as it does in every walk through the image
