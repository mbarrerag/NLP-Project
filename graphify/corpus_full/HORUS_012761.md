# Extending and compressing multiphases during the cretaceous in the Upper Magdalena base: a source-to-sink analysis.

The extensional and compensional basins can evolve over tens of millions of years through multiple stages under the same tectonic regime. The Cretaceous tectonic evolution of the Colombian Andes is characterized by changes between compressive, neutral and extensional tectonic. The response of the superior plate to these changes is recorded in the Cretaceous sedimentary rocks along the western, central and eastern mountain ranges, as well as in the adjacent basins, including the Upper Valley of Magdalene Basin. In this work we integrate field observations, petrography, geochronology and thermochronology in the Cretaceous sedimentary units preserved in the south of the Upper Valley of Magdalene and in the base adjacent to the basin to assess the origin of these units, the exhumation patterns of the source areas and the response of the sedimentary systems to the tectonic changes, the marginality and the interpretation of the 100 million.
