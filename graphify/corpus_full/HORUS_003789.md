# IMPACT OF SOCIOECONOMIC FACTORS IN COCA CULTIVE TAX IN COLOMBIA BY A BETA RETURN MODEL WITH EXCESSING OF CERES

Colombia has been recognized for decades as the world’s leading coca grower. Therefore, it is important to understand the various factors, both social, economic and environmental, that affect the increase or decrease in the rate of coca crops at the municipal level in the country, especially after the peace agreement with the FARC. This study focuses on analyzing the coca crop rate in 250 Colombian municipalities between 2017 and 2020, using a longitudinal Beta regression model with zero inflation. The model used includes two parts: a logistic regression component to model the presence and absence of coca crops in Colombia, and a Beta regression component to model different rates of zero. Each component includes a random effect to take into account correlations between measures carried out over time on the same municipality. The relevance of various covariables such as educational coverage, municipal unemployment, incidence of armed conflict, anti-personnel mine events, school dropout rate, flood events, health indicators and drought events, in relation to the coca crop rate is examined. As a result, it is evident that variables with the greatest weight are the incidence of armed conflict, followed by anti-holistic mine events, anti-personnel attacks, flood events, flood events, floods, health indicators and drought events, in relation to the coca crop rate and drought
