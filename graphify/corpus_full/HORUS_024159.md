# Morphological variations and abundance of populations of Dalbulus maidis (DeLong) (Hemiptera: Cicadellidae) from the Huila maize-producing region, Colombia

Dalbulus maidis is the main vector of the causal agents of the disease of the corn-claying complex that decreases the yield of the crop by up to 90%. In the areas of greatest affectation by this disease, an increase in insect abundance is present. This research evaluated the abundance and morphological characterisation of populations of D. maidis collected in the department of Huila and determined possible morphological variations within these populations. Insect samples were collected in maize crops between 40 and 70 d after planting in eight locations of the Huila maize-producing region. A total taxonomic determination was carried out and twelve morphological characters were compared to evaluate variations between populations. Vary analyses were performed to determine significant differences between individuals in three levels: abundance of specific areas of maize production, morphotype and sex. 6722 individuals were found in total, separated into four morphotypes. Total length, head length and distance between eyes presented significant differences. This study could be the basis for a better understanding of the vector for future investigations in the regions of maize production in Colombia
