# Methodology for vibration analysis of the wing of an aircraft category FAR 23.

The ground vibration test (Ground Vibration Test, GVT) is one of the most critical test procedures for aircraft certification. Its correct performance allows to determine distributions of rigidity, natural frequencies, modal forms and structural damping, so its results are especially important. This work focuses on the development of a methodology for determining the dynamic properties of an aircraft wing from analytical and experimental methods. The development starts with a bibliographic compilation of methodologies used for ground vibration analysis. Subsequently, the procedures used for the development of a GVT test on an FAR 23 aircraft using two cases of load (0 % and 100 % of fuel). Finally, two numerical models based on the finite element method are developed to estimate the vibration response of the experimentally tested aircraft wing structure, the first using simplified elements type Euler-Bernoulli and the second using type elements were cracked by means of the commercial software ANSYS. The numerical models present good correlations with experimental ones in relation to the forms and their corresponding natural frequencies
