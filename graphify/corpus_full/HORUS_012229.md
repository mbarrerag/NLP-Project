# Management patterns of deforested agroecosystems and life strategies of Amazonian farms in Cacoal, Brazil

The human populations in the Amazon are part of the ecosystems in which they live. The scale and intensity of human activities have a significant impact on these ecosystems, with local, national and global effect. The objective of the study was to evaluate the management patterns of agroecosystems in some farms in the municipality of Cacoal, Rondonia, Brazil, in some biophysical (environmental) and socio-economic (family) terms, with the aim of quantifying the impact of 30 years of deforestation, related to their colonization process. The study was implemented in fifteen rural properties within 3 different regions in the municipality of Cacoal. We could conclude, in the farms studied, that the area is not a determining factor for the size of the legal reserve area. The level of education of the owners of the farms is also not a comparison for the size of the legal reserve. The types of crops used are not parameter for a farm to be more or less deforested. The management of the land holder does have direct influence in the forest area that exists in these farms
