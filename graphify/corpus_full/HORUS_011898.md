# RISK OF VIOLENCE, SELF-REPORTED ILLNESSES AND FRAILTY IN HOSPITALIZED ELDERLY PEOPLE

Summary Objective To analyze the relationship between the risk of violence and self-reported illnesses and frailty syndrome in elderly people treated in hospital institutions. Methods Cross-sectional, multicenter study, carried out with 323 elderly people in two university hospitals in Paraíba.The Hwalek-Sengstock Elder Abuse Screening Test instruments, a self-reported illness questionnaire and the Edmonton Frail Scale were used.The data were analyzed using descriptive and inferential statistics, using the Chi-square test and Multiple Logistic Regression. Results The risk of violence was associated in elderly people with depression (p=0.001), arthritis or rheumatism (p<0.001), osteoporosis (p<0.001), frailty syndrome (p<0.001).Furthermore, those with arthritis and frailty syndrome were, respectively, 2.37 [CI= 1.43-3.91] and 3.07 [CI=1.88-4.92] more likely to be at risk of violence. Conclusion Elderly people with chronic illnesses and frailty syndrome are more susceptible to the risk of violence.Therefore, discussion on the topic during the training of future nurses is essential with regard to gerontological nursing care in the face of this phenomenon.
