# Effects of deforestation on climate and hydrological cycle.

The excessive deforestation of the Amazon could significantly reduce water availability in tropical South America. The reduction of evapotranspiration rates and the occurrence of moisture represent possible feedback mechanisms that favor the savanization of forests. This process has been associated with the emergence of spatial patterns, observed in satellite images, that denote characteristics of self-organization of vegetation. In this study, reaction-dissemination systems are used, capable of generating similar patterns, to analyze the stability of this type of ecosystems, emphasizing the double-track relations between soil, vegetation and atmosphere. In addition, a regional climate model is constructed to simulate the climate of tropical South America under an idealized scenario of deforestation, the results of which indicate that rates of evapotranspiration and precipitation would decrease with respect to pre-industrial climatic conditions. Finally, using data generated from satellite images, changes in the type of coverage in the same region of study are quantified and the influence of the climate on the occurrence of forest fires, which tend to occur in periods of drought and high temperatures, are analyzed
