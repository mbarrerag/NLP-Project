# Quantum measurement learning for medical image classification

The deep neural networks are at the forefront for the classification of medical images. However, these models require very large data sets for their training, and their predictions lack interpretability. Recently, several methods of artificial intelligence based on quantum mechanics have been proposed, which can be implemented in classical or quantum computers. One of these methods is the recently proposed \textit{Quantum Measurement Classification} (QMC) [1]. In this thesis work, we present different classical and quantum machine learning strategies, which combine convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and some QMC-based methods [2] for the supervised learning of medical images. First, we raise the classification problem with a deep and probabilistic regression model, showing that it is competitive and more interpretable compared to conventional architectures of deep learning (CNNs) and some methods based on QMC [2] for the task of learning supervised medical images. Secondly, we present the classification problem with a deep and probabilistic regression regression model, showing that it is more competitive and more interpretable compared to conventional architectures of profound learning.
