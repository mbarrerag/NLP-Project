# Recovery of the wild phenotype of a supplemental strain of Mycobacterium tuberculosis H37Ra defective in the ctpH gene, a calcium transporter of the plasma membrane and verification of its complementation by cellular viability tests to oxidative and nitrosative stress conditions.

Tuberculosis (TB) is an infectious disease considered the main cause of death by an agent of bacterial origin, and the second most deadly infectious disease, after COVID-19. Its etiological agent, Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb), is a bacillus aerobio acid-alcohol resistant (BAAR) that has a cell wall rich in mycolic acids associated with the resistance of bacteria to antibiotics and its defense mechanisms against the immune system (Dorronoro, I. et al., 2007). Currently, the only vaccine available against TB is the attenuated M. bovis strain known as Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG), which has limited efficacy for cases of infection in adults (Pai, M. et al., 2016). For this reason, tuberculosis is still a significant challenge for public health worldwide. This is due to its persistent high incidence, the emergence of strains of Mycobacterium tuberculosis multi-resistant to drugs (MDR) and, more worrying than the extremely resistant of the tuberculosis (XDR). and sodium, as well as oxidative and nitrosative stress-generating species with respect to the non-deletion strain. In addition, by gene expression analysis it has been determined that ctpH plays a fundamental role in supporting the activity of other Ca2+-ATPas as ctpF (Maya, M. et al., 2019). Therefore, this project aims to complement the strain of Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb) that presents a mutation in the ctpH gene using a specific site integration system, in order to evaluate whether the wild phenotype is recovered. This approach will allow to determine the role of CtpH for cell ion homeostasis and as a possible objective for attenuation, crucial in the design of vaccine candidates against tuberculosis
