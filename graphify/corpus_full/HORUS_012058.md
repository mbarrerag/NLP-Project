# ENZYMES PRODUCTION FROM FRUIT AND VEGETABLE WASTE AND THEIR INDUSTRIAL APPLICATIONS

Agriculture is one of the most important sectors worldwide since it is one of the main sources of food and therefore a guarantee of food security. Likewise, the development of agriculture is related to the development of a country, contributing to the reduction of poverty (Bula, 2020). According to statistics recorded in FAOSTAT, for the year 2019, there was an approximate production of 2000 million tons of fruits and vegetables, with the main producing countries being China, Indonesia, India, the United States, and Brazil (FAOSTAT, 2019). As with any industrial activity, during the harvesting, transportation and processing of fruits and vegetables, large quantities of residues or by-products are produced, including leaves, seeds, skins, pulp, and among others. Banana, papaya, apple, pomegranate, pear, citrus, and grapefruit some ones of the fruits with more waste production. Worldwide production of citrus fruit was more tan 157 million tons in 2019, and about 30 million tons are used only for juice production, making about 15 million tons of citrus peel waste. Likewise, the production of primary fruits worldwide was approximately 883 million tons in 2019, respectively, generating between 25% and 40% waste after processing (FAOSTAT, 2019). Some regions in both East and Southeast Asia and sub-Saharan Africa reported high postharvest losses. During storage processing and packaging, the losses were significantly high, with storage being over 20% in East and Southeast Asia. In sub-Saharan Africa, there were losses on the farm and in wholesale markets. In Central and South Asia, wastes showed a tendency to be lower, with the transport stage being the highest (FAO, 2020)
