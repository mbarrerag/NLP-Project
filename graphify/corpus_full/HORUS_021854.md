# Role of co-infection by the different serotypes of dengue virus in patients who died during the 2010-2011 epidemic in Colombia.

The infection by dengue virus is considered one of the main arbovirosis of interest in public health throughout the world. Colombia is considered endemic for the disease, with about 50 years where the viral circulation has been constant. In the country at least 15,000 new cases are reported annually. The circulation of the 4 serotypes, the genetic variability of the strains, the high infestation of the vector, and the sociodemographic conditions have favored that in Colombia there are large outbreaks of the disease with epidemic peaks approximately every two to four years. In the year 2010-2011 a great epidemic of the disease was documented where 187,896 cases were reported to the surveillance system. During this period the circulation of the 4 serotypes of the virus was detected in the majority of the regions and the severity of the disease was estimated in about 5% with 534 more likely deaths. In accordance with the findings of the literature, the characteristics of this epidemic could favor the occurrence of infections by more than one serotype in the same individual.
