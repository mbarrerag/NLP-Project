# Comprehensive analysis of MPC-based energy management strategies for isolated microgrids empowered by storage units and renewable energy sources

Optimal energy management is a useful procedure for controlling microgrids, ensuring a safe, reliable, efficient, and cost-effective operation. An interesting approach to improve this method is by incorporating receding-horizon strategies, considering the scheduling of storage units to establish robust controllers in case of stochastic variations in power generation and demand. This work proposes an analysis of strategies based on model predictive control (MPC) for the optimal active and reactive power dispatch of isolated microgrids composed of storage and generation units. For this purpose, an integral microgrid model with linearized and efficient power-flow equations is obtained, and two predictive management strategies have been developed to obtain optimal and efficient solutions. The proposed algorithms optimize operating costs, schedule the charging and discharging of the storage units (SUs), perform voltage regulation, reduce active power losses, and ensure the balance between generation and demand. These processes provide valuable insights into the MPC concept for adjusting predictive energy-management strategies. Quantitatively, incorporating a 24-hour predictive horizon reduces power losses by 35.23% compared to non-predictive techniques, and operational costs by 1.6% relative to shorter horizons. Additionally, using ARIMA forecasting decreases the error in battery lifespan estimation by 6.8% compared to average estimations. Validation of these results is achieved through a co-simulation using Matlab and DIgSILENT on a modified version of the IEEE 13-bus test feeder. This simulation integrates two different storage technologies, generation and demand prediction models, and a battery degradation model to evaluate capacity loss under various conditions
