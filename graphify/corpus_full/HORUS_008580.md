# EXCHANGE AND CONSERVATION OF CRIOLSE SEMILS FOR FOOD SECURITY WITH INDIGENOUS COMMUNITIES CHEAM OF THE ANTIOCIA SUROESTE (COLOMBIA).

According to the National Population and Housing Census, the indigenous population of Colombia, grouped into 115 villages, reaches 1,905,607 inhabitants, corresponding to 4.4% of the country's census (Dane, 2018). The prevalence of chronic malnutrition (retarded in size/age) in the indigenous population under 5 years old is 29.6%, compared to 10.8% national average. The global malnutrition (low weight/age) is 7.2% in the indigenous population and 3.1% at the national level (Third survey of the nutritional situation in Colombia, 2015). The malnutrition of the indigenous peoples is a multifactorial cause: changes in the use of the soil, abandonment of territories due to the armed conflict, loss of criollas seeds adapted to the local agroecological conditions, abandonment of knowledge related to the use of autochthonous foods, and the introduction of the ultraprocesed products of the Autonomous City of Karma, Garden and Antioquia, expressed their social and cultural heritage. and strengthening the capacity to control their own food system, and thereby to ensure their food and nutrition security
