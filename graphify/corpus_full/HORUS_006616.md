# Design, manufacture and characterization of an aiding device for the treatment of disc hernias

The current therapeutic and surgical treatments for the treatment of discalal hernias do not represent effective solutions because their effects, in most cases, only have results in the short and medium term. Furthermore, they generate adverse and counterproductive reactions such as increase of pain, inflammation, infections and failures in prostheses that lead to repeated surgical interventions. This research seeks to contribute to the study of these lesions, generating two adjuvant devices for the treatment of discalal hernias in order to improve the adaptability of the prosthesis to the host and reduce the adverse effects. For this purpose, two prototypes of intervertebral discs, elaborated by additive manufacturing (FDM) and covered by electrospinning with PCL and CBD. The prototypes were characterized mechanically by compression tests; morphologically, with SEM microscopy; chemically, with NIR. In addition, the biocompatibility was evaluated using the contact angle test. The results of the mechanical characterization gave a response to the load of the intervertebral discs with both designs. Likewise, the product was able to improve the product, developed with the initial and the initial. the prosthesis to the host and alleviate adverse effects. The prototypes underwent comprehensive analysis, including mechanical characterization through compression tests, morphological analysis via SEM microscopy, chemical assessment using NIR, and biocompatibility assessment through the contact angle test.
