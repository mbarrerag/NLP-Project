# OPTIC MANIPULATION FOR THE HANDLING OF INSPECTS IN CULTIVES OF CORT FLOWERS: PERSPECTIVES FOR THE HIGH COLOMBIAN TROPIC

The search, selection and use of plants by herbivorous insects is mediated by visual and olfactory signals.Insects recognize the color, shape, size, architecture, angle of orientation and the background of contrast of the plant, characteristics that depend fundamentally on the light and the photosensors of the herbivores.This process is complementary to the recognition of organic volatile components (VOCs) emitted by plants or their conspecifics that determines a chemical communication mediated by pheromones, kairomonas, alomonas that defines the taxis and kine-sis of response of the insect to these stimuli.Once the herbivore lands in the plant, it recognizes tactile and taste signals associated with the palatability, nutritional status, competition with other organisms, among others, that will define its acceptance to feed, ovipositar or reproduction.: 1) Passive light traps, i.e. traps that present color by reflection; 2) Active light traps, i.e. traps that generate their own light. The colour characteristics of passive light traps will depend on the material and the surface where the light affects, so, the tone, saturation and brightness of the reflected color will be fundamental for the effectiveness of the trap as an attractant of insects. In most cases, these traps require an impact that allows, after attracting the insect, trap and kill it. The adhesive that is added to these color traps for capture, usually alters the properties of the trap, then the color characteristics can change with respect to those of the material without adhesive. Today, it is intended to integrate elements of innovation and technology, so that they can be added nanopar-ticles that adequately define the surface characteristics for the incident light desirable for the trap as attractants of insects.
