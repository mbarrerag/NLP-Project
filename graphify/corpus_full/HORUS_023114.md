# Abandonment and Staying in Higher Education: A Multilevel Analysis for Ibero-America

The aim of this research is to identify the variables that influence the abandonment and permanence in higher education in the Ibero-American context from a multilevel perspective; the survey carried out by the Alfa-Guide project in 2013, which includes 11423 students from 19 Higher Education Institutions (IES) located in 14 Ibero-American countries, was used. The methodology used was a multilevel logistic model of two levels for binary dependent variable (active or abandoned student) including a random covariate effect determined by the economic support received by the student. The review of theoretical and empirical approaches to the phenomenon of student abandonment demonstrates that this is a complex, multi-cause and dynamic problem, which is linked to a series of endogenous and exogenous factors to the student and to the IES where it occurs. The results of the estimated econometric model indicate that there is significant evidence of the variation of the dependent variable between units of the second level (IES) and that variables such as gender, civil status, economic resources, educational gap and satisfaction of the student with their program, are significant for the decision of abandonment. The results of the estimated model show that there is significant evidence of variation in the former variable sample of the dependent variable number (HEI) and that variables such as as gender, marital status, economic resources, educational gap, and student satisfaction with their program are significant for the decision of abandonment. Therefore, it is included that it is necessary to identify the identification of the determining variables of abandonment, linking them in a social, economic, and cultural context
