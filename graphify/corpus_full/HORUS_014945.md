# NATURAL GAS OCCURRENCE IN THE SHALLOW AQUIFER OF THE MAGDALENA MIDDLE VALLEY BASIN, COLOMBIA

Natural gas occurrence in aquifers could be associated with biogenic or thermogenic migration. Fractures, aquitards, or leaky wells could create preferential flow paths connecting deep formations with shallow aquifers affecting for instance water quality. Here we measured isotopes of carbon (d13C) and hydrogen (d2H) in methane, ethene, propane, butane, and pentane in groundwater to identify the presence of natural gases in the shallow aquifer of the Middle Magdalena Valley Basin in Colombia (Figure of Location), one of the most important oil and gas basins in the country. Eleven (11) samples were taken from wells between 300 ¿ 350 m in depth. Methane was the only gas detected in all samples and all concentrations were below the limit value according to the Colombian normativity, except one. Considering the presence of oil seep, we compared methane d13C in groundwater with the reported values for the main petroleum fields in the zone and used hydrocarbon gas isotope fingerprinting approaches for determining sources of natural in the shallow aquifer (Figure of Genetic Diagram). Groundwater d13C methane values were below the reported in the petroleum fields, which may be the result of diffusive effects in the gas migration towards the surface leading to more negative values than the original source. Three wells displayed values associated with organic matter decomposition and based on the fingerprints, two wells have thermogenic methane, and one has an abiotic origin
