# Electronic, structural and spectroscopic properties of encapsulated molecular systems.

In this project we propose the theoretical study of nanoconfined molecules in molecular systems such as fullerenes, which form cavities in which it is possible to introduce small molecules (H2, H2O, CO2,...).The nature of the interactions of the molecule encapsulated with its environment can modify the energy structure of the isolated molecule and mix the vibrational, translational and rotational modes that were discoupled in the gaseous state thus appearing a new set of energy levels.[1] Molecular systems of this type have been postulated as candidate materials for use in technological applications such as energy storage, the removal of greenhouse gases or quantum computation [1-4]. The theoretical study of these materials appears as a fundamental strategy to understand and orient the possible applications of nanoconfinated materials. However, despite the importance and possible extension of the study of these systems, the high computational cost of the theoretical quantum calculations has resulted in simplifications of the dimensionality of the systems, or in the use of classic treatments that are unable to exploit the richness of the quantum effects necessary to explore and understand the interesting molecular properties of these systems. cular that includes interactions between many bodies and of dispersive origin from DFT/DFT-D techniques.
