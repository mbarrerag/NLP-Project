# ETHNOGRAPHING INTERSECTIONAL INEQUALITIES 1

Considering that there is little empirical anthropological research documenting how intersectionalities occur and operate in specific moments and contexts, this chapter seeks to: 1) explore the potential of feminist ethnography-as a way of dissolving the boundaries between “subject-knower” and “object-of-knowledge,” and as a source of knowledge from situated political positions-for expressing the situationality of intersecting categories; 2) show-through ethnographic examples from our experiences and research contexts, and from a feminist critique of the very act of categorizing those we study-how different categories operate at their intersection; 3) deconstruct the idea that (social, sexual, racial, etc.) identities are fixed and monolithic categories that act in the field, based on a notion that a person experiences and transforms herself, in relation to others; 4) restore the value of ethnographic knowledge of intersectional inequalities elaborated from our own bodies, and as scholars who experience, feel and are moved, against the assumption of neutrality and objectivity attributed to all scientific knowledge
