# Abundance and distribution of Microplastics and zooplankton in the Protected Marine Area of the PNN Corales del Rosario and San Bernardo, Colombian Caribbean

The accumulation of marine plastic garbage is a problem that affects the oceans at different scales and has been increased in recent years. The majority of marine garbage that floats in the sea is composed of microplastics (MPs) particles below the 5 mm of diameter. These MPs are the same size as many organisms that are found in the zooplankton and that are fundamental part of the trophic networks in the marine pelagic systems. So the co-occurrence and relative abundance of microplastics and zooplankton constitute a problem, because they can be confused as food and be consumed, both by the zooplankton and by its predators. This has made it have a special interest in understanding the effect of the microplastics on these organisms particularly in coastal ecosystems. It is therefore necessary to investigate the mechanisms of response of different taxons towards these anthropogenic particles and, until now, studies tend to focus on marine mammals, turtles, fish and little on invertebrates and plankton.
