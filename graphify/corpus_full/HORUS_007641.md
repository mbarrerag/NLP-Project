# "Where there is power, there is resistance." Campaign, investment and imprisonment in the war against the Carare Indians,1574-1630.

Research on indigenous conquest and resistance is of the utmost importance, because it allows us to understand and interpret the historical processes that have shaped contemporary societies. This study will not only reveal the dynamics of power, which characterized the processes of conquest and colonization in the New Kingdom of Granada, but will also highlight the resistance of the indigenous communities to these sub-stitions. To date no specific studies have been carried out on the campaign of conquest against the Carare Indians, which is why the importance of the Magdalena River during this war will be investigated, its resistance to the Spanish conquest, the dynamics of colonization in the region and, at the same time, it will be given voice and recognition to a crucial aspect of indigenous history in this territory, that of its struggle to preserve its autonomy. 1574-1630 was chosen, because it is the one in which the war was most intense. The critical moments and the measures taken to the fragor of war will be highlighted, which will allow a deep and matatized understanding of these events. The above provides an approach to what happened and its impact in terms of everyday life, economy, social dynamics, among others
