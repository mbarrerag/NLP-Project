# Cost analysis – diagnostic effectiveness of the use of Computarized Tomography compared to Magnetic Resonance, as the first diagnostic modality in patients with seizure, users of the National University Hospital of Colombia until 2021.

A cost-effectiveness analysis of the use of Computarized Tomography (CT) of a simple skull compared with a simple cerebral Magnetic Resonance (RM) was performed in patients with convulsive crisis hospitalized in the National University Hospital of Colombia (HUN). The specific interest scenario to be evaluated was defined as: convulsive crisis of new appearance, without history of trauma. This was selected due to the fact that the American College of Radiology (ACR) has estimated that the simple CT and simple MR are equally suitable for the initial imaging approach in this clinical scenario. Our main objective was to investigate which of these modalities is the most cost-effective being used as an initial image in our environment. The study was carried out in a fourth level hospital, using a retrospective image database, acute ischemia, the mass, the hydro-displacement and the intermediate care unit during the period 2016-2021. The effectiveness was defined as the detection rate of critical findings: intracranial bleeding, acute ischemia and the disease was the condition, the mass, the effect of the patient was affected, the secondary and the secondary level of the secondary level of the secondary level. The costs were calculated from the invoices generated to the paying third parties. The average cost of a CT was $183,958 and an MRI was $600,064, with a higher cost for simple cerebral MRI, with an incremental cost of $416,106, in favor of simple skull CT. The ICER derived from our study was $5,188,347, which implies that an additional cost of this amount is incurred for each critical finding identified when using simple cerebral MRI compared to simple skull CT in the study of patients with first-time convulsive crisis, without prior trauma. The ability to generalize the results obtained to the general population is limited, given that it was performed in a fourth-level hospital without emergency service, but it is applicable to institutions of equal level of complexity in the country
