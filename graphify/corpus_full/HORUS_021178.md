# Impacts of land use changes on the eco-hydrological functioning of Bolivian forests. A multi-scale approach.

Changes in land use are one of the main drivers that determine the ecohydrological functioning of ecosystems, along a wide range of spatial-temporal scales. Therefore, human activities, which generate changes in land use, have an impact on alterations in the hydrological functioning of basins, among other consequences. This work aims to assess the impacts of land use changes on the ecohydrological functioning of basins with Bolivian and grassland Tucuman forest, through a paired basin configuration. For this purpose, a distributed and physical-based hydrological model (MIKE SHE) will be used to characterize and quantify the ecohydrological functioning and processes (in terms of performance and water regulation in the event and annual time scale) in response to different types of land use scenarios. at basin scale (local scale) and to link these processes at local scale to regional scale, such as: ecohydrological functioning of the Natural Area of Integrated Management (ANMI) through scaling methods
