# VALIDATION OF THE PALLIATIVE CARE QUESTIONNAIRE FOR NURSING (PCQN) IN COLOMBIAN NURSING STUDENTS

The Palliative Care Questionnaire for Nursing (PCQN) was designed in 1996 to assess three aspects of palliative care: philosophy and principles, control of pain and other symptoms, as well as psychosocial aspects. It has been widely used and validated in Canada, Arabia, Iran, Indonesia, France and Spain. Although it was designed for professionals, it has been used to measure knowledge of nursing students in different contexts, in which adequate validity and reliability were reported. The purpose of this study was to adapt and validate the Palliative Care Questionnaire for Nursing (PCQN) in the Colombian context. Psychometric validation study conducted in 2022. Content validity was carried out using the Lawshe methodology modified by Tristan with the participation of 13 experts who evaluated the scale according to 5 aspects: clarity, comprehension, precision, relevance and coherence. The Lawshe index modified by Tristan was calculated, where a CVI greater than 0.58 was considered as adequate content validity. Facial validity was assessed through a cognitive interview based on the categories: comprehension, recovery, judgment and adequacy of the items, explored in 17 Colombian nursing students. Relative frequencies were calculated to assess compliance with the analysis categories in the cognitive interview. The recommendations made by the expert judges regarding the semantic education or content of the items were considered. For the content validity, the 20 items of the questionnaire presented an adjusted CVI greater than 0.6, which is evidence of its validity. Regarding facial validity, in the cognitive interview, 100% of the items presented adequate comprehension, recovery, judgment, and adequacy. After the qualitative analysis of the responses of the 20 items, 14 required semantic adjustments, 3 content adjustments, and 3 did not require adjustments. The Palliative Care Questionnaire for Nursing (PCQN) has acceptable content and face validity for use in nursing students in Colombia. A version of the instrument adjusted at the semantic and content level was proposed. The questionnaire can be used in the Colombian population, however, future validations based on item response theory are recommended
