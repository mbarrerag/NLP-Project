# Amazonian women, borders and visuality. Colombo-Peruvian War and the narratives in dispute

The research aims to visualize the way in which the Colombian State used the image of the Amazonian women as a mechanism to legitimize their material and symbolic domination over the Amazonian territory during the Colombo-Peruvian War, between 1932 and 1933. For this, printed graphic sources such as El Tiempo, El Gráfico and Cromos were selected, since they allow to show the visual discourse reproduced at that time from a liberal and elite perspective that reflected the Colombian State’s ideas to coincide. The research makes a press review, from which a corpus of 700 images is extracted, the most significant ones are selected and a visual analysis is carried out from an intersectional perspective. Thus, an alternate narrative on the War is given on the representation and aestheticization of the Amazonian bodies, positioning a critique from the Amazonian State, classist, heteronormated and centralist on which the history of this war event has been written from the first to the second place. created and that differences from the homeland, race, classist, heteronormative and centralist discourse on which the history of this war event has been written. Thus, the different instances of domination produced from the representation and aestheticization of Amazonian bodies are highlighted, positioning a criticism from the Amazon and giving rise to the visibility of indigenous women in the region
