# Molecular coupling on Trypanosoma cruzi dUTPase protein for the discovery of inhibitors in the treatment of Chagas disease

Chagas disease is endemic to tropical areas of Latin America and has an important prevalence, however, there are few treatments available on the market so the search for molecules with pharmacological potential that act on the parasite Trypanosoma cruzi, which causes the disease, is necessary considering the serious complications. to evaluate the potential white proteins, available in the database of PDB conside-ran as initial parameter, the similarity with human proteins and identify potential inhibitors of the target chosen by molecular coupling. an evaluation of the proteins of the parasite was carried out by means of alignment of sequences and then a virtual screening by molecular coupling with databases and computer resources available at the Advanced Computation Center of the University of Texas (TACC), and the best results were evaluated in function of affinity, pharmacokinetics and toxicity. the molecular bank chosen was the dUTPase. Posterior to the virtual screening was selected-na 12 molecules that present potential inhibitor of these, la4-{3-[3-(trifluoromethyl)phenyl]isoxazol-5-yl}pyrimidine-2-forminanules
