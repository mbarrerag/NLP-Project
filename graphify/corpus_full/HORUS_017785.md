# GENERAL PANORAMA OF THE USE AND PRODUCTION OF INSECTS IN LATIN AMERICA

In Latin America, insect production is common in countries such as Mexico, Brazil, Ecuador and Colombia, especially among indigenous and local communities. Insect breeding and harvesting requires little technology and capital, providing opportunities for urban and rural populations. Small-scale production is the most common and is divided into traditional, commercial and livelihood initiatives. Commercial production focuses on species such as A. domesticus, G. assimilis, H. illucens and T. molitor. Challenges include the lack of regulation and the need to establish standards for sustainable harvesting and conservation of wild insects. Bioprospecting of new species could boost the growth of industry, but legal and equity problems must be addressed. Promoting sustainable and equitable growth of industry is fundamental to harness the economic, social and environmental benefits that insect production can provide
