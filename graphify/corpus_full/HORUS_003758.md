# Actors and networks in the construction of the tul nasa agroecosystem in the indigenous reserves of Toribío, Tacueyó and San Francisco in the department of Cauca

This research is based on the conceptual and methodological contributions of the analytical framework of the Actor-Red Theory (TAR) to study the ecosystem-culture relations in the agrarian field. Through the study of the case of the agroecosystem Tul Nasa in the indigenous reserves of Toribío, Tacuyó and San Francisco (municipio de Toribío) in the department of Cauca, Colombia, the construction process and the transformations it has undergone are analysed. It is discussed how this approach allows to conceive this agroecosystem as a network of agency-provided actors, track its transformations as a heterogeneous network of human and non-human actors and, following the proposal of the authors of the TAR, feed on the controversities. This research reveals how the construction of the Tul Nasa and its transformations responds to a complex process in which artifacts, technologies, regulations, knowledge, institutions, social actors, economic resources and socio-environmental conditions (human and non-human actors) produce, represent and defend its interests. .
