# Study and characterization of a biocomposite made from polyethylene (LLDPE) and natural fiber produced by rotational moulding

The growing concern for the environmental impact of conventional plastics and has driven the search for sustainable alternatives in the material industry. This work focuses on the production and characterization of a biocomposite produced from low density linear polyethylene (LLDPE) and cocoa foam shell, with the aim of assessing its viability for the manufacture of storage tanks through the rotomolding process. The study was carried out in various experimental stages. LLDPE mixtures were designed with different percentages of cocoa husk (6%, 12%, 18% and 24%), treated with silan and acetic acid, and compared with the untreated material. The characterization tests were performed that included chemical analysis, SEM, FTIR, TGA, DSC, and mechanical tests of traction, flexion and penetration. The results of the chemical analysis showed that the applied treatments significantly modified the properties of the biocomposite. The treatment with silano improved adherence and thermal stability, showing an increase of 20% in the resistance to flexion compared to the LLDPE without reinforcement. The results of the analysis of the acid treated skin showed that the increase in the performance of the acid. The study was carried out out out in a severe experimental stages.
