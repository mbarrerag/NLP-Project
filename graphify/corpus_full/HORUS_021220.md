# Discovering the genetic diversity of bean species of the genus Phaseolus related to domestication traits and agronomical interest

The genus Phaseolus has five cultivated species of agricultural importance. Of these, the common bean and the Lima bean are the most important economic, although the first is the most studied species. The common bean has ecological limitations that can harm its yield, especially in areas where the cultivation suffers from stress due to water deficit or high temperatures. In conditions of abiotic stress it has been reported that also the nutritional quality of the common bean seed decreases. To address this problem, it is necessary to have a deeper knowledge about the variability in the nutritional composition of the seed and its genetic bases. On the other hand, the Lima bean is also limited to the development of improved varieties. Therefore, the present project proposes to address these problems. On one hand, to evaluate the necessary nutritional content of the common bean collection and allow for the lack of knowledge about the genetics, especially in the common agricultural traits and the common agricultural base. The study aims to analyze, in its genome, the distribution of microsatellite loci (SSR) and the possible relationship between SSR loci with genes of domestication and agronomic interest. For this, the available sequence of a reference genome of Lima bean will be used to locate sequences of SSR loci. Subsequently, complete genome resequence data of 60 wild and domesticated Lima bean accesses will be used to evaluate in silica the polymorphism of the identified SSR loci. For information loci, primers will be designed for subsequent validation by PCR. Finally, those SSR loci associated with gene sequences will be identified. These loci SSRs associated with gene sequences. Of these, those will be selected for a silic analysis that shows high differentiation between wild and domesticated accesses, and that are close to genes reported for the traits of domestication and agronomic traits. These loci SSR associated with gene regions will be used for a later validation of the possible functional consequences of the SSR sequences.
