# Spectral characterization of soils in Colombia through near infrared spectroscopy (NIR).

This study evaluated the texture of some soils in Colombia using the technique of NIR spectroscopy, comparing it with traditional laboratory methods. NIR spectroscopy is not invasive and prevents the use of chemicals in obtaining soil information; a vital resource for biodiversity. Initially, samples were characterized using Pfeiffer chromatography to understand their relationship with organic matter, microorganisms and minerals present when collecting them. Subsequently, samples were processed by NIR spectroscopy; with spectral responses it was revealed that, at a wavelength of 2000 nm, clay contents were identified in 9 soil samples from the departments of Cesar, Quindío, Cundinamarca, Meta and Atlantic. The calibration models used to evaluate soil specific properties (A, L, Ar, CO, Ca, BT, Mg, pH) were found to be the most defective (R^2) > 0.6, except for potassium (K) obtained by soil.
