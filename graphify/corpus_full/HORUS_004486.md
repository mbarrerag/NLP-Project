# Stability analysis of slopes by 3D modeling with vegetation considering their temporal variation

The stability of the slopes within the civil engineering has led to the search of solutions that minimize the threat to the failure, with which several alternatives have been developed such as: retaining walls, soil nyloning, meshes, screens, concrete thrown; however, given the impact and cost of these solutions in recent years have been sought options such as bioengineering techniques, which include vegetation as an element that contributes to the stability of a slope, by means of mechanical reinforcement. In this work the evaluation of stability in 3D through SCOOPS 3D superficial is presented by means of a tool of free access to the geological service of united states (USGS), which allows to evaluate the stability by means of the Bishop method with a DEM, for a study area of 0.44 km2 in the municipality of Apia department of Risaralda. The above analysis is carried out by the hydrologic and mechanical effects of the vegetation on the slopes, considering the advantages and disadvantages estimated, because the soil-root interaction is complex given that many variables are involved, such as soil type of moisture, the mineral composition, diameter, density and inclination of the roots, and inclination of the roots. The results showed an increase of the safety factors in some sectors compared with the landscape without vegetation. This allows to conclude that the vegetation by means of its roots if it can have positive effects on the stability of the slopes, although these changes are not considered a definitive solution can be a preventive or complementary solution to alternatives such as soil, mantle against erosion or geotextiles in sectors where due to its characteristics the development of mass movements is previewed. .
