# USE OF MACHINE LEARNING IN IDENTIFICATION AND CLASSIFICATION OF MICROPLASTICS APPROACHED IN THE EXPANDED POLYESTIREN

Microplastics represent a challenge for water resources sanitation and potability systems because the efficiency of filtering methods to retain these particles is not known, so it is essential to implement models that allow the detection of these materials with the aim of developing strategies that mitigate their impact on aquifer systems. Therefore, the results obtained in the evaluation of a methodology for the identification and classification of microplastics composed of expanded polystyrene (EPS) using an electronic language and Machine Learning algorithms are shown in this paper. Experiments were developed that simulated different water environments where these particles could be found. The microplastic sizes used in the experiments ranged from 500, 1000 to 1500 micrometers. The methodology assessed different preprocessing methods, as well as different Machine Learning techniques, to determine which one showed greater accuracy in detection and classification. More than 80% results were obtained in the evaluation of four algorithms: KNN, SMV, neural networks and Naive Bayes. The use of electronic languages and electrochemical technique of the micro-scopia of impeda (IS) was demonstrated in different levels of contamination
