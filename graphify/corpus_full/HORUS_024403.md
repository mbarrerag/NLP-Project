# Mass transfer as a culture scaling parameter for a CHO cell line for the production of monoclonal antibodies.

Currently, monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) correspond to the most important segment in the market of biotechnological products with therapeutic effect. The synthesis on an industrial scale of this type of recombinant proteins is carried out in bioreactors using cellular lines coming from a mammalian, which are genetically modified to include within their metabolism the synthesis of mAb. To better understand the way in which these cells should be cultivated, laboratory-scale tests are carried out, in which the most appropriate conditions for production in the bioreactor at an industrial scale are determined. Within the most challenging challenges in technical terms to replicate in reactors at higher scales than in the laboratory is found the rate of transfer of oxygen mass. This gas is vital for the cultivation of the mammalian cells given its role in breathing, therefore, in obtaining energy for the cellular life. However, given the low rate of oxygen in water, it is necessary to shake and/or bubble the medium, and thus gas will solubilize such a lotter in the liquid.
