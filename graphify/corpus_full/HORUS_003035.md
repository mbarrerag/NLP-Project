# DEVELOPMENT AND CHARACTERISTICS OF SACHA INCHI OIL OLEOGELS STRUCTURED WITH SACHA INCHI PROTEIN CONCENTRATES AND METHYLCELLULOSE

The continuous seeking for new ingredients to replace saturated fats and partially hydrogenated vegetable oils in high-fat food products, has pushed the development of new techniques to structure unsaturated vegetable oils that can provide similar technological functionalities than saturated fats. In this work, we prepared a novel formula of healthy Sacha Inchi oleogels (SIO) following the indirect emulsion-templated method, using separately Sacha Inchi protein concentrate (SPC, 75% protein, 21.8% carbohydrates) and Sacha Inchi oil press-cake (OPC, 57% protein, 28.6% carbohydrates) as structuring agents, in combination with Xanthan gum (0.6%). The effects of adding SPC and OPC at two different levels (4 and 6% with regard to the protein content) on the physicochemical properties of the SIO were investigated, and compared with those of the SIO prepared under conventional procedures using methylcellulose (1 and 1.25%) and Xanthan gum (0.45%) as organogelators. The SIO were characterized in terms of their visual appearance, oil binding capacity, rheological behavior, textural properties, morphology, color, thermal properties, and oxidative stability. The results indicated that all the SIO displayed high oil binding capacities (>80%), and thermal and oxidative stability. The SIO exhibited elastic solid-like behavior with G¿>G¿¿, and uniform microstructures, which are influenced by both the type and concentration of the structuring agent. These oleogels obtained present high potential as nutritional substitutes for saturated fats in food formulations, and their production could contribute to the by-products valorization of the Sacha Inchi oil industry in a circular economy context
