# Parasitoids (Hymenoptera) of the Galapagos archipelago, factors associated with their diversity

The understanding of the factors that drive the diversity of a group in a region is fundamental to understand its characteristics. The archipelagos offer exceptional opportunities to investigate these phenomena since each island can be considered a repetition of these processes. Despite the importance of the Galapagos archipelago in biology, there is still little knowledge about an order as important as Hymenoptera. The relationship between the diversity of the parasite hymenopteras of the Galapagos Islands with the factors: age and area of the islands, isolation, topographic complexity, disturbance, diversity of habitats and the richness of their potential hosts was analyzed. The hymenoptera were collected in ten of thirteen main islands of Galapagos by means of the first structured sampling area for this group, using jameo, yellow trays and Malaise. We calculated richness of species, the index of Margalef and the phylogenetic diversity of the hymenopteras of Galapagos. We analyzed these variables with different factors, using the Pearson correlation index and we elaborated explanatory models through GLM. We captured 4994 specimens. These specimens were classified in 3LM, and we analyzed these variables. The study highlights the importance of Isabela, Santa Cruz, Floreana, Fernandina and Santiago both for their wealth and diversity. .
