# Design of antimicrobial peptides in silica by analyzing proteomas of viruses, bacteria and fungi

Due to the increasing resistance of pathogenic microorganisms to antibiotics, new antimicrobial compounds have been identified as treatment options. In response, alternative approaches have been adopted, including antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) that are a natural part of the immune system of all organisms. Studies have demonstrated the advantages of APMs compared to usual antibiotics such as their broad spectrum activity, mechanisms of action and selectivity towards bacterial cells on host cells and less likelihood of generating resistance. Reasons why this research focused on the identification, selection, modification and insilicon assessment of APMs found in the proteoma of viruses, bacteria and fungi through the use of specific bioinformatics and artificial intelligence tools, which estimated parameters such as structure, function, possible mode of action, hemolytic capacity, toxicity, membrane binding capacity, and their antimicrobial capacity. This suggests that new peptides found in this study are candidates for future in vitro and in vivo trials as an effective alternative to traditional antibiotics
