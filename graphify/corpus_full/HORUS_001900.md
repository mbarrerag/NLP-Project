# Evaluation of the protein content of small extracellular vesicles derived from human melanoma cells treated with Doxorubicin

Extracellular vesicles (EV) are structures of membranes released into the extracellular space, which transport biomolecules and allow the modulation of local and distant microambients. In particular cancer cells release EV, including small extracellular vesicles (SEV) as exosomes, which participate in intercellular communication. In vitro studies have shown that treatment with Doxorubicin (Doxus) increases the level of secretion of sEV. This chemotherapeutic agent can induce cellular stress responses that alter the tumour microenvironment and, in addition, has been associated with side effects such as cardiotoxicity. This study evaluated the effect of Dox (10 nM) on the biogenesis, production and content of sEV derived from human melanoma cells A375, performing a multidimensional analysis. Intracellular, immunocytochemical analysis showed a slight increase in characteristic markers of sEV, showing particularly CD81 at 96 hours (p = 0,0083). Also, an increase in the positive structures was observed (in the case of multidimensional analysis). The bioinformatic analysis suggested that some of the cytokines found in these SEV could be involved in tumour progression and treatment-induced cardiotoxic effects. Overall, the results showed that A375 to Doxo 10nM cell exposure enhances the biogenesis and release of sEV, suggesting that the spread of sEV with high TGF-β content may contribute to the effects at paracrine and autocrine level in its microenvironment, and to side effects attributed to Doxo .
