# Impact of the inertia reduction on frequency stability in electrical power systems.

The electrical power systems historically have been characterized by having generators with rolling masses, as are hydraulic generators or thermal generators, the latter have as primary source of energy coal, natural gas, oil, among others. In an effort to dispense with the use of fossil fuels as a strategy to mitigate climate change, throughout the world there is an increase in the integration of generation from non-conventional renewable energy sources into power systems, among non-conventional renewable energy sources are wind generation and photovoltaic solar generation.One of the main differences between conventional generation and generation from non-conventional energy sources, is that this latter does not attach to the electrical system through synchronous generators of rolling masses, on the contrary, it does so through electronic power devices, which do not provide inertia to the system when the frequency is presented and the minimum factor of inertia in the system is achieved, in order to achieve this condition additional challenges for the planning and operation of electrical systems.The objective of this work is to evaluate the dynamic behavior of the frequency in such a system of test, which does not include the frequency of inertia in the system, and in the event, to achieve this condition implies the functional efficiency of the electrical power
