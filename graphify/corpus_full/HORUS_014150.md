# Application of computational tools for the identification of potential replacement agents to chlorpyrifos

In Colombia, agriculture, especially banana and banana cultivation, plays a crucial role in the economy due to its significant contribution to GDP.However, the sector faces several challenges that limit its development and competitiveness, such as high production costs, technological barriers, and difficulties in health eligibility.These factors are aggravated by low productivity, resulting from pests and diseases affecting crops, which traditionally have been used by farmers to use synthetic insecticides, such as chlorpyrifos. Unfortunately and despite its effectiveness, this substance presents high toxicity for animals and humans, leading to its striction by the European Union and the United States, negatively impacting Colombian fruit insecticides exports, and the packaging industry. Faced with this scenario, there is a need to develop more safe, effective and accessible alternatives for pest control. Volatile compounds (VVs) as potential insecticides have a key role in solving the problems of synthetic insecticides. CVs have a lower environmental incidence, low or zero in human toxicity, and at the same time can be an easy to use alternative in the way of application. This approach improves the selectivity of selected compounds and reduces the possible toxicity to mammals, including humans. However, today, of the 7 types of known octopamine receptors, none has an elucidated three-dimensional structure. Therefore, it is necessary to use three-dimensional structure prediction tools (AlphaFold and/or Robetta) to generate a better prediction model. Once these models are obtained, the computational methodology will be identical to that already performed with the ACHE, only that for the prediction of activity on octopamine receptors will be counted with natural ligand (octopamine) as an agonist and an antagonist reported in literature (epinastin) as benchmarks. Finally, the or compounds that present multi-diana action (on the ACHE and on the octopamine receptors) will be evaluated in vivo and in the field in a future project against D. melanogaster to ensure its insecticide action.
