# WHO'S GOT TO GET, THIS IS FORZOUS: GIVE AND RECEIVE GOLDS IN SOME BOOKS OF SPAIN'S HAIRERS

The gesture of giftgiving is a manifestation of liberality and generosity, essential virtues in the ethoscaballeresco, which were part of the noble lifestyle that is recreated in the books of Spanish cavalry of the sixteenth century. In the private sphere, gifts were shown as a sign of intimacy and affection, while in the public sphere they were considered as important instruments of courtesy and diplomatic etiquette and constituted a social and political fact. But, during the early Modern Age, gifts were also the most effective means of obtaining favors, recognitions and earning wills; in this sense, according to Mausss, giving a gift implies for those who receive it a correspondence obligation. This work aims to explore how the social and cultural fact of giving and receiving gifts is recreated in the books of Spanish cavalry, both in the public and in the private sphere
