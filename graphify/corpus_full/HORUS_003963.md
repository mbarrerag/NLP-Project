# ASYMMETRIC FRICTION CONNECTIONS (AFCS) FOR STEEL BUILDINGS - HYSTERESIS LOOP MODEL

Asymmetric Friction Connections (AFCs) are bolted connections used to dissipate seismic energy at beam to column joints, column bases, or within braces of steel frames. AFCs can be assembled by clamping one slotted plate placed between a fixed plate and a floating cap plate with proof loaded high strength bolts. To ensure AFCs stable hysteretic behaviour, high hardness shims are inserted either side of the slotted plate. Energy is dissipated via friction as the slotted plate slides between the adjacent shims and plates. The AFC hysteresis loop shape has a bilinear or a rectangular pinched shape. To date, there is no model to describe these hysteresis loop shapes considering the bolt inclination during the sliding of the slotted plate. This paper describes the sliding mechanism and proposes a hysteresis loop model for the AFC considering the effect of the bolt inclination. Cyclic testing of five AFCs was undertaken. AFCs were assembled with one A325 bolt (Fy= 635MPa, Fu = 835MPa), A36 steel (Fy = 250MPa, Fu = 350MPa) fixed, slotted, and cap plates, and tempered steel shims with a Brinell hardness of 500BH. It is shown that the AFC's hysteresis loop shape is dependent on the slotted plate displacement amplitude, a, and on the bolt hole oversize, O. For 0.1O 1.5O, the hysteresis loop shape is penta-linear. That is rectangular pinched shape comprising the trilinear shape plus a linear trend representing the bolt bearing on the AFC plates after the bolt inclination, and a plateau representing the sliding of the slotted plate. The described AFC sliding mechanism shows that the bolt bearing on the AFC plates, and the subsequent bolt inclination reduces the AFC initial stiffness given by the AFC plates. A model for the three experimentally observed AFC hysteresis loop shapes is proposed considering the stiffness of each AFC component, and the dry friction theory of Coulomb. A good agreement is shown between the experimental results and the proposed model
