# Microencapsulation of synthetic peptides in copolymers of poly(lactic-co-glyclic) (PLGA): Effect of hydrophobicity of peptide.

In this study, the effect of the hydrophobicity of synthetic peptides during their microencapsulation in microparticular systems developed with PLGA was evaluated. From the database of the Fundación Instituto de Immunoología de Colombia (FIDIC), 280 peptide sequences were preselected designed and evaluated as potential candidates in the search for a synthetic malaria vaccine, and were classified into three groups, according to their hydropathic index (hydrophilic, neutral and hydrophobic). Of these, six sequences (two per group) were selected and synthesized by synthesis of solid phase peptides (SPPS) using the t-Boc/Bzl strategy and were appropriately characterized and purified. Subsequently, these peptides were encapsulated in microparticles developed with the PLGA 50:50 copolymer strategy by the double emulsion-evaporation technique of the solvent (W/O/W). The microparticle systems were evaluated in their size, form, load, or in-use efficiency of the independent release profile of the solvent.
