# BUILDING OF CHARGE IN THE VIRRENATE OF THE NEW GRANADA KINGDOM: A HISTORY OF PARTICULAR INITIATIVES AND INSTITUTIONAL REPLIES FINAL OF THE XVIIIth AND XIXth INICIES

This chapter seeks to demonstrate that, contrary to what has usually been thought, the strengthening of the justice apparatus and the building of prisons in the second half of the eighteenth century and in the first decade of the nineteenth century in the Viceroyalty of the New Kingdom of Granada would not be exclusively due to the Borbonic reforms, but would also respond to community initiatives undertaken by the neighbours as well as to constant demands in the face of the critical security conditions of their territories. Through a diverse documentary collection, the text makes an approach to the particularities of the prison in the modern era to the two sides of the Atlantic and places the focus on neo-Granadina recluses that are little known for being located in forgotten territories, in which the locals demanded in various ways the presence of the Crown
