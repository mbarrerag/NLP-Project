# In vitro modelling of mechanical and biochemical aspects involved in the cellular migration processes related to colorectal cancer metastasis

Metastasis is one of the most lethal manifestations of cancer, with an estimated 90% of cancer-related deaths. Recently, it has been demonstrated that tissue mechanics and the biological response of cells to different biophysical stimuli play an active role in the development of cancer and metastasis. Since this disease is a public health problem, the understanding of the biology of metastasis and the way in which the mechanics affect this process is essential to develop new therapeutic strategies to attack it and even prevent it. The main objective of this thesis was to study the role of the mechanical environment and the availability of nutrients in the evolution of colorectal cancer (CRC), one of the main causes of cancer death in the world. For this, different parameters of manufacture of type I collagen hydrogels previously described in literature were varied.
