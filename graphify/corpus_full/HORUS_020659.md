# Morphometric assessment of a population ofAraucanian creole pigs (Colombia)

Morphometry is a tool for quantifying and analyzing morphological variation in the size and shape of the animals. The aim of the study was to contribute to the morphometric characterization of the Araucanian Creole pig to know some of its functional and ethnological characteristics. 58 pigs (27 females and 31 males) were studied, with ages between 4 to 48 months, located in three villages of Arauca, eastern Colombia. It they obtained 11 individual body measurements and three indices were constructed. It was applied descriptive statistics and a univariate analysis to measure the environmental effect on body measurements and indices, using age as a covariate. The pigs presented a mean variability in body measurements associated with a median harmony. The pigs are considered dolichocephalous, mesoline and not meat fitness. Females tended to be larger than males. The sidewalk was the only effect that influenced the linear combination of morphological variables and indices. Given the variability of the measurements according to the veredas it could be inferred that in the Araucanian pigs studied they have different biotypes
