# The role of observation on selection and correction control by some discriminatory compositions

Studies with rats and pigeons have found control by some components of visual stimuli composed S+. Varied measures can reveal control by other components S+, and even by S- components via rejection. Studies with humans have replicated these results, suggesting observation as a measure of control of stimuli. An intra-subject design with concurrent programs of fixed reason examined how direct observation correlates with control by selection or rejection in 15 human adults. During the acquisition, nine subjects were reinforced by responding to a red circle or triangle, and punished by choosing a green circle or a square. The second phase reversed the contingency.
