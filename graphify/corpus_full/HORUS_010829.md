# Biomass production and chemical characterization of Lentinula edodes grown in bioreactor under different conditions

Biochemical engineering plays an important role in the technological development of the processes of obtaining either fruity bodies of mushrooms with medicinal potential for direct consumption, or in the manufacture of nutraceutics and/or dietary supplements. This research implemented a bioreactor methodology for the cultivation of the macromycet Lentinula edodes fungus, commonly known as Shiitake, with the aim of evaluating the effect that agitation speed and air flow have on the production of biomass and metabolites of medium and low polarity. It was determined that the highest yield and productivity of biomass production is achieved at conditions of 80 rpm and 0.50 vvm obtaining 0.0260 g/g and 0.00433 g/L*h respectively. Likewise, as to the composition of this biomass, it was concluded that maximum protein content (33.4% of the lyophilized mycelium) is generated at conditions of 140 rpm and 0.75 vvm, the sterols reached a percentage of 89.32% of the extract in DCM at 80 rpm and 0.5 vvm.
