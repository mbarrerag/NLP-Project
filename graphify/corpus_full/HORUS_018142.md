# CHAPTER 6 INTIUTION AND EMERGENCING STRATEGY IN ORGANIZATIONS

Intuition has been called a sixth human sense; it has been described as a form of perception based on relationships, meanings and possibilities (Brown, 1990; Myers and McCaulley, 1985), which surpasses the conscious mind, provides alternatives of behavior and is synthesized as a form of knowledge in which we often do not know how we know what we know. (Vaughan, 1979: 19). To analyze intuition, many authors rely on Jung's theory, which points out that people have four basic functions: intuition, feeling, feeling, and thinking, and, in addition, divide these functions into concrete functions (thinking and feeling), in which people evaluate unrational or abstract experiences and findings (intuition and feeling), in which people capture experiences and collect information (Fields, 2001: 25)
