# Impact of a short pre-session mindfulness exercise, conducted by therapists in training in the city of Bogotá, on therapeutic empathy, anxiety, attention and the therapist's presence

The new therapists face a number of difficulties when starting their clinical practice that interfere with their empathic capacity. This is problematic because empathy is the common factor that has been pointed out as the most powerful predictor of the progress of the consultants in therapy and as necessary condition for change. Despite this, empathy has the ability to be developed, learned and enhanced, and mindfulness has proven to be a useful tool for this purpose. The present study aimed to know the impact of a short exercise of mindfulness pre-session on the therapeutic empathy of the clinicians practicing, on three variables that can influence this empathetic ability: anxiety, presence and attention during the session and, on the usefulness of the session perceived by the consultant. For this purpose, a mixed sequential exercise in which they participated 5 days formed by therapists with less than 2 years of clinical experience and consultation in which the partial observation was between 21 and 27 years (3 women and 2 men). The quantitative phase consisted of an intrasujeto design of repeated measures, the data were analyzed using non-parametric statistics, descriptive statistics, trends, indices of change in the trend of individual change.
