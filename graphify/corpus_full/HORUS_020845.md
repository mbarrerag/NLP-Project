# ResCaTe Mar 2.0: Coastal resilience based on bioeconomics and knowledge construction in marine-coaster territories

The coastal community of Punta Soldado (PS) in the Colombian Pacific faces the greatest challenge of its existence, the erosion of its beaches and flood of its village has threatened in recent decades to become climate displaced due to climate change and the rise of the sea level.From the joint work and results obtained in the project _x0093_ResCaTe Mar: Coastal resilience based on the construction of knowledge in the territories Marino-Costeros_x0094_, executed by the OCEANIC Research Group through the national call for solidarity extension 2019, the need to strengthen the community for the development of alternatives and sustainable livelihoods was identified, considering its realities, such as high biodiversity, its ancestral practices and knowledge, the recent official reactivation of tourism in the economic correction, as well as its urgent adaptation due to the consequences of the erosion of its beaches and the threat of flooding of its village.In particular, from the work spaces in which a model of decision-making was developed for the management of change and adaptation to the environmental change and increase of the sea level has been identified as a pilot test of the green infrastructure. The community sees itself in the need and urgency of sustainable livelihoods, to improve and project responsibly the businesses derived from the use of natural resources. Linked to the processes developed for the increase of the technical capacities of a group of leaders, this project seeks to promote the development of biotrade as a sustainable means of life, through the open and participatory structuring of a Territorial Centre of Scientific Tourism of Nature, as a strategy of transformational innovation for adaptation to climate change based on communities, in the community of Punta Soldado, Buenaventura.
