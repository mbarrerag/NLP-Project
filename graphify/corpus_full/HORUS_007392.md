# Inequality in Colombia in the 21st Century: Challenges in Income Distribution lingering in the face of economic growth

This research addresses the discussion on structural inequality in income distribution in Colombia using as a reference the evolution of economic growth. Despite the significant expansion of the Gross Domestic Product that ranked the country as a regional leader, the concentration of income has presented persistence traits in significantly high levels from the international point of view. Thus, the internal reductions in indicators of inequality have been insufficient to avoid the stagnation of the country in the first positions among the most unequal in the world. Consequently, this research investigates the relationship between the Colombian productive structure and the distribution of personal income. As a methodological strategy micro-decompositions of the Theil index are used that allow to separate its constituent elements. Subsequently, the components obtained are disaggregated by economic sectors to dilucate their interaction with the trajectory of inequality. The results suggest effects of the increase of the concentration coming from sectors that have gained relative participation in the GDP. With the changes in the structure of the product, the economic activities with redistributive potential have lost relative importance. The use of the labor factor is a differential element that separates sectoral effects.
