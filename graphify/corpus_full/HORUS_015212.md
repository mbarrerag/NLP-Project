# AUTOMATIC CLASSIFICATION OF MICROPHOTOGRAPHY FOR VETERINARY PARASYTOLOGICAL DIAGNOSTIC

The importance of veterinary diagnosis and monitoring of animal diseases is fundamental within the control strategies. Computer tools for automated learning and computer vision have the potential to increase efficiency and diagnostic capacity to reduce time and subjectivity for the emission of results. In this study, stool samples were collected from a sheep farm in the department of Sucre using a parasitic control model in small ruminants, where nematode infections are one of the main limiting factors of production. These samples were processed by the modified McMaster technique to identify the presence of Helminth eggs. With the eggs obtained from positive samples, a photographic catalog was developed by optical microscopy. A set of nematode imaging data was generated (Strongylidos, Ascaridos, Tricuridos and Strogyloides) and the sample size of the eggs were processed by means of the diagnostic sample.
