# Biology of pollination

Pollinization represents a central ecosystem service in maintaining the biological diversity of terrestrial ecosystems and in the production of cultivated food. The pollinators crisis reflects the environmental crisis, whose negative consequences pose a challenge to human health, that of other species, and ecosystem health. Given the need for knowledge for individual progress and organizations, the continuous formation of new generations in assertiveness oriented to soil management, ecosystems, and biotic resources without compromising biodiversity and ecological functioning, these last engines of biotic wealth and social stability is necessary. Although the subject of pollination is a century old and there is a wide literature on plant-pollinizing interaction at the global level, this work contributes to promoting knowledge on pollination by presenting to students illustrated guides for the development of practices. The techniques used are based on protocols that were tested in the laboratory and in the pollinator garden
