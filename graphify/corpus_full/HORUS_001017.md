# SUSTAINABILITY AND TECHNOLOGY TO CREATE SOCIAL FABRIC.

This article presents the experience and results of a project that identifies the need to create "social fabric" within vulnerable communities displaced from rural areas to cities such as Bello and Medellín in Colombia. In response, the Universidad Nacional de Colombia, as a public initiative of the Nation, implemented a project combining research and solidarity extension. The main objective of the project conveyed the fundamental principles of solar energy and its contribution to the quality of life of residents in socioeconomically challenged urban sectors through a pedagogical yet rigorous approach. Utilizing participatory design and learning-by-doing process, a group of individuals was empowered to manage access to home lighting through photovoltaic solar panel technology. The panels were assembled and installed by the participants themselves with scientific and engineering support from the University. The project unfolded in four phases: 1) Imaginaries, 2) Re-signification, 3) Co-creation, and 4) Conservation, which are detailed in this paper. The results demonstrate the feasibility of implementing social processes centered around a technical object or technology to foster community cohesion and improve quality of life through economic savings on utility bills enabled by solar-powered lighting. What is new in this research is represented in the change of paradigm, which goes from welfare to social and technological empowerment on the part of the community
