# Modeling multidimensional quality in carrot from spectral responses, images and consumer perception.

The evaluation of carrot quality (Daucus carota L.) is a multidimensional challenge that integrates physical, nutritional, sensory and technological attributes. This study proposes an innovative approach that combines spectral analysis, remote sensing and consumer perception to develop an indirect methodology of quality assessment. Research is structured in three phases: (1) trend analysis through bibliometric studies, Google Trends and analysis of feelings in social networks to identify consumer expectations and market demands; (2) spectral characterization in laboratory by spectroradiometer, capturing reflectance data in the VIS-NIR range and correlating them with bioactive compounds, particularly carotenoids; and (3) field assessment with multispectral images acquired by UAV, analyzing vegetation indices and spatial variability in carrot quality at different phenological stages. Automatic learning models are used to integrate these data sets and develop predictive quality assessment tools. The results provide a robust framework to improve agricultural decision-making, optimize productive efficiency and strengthen competitiveness in the market
