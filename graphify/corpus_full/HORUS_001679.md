# Legal pluralism and the production of urban space: an approach based on scales of power

The purpose of the article is to explore the relationship between law and the social production of space, proposing legal pluralism as a theoretical and methodological alternative capable of shaping/understanding/recognizing the multiplicity of spatial configurations and their forms of production. In this sense, the question is asked: does the law take care of territorialities? How does law act in the production of space? To do this, a methodological perspective is highlighted from the scales of power, on how to conceive the relationships between multiple legal orders, rationalities and /or normative practices that sometimes dispute the production and appropriation of space. The article is about a theoretical elaboration that used literature review with qualitative approach as methodology. As a main conclusion, it has to be said that State law often fails to capture territorial dynamics and multiculturalism, imposing norms that do not reflect the needs of the subjects and places them in marginality, illegality and non-place. Thus, legal pluralism would be qualified as an approach that allows a greater empirical approximation and a more faithful understanding of normative social practices and thus allows a more democratic and participatory vision of the production of urban space
