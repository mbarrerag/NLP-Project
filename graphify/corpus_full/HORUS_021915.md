# Evolution of anteroarch basins in response to a changing subduction regime: Neogen geological record to the reciente del noroccidentante de los Andes Colombianos.

The growth of accretionary orogenes such as the Andes of the north, involves tectonic processes related to subduction, as well as the collision of exotic lands, including oceanic plateaux and arcs of islands.These contrasting tectonic regimes control the spatial evolution of deformation patterns and topography, whose signal is preserved in the tectonostratigraphic record of the marginal basins, which is particularly true for the anteroarch systems.This work presents a detailed geochronological and compositive characterization of the Cretaceous magmatic rocks and sediments Neogenes of the northern anteroarchus of Colombia (Atrato Cuenca), which record the long-term evolution of the north-western region of the Andes, from the early interactions with the Caribbean plate to the most recent flat subduction of the Nazca plate, which could represent the new petrochronological data of modern sediments of the Gulfs of Cupica and Tribá allowed to recognize a Late Cretaceous unit with a related American arch of islands that had not previously been documented, which could represent the early activity. The advance of the collision event triggered the reversal of the basin and the submersible of the accumulation depths during the Middle Miocene, as well as the exhumation by accelerated erosion of the continental paleo-capture and the increase of the silicyclastic flow towards the suture basin and the basin of the trans-arco in collision. Second, the transition from collision to subduction of the Nazca plate during the Mid-late Miocene caused the formation of a post-colisional arch in the noroccidental Colombia, and also the initial topographical survey of the recently established antero-arco basin (before-alochto) in the vicinity of the suture zone. This was accompanied by an important somerisation of the accumulation environments and a dramatic change in the detritic sign of the antearco deposits, which were isolated from continental sources (i.e. Western Cordillera) by recently lifted mountains.
