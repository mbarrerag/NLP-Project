# Identification of changes in DNA methylation and gene expression profiles associated with clinical response to chemotherapeutic treatment in paediatric patients with acute type B lymphoid leukemia.

The acute lymphoid leukaemia of B cells (LLA-B) are the most common neoplastic diseases in children. Survival rates in the Hispanic paediatric population are lower compared to survival in non-Hispanic children. Therefore, it is necessary to find predictive biomarkers for treatment response and prognosis of relapse and death in this population. The aim of this study was to identify biomarkers for response to induction treatment, which could also predict relapse and death, through the identification of differentially methylated and expressed genes between patients who responded or not to induction chemotherapy. DNA and RNA samples from 46 bone marrow samples from newly diagnosed Hispanic children with LLA-B were extracted. Thirty-two samples were used as a decryptive cohort (diagnostic 27 and 5 post-chemotherapy), in which the analysis of RNA and methylation of DNA were performed.The analysis of NLD was performed in the next team. that a greater expression of MIR4435-2HG increases the probability of therapeutic failure and the risk of dying. In turn, it was observed that DAPK1, CNKSR3 and MIR4435-2HG are also overexpressed in relapse samples. Finally, the overexpression of MIR4435-2HG in conjunction with the detection of positive minimal residual disease is associated with lower survival, and the high expression of MIR4435-2HG, DAPK1 and ASCL2 improve the risk classification of patients with normal karyotype. In conclusion, this study found that the expression of MIR4435-2HG is a predictive biomarker potential and prognosis in Hispanic children with LLA-B, and its detection at the time of diagnosis could improve survival rates in our patients
