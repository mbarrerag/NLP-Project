# Design and implementation of a prototype remote monitoring system for the protection of indigenous peoples' territories in isolation in the Río Puré National Natural Park

This paper describes the design and implementation of a preliminary prototype of a technological tool aimed at identifying threats and disturbances to the National Park Natural Rio Puré, complementing the strategies of surveillance and control, and contributing to the conservation of the biodiversity and culture of the humid tropical forest of the Colombian Amazon Northwest, as well as to the protection of the rights of indigenous peoples in isolation. To achieve the proposed objective, a systematic review was carried out in order to determine the most suitable existing technologies to design the prototype that would adapt to the particular conditions of the Amazon. Once these technologies were identified, the design of the preliminary architecture of the system was proposed. Subsequently, the implementation of the technological tool was carried out, which integrated three fundamental modules: a system of sensation and identification, based on a radar system and a PTZ camera (from Pan-Tilt-Zoom) for the visual monitoring; a communication system, based on a satellite link; and an autonomous electrical power supply system.
