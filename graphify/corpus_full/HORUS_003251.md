# CHARBON ACTIVATED TO TURN WOOD RESUDUES FOR ADSOLUTION OF AMOOXICILINE IN Aqueous SOLUTION.

The inadequate management of the waste generated by the wood industry represents a large environmental problem. The high organic load discharged into the water bodies and the accumulation of waste in open areas favour environmental pollution, the proliferation of pathogenic microorganisms and the appearance of pests and diseases in the flora and fauna [1].In this context, the conversion of these residues into materials with added value, as adsorbents for the treatment of waste water, emerges as a sustainable alternative to mitigate their environmental impact and promote the circular economy [2-3]. In this study, the production of activated carbon (CA) from wood residues of cedar (Cedrela odorata) as a strategy for their valorization and subsequent application in the removal of amoxicillin from wastewater. Amoxicillin is a broad spectrum antibiotic, which is widely used in veterinary and human medicine, since this is considered a primary product to combat bacterial infections, and its presence in surface waters represents a risk for public health and aquatic ecosystems [4]. Due to its high use, the amoxicillin can persist in the environment reaching concentrations between 10 ng/l and 5 %. The surface functional of the AC was characterized by the Boehm method and Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), allowing the identification of phenolic groups and lactone type. The zero load point (pzc) was determined showing pHpcz values for the CA between 3.3 and 4.8. SEM micrographs showed a development of pores on the surface of the material. Surface area analysis by the BET method revealed surface area values between 300 and 1000 m2/g. These results suggest potential versatility as an adsorbent material for removal of various pollutants, including amoxicillin. To assess the adsorption capacity of the material, a commercial amoxicillin solution of 50 ppm was used and monitoring was performed by UV-Vis spectroscopy (= 229 nm) and was quantified by calibration curve. The effect of the pH, adsorbate/adsorption ratio, contact time and maximum adsorption capacity was approximately 200 mg/g of amoxicillin
