# Effect of the enriched environment after maternal separation, on bdnf and astrocytes in the cerebral cortex of wistar rats.

The mother-child interactions influence the physiology, development and behavior during the first weeks after birth. As an adverse experience in early life, maternal separation (SM) produces disorders of behavioral and neuroendocrine functions in brain areas that construct the emotional axis. The studies in rodents have shown that prolonged separation causes a significant amount of stress. The consequences of this stress, particularly the hyperreactivity of the HPA axis (hypotalamic-pituitary-adrenal), are expressed in adult age and persist for life. The aim of this work was to evaluate the effect of 15 days of AE, on the neutral factor derived from the brain (BDNF) as marker of plasticity and astrocytes, as well as the functional elements of the MS.
