# The role of civil society organizations in ensuring price transparency

This chapter discusses the barriers and challenges faced, the resources, and the facilitating factors available to three Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) in their efforts to increase medicines’ transparency: Health Justice Initiative in South Africa, the “No es Sano“ campaign in Spain, and the Think Tank “Medicines Information and Power” in Colombia. Despite lengthy legal procedures, ingrained opacity, lack of funding, and government and industry opposition, CSOs have relied on national and international networks to expand their capacities and capabilities, using transparency regulations to highlight governmental and industry coalescence, increase transparency, and raise public and media awareness on the democratic and economic consequences of lack of transparency in the pharmaceutical market. Our analysis underscores the necessity of increased funding for CSOs, international collaboration and strengthened legal structures to foster transparency by design in the system, as key enablers to treat price information as a global public good
