# Conservation genetics of the largest capture population of the critically improved Orinoco crocodile (Crocodylus intermedius): a contribution for its survival

In the last century there has been an increase in the number of threatened species and the conservation through programs of breeding in captivity has become crucial for its survival.One of the main considerations for the design of reproduction programs is the preservation of the genetic variability that provides the raw material for adaptation.If the management is based only on the registered pedigree, the information may be incomplete or inaccurate and may lead to an underestimation of the relationships of parentesque.Incorrect management actions can alter the viability of the reintroductions due to the loss of diversity and genetic depression of the original population.In this Master's thesis, we use a system of 17 loci of microsatelites to characterize the genetic variation of the largest population ex-situ of the critically threatened Crocodylus intermedius in Colombia under the management of the Station of Tropical Biology Roberto Franco (EBTRF) with the aim of proposing management patterns and evaluating re-introductions past and future. In Chapter 1 we compare the genetic indices of the populations Founder and Viva and we find that the living crocodiles maintain much of the functional diversity, high levels and individuality.
