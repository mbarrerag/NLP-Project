# CIBERSECURITY AND BAYSISIAN STATISTICAL MODELS TO SERVE DIGITAL NATIVES, ALERTS IN RESPECTIVE INVITES IN SOCIAL NETWORKS

In the 21st century, digital natives (millennials and generation Z) make use of technology and its different applications to communicate, with their risks being elevated in cyberspace, in the face of threats such as sexting, cyberbullying and grooming, among others. The massification of social networks has become the main platform of communication among equals without validating aspects such as authenticity and integrity, basic criteria of cybersecurity. False profiles are often accepted, being this a risk factor in the face of cyberbullying by cybercriminals who try to violate their privacy and privacy. In this work, aspects of responsible use of social networks are addressed, and how data science, through a completely Bayesian model, can model a social network and the relationship between its actors, allowing us to extrapolate information between peers, detecting inconsistencies of false profiles and helping to mitigate risks or dangers online, due to inadequate education in these aspects, making us more vulnerable. The model presented allows us to study the properties of the network, as well as the social roles of the system actors, allowing us to detect data inconsistencies to the service of cybersecurity, being highlighted by the new statistical network
