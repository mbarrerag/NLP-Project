# Influence of the conditions of grinding on the occurrence of white layer in rails type R400HT for use in the Metro of Medellín

The grinding of rails constitutes a maintenance operation aimed at correcting surface and subsurface defects present in them, especially those derived from rolling contact fatigue (RCF) and the formation of corrugations. However, rail grinding can induce the formation of a hard and brittle layer on the surface known as a white layer. The presence of such a layer has been associated with an early appearance of FCR cracks. In this work, samples of R400HT rails were ground in laboratory under controlled conditions that promote the formation of white layer. Both frosted and non-frosted samples were subjected to accelerated contact fatigue tests at 20K cycles (8K dry cycles + 12K cycles with water) on a disc-disc type tribometer, in order to determine the wear rate and the friction coefficient. The results revealed that all the samples tested, under different grinding conditions, developed a white coating on their surfaces. Those samples with this white layer exhibited a higher wear and a higher crack density compared to the samples without grinding, for the same number of fatigue cycles. In addition, it was observed that the onset of cracking was influenced by the presence of the white layer, since the interfaz between the white layer and the pearlite became a preferential site for the onset of cracks. .
