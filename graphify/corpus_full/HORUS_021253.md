# Influence of environmental gradients on the structure and patterns of competition of massive coral reefs in Tayrona National Natural Park

Climate change, ocean acidification and other regional and local anthropogenic disturbances have accelerated the loss of coral coverage, generated demographic imbalance, altering the natural patterns of competition for space and other resources and driving changes of coral domain to the macroalgae domain. In the Tayrona National Park, the structure of coral formations, which are underdeveloped, respond to natural gradients such as the exposure to the waves, the depth, the characteristics of each bay and the bimodal regime between the rainy season with continental discharges loaded with nutrients and sediments from the Ciénaga Grande de Santa Marta, which generates a gradient of disturbance SW-NE and, a period of coastal surgence in the summer months. Studies have shown that, in reefs, the disturbance gradients generate decrease in the richness and abundance of species and functional groups. They modify the structure of communities and populations; they reduce the growth and coral coverage rates and favor the proliferation of competing organisms such as algal grasses.
