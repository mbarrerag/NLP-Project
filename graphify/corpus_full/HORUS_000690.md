# Parental values, parenting practices, perception of parental support and adolescent autonomy

This thesis explored the influence of parental values on adolescent autonomy, starting from the theory of personal values, and considering the role of mediator of parenting practices and the perception of parental support towards autonomy. A cross-sectional research design was implemented and a model of partial minimum squares of hierarchical order (PLS-SEM-HOC) was used. 210 parent/mother-teen days belonging to two-parent families in Bogotá and surrounding municipalities participated. Adolescents were active students of public or private institutions in grades 9, 10 or 11. Self-reporting instruments were applied to both parents and their adolescent children. The results showed a direct effect of the perception of maternal support on adolescent autonomy and an indirect effect of parental values, mediated by parenting practices and the perception of maternal support. No significant differences were found by gender of the parent or adolescent. These findings highlight the importance of parental values, but also underline the central role of the maternal figure in promoting adolescent autonomy. Among the limitations of the research, it is suggested to explore specific profiles of parental support towards autonomy and adolescents.
