# Study of discrepancies in sky types for dynamic daylight analysis according to available climatic files. Colombia case

In order to carry out an accurate lighting study, it is essential to know the predominant types of sky in a locality, as these are key to estimating the lighting performance of indoor environments. The objective of this research is to analyze the discrepancies in the assessment of the performance of natural light of indoor spaces according to the climate files used and considering the differences in the predominant sky types according to the radiation data of these. To do this, using the Clarity Index, by All Pérez All Weather Sky Model, the sky types were estimated and compared based on the radiation information from climate archives available for thirteen Colombian cities. Additionally, dynamic simulations of natural light on hypothetical environments were performed, with different climate files. The results of the research show, for each analyzed city, notable differences in terms of the predominant sky types according to the climate file used. These differences led to notable discrepancies - in many cases, of more than 10% - in the application of dynamic metrics using the climate files available for each city. Although it is not the objective of the research to conclude which of the climate archives is more reliable, it does highlight the need to have reliable radiation data in climate archives, in order to favor accuracy in the assessment of the lighting performance of indoor environments through computational simulation
