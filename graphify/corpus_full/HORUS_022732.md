# Plant trait assembly in species-rich forests along elevation in the northwest Andes of Colombia

The Andean forests have a wide diversity, which makes the understanding of the assembly of the communities complex along their altitudinal gradient. In this study, we use the multivariate methods RLQ and Fourth corner to understand the assembly of the Andean forests of northwestern Colombia. These methods assessed the relationship between six functional traits and three groups of impellers: environmental (climate and soil fertility), associations of symbiotic roots (mycorritis and nitrifying bacteria) and tree size (large and small). Our results showed that the communities of the Andes are represented by five functional groups, where 95.75% of the variability is explained by two main axes. The first is associated with the economic spectrum of the leaf and the second with the balance between survival/growth. Furthermore, we find that the interaction of regional factors (variable climaticities) and variables between the two sides, and factors (fertility of the roots and size of the trees) determined the assembly of the communities in our study area. .
