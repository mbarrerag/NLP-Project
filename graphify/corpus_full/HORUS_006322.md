# My life with Carurú Cachivera in the Lower Vaupés River: a Doubly Reflective Anthropology

The present research work takes place in the lower basin of the Vaupés river, between the binational border of Colombia and Brazil. In this part of the river, the cachiveras are hydrobiological borders for a special group of fish, while they are sacred places for the linguistic families tukano and arawak. Between these borders is Carurú cachivera, which is characterized by having a series of petroglyphs. The first encounter of this place with the historiography of the river Vaupés is given in 1852 when the recognized naturalist Alfred Russell Wallace ascends by the river to study the fish of the region. The second encounter is given by means of a dream that I had hours before traveling to the Váupés river. In this journey I was to document the expedition that would continue 167 years later, the study on fish that Wallace had begun and did not achieve completion. And the third encounter occurs when the expedition that I completed violently on this Cachivera.
