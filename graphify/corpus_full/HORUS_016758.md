# Practical work in community and ecosystem ecology as a strategy for the development of scientific skills in an international baccalaureate context.

The practical work is important in the teaching of the sciences as a method of bringing the student closer to the development of the skills of a scientist. However, they have been the subject of controversy because they have been found to lack clear objectives in their application in the classroom. This final master's work proposes the design of practical works in community ecology and ecosystems for the development of scientific skills in students of an International Baccalaureate College (IB). It was developed in three phases. The first was the diagnostic with which the entry profile of students of biology in the IB Middle Level was characterized in terms of their performance of scientific skills. Subsequently, three practical work guides were designed under the Teaching for Understanding (EpC) approach where activities were carried out that allowed the development and strengthening of these skills that are required within the rigour of international curricula. Finally, an exit evaluation was applied to identify skills that were strengthened and those that require strengthening. The population was students of tenth grade belonging to the biology subject, to show the level of progress in each of the proposed skills.
