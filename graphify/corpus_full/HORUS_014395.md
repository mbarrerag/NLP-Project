# Relationship between body composition and feeding habits on the neuromuscular performance of the lower train in cadets entering the José María Córdova Military School.

In the military population emphasis is placed on physical and tactical training, seeking to maximize operational performance capacity. During training, the cadets are exposed to different factors: physical, environmental, psychological and nutritional, which can influence and even affect the adaptation to training and their subsequent performance or performance. An observational and descriptive correlational study was carried out, in which consecutive cadets were selected (n=105 baseline and n=91- 4 months later) between 18-26 years, entrants to the ESMIC in Bogotá in 2022. The baseline condition of the cadets and 4 months after their training was evaluated, to associate these values with the performance by means of a jump test (CMJ).A questionnaire of 5 segments and a non-invasive examination (bioimpedance) was included, so the risk of the study was minimal according to the coefficient of the variables.In addition, the ethical principles for the medical research in human beings of the Helsinki declaration were respected.For the analysis phase of the information, Shapiro-Wilk was executed to verify the results of the variables. The results were worse, showing asymmetries in the landing phase, which can be explained by the accumulation of fatigue resulting from the type of training performed, and therefore it is considered that their current training does not favor physical abilities and increases the risk of overuse injury. The taking of anthropometric measures and biomechanical analysis have proved to be easy tools to apply in the evaluation and monitoring of people with a high level of physical activity, so the preventive approach seeks to avoid disadaptative discharge. Consequently, there is an interest in identifying modifiable factors that can alter the body composition, performance and health results associated with military personnel during their training phase
