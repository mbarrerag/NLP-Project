# USE OF PAPAIN IN DISTRIDING PEDIATRIC INSTRUMENTS

In a wound, the remains of the destroyed tissue and the exudate deposited on the wound are generally known as necrotic tissue. The presence of necrotic tissue and bacteria in the bed of a wound exacerbates and prolongs the process of inflammation, can mask signs of infection, prevents the assessment and determination of the depth of an injury and is the main obstacle to the healing process by preventing adequate blood irrigation and is a culture medium for bacterial proliferation, which is why its removal or debridement is necessary.This debridement can be performed in different ways, including the use of enzymes such as papain consisting of a complex mixture of proteolytic and peroxidous enzymes present in the latex of Carica papaya. This work aims to present four clinical cases of paediatric patients in which lyophilized papain was used for the process of wound debride and to establish the average time needed to achieve the therapeutic effect of lyphilized papain in the children studied. In addition, the total costs of wounds are presented
