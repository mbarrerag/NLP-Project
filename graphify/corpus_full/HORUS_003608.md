# CHARACTERIZATION OF ZNO PARTICLES OBTAINED BY MECHANICAL MILLING

The study of semiconductor materials for their application in the optoelectronics industry is of great interest since it is necessary to find ways to reduce production cost and increase optoelectronic device performance. One of the attractive materials in this regard is zinc oxide (ZnO) due to its piezoelectric properties, high optical bandgap, high thermal stability and mechanical resistance to ambient temperature. That is why we focused on obtaining ZnO nano particles by mechanical grinding for their possible application in optoelectronic devices such as solar cells. The experimental work to be reported here was done on a Pulverisette 7 premium line equipment, using a ball to powder mass ratio of 10:1, for milling times from 1 to 20 min at 500 rpm. The characterization of ZnO particles was carried out by X-ray diffraction (XRD) and Raman spectroscopy to determine the hexagonal wurtzite-type structure. The morphology and distribution of the powders were analyzed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), finding that the grain size decreased from 100 μm to 1 μm and the crystallite size decreased from 31nm to 24nm as a function of milling time. It will be observed that there is a dependence of the structural and optical properties as a function of the grinding time: the crystallite sizes decrease with increasing milling time, additionally there is a shift of the peak (101) in x-ray diffraction to the left while for Raman, the change in the position of the E2 high Raman band is evident. The above can be attributed to change in the lattice parameter and crystallite size as an effect of the mill process
