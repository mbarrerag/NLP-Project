# Energy transition in the automotive land transport sector to zero- and low-emission technologies

The energy transition in the transport sector is a global effort oriented to the reduction of greenhouse gas emissions (GHG) and to the fulfilment of climate commitments. Since transport represents 24% of the total CO2 emissions, its decarbonisation is a priority. Although there are policies to promote the energy transition in this sector, its effectiveness and the assessment of the impact of the strategies implemented are favoured by the availability of assessment tools that allow to measure accurately and continuously their results. To have an adequate tool would facilitate the identification of the most effective initiatives and would allow to make timely adjustments based on the evidence obtained, which would contribute to more informed decision making This research presents a simulation platform for the integrated evaluation of energy transition policies in the terrestrial transport over time, calibrated mainly with data from Bogotá-Colombia, however, it also made an application for Mexico in the pickup category. This tool allows to analyze four categories of vehicles: buses, taxis, trucks (up to 10.5 tons) and light vehicles (including cars and SUVs, as pickups)., could reduce total energy demand. Likewise, the results show that the design and implementation of differentiated policies according to category and vehicle technology, together with solid incentives for zero and low-emission technologies, favour the energy transition in terms of emissions reduction and energy demand. However, the costs associated with the implementation of these policies and the promotion of transition are high, and should be analysed in detail by decision-makers.
