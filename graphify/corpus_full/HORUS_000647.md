# Pumpkin powder with co-products seeds and peel: effect on the physicochemical and functional properties by spray drying

The incorporation of pumpkin co-products (peel and seed) rich in phenolic and fat-soluble bioactive compounds, together with its pulp, allowed the development of a new pumpkin powder mixture (PPM) by spray drying (SD). The main factors that can affect its physicochemical and techno-functional quality and bioactive compounds are: inlet air temperature (IAT), outlet air temperature (OAT), atomizing disk speed (ADS), and carrier agent concentration (Maltodextrin, MD). The aim of the study was to evaluate the influence of these factors on moisture content (Xm), water activity (aw), volume mean diameter (D[4;3]), CIE-L*a*b* color, cold water solubility (CWS), oil absorption capacity (OAC), total phenols (TP), antioxidant capacity by DPPH• and ABTS+•, β-carotene (β-car), and α-tocopherol (α-toc) of the PPM. The response surface methodology with a central composite design was used for optimization. SD conditions were significant (p < 0.05) in the evaluated variables, excluding aw, L*, and b*. The optimal conditions were achieved: IAT: 172.3 °C, OAT: 81.6 °C, ADS: 23,893 rpm and MD: 4.0 %. PPM was obtained with Xm: 6.9 % db (dry basis) ± 0.7, CWS: 89.8 ± 1.2 %, OAC: 0.47 ± 0.00 g oil/g db, TP: 450.3 ± 23.3 mg gallic acid equivalent (GAE)/100 g db, DPPH•: 1090.4 ± 78.1 μmol Trolox Eq ​​(TE)/100 g db and ABTS•+: 817.0 ± 52.2 μmol TE/100 g db, 21.0 ± 1.5 mg β-car/100 g db, and 1.3 ± 0.3 mg α-toc/100 g db. The quadratic regression method allowed optimizing the SD conditions to obtain a PPM with the best quality characteristics and functional properties
