# EXPLORATIONS IN THE SURCENTIAL CARIBBEAN: RENEWAL OF THE SPANISH MIRADE ON THE LITORAL TERRITORIES OF CENTRAL AMERICA, 1750-1800.

This chapter analyses how, in the context of imperial confrontations between Spain and England, in the eighteenth century, territories that had had little importance for the Hispanic monarchy in the Caribbean, because of the absence of precious metals or because they lacked geostrategic value, acquired importance, not only because they offered the possibility of neutralizing the interests and influences of enemies, but because, in many cases, as in the south-western Caribbean, the dispute over control of resources and ports became an obsession in the imperial struggle, given the demands of new products in European markets. In the eighteenth century, with the arrival of the enlightened vision and the Borbonic reform process, many of the strategies to sustain the empire were transformed.
