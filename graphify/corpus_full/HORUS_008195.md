# A new model for estimating living biomass and carbon stored in mangrove forests using remote sensing and machine learning: case study Tumaco-Nariño

The mangroves play a crucial role in mitigating climate change by absorbing and retaining up to five times more carbon than other forests. It is important to determine the living biomass and carbon stored in these ecosystems to provide a solid basis in government planning and management. This study presents an innovative approach since it uses remote sensing tools together with data collected in field, Worldview images 2 and evaluates two machine learning algorithms, Random Forest and Support Vector Regression. The case study in the Tumaco mangrove, Nariño, included the calculation of the forest surface by means of a supervised classification, the estimation of the live biomass (aboveground and below) and carbon stored, and the evaluation of the models. The results revealed a global accuracy of 87% in the coverage classification, with average values of 192.50 ± 102.78 for the aerial biomass, 79.95 ± 56.85 for the underground biomass and 127.43 ± 73.49 for the carbon stored.
