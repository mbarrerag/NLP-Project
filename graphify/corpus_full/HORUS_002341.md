# The impact of plant cover on soil erosion in the drainage network of banana cultivation

In Urabá (Colombia), precipitation generates high soil erosion rates in banana drainage systems due to the intensity, frequency and susceptibility of the soil resulting from exposure. An alternative to mitigate this is the use of plant cover. The objective of this study was to determine the impact of such coverage on soil erosion rates in the drainage systems of a banana plantation. To this end, a comparison was made during the last quarter of 2022 between the erosion rates measured experimentally, simulated in a greenhouse, and the erosion rates observed using sedimentation boxes in the drainage channels in field, together with a potential estimate using the USLE equation. In the greenhouse, the soils without coverage presented higher losses with 38.16 t ha-1 year-1, differentiating statistically from conventional management treatments (TMC) and plant coverage (TCV), which had values of 24,70 and 18,97 t ha-1 year-1 year-1, respectively. This trend was observed similarly in the field.
