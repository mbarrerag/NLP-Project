# Open City Model: Improving Public-Private Relationship in Race Corridor 3 between 19th and 22nd Streets, Bogotá

This final master's work (TFM) is proposed as the topic to study the public-private relationship on the ground floor, in response to a certain tendency in some architectural projects to move away from the problems of the city, ignoring its urban environment and its relation with the street. This problem is presented notably in the urban part of the race corridor 3 between 26th and 19th streets, in the city of Bogotá. The TFM was designed from three phases: a theoretical, an analytical and a propositional one. In the first phase the relationship between architectural solutions and the hierarchy of the streets was identified, the good practices were defined and 8 strategies to solve the problems identified in the analytical phase. In the second phase the analysis of the site was carried out that allowed the identification of the bad practices for the public-private relationship and 8 problems were defined. In the third phase the urban project references, the urban and the urban design work group were identified and the three-scale strategies were proposed to solve the problems identified in the analytical phase. To improve the public-private relationship the project implements 7 typological operations built from the 8 concepts, 8 problems and 8 strategies, in a design piece, which are focused on the urban and the four-scale.
