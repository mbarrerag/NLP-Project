# Evaluation of the functionality and quality of life of adult patients with traumatic amputations and users of lower limb prosthesis in the Central Military Hospital.

The rehabilitation of the patient with amputation due to military trauma has focused on achieving maximum functionality, exploring other aspects of the quality of life subjectively. To evaluate the functionality and quality of life of adult patients with traumatic amputation of lower limbs users of prosthesis, who attend the consultation of prosthesis and amputees. This is a prospective descriptive study of cross-section; where 72 patients over 18 years of age with traumatic amputation of lower limbs were included, who attended the consultation of prosthesis and amputees of the Central Military Hospital, between 2020 and 2021. The questionnaires were applied at one time: PEQ - MS, WHOQOL-BREF and SF-36 V 1.0 RAND and were performed with physical tests: 2-MWT, L-TEST and TUGT. 72 patients were included, in their total men. It was found that 70.8% had a transtibial amputation level. The most frequent side of amputation was the patient's extreme health in 58,3% of cases, with an average time of 12 years (DE) lower tremity present an optimal quality of life, being the favorable health state, besides presenting both mobility and adequate functionality; These results are a first approximation to the functional characteristics of adults amputated in our community and can serve as a basis for defining therapeutic behaviors
