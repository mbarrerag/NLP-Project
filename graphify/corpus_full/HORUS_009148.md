# Bioinspired algorithm of robot network for assistance in search and rescue operations

This thesis proposes a bioinspired algorithm for networks of robots that assist in the operations of search and rescue scenarios. We consider ants as social animals to study and abstract behaviors that can be useful in the framework of search and rescue by robots. We consider three main topics to address when robots are used to help rescuers. First, the exploration and mapping of disaster zones. For this, we consider the mechanisms and interactions of ants to explore their environment, search for food, avoid predators and explore better places to establish a nest. Then, we deploy robots to explore the environment and discourage robots from entering regions that other robots have explored using pheromones as markers for robots. We also abstract the randomness that ants use to explore and implement a Q-learning algorithm that allows robots to explore regions not visited. Second, navigation and detection of vıctimas.
