# Affective Interstice: A New Decolonial Feminist Approach to Public Policy Analysis

The traditional and interpretive-critical paradigms of public policy analysis have underestimated the role of emotions in the understanding of reality, the questioning of domination relations, decision-making, social change and the construction of knowledge. It is therefore necessary to explore alternative approaches from Latin American knowledge that address these limitations and contribute to the field from a more complex perspective. The research adopted a transdisciplinary approach and focused on the critical analysis of public policies of childhood cancer in the National Institute of Cancerology E.S.E. (INC). This ethnographic design complemented with art-based research, the methodology involved 14 months of participant observation with auto-ethnography, photo-ethnography and analysis of cultural artifacts. 32 interviews were carried out and 40 workshops of artistic creation were organized; 39 governmental documents were analyzed, and the scientific literature of public health, political science, psychology of health, psychoanalysis, sociology, anthropology and philosophy were used. The application of the T.I.A. in the implementation of child cancer policies in the INC reveals that the hegemonic modes of public health policies, together with the discourse of cancer control, have a significant impact on the perception of the health-disease-care process. This generates an emotional dynamic that perpetuates significant factors related to commodification, medicalization, new public management, adult-centrism and the neoliberal approach to participation in health.
