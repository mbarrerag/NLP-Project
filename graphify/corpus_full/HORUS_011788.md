# ENVIRONMENTAL CONTROL MEASURES FOR THE CONTROL OF TUBBERCLESIS

Tuberculosis has been present in the global and national epidemiological scenario, in the history of humanity and its presence in hospital settings has been documented in scientific literature.Continuous contact between patients with tuberculosis and health personnel is a risk factor in workplaces, which leads to these workers being more likely to be at risk of infection, compared to the general population. [1,2] It is important to remember that bacilli that generate the disease are aerobic, grow slowly and are resistant to adverse environmental conditions. Its only reservoir is human beings and its predominant transmission form is given by air, by means of infectious droplets of people with pulmonary tuberculosis, without treatment, that expel them when speaking, coughing, sneezing and that can remain in the air for hours. Other forms of transmission have been little documented. According to Decker [3], the probability of contracting the disease depends on the concentration of nuclei of droplets in aerosol, the duration of exposure to infectious persons or the carrying out of aerosol-generating procedures
