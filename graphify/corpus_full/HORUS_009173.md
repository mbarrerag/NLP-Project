# Mucocutaneous lesions associated with lymphoproliferative disorder: one case report

Leukaemia is a hematological cancer characterized by the accumulation of mature or immature cells in the bone marrow (MO), peripheral blood, lymph nodes and lymphoid tissues. These cells can cause extranodal infiltration into tissues such as the skin (leukemia cutis [LC]) and into the mucous membranes, being these rare locations. Presentation of the case. 24-year-old man with painful submaxis, nasal obstruction and skin lesions who attended the emergency department of a second-level hospital in Bogotá D.C., Colombia, where leukocyte reaction was reported in hemogram and blasts in peripheral blood smears, so that he was referred to a university hospital where MO tests and computed tomography were performed, in which he observed peripheral lymphatic involvement and opacity of sinus cells (PNS) maxillary. Subsequently, the patient underwent functional endoscopic surgery of PN and biopsies of the skin lesion of the PNS. Based on the results of the MNE tests, the patient was diagnosed with acute lymphous cells (PN)
