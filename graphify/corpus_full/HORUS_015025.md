# The allegation against abusive power: the search for legal protection and its constitutional practice by the New Granadans between 1781 and 1820.

This paper shows that, in the period between 1781 and 1820, in the transition between the former viceroyalty of New Granada and the Republic of Colombia, the meanings of essential words as a constitution were not only transformed, expanded or supplemented by semantic references that opened up to a horizon of expectations anchored to the enlightened revolutions, but that those meanings were fixed in the scope of the dispute against the authorities, the Old Regime or the revolutionary era. These disputes, which appear in the sources under the name of grievances or protection, are called in this the category of argument against abusive power, which not only refers to the type of arbitrary power that, economically, exercised by some authorities emanating from the Spanish Crown, but also to individuals who, deprived of public authority, abused any dominant social position to overwhelm other people. In this sense, it is a work around the history of the constitution, but also of its assimilation by subalternal classes that are usually marginalized from the historical narrative
