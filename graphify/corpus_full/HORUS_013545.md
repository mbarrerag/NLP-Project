# Looking for Nemo in the Blue Ocean: How Communication Influences Entrepreneurial Performance

This study aimed to evaluate the impact of different teaching methods of soft persuasive communication ability, focusing on presentation sequence, within groups of participants through a workshop. Specific objectives included comparing groups to identify the impact of the training order on persuasive communication and to study changes in communication skills before and after the workshop. It was intended to investigate the impact of specific stimulus sequences on the communication skills of entrepreneurs, suggesting a more effective methodology. In addition, it was proposed in a complementary way that there could be improvements in persuasive communication with training, regardless of the sequence. The results, based on a sample of 40 participants, indicated a diverse demographic and entrepreneurial representation. The training workshop significantly improved overall competence in persuasive communication across groups, confirming the effectiveness of the intervention. The analysis of variance did not show a significant impact of the training order to the persuasive communication. An additional analysis showed that all groups experienced significant improvements in several subcompetencies, highlighting the groups with the most pronounced overall improvement.
