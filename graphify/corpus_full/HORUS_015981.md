# A PASSIVE PAPER-BASED BLOOD PLASMA SEPARATION MICROFLUIDIC DEVICE FOR POINT OF CARE DETECTION OF PROTEIN BIOMARKERS

Sample preparation is usually an important limitation for point of care (POC) devices. Whole blood contains several components that could create noise for the detection technique, reducing the device ́s capability to measure low concentration of a certain biomarker. In our group, we developed an electrochemical POC device for the quantification of S100B as new tool for the traumatic brain injury severity determination, and monitoring. This abstract shows the development of a plasma separator that allows us to use a whole blood sample in the measurements with results compared to those obtained when we use sample separated with traditional methods. The blood plasma separation (BPS) μPAD was designed based on a NaCl modified VF2 bound-glass fiber filter paper used as a sample collection pad and an MF1 filter paper for plasma retention, which together are capable of yielding 50 microliters of plasma after 3.5 minutes from 300 microliters of whole blood added on the VF2 sample collection pad. The BPS-μPAD efficiency was assessed by comparing the number of RBCs present after the separation is completed versus the number of RBCs in a whole blood sample from the same volunteer. Both samples were analyzed using an inverted light microscope, a scanning electron microscope (SEM) and image processing software (ImageJ).
