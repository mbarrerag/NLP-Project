# Towards strengthening climate change education with an interdisciplinary approach

Climate change is one of the most urgent challenges of the twenty-first century, linked to a crisis of thought and the model of production, consumption and unlimited economic growth. Education for Climate Change (ECC) is fundamental for the transition to sustainable socio-economic models, however, there is a gap in action that indicates insufficient progress in this area. This study analyzes 369 articles on ECC published between 2014 and 2024, in formal and informal education contexts, with the objective of generating guidelines to strengthen the ECC, exploring trends and approaches in research and practice. Through a systematic review of literature and bibliometric analysis, it is revealed that 79% of knowledge about ECCs comes from the global north, with the United States as a leader in production and citation, while the participation of the global south is limited. There are three epistemological approaches: positivism, social constructivism and critical transformational approaches. Since 2018, publications on ECC have grown exponentially, with a view to the importance of the global north, showing a growing convergence between approaches to climate justice and mental health.
