# LACTOBACILLUS SPP. INSAILED CACCOA TO REDUCY THE BIOACCESSIBILITY OF CD OR AS

The role of intestinal microbiota in the absorption of toxic elements in the human organism and the consumption of probiotics as an alternative to detoxify the human body and prevent diseases has been an important research topic recently. The aim of this study was to assess the potential of lactic acid bacteria isolated from cocoa in the fermentation process, to remove cadmium and arsenic under in vitro conditions of acidity and salinity that simulate the human digestive system, in order to verify its potential in future applications of detoxification. A total of 38 strains of Lactobacillus spp. resistant to Cd(II) or As(III) were isolated from cocoa fermented in Caldas, Colombia, and identified by molecular methods. The most resistant bacteria in Cd(II) or As(III) were selected by the minimum concentration Inhibitory, and/ or by the group of this group. The most tolerant plants in vitro conditions of acidity and salinity were selected by the reference strain L. plantumin, JCM 1055 and the NS of L. planta, plantample, and L. plantarum C16, and L. Cd(II) or As(III) that could be evaluated by in vivo models
