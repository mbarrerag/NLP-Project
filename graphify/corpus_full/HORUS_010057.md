# JOSE MARIA VERGARA AND VERGARA, THE AGIOTIST: THE INVENTION OF THE CREDITICIAL BODY OF NATION IN THE TABLES OF COSTUMBRES

From a rereading of the work of Colombian conservative lawyer José María Vergara and Vergara (1831-1872) in light of the financial anxieties of the mid-century, this article argues that the writing of types and customs is an aesthetic of accreditation. Although recent research has emphasized their biopolitical role, these valuable contributions have overlooked how these tables invented the nation’s credit corps. In them, the Creoles put in circulation types whose moral traits, inscribed in the body, functioned as an ethnofinancial profile that assessed their supposed potential to create value. If in a geopolitical plane, the Creole imagines its literature as an imitation of European genres that is negotiated at discount, is conceived within the state-nation the Creole as the universal equivalent.
