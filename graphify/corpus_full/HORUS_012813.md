# A deep learning approach for image-based semantic segmentation with preserved interpretation

Semantic segmentation is fundamental in several industries and shows its significant impact in numerous applications. Semantic segmentation offers valuable information that drives advances in fields such as autonomous driving, surveillance, robotics and augmented reality by allowing precise identification and labelling of objects within an image. The precise segmentation of objects allows autonomous vehicles to navigate in complex environments, surveillance systems detect and trace specific objects, robots handle objects efficiently and augmented reality applications combine perfectly virtual objects with the real world. However, in the medical industry, the importance of semantic segmentation has become truly profound. Medical imaging techniques, such as computerized tomography and magnetic resonances, generate large amounts of data that require a meticulous annotation for their analysis, and thus reducing the need for medical imaging, is an analysis of the manual notation and the precise analysis of the medical images. This variability leads to differences in form, size and texture, which makes it difficult for models of segmentation to delineate and analyze regions of interest with precision. Consequently, guaranteeing consistent and reliable segmentation results in various medical images has remained a critical challenge. Furthermore, there is a pressing need for systematic and quantitative assessments of the interpretation in models of segmentation based on deep learning. Without such assessments, trusting in these models for clinical decision making becomes a challenge. Physicians must understand in a comprehensive way how and why these models reach their conclusions to incorporate them into their practice with confidence. The absence of standardized evaluation methods prevents progress in the construction of systems of analysis of interpretable and reliable medical images. This work addresses the challenges in the segmentation of medical images.
