# POLLUTION BY MERCOSUR IN ARTESANAL COMMERCIAL IMPORTANCE FISHES OF THE COASTERS AREAS OF THE CAUCA AND MAGDALENA VALLE

Arti-craft fishing is one of the main socio-economic activities and food source for coastal area inhabitants, so that mercury pollution in marine organisms represents a risk for the food security of coastal communities and human health in general. This study assessed the concentration of total mercury (THg) in the muscle (d.w.) of 64 fish, between the bays of Bunaventad and Malaga, Pacific and 75 fish, in the coastal areas of Magdalena, Colombian Caribbean. Two families (Lutjanidae and Carangidae) were assessed in one group with four species each, the fish were caught by artisanal mesh-fish and hook in 2023. The average concentration of mercury for all fish was 0.696±0.572 μg/gg/g in the Pacific and 0.588±0,460 μg/g in the Caribbean. In the coastal areas of the Cauca Valley, the highest concentration of THg was for the family Lutjanidae (1,011±0,081,811 μg/g/g/ggg), while for the coastal areas of the Magdalena was for Carangidae, 0, 65/65 μ μ μ μ μg/μg/g These results show increased THg contamination in Pacific and Caribbean fish, contrasting with similar studies in Mexico (C. caninus) and Cartagena Bay (Caranx crysos ¿L. synagris), respectively. The average THg levels of 47% of the species evaluated for both Pacific and Caribbean exceeded the WHO recommended limits for the general population (0.5 μg/g). 93% of the individuals evaluated in coastal areas of the Cauca Valley and 85% of the individuals in the Magdalena coastal areas were found above the recommended concentration for the vulnerable population (0.2 μg/g), so that they could put at risk fishing communities, their families and coastal rural settlements, where fish are essential for their daily diet
