# Methodology for predicting student performance in virtual university courses at a distance.

The massive increase in distance virtual university courses in universities and institutions of higher education worldwide has led to the increase in the generation of information related to student academic performance; this information can be used to predict academic performance and prevent academic mortality and dropout. Based on the results of the systematic literature review, it was identified that there is no methodology that allows teachers of virtual university courses to predict student academic performance at a distance; some researches present classification exercises on student performance; but they do not establish a formal procedure that can be employed by teachers of any area of knowledge that dictates this type of courses. The main contribution of this doctoral research is the creation of a methodology to predict academic performance (Atest/Review) in virtual university courses at a distance. In short, the methodology is shaped by the following steps; determining the variables to be analyzed, building the database, building the models of prediction, evaluating the models and visualizing the prediction. The methodology goes beyond Machine Learning since this considers relevant aspects of the educational context that must be considered for the predictions to be meaningful. It concludes that the methodology formulated has a high precision and involves academic factors
