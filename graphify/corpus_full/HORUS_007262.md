# Effects of burning on the composition and function of microbial soil communities in the grassland savannah of Arauca, Arauca, Colombia

The soil is a complex mineral and biological matrix. It inhabits different groups of microorganisms, which fulfil specific functions in the nutrient cycle (e.g., carbon and nitrogen) and interact dynamically with each other. Physical, biological and chemical disorders can affect these interactions, generating decrease in soil quality. These include the burning and forest fires, whose effects on soil microorganisms have been studied during the last years, showing changes in the richness and abundance of species. Among the systems prone to burning or forest fires we find the savannas of Arauca, located in the region of Orinoquia de Colombia. This ecosystem is susceptible to burning due to its vegetation (e.g. herbaceous and grassy), climatology (unimodal regime) and agricultural activities related to the chronicities (e.g. extensive livestock). This work evaluated the effect of the burnings in the pastical soil of the Municipality of Arauca, Arauca, Colombia.
