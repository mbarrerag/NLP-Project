# Transcribal and physiological dynamics of response to progressive water deficit in gulupa.

Climate change, especially water scarcity, has had within its consequences, some devastating for agriculture in the last 50 years; directly threatening the food security of the communities involved in food production and other consumers. For the 21st century, the change in the terms of drought is expected in different parts of the world, resulting from an increase in the evapotranspiration, accompanied by a reduction in precipitation. In the Andean area, its effect has been mainly seen in the accelerated decline of glaciers, which provide water to the surrounding agricultural systems, and therefore a decrease in the water supply capacity of the Andean communities and their agricultural systems is expected. Colombia is a country that is trying to position itself in agricultural exports but due to climate events such as the water deficit, many of these crops are threatened. Among these are the cultivation of gulupa or fruit of the passion (Passiflora edulores Sims f. edulis), fruit whose export has grown considerably in the last decade, generating economic inputs to the country around 35 million dollars. Understanding the different response mechanisms and being able to integrate the different levels, molecular, biochemical and physiological, is a proposal currently widely used to feed the foundations of improvement programs, in this way protecting food security
