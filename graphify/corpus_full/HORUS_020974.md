# Study of electrooptic properties of semiconductive coupled point-ring nanostructures and their preliminary evaluation as candidates for applications in quantum information systems

The progressive evolution of the growth techniques of nanometric semiconductor structures, motivated by the processes of miniaturization in the field of electronics, has recently allowed the obtaining of a novel system called acuple epitaxial quantum point-ring phenomenon (AEPAC) [1,2]. This nanosystem manufactured by epitaxis drops can be conceived as a double structure, constituted by a quantum point (PC) of InAs surrounded by a quantum ring (AC) of GaAs [1]. Alternatively, both the PC and AC, can be manufactured in GaAs and subsequently be embedded in a matrix of AlGaAs [2]. As is known, the systems of few particles (SPPs) as electrons, neutral or ionized donors that are accommodated in the AEPAC, will experience the effects of containment due to the low dimension of the structure and this would lead to the discretization of their energy spectrum [3,4]. In addition, the behavior of the PPPs in AEPACs and their physical observables would be described appropriately by the quantum, SPPs [3state and the functional functions. One of the physical quantities that allow to evaluate its potential as candidates for quantum switches is optical absorption. AEPAC is very interesting system since the PPPs that could be accommodated in it, would not only experience the quantum containment effects of a single structure (such as a PC, a strand or quantum well, etc.); but that could additionally result in the emergence of quantum tunneling and coupling of spin between particles that reside separately in the PC and AC. This latter phenomenon has projected the nanostructures in general as candidates to form the blocks of quantum computing systems (qubits) that promise new paradigms in the manipulation and processing of information [6,7]. Considering the above elements, in this proposal it is desired to carry out the numerical calculation of the self-states and optical absorption of some SPPs confined in AEPACs in the presence of external fields. This will allow to determine their electrooptic properties from fundamental physics for potential technological applications. In addition, the potential of these systems to be implemented in applications of quantum information will be evaluated in a preliminary way
