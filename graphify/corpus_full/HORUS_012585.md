# Development of a scale for assessment of disease severity and impact of tomato brown rugose fruit virus on tomato yield

Tomato brown rugose fruit virus (ToBRFV), belonging to the genus Tobamovirus, is an emerging threat with presence and impact in different tomato-producing countries in the world, including Mexico. The two-fold objective of this study was to design and validate a severity disease scale for ToBRFV in leaves and leaflets of tomato cvs. Macizo and TOP-2299, as well as to quantify the effect of ToBRFV on fruit production in commercial tomato cv. TOP-2299 greenhouses. For the validation of the severity scales at the leaf and leaflet level, 25 raters with two repetitions at different times were used, evaluating four models through a Confirmatory Factor Analysis, while for the evaluation of disease effect on tomato production, fruit weight (cv. TOP-2299) was recorded in five greenhouses. The cvs. TOP-2299 and Macizo exhibited foliar symptoms such as mosaics, blistering, distortion, and reduction of the leaf blade. On fruits, both cultivars showed symptoms of roughness, deformation, brown spots, and irregular ripening. Additionally, the presence of ToBRFV was confirmed from fruit and leaf samples of both cultivars through RT-PCR using specific primers. The severity scales developed showed a better fit in leaflets than in leaves and improved the existing descriptive scales. In terms of yield, there was a 25 to 40% reduction in the average fruit weight of cv. TOP-2299. This study demonstrated the importance of using a severity scale for the disease caused by ToBRFV in tomato, in addition to estimating yield losses in greenhouses with commercial tomato production
