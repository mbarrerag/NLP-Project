# CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE OF IL-17A AND IL-20 AS POTENTIAL PROGNOSTIC BIOMARKERS IN SYNOVIAL TISSUE OF PATIENTS WITH EARLY KNEE OSTEOARTHRITIS

Background/Objectives: Primary osteoarthritis (OA) is a chronic cartilage destructive disease, caused by genetic risk factors, obesity, aging, with high incidence in the population (1). Is considered late diagnosis and no clinical and serological biomarkers to establish a diagnosis in early stages. There is evidence of the inflammatory component of the synovium as a pathophysiological mechanism triggering joint involvement. Therefore, it is suggested that identifying the inflammatory component of the synovium by determining interleukin-17 (IL-17A) and interleukin-20 (IL-20) in isolated fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) and mesenchymal cells (MSC) in culture would help to understand the associated pathophysiological processes, and could be early biomarkers of the disease (2). To isolate FLS and MSC from knee synovial biopsy and evaluate IL-17A and IL-20 expression level and cellular distribution. The study included patients selected by a rheumatologist. Synovial membrane samples were collected from 10 patients diagnosed with early knee OA by American College of Rheumatology (ACR) Criteria with minimal involvement grade 2 on Kellgren and Lawrence scale, from which FLS and MSC were isolated until confluence was reached and stimulated with TNF-alpha for 24h. The expression level and cellular distribution of IL-17A and IL-20 were qualitatively evaluated by fluorescence microscopy. FLS and MSC are key effector cells of the synovial tissue, their isolation allowed understanding the physiological, morphological and molecular processes that occur in patients with early knee OA, providing a cellular model to determine the culture requirements and optimal conditions for their development, facilitating the study of the characteristics that promote growth and morphological changes in response to a proinflammatory stimulus. This contributes to the characterization of both FLS and MSC to study early knee OA. Cell cultures were established from which MSC and FLS were isolated (Figure 1), with which the intracellular secretion of IL-17A and IL-20 was identified by means of fluorescence microscopy where both molecules were observed at cytoplasmic level, such fluorescence intensity allowed evidencing the presence of these cytokines in an inflammatory environment (Figure 3); IL-17A and IL-20 production levels were also quantified including quantification in synovial fluid by ELISA technique (Figure 2). Based on these findings, the involvement of IL-17A and IL-20 in the pathophysiology of OA suggests their potential as supportive prognostic markers whose increased levels are indicative of inflammation caused by the disease. Further studies involving a larger number of patients would be necessary to establish their usefulness
