# Ecosystem Services and Human Welfare: Regulatory and sourcing services for the population of the Southwest of Antioquia, in a context of climate change

The accelerated loss of biodiversity, driven by deforestation and climate change, has deteriorated ecosystems and affected human well-being. In Colombia, Antioquia has experienced a significant reduction of forests, mainly due to livestock and agricultural expansion. This research aims to assess the relationship between ecosystem services of sediment retention, carbon capture, food supply and water supply and human well-being in the Southwest of Antioquia, in a climate change context, so that at first the degree of transformation of natural ecosystems was determined in 2002 and 2018, then the offer of ecosystem services was estimated through the software InVEST, and finally, the offer of human well-being and climate change projections, using the Pearson method and the software GeoDa for correlations and ArcGIS 10.4 for the analysis of results. The findings indicate a loss of approximately 23,526 hectares of forest, mainly converted into agricultural and grassland areas and, although the most preserved ecosystems offer more ecosystem services evaluated, the relationship with the human well-being is not associated with the results. 2002 and 2018 was determined, then the supply of ecosystem services was estimated using the InVEST software. Ultimately, the ES supplied was related to climate change projections and human well-being, using the Pearson method and GeoDa software for correlations, and ArcGIS 10.4 for results visualization. The findings indicates to the rest of approximately 23,526 hectares of forest, mainly transformed into agricultural areas and pastures. Although the most conserved ecosystems have a higher supply of the ES evaluated, the relationship with human well-being is not linear: in 2002, great availability of services was associated with low well-being, while in 2018 it was the alternative.
