# A DISCUSSION ON ADHABITIONS FROM ANPHIBIUM TO LOW EARLY: TROPICAL ALTURES IN PERSPECTIVE

In the last 30 years, there has been an interesting amount of associated information The tropical and subtropical contexts of the Andean mountains have presented biomes with pronounced thermal conditions such as the high Andean forests, the punas and the moors. Within these systems the freezing temperatures must have had a structural and limiting role in ecological radiations. In addition, there is important latitudinal variation that particularly affects the temporal patterns associated with the risk of freezing, so that daily variation gains importance in the tropics. Studies on the ecology and physiology of high Andean amphibians have grown over the last 10 years, being clear now that there are ecological, physiological and behavioral characteristics integrated into adaptive processes. Reports highlight the importance of selecting thermal shelters (in space and time), accumulation of chronic protetors in certain cases associated to circadian cycles, and high capacity for locomotion with very low body temperatures.
