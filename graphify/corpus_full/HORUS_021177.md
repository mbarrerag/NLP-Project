# Effect of intake of a drink with glycerol and sodium on hydration and maximum aerobic potency of Colombian endurance cyclists

One of the variables with great impact on sports performance is the hydroelectrolytic equilibrium, which translates into the search for the homeostasis of water and electrolytes lost by the body as a result of physical and/or sport activity (Vega et al, 2016).Dehydration by both the volume of the sport, which can affect the performance of the sport, may result in loss of the thermoregulating effect of the organism causing states of hypothermia (Body temperature <35°C), drastically decrease the concentration of minerals in blood and with this result the correct use of the body (Urdampilleta ET AL, 2013). In the sports nutrition guides, a variety of nutritional ergonomic aids such as sports drinks, glycerol or sodium, which seek to promote and preserve the optimal hydration state for an adequate performance. The drinks have been studied and approved for use by the sports, especially the resistance modes and are characterized by providing a balanced amount of liquids and electrolytes that allow the athlete to hydrate in the exercise, avoid, decrease and/ delay these states of dehydration and improve performance (IS, and has been used by the sports agents, especially. l Anti-Doping Agency) in 2010 and, after its removal from the list of prohibited substances in 2018, the studies around it have been minimal. In addition, the few previous and current studies have evaluated its effect on hydration and performance variables using hydration protocols, doses, added fluids and very variable control beverages and mostly on individuals trained recreationally, finding inconsistent results at the level of the magnitude of the effect, but consistent with the level of hydration and performance variables and with high ergogenic potential (van Rosendal et al., 2015).
