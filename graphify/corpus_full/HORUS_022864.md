# GENOMIC SURVEILLANCE OF SARS-COV-2 IN THE CITY OF SANTIAGO DE CALI, COLOMBIA

The SARS-CoV-2 coronavirus responsible for the COVID-19 pandemic has tested Latin American health systems, making it imminent to carry out surveillance studies and implement capabilities to detect imported cases and limit transmission. Therefore, the genomic surveillance of SARS-CoV-2 using portable sequencing technologies such as Oxford Nanopore, has been proposed as a quick and effective means to evaluate the evolution of the pandemic. In the city of Santiago de Cali in Colombia, 281,516 cases of COVID-19 have been reported between March 2020 and September 2021, of which 400 complete genome sequences are available. Phylogenetic and bioinformatics analyses were carried out to obtain information on the circulation of viral lineages, demonstrating the presence of variants of interest (VOI) such as Lambda and Mu and variants of concern (VOC) such as Alpha, Gamma and Delta; allowing to describe the dynamics of these in the city. In addition, a comparison was made of the geographical distribution of SA-CoV-2 isolates of Cali with those reported in GISAID for the use of the Department of the Cauca Valley
