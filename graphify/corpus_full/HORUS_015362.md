# A CONTROLLED DRUG DELIVERY SYSTEM BASED ON BIOMOF ASSEMBLED FROM PROTOCATECHUIC ACID AND MAGNESIUM

In the last years, biological metal-organic frameworks (bioMOFs) have been studied as controlled drug delivery systems thanks to their organism biocompatibility. This is because biomolecules are used as organic linkers, being amino acids, peptides, and cyclodextrin, among others, the most studied, leaving aside phenolics acid with their outstanding antioxidant properties. Here, the study of controlled delivery in water and phosphate buffer solution (pH 4.0 and 7.2) of a new bioMOF based on protocatechuic acid (PCA), and their preliminary evaluation as a drug carrier, using cannabidiol (CBD) as a drug model, is presented. The PCA and CBD delivery profiles were studied through release kinetic between 0 ¿ 48 hours and 0 ¿ 8 hours, respectively. The results show PCA-controlled delivery and some differences in quantity release depending on the pH of the medium. In the case of CBD, similar behavior in the pH of the medium, and a low percentage of CBD release were observed. This preliminary study encourages deeply the optimization of conditions to achieve a higher drug loaded in the new bioMOF studied
