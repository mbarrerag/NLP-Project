# - MARITIME EXTRACTIVISM, DISPOSSION AND RESISTANCE IN RURAL AND ETHNIC COMMUNITIES OF THE COLOMBIAN CARIBBEAN

This article offers a general overview of the impacts of the extractive development model promoted by the Colombian state on the maritories of peasant, Afro-descendant and indigenous communities in the Caribbean region. It reflects on the implications of gradual entry into national public policy, of notions as a blue economy. It briefly analyzes the impacts of activities such as port infrastructure, the exploitation of hydrocarbons and minerals, tourism and industrial fishing, using some examples collected in different places in the region, which demonstrate the growing processes of dispossession. The article ends with a reflection on the various forms of resistance that these communities use today to face the dispossession of their places of life associated with the sea and the coast
