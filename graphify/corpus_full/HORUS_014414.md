# Provide spectral analysis for soil organic carbon estimation in citrus crops.

The spectral analysis has emerged as an efficient alternative for the study and characterization of soil properties versus conventional methods. The organic soil carbon (COS) is a key indicator to understand the state of the soil and to be able to develop sustainable practices of land use. This research work evaluates the potential of the spectral analysis for the estimation of the COS in citrus crops in the municipality of Simatota in the department of Santander. To this end, protocols were adjusted and applied for the taking of soil samples and for the execution of spectral measurements. In total 490 soil samples were acquired in the region, to which spectral signatures were taken in the visible range (Vis) from 400 to 900 nm and in the range of the near infrared (NIR) from 900 to 2500 nm. Different preprocessing methods were applied to the spectral data. The Spectrals were used to improve the spectral characteristics and reduce the noise, as well as methods of dimensionality, with which the most important wavelengths for the estimation were identified
