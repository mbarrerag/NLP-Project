# Proteomic analysis of the bee Apis mellifera exposed to herbicide glyphosate and imidacloprid insecticide

At present, herbicides such as glyphosate and insecticides are used as imidacloprid in order to control pests or weeds or arvense flora that interfere in food production at a global level to support population increase. Uncontrolled use of these pesticides causes an annual reduction in the number of pollinating agents, including bees. The studies aimed at analyzing the effects of pesticides on bees are increasing and have reported conditions at both the metabolic and neurological levels, associating these conditions with the losses of these pollinators. In this work, the differential expression variations of head and chest proteins-abdomen were studied A. mellifera treated acutely with subletal doses of the herbicide glyphosate and the imidacloprid insecticide. In this study, the analysis of protein extracts both head and thorto-abdomen were evaluated, the results of these were obtained from the ammonium bicarbonate and the results of the precipitation (trichlorinal acid, trichlorinated acid). The imidacloprid affects the transcription of genes essential for the metabolism and cell structure causing motor and cognitive failures. In both the chest and abdomen, proteins related to oxidative stress and detoxification were found in high expression, suggesting specific defense mechanisms. The results show that glyphosate and imidacloprid, although with different action targets, generate adverse effects on the physiology of A. mellifera's Africanized bees. This study highlights the need to evaluate the effects of pesticides of common use in agriculture on bees, suggesting biotechnological approaches to sustainable development that protect these important pollinators .
