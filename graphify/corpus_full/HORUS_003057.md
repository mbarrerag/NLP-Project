# Rethinking the disciplinary role in Colombia: Is it really necessary for the Attorney General's Office to discipline elected officials?

This document presents the results of an investigation that seeks to question disciplinary control towards public officials of popular choice, when the sanctions imposed have the potential to affect political rights. The above, with the aim of criticizing the constitutional and legal framework, as well as the jurisprudence that has endorsed these functions and the arguments that have been used to maintain them. To this end, we ask about the entity that can exercise this power, looking from its constitutional history for what is the origin of these functions. Secondly, we explore what is the existing jurisprudence on the subject to reconstruct the jurisprudence line of the Council of State and the Constitutional Court. Third, we do a study compared with Mexico, Spain and Ecuador on the disciplinary control towards public servants of popular choice. Finally, we do a small conceptualization on democracy, political rights and democratic erosion.
