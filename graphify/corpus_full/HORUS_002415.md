# SY52A-06 HARMONIZING SEISMIC PATTERNS: EXPLORING THE BUCARAMANGA SEISMIC NEST THROUGH MUSIC FOR EVENT PREDICTION

Natural phenomena are governed by cycles, rhythms, waves, and repetitive patterns that reveal a profound regularity in the natural world. This concept of recurring patterns is fundamental not only to the physical processes observed in nature but also to the art of music. Music itself is built upon a foundation of rhythmic, tonal, and phrasing patterns, which create a coherent and harmonious auditory experience. This parallel between natural cycles and musical structures suggests an intriguing possibility: that the patterns observed in natural phenomena, such as seismic activity, rock strata succession, and variations in magnetic and gravimetric fields, could be translated into musical compositions. Seismic events, much like other natural occurrences, display cyclical patterns in both their frequency and recurrence. Understanding these patterns is crucial for scientists who aim to predict seismic events and mitigate their impact on communities. In this context, the magnitude of earthquakes could be conceptualized as a musical scale, while the intervals between seismic events might correspond to musical timings. By applying musical principles to seismic data, it is possible to create melodies that are not only aesthetically pleasing but could also reveal underlying patterns in seismic activity. An experienced musician might even be able to detect subtle tonalities that reflect seismic patterns, potentially anticipating future events in a way similar to how one anticipates the tonic in a musical piece. This approach offers a novel way to interpret seismic data and could enhance our understanding of the cyclical nature of earthquakes. To explore this innovative idea, a musical piece was created using seismic data from the Colombian Geological Service, specifically for the Bucaramanga Seismic Nest. This area is known for its high frequency of seismic activity, making it an ideal candidate for this experiment. The composition is based on seismic events recorded from June 1, 1993, to March 10, 2015, culminating with the significant 6.3 magnitude earthquake that occurred at 20:55:44. By converting the seismic data into musical structures, the goal is to investigate whether such compositions can provide insights into predicting seismic events. The musical piece derived from this seismic data is intended to be shared with musicians and researchers, inviting them to analyze and interpret the tonalities and patterns present in the composition. The hope is that these musical interpretations might uncover subdominant tonalities or patterns that could suggest the presence of future seismic activities. This approach not only bridges the gap between science and art but also opens new avenues for understanding seismic activity. By integrating scientific data with musical theory, this project aims to explore the potential for music to provide a unique perspective on natural phenomena. If successful, it could lead to innovative methods for predicting seismic events and enhance our ability to prepare for and respond to natural disasters. The intersection of music and science in this context represents a promising frontier for both fields, offering new insights and methodologies for analyzing and interpreting the natural world
