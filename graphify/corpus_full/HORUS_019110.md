# SCOPING REVIEW OF RESPIRATORY MUSCLE FIBER TYPE CHANGES IN COPD: IMPLICATIONS FOR CYSTIC FIBROSIS

Purpose/Hypothesis: Chronic Lower Respiratory Disease is the 4th leading cause of death in the USA. Two examples within this category are Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) and Cystic Fibrosis (CF). COPD is characterized by airflow blockage leading to air trapping and increased effort of breathing. Cystic fibrosis has a similar increased effort of breathing but is due to increased mucus and fibrosis in lung tissue. Studies have explored respiratory fiber type changes related to COPD; Little to no research has been done in CF. To determine diaphragm and skeletal respiratory muscles adaptation in COPD and to highlight the need for further research in CF. Number of Subjects: 149 control and 225 COPD subjects. A comprehensive search using PubMed was performed using keywords and phrases such as ¿Diaphragm¿ ¿respiratory muscles¿ ¿fiber type¿, and ¿COPD¿. Inclusion criteria were studies comparing two groups with at least one COPD group, biopsies of diaphragm/respiratory muscle, and must measure at least 1 of the following outcomes: fiber type proportions (%), cross-sectional area (CSA) (μm2) or Myosin Heavy Chain (MHC1) proportions (%) in humans. 903 results were found. 18 studies were included with their quality assessed via the PEDro scale. A separate PubMed search with the same keywords but replacing ¿COPD¿ with ¿CF¿ was performed resulting in 0 articles found. Two blinded individuals searched and reviewed the studies. Each of the 18 studies measured at least one of these outcomes: fiber type proportion, CSA, or MHC proportion. 8 studies reported proportions of type 1 fibers (T1F) in diaphragm biopsies of controls vs COPD with 6/8 studies finding significantly increased proportions of T1F in COPD groups. 1 study examined diaphragmatic fiber type changes in COPD patients following inspiratory muscle training (IMT) and found that IMT significantly increased the proportion of T1F. 5 studies examined proportions of MHC1 in diaphragm muscles of COPD groups vs controls with 4/5 studies finding a significant increase in MHC1 in COPD diaphragms compared to controls. 7 studies assessed T1F CSA in diaphragm biopsies of control and COPD groups with 3/7 studies finding that T1F CSA was significantly less than controls. The PEDro scores ranged from 3 to 8. COPD appears to elicit significant increases in the proportion of both T1F and MHC1 in the diaphragms of patients with COPD. IMT in patients with COPD may increase the proportion of T1F, but further research is warranted and evidence of the T1F CSA changes in the diaphragms of patients with COPD is unclear, needing further investigation. Clinical Relevance: This review found that structural changes in the diaphragm of COPD patients favor an increase in T1F characteristics, the proportion of which may be increased with IMT. It is speculated that similar adaptations may occur in other obstructive pulmonary diseases though evidence is lacking. Further research regarding respiratory muscle fiber type characteristic changes in patients with CF may provide insight into the effects and treatment of the disease
