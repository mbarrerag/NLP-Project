# Study of microbiota-hospital interaction by exposure to gliadine in an animal model of Caenorhabditis elegans

Gluten is a set of proteins found in different cereals, mainly in wheat (Triticum aestivum), rye (Secale cereale) and barley (Hordeum vulgare).The components that are associated with toxicity are prolamin-type proteins, including gliadin and glutenine, which in turn contain repetitions of proline-rich amino acids and glutamine.In the western diet, one of the widely consumed foods is wheat, present in many bakery products by its palatable organoleptic qualities.However, it has been reported that the digestion of gluten proteins is not total, partly due to the resistant chemical structure of prolamin. Depending on the immunological response of the body to gluten-based metabolic products, this could lead to the generation of sensitivities and gastrointestinal symptoms, which can even affect other systems and organs. It is there when it highlights the effect that the intestinal microbiota exerts in eubiosis, contributing to the degradation of gluten through the enzyme action of the proteins present there and related to the type of endopetipitis. elegans, and that allows generating an approximation of the knowledge that is faced with the effectiveness of the microbiota in the degradation of gluten and its beneficial effects on the health of the host
