# GENOMIC PREDICTION OF CLIMATE CHANGE ADAPTATION IN COMMON BEAN (PHASEOLUS VULGARIS L.) × TEPARY BEAN (P. ACUTIFOLIUS A. GRAY) INTER-SPECIFIC HYBRIDS AT THE CARIBBEAN COAST OF COLOMBIA

Bean species span a wide range of natural adaptation across the Americas. However, the polygenic nature of this adaptation often precludes its forecasting and utilization. Therefore, in this study we explored the feasibility of Genomic Prediction models of the adaptive potential in common bean (Phaseolus vulgaris L.), the drought/heat tolerant Tepary bean (P. acutifolius A. Gray), and their inter-specific hybrids, by relying on Bayesian regression algorithms to forecast novel sources of adaptation to the humid and dry sub-regions at the Caribbean coast of Colombia, where common bean typically exhibits maladaptation to extreme heat waves. A total of 87 advanced lines with inter-specific ancestries were Genotyped by Sequencing (GBS), leading to the discovery of 15,645 Single-Nucleotide Polymorphism (SNP) markers. Three yield components and two biomass variables were recorded for each genotype across three localities in the Colombian coast using a Completely Randomized Block Design (CRBD) with three repetitions at each locality. Genetic and phenotypic data were merged into several Bayesian regression models to identify the top genotypes with the best Genomic-Estimated Adaptive Values (GEAVs). The model with the best performance for all traits and environments was BayesC. Also, we compared the utilization of all markers and only those determined as associated by a priori Genome Wide Association Scan (GWAS) models. Better prediction ability with the complete SNP set was indicative of missing heritability as part of GWAS reconstructions. Furthermore, optimal SNP sets per environment and trait were determined to the top 500 most explicative markers according to their ß regression effects. These 500 SNPs, on average, overlapped in 5.24 % across localities, reinforcing the environmentally dependent nature of polygenic adaptation. Finally, we utilized the GEAVs to select for the top 10 genotypes at each environment and trait as part of a recommendation scheme targeting narrow adaption. The prioritized provenances of adaptation, according to their GEAVs, could become candidate allelic donors for abiotic stress tolerance to be used in down-stream hybrid generations as part of the long-term bean improvement for coastal tropical regions
