# Convocatoria Universidad Nacional de Colombia Universidad de Purdue UREP C 2022 2 (Undergraduate Research Experience Purdue Colombia) Student research visitor program agreement. Identification of the role of the cerebellum in Manganese neurotoxicity.

--The overall objective of this new R01 project is to define the spatial-temporal toxicokinetics of manganese (Mn) in the human brain exposed to welding fumes, and dose-dependent relationships of brain Mn to oxidative stress markers and neurological outcomes. Chronic high exposure to Mn is neurotoxic, causing psychological, cognitive and motor sequela that resemble Parkinson_x0092_s Disease, for which no treatment is available. The safe exposure limit to airborne Mn is still unknown. Even in today_x0092_s occupational settings accumulation of brain manganese, neurochemical changes, and changes in mood, cognitive and motor function are reported. A recent study on over 800 welders showed that exposure to Mn through welding fumes put them at risk for parkinsonian phenotypes. Yet, no study has examined the dynamics and dose-response relationships of uptake and elimination of Mn into specific brain regions of the human brain. While it is known from animal studies that oxidative stress and neurotransmitter imbalances are implicated in the mechanisms of Mn neurotoxicity, brain oxidative stress markers have not been quantified in Mn exposed workers. Thus, there exists a significant knowledge gap on the exact relationship between an individuals_x0092_ Mn exposure, changes in Mn brain levels and the neurochemical changes within particular brain regions. How such changes further mediate mood changes, cognitive and/or motor dysfunction, and which of these outcomes are reversible or not, has significant health implications
