# APOLIPOPROTEIN GENOTIPES AND PATIENTS IN DISEASE OF ALZHEIMER, APPROACH OF PERIODONTIS/GINGIVITIS ( PRELIMINARY CONSIDERATION)

In the literature, some reports have been made on the possible relationship between the Periodontal Disease (EP) and Alzheimer's Disease (EA); however, the multiple factors that may influence this association are not yet fully elucidated. The APOE4 allele is considered the most important genetic risk factor for the development of the AE; in individuals with EP it has been related to a higher gingival index, in addition, associations of this allele with periodontal disease have been found in crevical fluid measurements. The APOE2 allele has been considered protective for the AE3 allele, while the APOE3 has been categorized with neutral effect. To date, few reports on possible common genetic bases between the AE and the AE have been found from the APOE gene alleles. The current study proposed approach to genetic bases of the AE2 in individuals with AE, according to the Garlet et. al. (2012) criteria have been proposed a case approach and controls to investigate genetics in the periodontal disease, classify patients with ging and those who have been considered as controls and those who have suffered from the disease. According to the allele distribution for the participants, it was observed that for the APOE4 allele there were 2 patients with periodontitis diagnosis and no patients with gingivitis, for APOE2 there was one patient with periodontitis and none with gingivitis and in the APOE3 group there were 3 individuals with gingivitis and 8 patients with periodontitis (of which 5 were homozygous). The conditions of gingivitis or periodontitis were not related to any particular APOE allele, it is recommended to expand the sample size and modify the categorization of gingivitis or periodontitis according to current classifications. KEY WORDS: periodontal disease; dementia; Alzheimer's disease; Apolipoprotein E; APOE4
