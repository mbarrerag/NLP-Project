# Adoption of the land administration domain model for Colombia (LADM-Col) in marine areas: A study for incorporation into the multipurpose cadastre

The strengthening of integral governance in Colombia requires the implementation of the Marine Catastro as an essential tool to register and efficiently manage resources and activities in coastal and maritime areas through its legal regulation. Colombia has a wide maritime extension that includes inland waters, territorial sea, contiguous zone and exclusive economic zone, which represents 44.86% of its territory. In addition, it has extensive coastal areas in the Caribbean Sea and the Pacific Ocean, as well as more than 3,189 km of coastline and a varied island region with islands, cayos and islets (DIMAR, 2005). In view of this scenario, the administration and effective protection of the various services offered by marine ecosystems and the activities developed in them is a priority. The analysis developed seeks to consolidate for the first time in the country the concept of Marine Catastro, providing a clear vision of this complex problem for the development of effective strategies to preserve natural resources and promote sustainable development in all regions of the country. This is framed within the Land Administration Model in Colombia − LADM_COL, based on the standard of the ISO 19152:2012, which supports the Multipro deposit system in the country. This strategy becomes a reference point in the Marine Catastro, contributing to the construction of the territorial administration system ‐ SAT, which will outline the governance strategy in relation to the management of territorial objects identified by the entities or actors responsible within the framework of the LADM models. From the marine-coster perspective, this strategy promotes the formulation of key concepts, the elaboration of guides for extended models and considerations for the management of the core model LADM_COL in the representation of the other half of the country .
