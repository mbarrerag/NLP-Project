# In-silico design of a biological platform for the production of ethylene glycol from raw glycerol using metabolic engineering tools and thermodynamic principles.

The objective of this study is to design a biological platform in silico for the production of ethylene glycol from raw glycerol through a thermodynamic analysis, which will allow to establish the maximum theoretical production that would be possible to obtain, and tools of the metabolic engineering, which allow to evaluate and implement the different metabolic routes and related heterological genes to carry out this process. Initially, models of black box representing the biological production of ethylene glycol from the main sources of carbon that are found in the raw glycerol, glycerol and methanol. These models were first constructed for each substrate separately, considering different electron acceptors including ethylene glycol. Because there is an interest in performing this process using both substrates at the same time, the importance of implementing a methodology that allows to construct black box models for multiple substrates (SD and PDP) were constructed for three different microorganisms (E. coli, S. cerevisiae, P. pastoris) in order to represent the production of ethylene glycol study through different biosynthesis routes (SD and P. Based on a methodology already proposed for a single substrate. This highlights the presence of new parameters that consider the effect of the presence of multiple substrates in the different cellular processes. These, however, remain to be determined because it was not possible to establish a mathematical correlation that would allow them to be calculated. Moreover, it was observed from the analysis of the metabolic models how the model for E. coli provides the best scenario for obtaining ethylene glycol given the highest yield obtained and the amount of energy intermediaries generated during the process. For this model, it was found how the production of ethylene glycol is mainly given at low rates of biomass growth, demonstrating that both processes compete directly for the flow of carbon entering the metabolism. Finally, it was found that some reactions within the routes of synthesis of ethylene glycol are sensitive to the variations of intracellular concentration, which would lead to the generation of bottlenecks in the process decreasing the yield of ethylene glycol.
