# The horizontal boundary rethinking: the construction of the site in the Alberto Lleras Camargo building of the University of Los Andes, designed by Guillermo Bermúdez and Daniel Bermúdez, Bogotá, 1989

This Thesis of Maestría rethinks the utility of the soil in the architecture by means of a critique of its subsidiary definition vis-à-vis the other archetypal elements, which reveals, on the contrary, the fundamental role that plays in the process of implementation. The research part of the idea that the act of implant resides in the architectural definition of the horizontal limit and that this, as such, involves the inaugural division between the artificial plane and the site. In such logic, the role of the soil as the essential tool of the site consists in reconstituting the encounter between man and the Earth, in a rite that reaffirms its orientation towards the horizon line. The practice of founding a place finds its meaning in that original separation, so that the soil is the foundational element of the architecture since it has the double virtue of establishing the primary structure of the horizontal limit, necessary for the instance of life, and of directing the rite in space and time, as it gives it a precise position in the Universe. The dialectic between the soil and the natural ground is analyzed in three instances: the instance of the element as an architectural, and as an example, and as an element as an example, in the rite, in the space. In such logic, the role of the ground as the main tool of deployment consist in restoring the counter between man and the Earth, in a ritual that shall restore his or her orientation centrality within the line of the horizon.
