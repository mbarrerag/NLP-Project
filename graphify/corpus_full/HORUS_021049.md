# Domestic borders in modern architecture. Limiting three projects in Bogotá in the 1950s and 1960s

This master's thesis reveals the profound sense of the interaction between frontality and torsion as notions of a duality that remains and affects conception and perception in architecture. This is done through the study of the Giraldo Building by Fernando Martínez Sanabria (1958), located in Bogotá. The thesis will consist of three chapters: the first chapter is a presentation of the case study, an intentional description of the Giraldo Building seen from the activity, technique and form, and in a transversal way to the project, from the frontality, torsion, and the relationship between the two concepts in the project; the second chapter will deal with a description of the problem of the duality of the concepts mentioned in the Giraldo Building and in other projects of the work of Fernando Martínez Sanabria, as well as its analysis in which project classifications are presented; the third chapter demonstrates the problem of the duality between frontality and twisting, and other possible contradictions between interior and exterior in different projects of the history of architecture and will seek to formulate definitions and new related questions
