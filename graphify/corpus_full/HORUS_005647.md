# Determination of the impact of IGF2 on the communication between trofoblast and dNK cells

In the process of blastocistic implantation there must be continuous communication between trofoblast cells, dNK cells and other immune populations. This communication focuses on exercising immuno control to maintain maternal receptivity. The increase in the concentration of factors such as IGF2 has been associated with an increase in the invasivity of trofoblastic cells, it being relevant to study the mechanisms that modulate communication between trofoblast cells and dNK cells in the presence of IGF2. For this purpose, an in vitro model was proposed, in which PNK cells were differentiated towards a tolerogenic phenotype similar to dNK cells (i-dNK) to co-cultivar them with HTR-8/SVneo.
