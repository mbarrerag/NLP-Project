# Methods for features classification in point processes on linear networks

In this work two methods are proposed for the analysis of point patterns in linear networks with different approaches. Methods developed in studies prior to this more complex geometric context, where the classical properties of a point process change and the visualization of the data is not intuitive. The first method serves to detect groups of points within point patterns grouped into linear networks, focusing on the classification of point processes. This approach takes advantage of the distribution of the volumes of the closest neighboring K-ths in linear networks. The methodology is suitable for analyzing point patterns that comprise two overlapping Poisson processes that occur in the same linear network. The second method considers the problem of testing the hypothesis of spatial randomness in point processes homogeneous in linear networks. A statistical hypothesis based on the fractional dimension, calculated by the method Box-counting, is proposed.
