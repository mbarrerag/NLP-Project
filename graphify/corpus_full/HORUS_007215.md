# Determinants of Inequity in Safety in Patient and People's Health Care in Colombia. Approach from Social Determinants of Health DSS

Health services often fail in their access, quality and quantity, especially for some sectors of the population favoring the occurrence of unwanted events in health. Differences in relation to health and care in the regions are presented independently of the political or economic system that prevails. When these differences are unnecessary, avoidable, arbitrary and unfair, it is considered that there is inequity in health.The perspective of patient safety, which considers an approach of equity is recent and little explored. To establish the behavior of patient safety and adverse results in patients and in people in terms of the social determinants of health, to verify if there is inequity in such results and the magnitude of the same. From the question what are the determinants of the inequalities in the health care of the patient and the people in Colombia? the factors that determine the inequity in the safety during the interaction with health services were explored.For this purpose four studies were elaborated with different and complementary objectives of the health patients, which allowed: to identify the results of non-desirable in health during the activities of disease prevention and the events. prevention: alterations of nutritional status such as malnutrition and overweight or childhood obesity, cases of congenital syphilis, neonatal tetanus, events allegedly attributable to vaccination and immunization ESAVI, immunopreventable diseases due to failures in vaccination processes, promotion: direct as obesity, overweight, and estimated indirectly for the study as otitis media in infant population, malnutrition, adult cardiovascular disease and excess mortality due to sedentaryness, and also diseases transmitted by food due to the implementation of promotion strategies to improve food in school environments without adequate hygiene measures and for failures in the care proper of a health condition, outside or inside health institutions: worsening of various underlying diseases due to non-care or non-timely care that explained events such as: epileptic status, complicated urinary infections, secondary anemia to abnormal bleeding, untreated infections associated with healthcare among others.In total, more than 50 contributing factors for the occurrence of unwanted events were obtained from the study, many of them with a more prominent role in some activities, such as the failures of quality of care, human errors and availability of inputs to be considered as a disease., the family structure and the lack of basic sanitation, all of them illustrate how social inequalities amplify the risks of unwanted events in health. Other determinants involve the characteristics of the health system, leaving it to see how the precariousness of information systems, the lack of human talent, and administrative bureaucracy hinder equitable and quality care and put at risk people who demand care. Finally, widely recognized determinants in the national territory such as poverty, armed conflict, geographical barriers and culture reflect structural conditions that increase the probability of occurrence of EREND in Colombia in vulnerable groups. Each EREND analyzed denotes a failure in the process of providing care to the population and together with proximal, intermediate, health system and structural determinants, shape an unequal concentration of the risk of occurrence of these events in the Colombian population. The Colombian health system presents multiple barriers that affect access, opportunity, quality and continuity of health care, these barriers are translated into non-desirable health outcomes (EREND), especially among the most vulnerable populations.
