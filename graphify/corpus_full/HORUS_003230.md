# EFFECT OF THE OVERALL INTERNIZATION TEMPERATURE IN THE VOLUMETERIC CONTRACTION OF PIECE IN ALUMINA (AL2O3) MADE BY FOUND FILM MANUFACTURING (FFF)

The Aluminium Oxide (Al2O3) is a ceramic material known for its thermal, chemical and mechanical resistance, which faces challenges in the manufacture of complex geometries by traditional methods such as hot isostatic pressing and injection moulding. The additive manufacturing, specifically the FFF-based Filament Manufacturing Technology, emerges as a promising solution for obtaining complex geometries and small batches of parts. The aim of this research is to explore the relationship between the sinterisation temperature, volumetric contraction and density in pieces of Al2O3 formed by FFF. For this study, a filament consisting of a multi-component polymer system and Al2O3 powders was developed with an average particle size of 0.3 μm and a volumetric proportion of 35 % by weight of dust. The filaments were used for the printing of components by FFF only and subjected to depolymerized and sinterised processes (SDS), varying the sinterisation temperature of 1100 °C to 1500 °C. The results of this research were revealed by a non-converting factor of 45 °C. The results of this research were used for the FFF and submitted to depoliser and sinterized processes (SDS. It was found that, as the sinterization temperature increases, the discrepancy between linear contraction in height versus that of width and length is reduced. For example, at 1100 °C, linear contraction in height is 5.03 %, in width of 2.83 %, and in length of 3.60 %; instead, at 1500 °C, these values are 19.13 % for height, 19.03 % for width and 16.08 % for length. The results highlight a greater volumetric contraction at higher temperatures, with significant variations in linear contraction in different dimensions of the specimens, suggesting a need to adjust the factors of overdimensioning to achieve the desired dimensional accuracy. This knowledge is vital for the optimization of future material formulations and expands the potential applications of alumina in environments that demand high precision and adaptability of additive manufacturing techniques. Information derived from this research is crucial to develop correction factors that compensate for the contractions observed and allow for more accurate dimensional control of sinterative alumina pieces by FFF
