# ORGANIZATIONAL SOCIAL COMMITMENT AND EMPLOYEE WELL-BEING: ILLUSTRATING A CONSTRUCTION MINING APPROACH IN R ORGANIZATIONAL SOCIAL COMMITMENT AND WELFARE OF EMPLOYMENT: ILLUSTRATING A CONSTRUCTIONAL MINERITY APPROACH IN R APPROACH

The way employees react to the ethical/social initiatives of an organization has had little empirical support. This article examines employees' perceptions of organizational social commitment (SOC) and their association with employee welfare (WB). The sample consists of 289 participants from a health organization in Colombia. A comprehensive methodology is used to mine psychological/management constructs in R, which includes six processes (observing, exploring, confirming, explaining, predicting and reporting). Evidence is provided about plausibility, reliability, convergent/discriminating validity and equity of scales. The relationship between CSOs and WB is contrasted with the use of models of structural equations with Bootstrap. The article examines the ability of CSOs to predict WB using machine learning methods. A positive relationship is found between constructs, suggesting that CSOs is a valuable strategy to contribute to employee welfare from a 'well-being' perspective. The article stimulates/facilitates future research and teaching-learning initiatives in the analysis of latent variables using RSC language
