# THE PLANNING EYE AS A MODEL FOR PHOTOTRASDUCTION STUDIES

The vision, essential function of the nervous system, has been extensively studied, but its evolutionary history has not yet been fully clarified. Eye studies in multiple species document an amazing anatomical diversity, but at the cellular and functional level the research has been confined only to a few experimental models. This gap is especially clear regarding organisms endowed with simple eyes, which could help clarify the origins of the vision. This work was undertaken to examine the eyes of the planary, belonging to the group of the platelminths, one of the most basal Bilateria. First we developed procedures to dissect the tiny eyes; preliminary electrophysiological records in full eyes, revealed the presence of active currents. By means of enzymatic dissociation, different types of cells were obtained on the apparently viable, according to the exclusion tests of the gutter blue. As a first step to identify the photo-sensitive cells of planaria, we did immuno-marking tests in sections of fixed eyes and impregnated in paraffin.
