# Experimental and analytical study at maximum load of composite slabs with and without conventional reinforcement, applying the serial/parallel mixing theory and FEM

This article presents an experimental and numerical study of three slab systems for a mezzanine floor: composite slabs without conventional reinforcement (CSsCR), composite slabs with conventional reinforcement (CSwCR), and reinforced concrete slabs with a multi-trapezoidal cross section (RCST). For each system, specimens were made with a variation in the slenderness ratio from compact slabs (with a slenderness ratio less than 20) to slender slabs (with a slenderness ratio greater than and equal to 20). Experimental bending tests were carried out on 27 specimens in real scale. Numerical models were built applying the finite element method (FEM), the serial/parallel mixing theory, and constitutive models of simple materials: elastoplastic model for steel, and isotropic damage model for concrete. The results obtained were analyzed in light of the effects of flexural strength, slenderness, deflection, maximum load and stress on the compound and on each component. The results established that the flexural capacity depends on the horizontal shear bond, in function of the slenderness ratio and the connection between sheet and concrete. In the numerical analysis, there was a great difference in behavior between compact slabs and slim slabs. In the case of compact slabs, the numerical model was not adjusted to the experimental results
