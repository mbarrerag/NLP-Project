# Evaluation of the quality of orange powder juice obtained by spray drying

In this work, the physical and chemical properties and stability of the orange powder obtained by spraying were evaluated with a prototype designed and constructed in the post-harvest laboratory of the National University of Colombia, using maltodextrin as an encapsulative agent. Eleven experimental tests were carried out, varying drying temperature and encapsulant concentration, following a central rotational composite experimental design (DCCR) with two factors. The key parameters analyzed included °Brix, moisture content, color, titorable acidity, pH, water activity (aW), whiteness index (IB), hygroscopicity, particle structure, process performance and energy demand. The powder presented aw and moisture content values below 0.3 and 4% respectively, indicating stability under adequate storage conditions. The IB showed high uniformity between the tests, suggesting an efficient drying process, while the majority of hygroscopicity between 16.27% and 18.12% revealed good resistance to the moisture absorption. The pH restored stable in managerial management and management excellence to moisturize absorption. A 20% reduction in civic acid content was observed, probably due to product decomposition.
