# Archaeology in Lower Magdalena: Environment and use of plant resources between the 8th and 16th centuries A.D.

It presents an analysis of dental calculation phytoliths of 10 sites of Lower Magdalena from where populations come that were called Malibu, and that inhabited different ecosystems between the 8th and 16th centuries AD, in order to document the consumed plant resources and respond to inferences on social differentiation in terms of age and sex. Plants such as maize, yucca, palm of wine, anon, chirimoya and coca are identified, as well as families such as poacea, cupurbitaceae, bromelacea and marantaceae, which shows a wide utilization of the Tropical Dry Forests especially, as well as through the review of paleoenvironmental studies, two climatic phenomena (ENSO and Small Ice Age) could affect the patterns of mobility and settlement of the prehispanic populations. It also shows that there was access to the homogeneous plant resources by parts of these populations. .
