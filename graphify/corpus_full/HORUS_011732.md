# THE ENTIRE ATMOSFERICAL FACT IN THE TROPIC

Air pollution is a complex environmental problem that is affected by industrialization, automotive traffic, atmospheric chemistry and meteorology. Air pollution may have serious consequences for human health, including respiratory diseases, circulatory system disorders and reduced labour productivity (Steinberga et al., 2019). It may also have undesirable economic effects, especially in urban and industrial areas (Rama Krishna et al., 2005). To improve air quality, it is essential to identify sources of pollution and establish limits of emissions and appropriate environmental regulations (Chan et al., 2012); (Goyal & Chalapati Rao, 2007b). A useful measure to assess the amount of pollution and the polluting load capacity of a region is the assimilating capacity (CA), which is an estimate of the maximum amount of pollutants that a region can accept without exceeding the pre-established air quality criteria (Panda & Nagendra, 2017). The CA has been evaluated in different cases such as petrochemical industrial complexes, highly populated cities, coal thermal plants and iron mining regions (Nandankar, 1999 and 2008). The AC and CV are approximations, as there are a number of factors that affect the dispersion of pollutants in the atmosphere. To study these terms include the influence of seasonality, zones with established industrial activities, impact of urban areas and the delimitation of atmospheric basins (Genc et al., 2010; Krishnan & Kunhikrishnan, 2004; Sujatha et al., 2016). Hence, the concept of atmospheric basin would allow conceptually to support air quality management and control by relating the variables to be considered in the CA estimation of the study area
