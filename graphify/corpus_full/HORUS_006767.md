# Physiological and acoustic analysis of the acquisition of English as L2 in late Spanish native apprentices

This research seeks to analyze the cognitive processes related to the creation of phonological categories for English as L2 by late apprentices native of Spanish. For this they are taken as main theoretical references to 3 models of learning phonology in a second language: first, the magnet model of the mother tongue (NLM); second, the model of speech learning (SLM); third, the model of perceptive assimilation (PAM). These models agree that to consider a speaker as competent it is necessary to perceive and produce in an adequate way, therefore, this research performs an objective measurement of these abilities in a population of ten bilingual speakers with ages between 18 and 30, of right-hand dominance, with Spanish as mother tongue and English as L2, who learned their L2 in post-adolescence ages. The production is evaluated by the acoustic analysis of the spectrogram of some vocals generated before different tasks as denomination or reading of words and pseudo-palabras, techniques that have been widely used in the studies of phonology in second language.
