# Parental beliefs about emotions, parental reactions and emotional regulation in children aged 8-12

In the Latin American context, there are few studies addressing parental beliefs about emotions and emotional regulation during the transition period between childhood and adolescence. Therefore, the present study proposed to check the influence of parental beliefs mediated by parental reactions on the emotional regulation of children aged 8 to 12 years. To this end, a cross-sectional design was proposed in which 217 parents and their children resident in the city of Bogotá (Colombia) responded self-support questionnaires. Subsequently, using the modeling of structural equations, it was found that parental beliefs on emotions showed a significant and direct contribution to the emotional regulation of children (-.26). In turn, these beliefs have a direct positive effect on the parental reactions of low support (-.52; p .26; p<.05). In turn, these beliefs have a direct positive effect on low-supportive parental reactions (-.52; p<.001), which negatively influence emotional regulation (-.28; p<.05). As for sustainable reactions, these did not show a significant contribution to children's emotional regulation. Conclusion, parental success about emotions directly influence children's emotional regulation, more likely mediated by low-supportive parental reactions
