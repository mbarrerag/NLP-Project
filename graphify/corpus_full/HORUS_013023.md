# Slavery, legal culture and ambiguous experiences in Latin America

Presentation of issue 44. This special issue of Historia y Sociedad presents its readers with evidence and analysis on the ambiguous nature of the legal experiences of many enslaved people in Latin America. When these people were in front of the magistrates and in front of the law, their fate usually did not depend solely on the precise application of a jurisdiction, norm, or legal principle established in advance and clearly understood. Rather, the fate of these men, women and children was defined by the random and contingent dynamics of power relations in which slaves, masters and magistrates coexisted. Therefore, there was a lot that revolved around the interpretation, the specific circumstances, the balance of local forces, the political and legislative environment of the moment, or the individual verve of a slave to achieve emancipation or of a free man to keep someone in captivity, no matter what the law or politics dictated. Slaves related to legal culture in spaces and moments that were not very transparent, but in situations of great social and cultural dynamism
