# ESTOMATOLOGIC CARE OF PERSONS WITH A ASSISTANCE OF INTELLECTUAL DEVELOPMENT

The intellectual development disorder (IDD) has been defined in different ways throughout history as mental retardation, intellectual disability and cognitive disability. TDI has been defined as "a group of developmental alterations characterized by a remarkable limitation of cognitive functions, learning disorders, and adaptive abilities and behaviors"; generating alterations in the level of intelligence and social adaptation; boys and girls with DTT learn, but take more time to learn to speak, walk, dress and/or eat. People with DTT may have different stomatological manifestations and these vary according to whether the person is institutionalized or not. Self-harm, bruxism, xerostomy, changes in the formation of enamel, erosion and dental malocclusion have been reported. As for the level of oral hygiene, oral hygiene has been shown to be more prevalent in institutionalized persons and generally needs for unsatisfied dental treatment
