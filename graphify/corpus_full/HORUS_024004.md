# Evolutionary trust model for emerging cooperation in ad-hoc networks through genetic algorithms.

In this thesis, an evolutionary model for agent interactions in ad-hoc networks was proposed. For this, a general review was made on the different types of models existing in the literature, in order to choose a model based on their relevance. The chosen model uses genetic algorithms complemented with stochastic games, which are expected to offer adaptive and dynamic behavior. The simulations were then designed to involve the nature of ad-hoc networks, where it was demonstrated that genetic algorithms are able to adapt to the network, but the process takes 1,000 interactions to achieve favorable indicators. Consequently, the inclusion of stochastic games, which reach the same indicators with only 250 interactions, was proposed. This result shows the advantages of the proposed model. Finally, the implementation of a pilot test on a real ad-hoc network was carried out, validating the behavior of simulations. The main contribution of this work is the adaptive model, which also shows an improvement in the rate of adaptation in adverse environments compared to works found in the literature
