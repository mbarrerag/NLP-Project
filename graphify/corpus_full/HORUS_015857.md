# ALOMETRIC COMPORTMENT OF THE NASAL PERFILE IN THE ARABUCAN HORSE OF COLOMBIA

In recent years, there have been numerous contributions to the ethnological study of the Criollo Araucano horse, a population that can be considered singular by its adaptation to the medium hard in which it develops and inhabits. From the ethnological point of view, the profile constitutes one of the three classical coordinates in the classification scheme of the Baron races. The cephalic profile is usually visually appreciated, classifying the animals in orthoids (right profile), cirtoids (convex profile) and celiads (concave profile). Quantifying and analyzing the morphological variation resulting from interactions is one of the objectives of the geometric morphometry (MG). This objective is complemented by the quantification of the co-variance between the morphology and other variables of genetic, climatic, geographical origin, etc. The objective of the study was to quantify this profile in the Araccano horse using techniques, which allows not only its objectivization, but also its analysis by multivariant statistical methods. A sample of 44 complete skulls of horses collected in different locations was studied in the municipality of Aracucano, From the analysis of the data, it was revealed that only 2.1% of the frontal alloidic change is explained by the change in the cranial size, indicating a clear isometry (p=0.425); but this for the facial profile, yes, a clear allometry is reflected (p=0.0082). Analyzed the correlation between the frontal and facial profiles, the coefficient R v was 0.091 (p=0.171), which shows that the change in the frontal profile does not correlate with the change in the facial profile. It is concluded from all this that, in the ethnological protocol, both profiles -the frontal and facial profiles - should be evaluated separately, since they do not present significant correlation, at least for horse races. In addition, facial allometry involves an extension in the path of postnatal development, of which it is assumed that the facial alloidic differences have a postnatal progression to take into account for ethnological studies. This is not the case for the frontal profile, in which the neonate already presents the same as in adult age
