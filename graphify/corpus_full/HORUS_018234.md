# COLONIZED AFFECTS: FAMILY INTERACTIONS FROM A DECOLONIAL LOOK

In this essay of reflection, the result of the doctoral thesis Tejiendo practices and affective knowledge with single parent families in Bogotá, Colombia, we analyze the ways in which the hegemonic ideologies imposed by European modernity after the colonization of the Americas, distort, reject and invisibilize the presence of emotions in the intimate relationships of families. It is argued that practices and knowledges about feelings in family interactions are permeated by the inequitable vision of genders, violence and the ignorance of emotions as part of human nature. Such impositions are assumed as "normal" by many adults and adults and interweave themselves in the exercises of care and upbringing. It ends with some recommendations for the decolonization of affections
