# Reflections on musical performance from the figure of Julian Bream

The separation between the roles of interpreter and composer, given from the cultural phenomena and the phonographic market during the twentieth century, redefined the field of action for musical interpretation. The educational model of conservatory and the dissemination of recordings have positioned the ideal of an interpreter that must meet very rigid standards, in which technical skill and precision prevail, with a view to ensuring the ability to repeat the interpretation based on a model; this has been imposed on the emotional and expressive connection with the music that is played. However, Julian Bream, one of the most important interpreters of the twentieth century, proposes musical interpretation as a creative act in which, from the conscious study of the partition, the development of the technique and the exploration of the “spirit” of the work — as well as of the emotional connection with the music —, improvisation is allowed from the separation and spontaneity at the time of touching the work. ique and the exploration of the spirit of the work -as well as the emotional connection with the music-, improvisation and spontaneous are allowed when playing the work. This opens space for the performer's own expression and allows the performance to be a living thing that can vary from one performance to another
