# HYDROCLIMATOLOGIC TRENDS IN PNN CHINGAA AND THEIR RELATIONSHIP TO DEFORESTATION IN AMAZON

Understanding the changes in hydroclimatological variables in high mountain ecosystems is essential to develop effective conservation strategies and ensure the continuity of ecosystem services they provide. Chingaza Páramo is part of Colombia’s high mountain ecosystems and plays a crucial role in supplying water to 70% of the population of Bogotá, the capital of Colombia. This article focuses on analyzing trends in climatological variables in the National Natural Park (PNN) Chingaza and its relationship with deforestation in the Amazon. By applying the methods of Sen Slope and Man-Kendall it was found that 89% of the precipitation series present mixed significant trends. As for the average flow rate, three of the five basins analyzed showed decreasing trends. And the evapotranspiration series present significant trends of the order of 6 mm per decade. The change in soil coverage was assessed from the annual maps of MapBiomas Amazonia. A previous validation, based on visual inspection, showed a general accuracy of 96%. The results showed significant decreasing trends in the forest, and significant increases in the forest coverage were assessed from the high forest
