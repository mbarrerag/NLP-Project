# Application of photosynthesis models to establish the light requirement in three cocoa clones

Numerous articles show that light, water, CO2, and chlorophyll are united in the perfect process of photosynthesis, however, is necessary to reveal the conditions that optimize, among others, the assimilation of CO2 in the cocoa crop, a subject with divergences conceptual due to its understory origin. Photosynthesis in cocoa clones, CCN51, FSV41, and LK40 was character-ized to establish their light requirements. The influence of levels of radiation to assimilation on CO2 assimilation (A) was evalu-ated. Measurements were made on the fourth leaf of mature branches, using the Light Unit (universal PLC3 -RGBW) and the portable Infrared Analysis of Gas Exchange (Ciras-3 PP SYSTEM ®) equipment, the soil moisture was simultaneously recorded every five minutes. The A / PAR curves were made, and photosynthesis models were tested, the fittest models for A / PAR curves were selected to estimate the parameters A max, Light saturation point (LSP), Light compensation point (LCP), Respi-ration Rd and Photosynthetic yield (¿). It was found that photosynthesis increases when the PAR increment. The highest A and (¿) was shown in CCN51 and the lowest in LK40. The best fit models for A were that of Kaipiainen, E. L (2009), Ye, Z. P (2007), and Smith, E.L (1935). The appropriate light range was established as the PAR for Amax and 0.5 Amax, for CCN51 it was 500-1500, for FSV41 it was 250-750 and for LK40 it was 546-1000 μmol photons m-2 s-1
