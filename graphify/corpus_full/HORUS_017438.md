# ARE WE OVERCOMING OUR INABILITY TO HAVE PEDIATRIC PATIENTS PROPERLY USE INHALED CORTICOSTEROIDS BY INAPPROPRIATELY ESCALATING THEIR THERAPY?

Despite relatively recent advances in our understanding of the pathophysiology of asthma and the availability of highly effective controller medications such as ICS, currently many pediatric patients fail to control their asthma. Although some of these difficult-to-treat asthmatic children have severe therapy-resistant asthma, most of them experience poor asthma control due to various modifiable factors, among which poor adherence to inhaled controller therapy and inadequate inhaler technique are the most common. Although improving adherence to ICSs is an important target in asthma care because non-adherence to treatment compromises its effectiveness and drives up healthcare costs, methods of assessing adherence must be as accurate and objective as possible. Although EMDs have been considered to be essential tools in identifying children with severe therapy-resistant asthma, their high cost and low availability have currently limited their use in many countries, especially LMICs. For these reasons, we encourage the weighing of inhaler canisters and self-reported validated questionnaires as alternative and valid options for assessing adherence to inhaled controller therapy. Furthermore, we encourage the use of validated instruments as an objective measurement of the adequacy of inhaler technique. Although recognizing poor adherence does not automatically lead to improved adherence, it is usually an essential first step in effectively targeting adherence behavior, especially if the reasons for low or erratic compliance are explored by means of non-judgmental healthcare provider-patient communication. These recommendations could assist in overcoming our inability to have pediatric asthmatic patients use ICS and in avoiding escalating their controller therapy toward more expensive medications, eventually reaching the use of biologics. Promoting the rational and cost-effective use of asthma controller therapy could help to optimize the limited health resources in many LMICs
