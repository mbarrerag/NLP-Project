# Neuronal network-based approaches and diffuse logic for precipitation estimation: a systematic review

The estimation of rainfall at the river basin level is essential for watershed management, analysis of extreme events and weather and climatic dynamics, and hydrological modelling. In recent years new approaches and tools have been used such as artificial intelligence techniques to estimate precipitation, offering advantages over traditional methods. Two main paradigms are artificial neural networks and systems of diffuse logic, which can be used in a wide variety of configurations, including hybrid and modular models. This paper presents a review of the literature on hybrid metaheuristic and artificial intelligence models based on signal processes, focusing on the applications of these techniques in the analysis and estimation of precipitation. The selection and comparison criteria used were the model type, the input and output variables, the performance metrics and the fields of application. An increase in the number of this type of studies was identified, mainly in applications involving neural network models, which tend to become more sophisticated according to the availability and quality of training data.
