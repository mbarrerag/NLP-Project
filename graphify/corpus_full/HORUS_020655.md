# Novel approaches toward bio-butanol production from renewable feedstocks

The biological production of butanol has been carried out conventionally through acetone-butanol-ethanol fermentation. This process is limited by the low concentration of product and yield; thus, novel microorganisms, synthetical biochemistry, microorganism consortiums, and recovery methods have been developed by applying both experimental and model-based approaches. As acetone has low properties as biofuel, isopropanol-butanol-ethanol fermentation and bio-isobutanol production have been proposed as alternative methods and are summarized in this review. These processes utilize several renewable feedstocks, such as corn stover, wheat, sorghum, barley, and sugar cane bagasse, among others. However, these fermentations still have low product concentrations, making prohibitive its separation by conventional distillation as has high energy intensity. As novel processes are required for more sustainable processes, research and development must focus on strategies for process design and simulation strategies allowing a better understanding of the operating conditions of reactor performance indicators. Herein, we described the challenges and opportunities of novel approaches for butanol recovery, model-based approaches such as heat-integrated distillation, and in situ or ex-situ product recovery
