# Electrical behavior of graphene-erbium oxide coatings sintered via electrochemical exfoliation and deposited on P-silicon substrates

This work presents the results of the growth of graphene and Graphene-Erbium Oxide (GERO) coatings sintered via electrochemical exfoliation and deposited on p-type silicon substrates via the drip method. The morphology of the coatings was characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), micro crystallography through X-ray diffraction patterns (XRD), chemically via energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) analysis, Raman spectroscopy and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), and electrically by current vs voltage curve as a function of temperature, both with and without a magnetic field, and the specific heat (SH) of the coatings as a function of temperature. Multiple characterization techniques confirmed the successful formation of the Er2O3/rGO (GERO) hybrid coatings. Raman spectroscopy revealed vibrational modes between 240 and 840 cm 1 associated with erbium oxide phonons (notably Er[sbnd]O stretching modes), along with the characteristic D (∼1350 cm 1 ) and G (∼1580 cm 1 ) bands of reduced graphene oxide (rGO). XPS analysis further supported this structure, where the deconvolution of the C 1 s and O 1 s spectra revealed oxygenated functional groups typical of rGO, and the Er 4d5/2 peak confirmed the oxidation state of Er 3+ within the oxide matrix. The electrical characterization revealed that the coatings exhibit spontaneous Zener diode behavior with a variable gap energy as a function of temperature, as well as evidence of the Klein tunneling conduction mechanism at low currents. The SH measurements indicated that the GERO coatings have a value of 0.400 J/g K. These results make these coatings promising candidates for device development in the electronics industry, particularly as low-power transistors and low-temperature sensors
