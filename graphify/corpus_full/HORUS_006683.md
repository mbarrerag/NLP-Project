# Fasting and Hypohydration in a 24-hour Period, Quasiexperimental Pilot Study in Natural Conditions in Volunteers in Bogotá

Presurgical fasting seeks to reduce the risk of pulmonary aspiration during anesthesia. However, despite current guidelines, fasting time frequently exceeds 12 hours before an elective procedure. Describe the effects of fasting on the water state, urinary output and plasma and blood volumes; evidenced by changes in weight, hematocrit and urinary volume. A prospective quasi-experimental study was carried out without control group; 18 subjects (10 men, 8 women) were taken to a mild hypohydration point by means of absolute fasting of solids and fluids within a maximum period of 24 hours; weight and urinary output was measured in a serial manner and blood samples were taken at the beginning and end of the fasting period. A hydrodeficiency of 16 ml/kg was found after 12 hours of fasting and a decrease of 1.038 g/kg/h was assumed. The mean time to reach hypohydration was 15:09 hours (±3.3h) and the subjects presented a decrease of urinary output to an average of 0.6 ml/kg/h and a decrease of blood volume and was calculated as a decrease of 2 mg/h..
