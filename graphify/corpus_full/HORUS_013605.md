# DURING MARIPOSES (LEPIDOPTERA: PAPILILONOIDEA) OF THE FRANCIS ENTOMOLOGICAL MUSEUM LUIS GALLEGO (MEFLG) ASSOCIATED TO CODE SEQUENCES OF DNA BARS (GIGN MITOCONDRIAL COX1)

The insects of the order Lepidoptera have inhabited the earth for some 105 million years witnessing the evolution and extinction of the dinosaurs, the appearance of the plants with flower and mammals and having survived two events of mass extinction, which reveals their high capacity of adaptation (Vélez-Estrada and Ríos-Málaver, 2018).This characteristic has derived in a wide diversity, with more than 19,000 known species in the world being highlighted Colombia for hosting approximately 20% of this diversity with 3,877 species of butterflies flying in the territory (Garwood et al., 2022; Huertas et al., 2022).With a wide curatorial history, the Entomological Museum Francisco Luis Gallego, has been in charge of protecting the specimens that, since its beginnings in 1937, have been obtained as a result of teaching practices, research projects and a large and detailed field work carried out by laboratory technicians, students and researchers. The order Lepidoptera is one of the most represented in the collection, including butterflies and moths, with approximately 15,903 specimens in the University of Medellín and the largest part of the country of the region of the region of Spain
