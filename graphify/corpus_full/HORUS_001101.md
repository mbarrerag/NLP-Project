# the languages of design in difference and inclusion

This research analyzes the tensions between the State, the family and the market in the context of disability and inclusion, questioning the role of design as control technology. The relationship between people with uniqueness and everyday objects is studied, exploring how design can perpetuate exclusion or generate new possibilities of interaction. The study adopts a qualitative approach, using the methodology Cordial Dialogues with Overlay Zones (DiConZS), where nine coinvestigators with various body experiences participated. Through the analysis of cases in Colombia and Mexico, strategies for negotiating knowledge and the use of objects are identified at three levels: internal adjustment (adaptation of the body), compensation (capacitating extensions) and reconfiguration of the environment. Concepts such as embodiment, micro-design and positive discrimination are examined, as well as design typologies (critical, inclusive, speculative, sustainable, biophilic). The findings indicate that the traditional inclusive design continues to replicate regulatory logics, so that a redefinition is proposed from singularity and the other creative. It concludes that the design must be removed from purely technical approaches (market, speculative, bio-based, bio-based).
