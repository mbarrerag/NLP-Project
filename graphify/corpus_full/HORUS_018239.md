# TO THINK OF A PHILOSOPHYC APPROXIMATION TO THE ARCHITECTURE

Introduction The architecture is the physical expression of the habitation civilized. The architecture when leaving record of the human actions, is an indescribable source of the values that mobilize a society. (Andrés Weil) The issues addressed in this book arise from a per- sonal concern about the meaning of the architecture beyond its physical and tangible plane. Will the physicality be the fundamental in the architectural fact? When we think of the architectural fact, we think of a thing that appears in the world constructed by man, which is implied anthropologically as an artificial means, according to Cassir. It is the high symbolic load that concerns the architecture an ontological and significative character, issues that have been evidenced in the development of our research as structural in its appearance, and that, ade- naly, prevail over its materiality and focuses on its importance and is the intangible in this way, and it is the question on the role of architecture in the life of man. It is intended, then, to show that the architecture as a constructed fact will transcend the tangible and it is focused on the intangible importance of this work. In this way man establishes a dialogue, understanding himself, conceiving himself and decoiling himself, the others and the world he inhabits, thus finding the true sense of inhabiting. Subsequently, we will ask ourselves if man dwells poetically, which will lead us to understand that his inhabiting can be poetic or not, to, from there, enter into the architectural fact, which will be key to man's time to build that so called genuine habitation. Then we will address in what way and to what extent these architectural facts allow us to this intended dialogue, and in that way facilitate the inhabitation. The architectural fact will conceive it, from the previous analysis, as something that contributes to giving an existential sense to our lives, in the measure that, as an artificial protagonist in the world, facilitates dialogue and the relationship with what we find, with what we build, with the other and with ourselves.
