# THE CONCEPT OF THE RUINE OF THE DEBT IN THE LEGAL IMPLEMENTATION OF THE CONTRACTUAL DERIVATION OF RESTRICTIONS BY THE COVID-19 PANDEMIA

The theory of unforeseeableness is the basis on which the present paper is constructed, understood as the dogmatic conception that an event considered as general and extraordinary, which cannot be foreseen and which is alien to the parties to a legal business, generates an imbalance between the contractors that manages to produce an excessive onerousity with respect to one of these. As I mentioned in previous texts, "the Theory of unpredictability requires for its application the materialization of a circumstance that, besides having not been positive and expressly regulated within the contract, must be known and objectively accepted by the contracting parties before contracting (...) The circumstance must be extraordinary, unforeseen and unpredictable, Thus the things, the context and consolidation of a pandemic is considered extraordinary, since almost a century ago there was no other one at the planetary level; same circumstances that were clearly both unforeseen and unpredictable (...)". The central theme of the text: the debtor's ruin is framed in a situation of such characteristics, so much so, that the Covid-19 pandemic and the restrictions that were decreed to mitigate the number of contangs around the globe, turned out to all unpredictable and irresistible lights for humanity
