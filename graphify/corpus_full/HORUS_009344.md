# FROM LAND RESTITUTION TO AGRARIAN REFORM: PROPERTY RIGHTS AFTER THE COLOMBIAN PEACE AGREEMENT

The institutional change in the property rights of land in Colombia in the XXI century has been three moments: the first began in 2011 with new laws that introduced the notion of land restitution to attend to victims of armed conflict such as peasants, indigenous and afro-descendant people. The fundamental change was the acknowledgement of the rights of access and ownership of land for groups who previously, to armed conflict, owned the land individually or collectively. The second starts with The Final Agreement for Ending the Conflict and Building a Stable and Lasting Peace, signed in 2016, that introduced the notion of comprehensive rural reform for the people without land or insufficient land. The third started in 2022. The new leftist government decided that the State would buy land from large-estate landowners to transfer it to dispossessed groups. This changed the peace agreement's conception of property rights into an agrarian reform. This paper examines this process based on two sources: a sample of land restitution rulings and the measures taken by the current government in its new conception of land reform
