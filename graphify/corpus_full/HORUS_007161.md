# Thermal effects on the structure of 2D sonic crystal bands

In this thesis the thermal effects are studied in the structure of bands of sonic crystals composed by dispersors of BST submerged in water, in a range of temperatures of 30 to 50°C. It begins analyzing a sonic crystal with a hexagonal network of cylindrical dispersors of BST, observing that the maximum width of the gap of bands to 30°C is, = 0,32684(2μc/a), increasing by 7,21% to 40°C and by 10,98% to 50°C. In addition, the modes in a cavity type H1 within the regular crystal are studied, finding that the fundamental frequency to 30°C is 1,3174(2μc/a), with a displacement of up to 3,72% towards frequencies greater than 50°C. Finally, the thermal effects in any of the observations, inclation, although incalculatory network of a Fractal type of Sierpinski. the regular crystal are analyzed, showing that the fundamental frequency at 30 μC is 1,31749(2 μcl/a), shifting up to 3,72 % to higher frequency at 50 μC. Ultimately, the thermal effects on a fractal sonic crystal with a square latece of quadrangular scatterers are examined for the first 3 iterations of a Sierpinski cardboard fractal. No complete band gaps are observed in any of the iterations, but partial band gaps are found all the μX direction, with a maximum band gap of approximately 0.1218(2 μcl/a) at 30 μC, increasing up to 2,34 % at 50 μC
