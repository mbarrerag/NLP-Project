# Development of a biotechnology strategy for improving the fermentation process of coffee through the use of native yeast strains - Phase I

In collaborative projects between the National University of Colombia, Medellín and the National Centre for Research on Coffee - CENICAFE, the National Federation of Coffeemakers, a series of projects have been carried out in which microbiota associated with the fermentation of coffee has been established, as well as the succession of the same during the process (Chaverra Muñoz, 2016).In these same studies, the presence of these microorganisms has been correlated with the quality of the drink obtained through chemical and sensory tests (Peñuela Martínez, 2018).In these, it has been determined that the presence of some strains of yeast increases the quality of the coffee obtained and therefore its score in the cup tests, which represents for Colombian coffee growers the increase in quality and therefore economic improvement when marketing the product.The initiating crops will currently be profiled as an alternative of great importance to influence the quality of the coffee (Martinez et al, 2017; haile and kang, 2019, martínez et al, 2021). In order to determine if the formulation of an inoculoculo or initiating (_0093_ instar) is feasible for the coffee, biolog® such as Hanseniaspora guilliermondii, Schizosaccharomyces pombe and Pichia membraneefaciens, so it is very important to carry out molecular identification and we also propose here, to do genome sequencing in order to establish strategies to monitor strains in the fermenting process in the subsequent phases (using specific firsts or probes). Once, determined, in this phase (I) various conditions of preservation of the strains were evaluated. In the later phases (II and III), the strains inoculated in the process and the factors or conditions that could influence their action, such as different varieties of coffee, environmental conditions, concentration of inoculums, volumes of coffee in fermentation, inoculation times among others, would be monitored
