# Communicating the revolution: an cultural analysis of FARC propaganda and its reception in Colombia (1964-2022)

This thesis analyses the production, diffusion and reception of the communication strategy of the Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia (FARC). From its creation in 1964 until its transformation into a political party in 2017, this Marxist-Leninist guerrilla has consolidated a social base through what it called "fariana propaganda". The study examines this phenomenon in the context of the armed conflict and the first years of the peace process in Colombia (1964-2022). Its interdisciplinary methodology is based on 1) interviews with ex-combatants, 2) participating observation in circles close to the former guerrilla, 3) analysis of the discourse and 4) of the narratives to the first person. The results show how, throughout their armed struggle, the FARC have practiced a singular form of propaganda that has allowed the formation of an absolute counter-public, in radical opposition with the traditional political system. At the same time, the group has developed more conventional communication strategies to attempt to reach a wider public, without great success.
