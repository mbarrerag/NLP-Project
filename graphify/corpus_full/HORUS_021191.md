# DISCRIMINATION OF LABIL AND CLAIMER CARBON IN OXISOLES WITH SPECTROSCOPIC QUIMIOMETRY

Organic soil carbon (COS) offers multiple ecosystem services and plays a fundamental role in regulating soil quality and function. In recent decades, there has been a growing interest in identifying, knowing and understanding the factors and processes that influence its dynamics at global, regional and local levels. In most articles, it is emphasized that the regulatory role of COS is enhanced through strategies that increase its concentration and residence time in the soil; and mitigate the effects of global climate change. For this, the COS must be quantified and monitored from accurate and quality data, and integrate this information with the factors or covariates that control its dynamics in the soil, and thus, improve the parametrization of the COS models used to simulate COS at different scales. However, the comparison between the results, the estimation of COS stock and its monitoring is increasingly difficult since there are more than eight methodologies for its quantification.
