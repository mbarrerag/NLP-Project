# Computer strategies to characterize topological properties of pathologically altered functional brain networks.

The alterations of consciousness, including the disturbances of consciousness (DOC), propose a challenging scenario from the perspective of prognosis and diagnosis. Functional images of the brain at rest reduce this desafication, offering alternatives to explore the cerebral activity in altered states of consciousness. In addition, variations in functional connectivity at rest have been related to alterations of consciousness. So, considering the evidence of changes of connectivity in altered states of consciousness, this thesis investigates in new computational approximations to describe topological characteristics associated with the state of rest connectivity for altered states of consciousness. This work presents two strategies for the characterization of topological traits underlying the graph models. The first strategy measures the properties of the graph or functional connectome, linking their variations with the level of consciousness. However, this approach is limited by the basis of interaction by pairs of the graph model, and the central strategy is adjusted for the rest state.
