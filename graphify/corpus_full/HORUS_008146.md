# Utility of Flaps Nanoperforantes (U. N. A. L) for defect coverage large on the face: case Series

Coverage defects on the face are frequent due to the high incidence of malignant pathologies, such as skin cancer, whose fundamental pillar is based on wide local resection, leaving as consequences facial deformities with great impact on emotional health and functional of the patients. Given this, several reconstructive techniques have been developed by facial subunits. However, for large defects, the technique of choice is free flaps. The objective of this study is to present a reconstructive alternative for these defects based on Ubiquitous nanoperforatorRandom Location Flaps (U.N.A.L), which arise from modifications to Keystone flaps, taking into account their definition of being an island flap with free design based on a random pedicle area that can be modified according to the anatomical region; These are a safe, simple and versatile option. A retrospective case series of 77 patients (89 flaps) is presented, without age restriction, with defects located only in the head and neck and who underwent surgery between 2012 and 2022. The demographic profile is described, as well as the etiology of defects, flap characteristics, and postoperative outcomes. 89 flaps were performed in 77 patients. The most common etiology was malignant tumors, the most common being basal cell carcinoma (37.6%) followed by squamous cell carcinoma (28.57%) and melanoma (10.39%). The majority of the flaps performed were used to reconstruct the cheek subunit (31.7%), using designs based on a 1:1 - 1:2 ratio with respect to the size of the defect and with an average pedicle area of 28%. Likewise, the most frequent movement was propeller (42.69%). Regarding the outcomes, complications only occurred in 33.7% of the flaps, being major in 16 flaps. The most frequent complication was partial necrosis (7.8%) and dehiscence (6.7%), without total loss of any flap. This study presents ubiquitous nanoperforator flaps of random location as an alternative for the reconstruction and coverage of defects in the face and neck, having the advantages of their versatility, simplicity in the surgical technique and vascular safety compared to reconstruction with free flaps
