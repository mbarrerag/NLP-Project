# Bioremediation of synthetic dyes through the combined method of adsorption and fermentation in solid state.

The environmental pollution of water effluents has increased alarmingly in recent years, becoming a serious problem facing our society.Industrialization is perhaps one of the main causes, since sectors such as textile, food and pharmaceutical discharge large volumes of effluents with a high content of colourants.These molecules are characterized by their high resistance to weather, light, and air.In addition, their color makes them visible in still trace concentrations, equivalent to 1 ppm, causing an aesthetic damage of the environment.However, even more serious impacts are associated with the detriment of the flora and fauna of the ecosystem due to the reduction of the synthetic process, as a consequence of a partial blocking of the solar root, affecting the trophic chain and increasing the chemical demand for oxygen [1,2].In addition, the biotoxicity of some colourants has been reported, given their more favourable character, the more efficient, the more attractive, the more attractive, the more the dissolving methods of the solvent, the more the more attractive, the more the more the more the more the more the more the more the more the less the less the less the less the less the less the less the less the less the less the less the less the less the less the less the less the less the less the, our Research Group SIRYTCOR has been working on a strategy for the efficient bioremediation of dissolved dyes, which articulates in its first stage the adsorption process and the FES as the biological process of the second stage.
