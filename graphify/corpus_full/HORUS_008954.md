# MEDICINE PLANTS FOR HEALTH CARE OF MISSING INDIGENOUS WOMEN AND THEIR COMMUNITY

Colombia is a multi-ethnic and multicultural country, it has 102 indigenous communities, governed by its own laws of origin, autonomy and governance, preserving its cultural identity through its millennial practices, customs, knowledge and spiritual forms, which guide its life at the individual, family and collective levels; in Bogotá there are 14 recognized communities, one of them is the Muisca Suba Indigenous Cabildo. In this community there is little documentary information about the ancestral practices of care of the health of women and their community. These practices allow to maintain the well-being of the individual and the community from ancestral knowledge and uses, and thus its conservation in time (1). Qualitative descriptive research with a focus on content analysis. Based on the inclusion criteria and prior informed consent, the health-care methods were carried out, and semi-structured interviews, which were recorded, and later transcribed and verified. The practices found are consistent with the literature obtained from other indigenous communities, many of them Colombian, such as the Ambiká-Pijao, Aiwakuna Tsepajbo and Panan Indigenous Reserve (3) (4) (5) communities. Limitations: Qualitative research requires time from both the researcher and the community, which are sometimes difficult to synchronize
