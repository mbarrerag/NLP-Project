# Physical and Bioprocessing Techniques for Improving Nutritional, Microbiological, and Functional Quality of Bee Pollen

Bee pollen has a rich content of nutrients and several bioactive compounds. However, its stability and digestibility have some limitations as a food product for human consumption. In the first place, fresh bee pollen has high water content and water activity, which makes it very prompt to microbial spoilage. Also, the microstructure of pollen grains can be highly resistant to several conditions, including digestion, hindering the bioavailability of its nutrients. This chapter discusses several technological strategies that have been proposed for improving its shelf life and its characteristics for human consumption, including physical processing (e.g., by drying, heat treatments, and high hydrostatic pressure), bioprocessing (e.g., lactic fermentation and enzymatic hydrolysis), and combined approaches. Finally, some examples of pollen inclusion as an ingredient of processed foods, such as fruit pulps, baked goods, and fermented milk, are presented
