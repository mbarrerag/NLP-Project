# Monetary policy transmission mechanism in the United States to Latin America and other emerging economies: an application of the GVAR model (2002Q1 - 2019Q4)

This study analyzes the impact of a contractional monetary policy shock in the United States on some of the main economies of Latin America and other emerging economies using the methodology of Global Self-Regressive Vectors (GVAR). For this purpose, a quarterly database covering the period 2002Q1-2019Q4 and comprising a total of 27 economies is used. Additionally, domestic variables of real activity, consumer price level, share rates, short term interest rates and sovereign risk measures and local financial conditions, as well as international prices of some commodities as global variables are considered. As for other emerging economies, the transmission mechanism is maintained but there is a lower impact, possibly due to lower commercial interconnection or greater economic resilience of these economies. Finally, this study also seeks to enrich the full-scale modeling literature GVAR from considering the regional economies, including the regional economies. The results of the structural impulse response analysis validated convention transfer channels and suggest that an increase in the Federal Reserve's policy rate negative impacts economic activity, currencies, and local stock price indexes; it increase sober risk and the policy interest rates of other economies. Regarding other emerging economies, the transmission mechanism remains but shows a small impact, possible due to lower commercial interconnection or greater economic resilience of these economies. Lastly, this study also seeks to enrich the GVAR modeling literature by focusing on Latin America, including economies such as Colombia, and considering regional interconnections, which could provide a more comprehensive view of the economic and financial dynamics in the region and global integration
