# Variability of the resting heart rate mediated by the vagus nerve and its association with the financial risk preferences under stress

The answer to stress has two components: one linked to the hypothalamus-hypophysis-adrenal axis (HHA) and the other to the autonomic nervous system (SNA). The literature on financial risk taking has focused mainly on the effects of cortisol, related to the HHA axis. However, this article examines the influence of the NSA, measured through variability of the cardiac frequency at rest mediated by the vagus nerve (VHRV). A total of 121 participants (60 women) were divided according to whether their VMHRV was below the median. Next, participants were randomly assigned to a stress test group (tsst) or to a control group. Financial risk preferences were assessed using an Eckel and Grossman task with a probability of 50-500% compatible with incentives. The participants in the TSST with high VMHRV had a higher probability of choosing high risk-level lotteries, compared to the other participants (P = 0.0189).
