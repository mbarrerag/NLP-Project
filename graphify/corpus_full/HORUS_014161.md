# Someros Corals as a Source of Seed for Restoration: A Strategy for Adaptation to Climate Change from Nature-Based Solutions

Coral reefs are highly sensitive and high risk ecosystems, product of the synergy between disturbances at global, regional and local scale. The area of Santa Marta and the Tayrona National Park - PNNT are not exempt from the current climate change scenario. Despite this, in the NPNT the values of coral cover have remained relatively stable in recent decades (approx. 30% overall average); therefore, they are classified among the best conserved in the Colombian Caribbean, as a corridor of connectivity between the Reserves of the Ciénaga Grande Biosphere of Santa Marta and Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta, and as a refuge for corals in the climate crisis. In contrast, the reefs of the Santa Marta area have lost more than 30% of their living coral coverage, affecting the well-being of the local communities. Despite the apparent stability in the values of coral coverage alive researchers in the PNNT reefs, the increase in frequency and intensity of the events of coral bleaching occurred in the last 25 years (Aprox. 11 events), suggests that the coral recovery capacity can be limited and causing the population to be caused by the lack of coral reefs. In a cross-sectional way, it is imperative to develop educational strategies that integrate various fields of knowledge, linking different sectors of society, to construct in a participatory way an alternative to address the loss of coral cover and its associated ecosystem services. The advantages of the proposed method include genetic diversity, selection of natural nurseries, reduction of lesions in coral tissue that affect the physiological performance and survival of colonies. This project will advance knowledge with scientific bases that contribute to understanding, restoration and protection of the coral formations of the NPNNT and the Santa Marta area, support the training of undergraduate and postgraduate students, and strengthen capacities in local communities through the training of artisanal fishermen in coral reef monitoring and restoration techniques, as a strategy for the empowerment of the territory and transfer of scientific and cultural knowledge
