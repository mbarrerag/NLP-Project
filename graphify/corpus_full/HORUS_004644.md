# Assessment of the integration of distributed energy resources based on restrictions of the distribution network. Case study for San Andrés Isla

The increase in the Non-Conventional Sources of Renewable Energy (FNCER) to small and large scale changes the dynamics of planning and operation of the electrical energy networks, gaining importance for the Network Operators (OR) to establish mechanisms for assessing the integration impact and the maximum capacity supported under parameters of operation and reliability. This thesis applies the concept of Hosting Capacity (HC) in the case of the study of the San Andrés Island network to evaluate performance indicators using stochastic and systematic simulation methods with DigSilent PowerFactory in integration with python. It is evaluated 25 levels of penetration and for each one of them is performed in the light of the variable nature of the generation, with a total of 25,000 iterations. The factor that most limits the integration of FNCER into the San Andrés Island network is the loadability of the transformers. However, the limit is higher than the percentage of penetration established in the regulation of the Non-Interconnected Zones (ZNI). Ventional Sources of Renewable Energy in the grid of San Andres Island is the loadability of transformers. However, this limit is high than the penetration percentage established in the regulation for Non-Interconnected Zones. Therefore, it is concluded that integrating Sources of Renewable Energy under regulation limits is safe
