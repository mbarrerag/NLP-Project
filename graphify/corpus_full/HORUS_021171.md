# Experimental campuses for climate change: physiological tolerances and ecosystem services of wooded trees in three locations of the National University

Climate change, the result of increased human activities associated with urbanization and industry, has generated increases in temperatures and frequency in droughts, with impacts on the performance of organisms, the provision of ecosystem services, the resilience and sustainability of ecosystems throughout the planet. Urban ecosystems, where more than half of the world’s population lives, are highly vulnerable ecosystems because their waterproof and high carbon emissions reinforce the impacts of climate change on species and their ecological function. Forests and urban woodland improve the quality of life in cities through the mitigation of the effects of climate change and the provision of ecosystem services. Using physiological and anatomical techniques in vegetation coupled with spectra analysis, the present proposal aims to evaluate the responses of plants to predicted environmental changes under climate change scenarios and their ability to offer ecosystem regulatory services, using three campuses of the National University of Colombia (Bogotá, Medellín and Leticia), located under adaptation conditions.
