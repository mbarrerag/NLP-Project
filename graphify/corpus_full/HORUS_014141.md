# Geochronology of the metamorphism of the La Pedrera Formation, Rio Caquetá: Paleogeographic implications and the evaluation of mineral resources in the Colombian Amazon

The Orient Colombian is a geographical region of great ecological and environmental importance, which houses a wide biodiversity and plays an important role in the regulation of the global climate. The central-orient segment of this region, far from the foot-mount Andean is characterized by the presence of an extensive geological register of an igneous character formed between 1850 Ma and 1340 Ma (Figure 1; Ibañez-Perez et al., 2021; Bonilla et al., 2021).This history includes a series of orogenic events that occurred prior to 1400 Ma and a later, more recent, more young extentional phase between 1400 and 1340 Ma (Bonilla-Perez et al., 2021; 2023). Associated with these events, mineralizations associated with chemical elements have been recognized associated with strategic elements for the energy transition and knowledge development (n, Nb, Ta, W, Ti; Cammer et al., 2011). Although in recent years, geological research has been carried out to improve the geochronological knowledge of these events (Coroni et al., 2016; Boilla-Pera-Perez et al., 2020, 2020, 2020, B., 2020, 2020 _x0094_ carried out with the sponsorship of Minciencias (Colombia) and the FAPESP foundation of Brazil (Project HERMES 50830).During the field work, geological locations were sampled where metamorphic rocks are exposed, including muscovistic schistes in the Caquetá River and biotytic neises in proximity to the municipality of Mitú (Figure 1). These rocks associated with the La Pedrera Formation and the Migmatítico Complex of Mitú (Bonilla-Perez et al., 2021), are a fundamental record of the deformative history Precámbrica that conform this region.
