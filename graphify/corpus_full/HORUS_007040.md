# Mechanisms to prevent the capture of the regulator in higher education

The regulatory power of the executive is one of the factors that have enabled to materialize the principle of just order preached by the State of Law, since it is the instrument that develops the will of the legislator to intervene in the market and perpetuate the general welfare. In carrying out these functions, it is expected that the executive can issue regulations with the highest technical rigour, without exceeding the powers granted by higher rules. However, one of the main causes that generate risks to these powers is the possibility that the recipients of the regulations or representatives of the political sectors of the government can influence the exercise of this administrative function with the aim of obtaining a private benefit. In the administrative sector of the higher education, the intervention scheme designed to ensure the provision of the service that also has the legal connotation presents several features that put at risk the objectivity of the executive at the time of issuing regulations.
