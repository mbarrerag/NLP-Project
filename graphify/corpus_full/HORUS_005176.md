# A random forest model to predict soil organic carbon storage in mangroves from Southern Colombian Pacific coast

Mangrove ecosystems serve as significant reservoirs of carbon stocks, particularly within the soil. However, these ecosystems face widespread degradation globally, underscoring the necessity for comprehensive technical insights to facilitate effective management. Enhancing the precision of soil organic carbon estimates within mangroves necessitates access to precise field data. Hence, this study aimed to assess the soil organic carbon stored in mangrove ecosystems along the southern Colombian Pacific coast. Employing a Random Forest model, constructed with data from 28 soil profiles spanning a depth of 2 m and 16 predictor variables, we evaluated the model's performance using repeated 10-fold cross-validation (with 5 random repeats). Uncertainty in carbon storage predictions was quantified through 95% confidence intervals. Our model successfully accounted for 88% of the variability in soil organic carbon storage. Depth, cation exchange capacity, pH, and sand content emerged as the most influential predictors of soil organic carbon storage variation. The study estimated a mean of 270.32 ± 52.75 t C ha−1, with a total carbon reserve of 25458088 t in the study area. Notably, this value falls below global estimates, suggesting a potential overestimation of carbon stocks in mangrove soils by existing global models
