# Biocalcification as a method of microbiological remediation of fractures in rocks.

The mechanical and hydraulic properties of the rocks are highly determined by structures such as fractures, empty spaces and discontinuities, existing both in microscopic and macroscopic scales. Various economic activities are carried out in and with rocks, being necessary costly and complex engineering works that require stability, economy and durability. Thus, developing properties of resistance and rigidity of the rocks is of high importance for the geological, civil, oil, hydraulic and mining engineering, among others. To cement the empty spaces in the rocks can be used the precipitation of calcium carbonate induced microbiologically (Microbiologically Enhanced Crack Remediation, MECR), which consists of the precipitation of carbonates in the spaces between fractures and in the pores of the rocks, to act as a cementing material, in a way that obtains a rock of superior properties in terms of resistance, rigidity and permeability, mainly.
