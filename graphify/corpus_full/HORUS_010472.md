# Prevalence of pathogens associated with neonatal bovine diarrhoea syndrome in Valle del Cauca, Colombia

Neonatal bovine diarrhoea (NBD) is a disease that affects neonates in livestock production. Its origin is multifactorial and generates great economic losses. The aim of the study was to determine the prevalence of etiological agents through a cross-sectional epidemiological study by selective sampling in animals susceptible to the syndrome DNB in the Valle del Cauca (Colombia). For the study, livestock stockings of intensive and semi-intensive dairy systems were selected. In the initial phase, the characterization of 25 cattle was carried out through a survey of producers. Randomly eight productive systems were selected from the respondents; in each of them five calves were analyzed, which were sampled directly from the rectum, with a gloved hand. In total, 40 calves under three months of age of the pure Gyrdia, Gyrolando, Jersey and mestizo.
