# THE ROLE OF SMALL RESERVES IN THE CONSERVATION OF MAMMIFES IN COLOMBIA.

The role of private reserves is generally underestimated due to its small size and discontinuous distribution. To examine its relevance in the conservation of mammals, with this work we address three objectives: 1) to identify the pattern of size, altitude and distribution of Colombian private reserves of the National System of Protected Areas - SINAP, 2) to examine, as a case study, the richness of mammals and geographical patterns of twelve reserves, and 3) to identify the contribution of small reserves to the conservation of Colombian mammals. For the first objective, we analyze the size, altitude and distribution of private reserves of SINAP discriminated by biogeographic provinces. For the following objectives, we evaluate the relationship between the wealth of mammals, with area, altitude and connectivity in the twelve reserves. We find that most private reserves have areas less than 100 hectares, they are between 1500 and 2500 m and they are located in the province of Norandina. For the twelve reserves examined, we find a wealth of 224 species of eleven mammal orders, with an average of 42.7 species (19 bats and 23.7 non-fly mammals).
