# MOLECULARY NETWORKS AS PREDICTORS OF CHEMICAL DIVERSITY AND RESPONSE TO FITOPATOGENS OF ARBORE SPECIES PRESENT IN REMANENTS OF ANDINOUS FORCES

Introduction of plant and fungi relations were established from the first lineages of their evolution, giving of a symbiotic or pathogenic type. To survive the parasitisttic of fungi, plants have developed structural defenses, which are consumable to avoid the establishment, and chemical, which are induced responses to detect and counteract the invasion. The study of these relationships can be done by means of metabolomic techniques to establish molecular networks, which allows to address groups of complex mixtures and to discover which chemical nuclei may be related to the adaptation to the environment from which the samples come from, or if they are restricted to a certain taxonomic group, and in turn if they are related to biological activity. This work determined the antifungal capacity of 48 species arboreas of five species of forest andin Colletrichum gloeosporoid and Fuses of etanolic extraction, submitted to tests of anti-methyl growth. micelial with the method of agar poisoned REF on three species of phytopathogenous fungi phytopatogen: Botrytis cinerea, Colletitis, Colletrichumum clo, and glotrichum gloospopopopo in tannins, pterocarpans, macrolactams, diterpenes, sesquiterpenes, tetraterpenes, saponins, benzeneides, phenylamines, phenolic glycosides, halobenzenes, oligosaccharides and pyridines
