# Food services freight (Trip) attraction in urban areas of emerging economies

Efficient freight transport systems facilitate the exchange of goods and services, generating a market growth in urban areas. The study aims to characterize the attraction of freight travel from food services in an urban area of the Metropolitan Area of the Aburrá Valley (AMVA). A database of the AMVA Cargo Transport Study was used. This classification contains data from 266 establishments. To estimate the attraction of cargo travel, the study developed linear and nonlinear regression models with the method of regression of ordinary minimum squares. Three final equations were obtained to model the relationship between weekly and variable cargo travel such as operating hours, which is a new variable that contributes to academic literature, and the total area, found that these independent variables that better describe the attraction of cargo travel. This means that the range of weekly cargo travel obtained is between 1 and 15 cargo trips. Operating hours vary from 4 to 24 hours, and the total area varies from 4 to 670 square meters. These results offer useful information for decision makers and owners of logistics companies or establishments to suggest accurate public use of fuel resources
