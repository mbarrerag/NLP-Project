# HYDROCLIMATE RESEARCH PROGRAM FOR THE ANDES (ANDEX) AND THE CONNECTION WITH RIFS

The Andes are the longest cordillera on Earth covering more than 7.000 km over seven countries in South America (Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Chile and Argentina). The Andes contain all kinds of ecosystems and life zones, climates and weather patterns (from the rainiest place on Earth on the Pacific coast of Colombia to the driest on the Atacama desert in Chile). At the same time, the Andes are pretty crowded with more than 50 million people demanding resources, water, energy, food, shelter, among others). Climate and weather in the Andes are controlled by a large suite of atmospheric circulation patterns in and around South America including the ITCZ, low-level jets, aerial rivers, mesoscale convective systems, and the dynamics of tropical, subtropical and extratropical systems causing a great deal of weather patterns, and their associated high-impact events like floods, droughts, heat waves, frost, with ample socio-economic and environmental impacts. The Andes and the Amazon River basin conform a coupled system, because the low-lying Amazon exports water vapor to feed rainfall over the Andes, and the Andes export river flows, sediments and nutrients to the low-lying Amazonia. Deforestation at both ends is affecting (and could collapse) the functioning of the whole system, converting the tropical rainforest into a savanna (much drier and hot), putting in peril the supply of water for many cities on the Andes and southeastern South America. The Andes are also connected with the Pacific Ocean by low-level jets, atmospheric rivers and weather patterns that are associated with floods and droughts in SA. Large portions of the Andes make part of the water and food basket of the continent. Glaciers and high Andean ecosystems like paramos provide water for many important cities including Bogotá, Quito, Lima, and La Paz, as well as for irrigation of agriculture which sustains food security in the continent. At the same time, intense storms from MCSs, summer convection and atmospheric rivers falling down over the steep and populated terrains of the Andes are a perfect recipe for disasters and risks. For these reasons we have proposed to establish a Regional Hydrologic Program of GEWEX focused on the Andes. We had a foundational meeting in Medellin, Colombia, on December of 2017, where we decided to prepare a White Book with the current knowledge and the main research gaps regarding the hydroclimate of the Andes, and discussed further steps. Later on, on October 2018 we had the first ANDEX meeting in Santiago de Chile, along with the meetings of the GEWEX Hydro-climatology Panel and INARCH, with more than 30 participants. We also had workshop in Quito, Ecuador, on April of 2019 to discuss the advance of the chapters of the ANDEX White Book. Other activities include oral and poster sessions at the 2019 AGU Fall Meeting in San Francisco, and also at the virtual EGU meetings of 2020 and 2021. As a result of these activities, we came up with three overarching questions to guide the scientific agenda of ANDEX. We also launched the ANDEX web page. It has been active for the last 6 months. It's of great help to gain visibility to ANDEX. Regarding the White Book, I should mention that it contains 9 chapters, and that most of them were peer-reviewed and published in this special issue of the journal Frontiers, Earth Sciences.
