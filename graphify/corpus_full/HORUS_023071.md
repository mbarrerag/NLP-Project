# CASE MANAGEMENT IMPROVE OUTCOMES IN POLYPATHOLOGY PATIENTS AND THEIR FAMILY CAREGIVERS: A QUASI-EXPERIMENTAL STUDY

Chronic noncommunicable diseases (NCD) have an increasing global weight. Cardiovascular diseases, cancer and metabolic diseases are the leading causes of worldwide morbidity and mortality. Although the topic about chronic diseases is making itself aware and world statistics present their individual epidemiological behavior, it is common for people to suffer from two or more simultaneous chronic diseases, a condition known as polypathology. When the person with pluripathology is approached, it must be considered that they are in a complex chronic condition and therefore highly vulnerable. The case management model has proven to be a viable and cost-effective alternative for treating patients with ploypathology. The purpose of this study was to determine the effectiveness of the case management model for improving the quality of life in polypathology patient and improving the role taking and burden in family caregivers. Quasi-experimental study with pre and post test measurements with a single group. 232 patients with pluripathogy and their main family caregivers received the intervention based on the case management model during the year 2019. The case management model is an intervention based on an interprofessional approach to the patient, it implies the assessment and multidimensional intervention in hospital and home settings. It also implies the development of a personalized care plan and follow-up through home visits and telephone contact. QoL SF-36 instrument was applied to the patients. 22 ítems-Zarit Burden interview and ROL-Role taking instrument were applied to the family caregivers. The study was approved by the ethics committee of the University Foundation for Health Sciences. Informed consent was applied. The sample of this study was 64.22% women, 43.97% married and 32.76% widowed, 71.12% have low income and 91.38 have medium complexity. The patients that received case management intervention increased their QoL (p <0.05) in the pain, social function and mental health dimension of the SF-36, whereas the physical function, physical role and emotional role dimensions of the QoL were decreased. The caregivers that received the intervention decreased their burden (p <0.05) and improved their role taking (p <0.05) Conclusion: The case management model proved to be an innovative and effective approach to improve pain, social function and mental health of QoL in patients with polypathology. In addition, it showed effects on the burden and role taking of their family caregiver. It is required to conduct RCT ́s to test the effectiveness of the model in Colombia. The case management model is a promising field for addressing polypathology in the Colombian health system
