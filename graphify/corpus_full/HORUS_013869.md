# Strengthening agroecosystems through the cultural appropriation of agroecological beekeeping in the Nima River Basin, Cauca Valley

Human activities are causing a rapid loss of biological diversity and ecosystem functionality, especially due to the intensification of agriculture (Dirzo et al., 2014). However, postmodern sciences, such as agroecology, have demonstrated that the agricultural landscapes can be heterogeneous and provide sustainable habitats (Tscharntke et al., 2007). Agroecosystems are not only managed for food production, but also for ecosystem services such as crop pollination and pest control (Landon-Lane, 2004). The structure of the agroecosystem is closely related to its functionality, and the complexity of the landscape and agricultural practices can affect the diversity of pollinators and plants (Power, Kelly, & Stout, 2012). In the context of agroecosystems coffee, wild bees play an important role as bioindicators of the quality of the agroecosystem. Although coffee cultivation does not favour the diversity of pollinators (Morales et al., 2022), the practices of the farms, led by women, often, have been observed by women. The aim is to identify the needs and opportunities for learning and managing bees, with the aim of co-creating strategies for socio-economic appropriation through meliponiculture and/or agroecological beekeeping, through co-creation processes involving local actors and establishing alternative production and complementary to coffee production, all of which are aimed at contributing to the strengthening of the Rio Nima community, which is hit by violent groups outside the law, by promoting the conservation of biodiversity and the generation of sustainable economic alternatives in coffee agroecosystems, while encouraging the cultural, environmental and productive participation of local communities, strengthening social fabric and networks in the territory
