# Multilevel model as a tool for cross-sectional analysis of data from the National Time Use Survey 2020-2021

Intersectionality establishes that the overlap of factors such as gender, ethnicity or the area where one is inhabited configure systemic inequalities, being a concept widely used in economic and political as a focus for the analysis of population data, since it allows to evaluate the level of affectation to social groups of specific situations such as a pandemic, the rise in unemployment, violence, among others. Therefore, it is proposed to construct a multilevel model with the data from the National Survey of Time Use - ENUT 2020-20-2021, using these intersecting factors as grouping variables to model the number of hours used for unpaid jobs such as the response variable. In addition, a comparison is made with the results obtained when the data are applied to these same data by the decomposition Oaxaca-Blinder, used usually for analysis processes with a particular focus. It is expected that the results can provide relevant information for the construction of public policies with intersectional and gender approaches in the country .
