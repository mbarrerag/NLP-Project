# Habitat effects of resettlement programmes: approach to the Manantial neighbourhood, San Cristobal locality (2000-2024)

The main objective of this final master's work was to determine the effects on the habitat generated by the resettlement programmes in the populations located in areas of non-mitigatable risk, taking as a reference the Manantial neighborhood between 2000 and 2024. The inquiry that was proposed established a dialogue between the governmental actors, the resettled people and the communities that are in risk zones. Methodologically, the socio-historic analysis proposed by Thompson and the theory of the collective action proposed by Ostrom, involving the concepts of habitat, informality, risk and resettlement. From the idea of global vulnerability, the idea of the resettlement in Bogotá was described as a normative way of implementing the ecobarrio as a form of resistance to resettlement. It was concluded that the main effect on the habitat caused by the resettlement is the historical and social fracture in the community dynamics, which implies the construction of other forms of habitat built in the informality and highlight the creation of the ecobarrio as a form of resistance to resettlement. It was also concluded that the main effect of the habitat caused by the social and historical structure in the community dynamics, which implies the construction of other forms of housing for those who remain in the territory and the context. and resettlement. From the idea of global vulnerability, how resettlement is implemented in Bogotá was described normatively and the perception of the officials of the entities in charge of this process was highlighted. In addition, the historical context of the social construction of the Manantial Neighborhood was explored to address the importance of the socially constructed habitat in the informality scenario and high the creation of the eco-neighborhood as a form of resistance to resettlement.
