# Ni catalysts from hydrotalcitas immobilized into monoliths for the transformation of greenhouse gases

The carbon dioxide and methane are the greenhouse gases (GHGs) most abundant in the atmosphere, which is the product, among other reasons, of the use of fossil fuels, the livestock and the emission of biogas in the landfills.The simultaneous use of these two GHGs in the production of valued products, which make part of profitable circular economies, is of great interest research, environmental and industrial.The dry reforming of methane is a catalytic process that can be used for this purpose transforming these two GHGs into synthesis gas, a key intermediary for the subsequent obtaining of oxygenated chemicals.In the process, catalysts of different types have been used, being those that contain nickel the greatest economic interest due to their low cost.However, despite the fact that the process is attractive because of the potential for fixing and transformation of GHGs that it possesses, the endothermic character and the tendency to formation of carbon have conditioned the process for its scaling. talcitas, in ceramic monoliths of cordierite, in order to generate a commercial presentation of the catalyst that has characteristics suitable for its possible scaling in the medium term. Given the fundamental role of Ce in the stability of the catalyst, the effect of the concentration of this metal on the physical chemistry of the solid will be studied and the effect of suppressing the treatment of the catalyst with hydrogen prior to reaction will be studied, with the aim of reducing costs and facilitating the operation. The project will allow the completion of a Master's thesis in Chemical Sciences, as well as will link two undergraduate students (Chemical) to carry out their undergraduate work, and will be articulated with the outreach and training activities carried out in an Intersed Research Semillero that includes a student of the Special Program of Admission and Academic Mobility (PEAMA)
