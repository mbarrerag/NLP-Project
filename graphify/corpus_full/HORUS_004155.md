# Ricardo Sánchez Ángel. A Hope Principle. The Russian Revolution. Bogotá: Tirant lo Blanch / Universidad Libre, 2022. 256 pages.

This book analyses a decisive period in the history of the struggles against capitalism and the consolidation of the popular revolutions on an international scale. Its guiding thread is the Russian revolution of 1917, a milestone around which the narrative is gravitated in the desire to explain both the origins and the lessons that can still be drawn from that workers' uprising. The book is composed of nine chapters that could well be divided into three parts. In the first (chapters 1 to 3), a context is offered for the years before the revolution; the interrelations between the Russian revolution and the Great War, as well as the way in which the diplomatic and organizational skill of the revolutionaries prevented the interests of the war powers from dragging the Russian people into the international conflict; finally it addresses the internal contradictions of the revolution. In the second part (chapters 4 to 6), the book develops one of the central the thesis: the effect of internationalization of the Russian revolution and its relationship with the American of the nineteenth century and the Mexican of the twentieth century. For this purpose, it exposes the way its authorship extends to different parts of the world, paying special attention to its influence in Latin America.
