# Alternative synthesis of dibenzazepine derivatives using essential oil of holy grass (Piper auritum).

The research work described in this document presents the results obtained in the search for synthetic precursors from renewable sources and their subsequent chemical transformation. To meet the objectives of this research, two stages were proposed where the first part focused on obtaining essential oil from holy grass (Piper auritum) and the second stage consisted in exploring its synthetic potential for obtaining azepin derivatives. The essential oil from holy grass, which has a high content of safrol (phenylpropanoid belonging to the list of illicit substances of narcotic drugs and psychoactive substances in Colombia, and the second stage consisted in exploring its synthetic potential for obtaining azepinic derivatives. The essential oil from holy grass, which has a high content of safrol (phenyl-phenylpropanoid belonging to the list of illicit substances of narcotic drugs and psychoactives in Colombia, was a total content of phenylpropanoid (4,26 g safrol/100 g of plant material) and the best quality was obtained by means of conventional and microwave-assisted by means of conventional hydrodestilllation using the aerial part (leaves, stems, stems and tal and inflorescences – Antioquia). The oil with the highest production yield, 5,18 % %p/p/g, This allowed the production of a derivative of dibenzazocin with a yield of 40%; moreover, the oxidation of a secondary amine to generate a nitrone and its subsequent intramolecular cycling 1.3-dipolar, which generated ten benzazepin derivatives with yields between 19 and 48 % for the two stages. In conclusion, both proposals (azocins and benzazepins) were carried out by 5 and 6 synthetic transformations respectively, having as overall reaction yields 10.4% and a range between 5 - 14%
