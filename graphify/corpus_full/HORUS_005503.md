# AFROCOLOMBIAN LIFE METHOD AND FISHERIES TERRITORIES IN CHOCO (COLOMBIA)

The Colombian Pacific is a territory-region occupied mostly by black communities that have a traditional way of life based on fishing, agriculture and traditional extractivism. However, with the arrival of developmental logics, this way of life begins to undergo transformations that place traditional territorialities in confrontation with other logics external to the community. Here it is intended (1) to characterize the Afro-Colombian traditional way of life, especially in its symbolic relationship with artisanal fishing, (2) to identify fishing territorialities and (3) to analyze the territorial conflicts present in Bahía Solano. For this, field work was done with participating observation, ethnographic reports, photodocumentation, interviews with semi-structured script and participatory social cartography, with the aim of providing this contribution, from a geographical and interdisciplinary perspective that is still absent in the investigations about this region. It is observed that with the insertion of communities in production logics connected with the outside, more profitable as tourism, drug trafficking and industrial fishing, new territorialities are superimposed.
