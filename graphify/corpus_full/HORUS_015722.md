# Genetic variation in Rhizophagus irregularis influences soil carbon fluxes in tropical soils under cassava (Manihot esculenta Crantz) cultivation.

Closing the gap between current and necessary food production, while mitigating anthropogenic carbon emissions to the atmosphere, is one of the main limitations for sustainable agriculture.Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) act as stabilizers of soil structure by means of the physical and chemical aggregation of soil, encapsulating organic carbon within aggregates and protecting it from microbial activity. Here and in previous field studies we have observed very large differences in the yield of cassava when inoculated with various isolated Rhizophagus irregularis depth, suggesting that the dynamics of carbon under the soil could be greatly boosted by the fungus. We carried out a field test in Colombia, Kenya and Tanzania to evaluate the effect of different isolates of Rhizophagus irregularis on the size of the carbon, the accumulation (inclusterity of soil) and emissions to the atmosphere (respirator of soil) in soils under commercial yuca crops.
