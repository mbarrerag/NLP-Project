# Apart from Magdalena and society: Bioarchaeology of marginality in a pre-Hispanic-historical cemetery in the port of Honda, Tolima

The bioarqueology of marginality is a recent line of research that allows to address and evaluate the health conditions of social groups marginalized by economic, political or ideological circumstances (Mant & Holland, 2019; Michael et al., 2021). The cemetery of Puerto Bogotá, excavated in 2018 and located on the eastern bank of the Magdalena river, is an isolated site of Honda society, and is an archaeological finding relevant to the area by having two periods of location and a large number of skeletal individuals, beginning to fill the information gap for the colonial Honda and complementing the information of the pre-Hispanic period. The osteological analysis, focused on understanding the living conditions of the two periods present on the site (prehispanic and republican) allowed to see that the traces of physiological stress were predominant in the historical portion of the population, although it was more frequently affected by the male individuals and the immature (the metabolic diseases) and the young and middle adults (the inflammatory processes, changes associated with labor and traumas). The sample, of 102 skeletal individuals, was composed by the historical period., and a good case to understand the lives of the decaded, filling a great information gap for colonial Honda.
