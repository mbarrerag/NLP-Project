# CONSTITUTION AND CONSTITUTIONALITY: THEIR CONCEPTUAL, HISTORICAL AND SOCIAL DIVERSITY

The term constitution can refer to three types of realities.One can say, for example, that a country has a strong constitution, similar to the way we say that a person has a strong or robust constitution. In this case we refer to a structure or a factual essence that societies such as objects or living beings would have. The ancient Greeks and the thinkers of the Middle Ages conceived constitutions in this sense. It is also possible to conceive of the constitution as the consecration of the model of society to which a country wishes to come one day.The Jacobins in France and some Latin American political leaders of the nineteenth century saw the constitutions in this way.We can also say that the constitution is a fundamental rule or law regulating the exercise of political power.In this sense a constitution looks like a basic code of public law.Are these complementary or exclusive meanings?If they are complementary, what does their relationship with them also give?And if they are excluding them, is there any of them that impose upon others?In order to answer these questions, it is necessary to stop briefly in each of these terms of the above-mentioned conceptions, although they are in the centre of what is called the constitutionalism, and if they are also give it is a
