# Computational study of the reactivity of copper complexes as catalysts and dismutase superoxide

The reactive oxygen species that occur continuously in metabolism are the causes of oxidative stress in cells, a phenomenon that has been related to several diseases and that represent a great risk to health such as cancer, diabetes and neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer and Parkinson. For this reason the study of these reactive species and the strategies on how to regulate them have been of great importance in recent years.There are several radical species involved in oxidative stress, one of them is the superoxide anion which is the most produced and can cause chain reactions forming other reactive species.However, in the organism is found the superoxide dismutase enzyme which is one of the most powerful antioxidants in the cells and is responsible for the reaction of dismutation of the superoxide anion in oxygen and present hydrogen peroxide work, two species less dangerous for the cells. Therefore, it has generated interest in finding molecules that are able to mimic the antioxidant activity of the previous superoxide dismutase enzyme in order to act as supplements to the action of the same proposed one, in case of damage of the oxidative stress and neutralizing the organism
