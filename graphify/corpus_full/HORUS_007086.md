# Structural risk assessment due to earthquake-tsunami events in the urban area of San Andrés de Tumaco, Colombia

Tumaco, a coastal city in Colombia, faces significant risks of earthquakes and tsunamis due to its geográ location and geological characteristics. This master's thesis aims to understand and quantify the complex interaction of these natural hazards, their possible impacts on the infrastructure and population of the city, and the implications for resilience to disasters. The structural risk assessment begins with an analysis of the seismic threat, which includes the characterization of three scenarios of earthquakes and tsunamis: those that occurred in 1906 and 1979, and a worse case scenario. This study also incorporates an exposure model that identifies and classifies the constructions of the city, considering the present structural typologies, the occupation classes and spatial distribution of these elements. Based on previous research on seismic events and tsunami in Tumaco, this work adopts a multiple threat approach for the assessment of structural risk. When assessing the complex interaction between these two natural phenomena, the threats are combined using the basic theory of probability, Boolean logical rules and assuming the statistical independence of the two phenomena.
