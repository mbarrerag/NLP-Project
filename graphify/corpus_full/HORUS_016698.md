# Functional connectivity as a conservation tool in the landsscapes of the north of Chingaza National Natural Park.

The central-north buffer zone of the Chingaza National Natural Park (PNN Chingaza) is important because it connects the east and west of the park and is one of the priority areas for conservation due to its ecosystem services, water reservoirs and high endemism. However, this area was not explored due to complicated access and public order problems, but prioritization is important. This study aimed to determine the structural connectivity of the landscape to explore functional connectivity using as model four plant species with a different dispersion syndrome. We characterized the climate, topography, coverage and uses of the soil. We use WorldClim data to determine the climate and use Sentinel 2B images for topography and classification of coverage and use of the soil. Then we used Fragstats and FragScape to determine the composition and configuration of the landscape.
