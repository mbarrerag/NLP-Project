# Intensified production of butyl citrates from a calcium citrate salt via solid-liquid reaction

This work developed and assessed an intensification approach for the downstream recovery of citric acid from a fermentation process and its further transformation into butyl citrates. Solid calcium citrate from the precipitation step of an industrial process was directly used as feedstock to produce citrates, circumventing traditional purification steps during citric acid production. In this case, solid calcium citrate was dispersed in butanol in the presence of H 2 SO 4 , causing acidification of the solid citrate, the release of citric acid in the alcohol solution, and the generation of solid calcium sulfate. Simultaneously, in a set of series–parallel reactions, citric acid is esterified with butanol to obtain butyl citrates. Therefore, the process occurs via a solid–solid–liquid multicomponent reaction. To develop a model for process assessment, the simultaneous reactions were decoupled and experimentally assessed in two stages: acidification and esterification. Initially, the esterification kinetics of citric acid with 1-butanol using H 2 SO 4 as catalyst was determined. The effect of temperature (313–353 K), butanol:acid initial molar ratio (8:1 to 16:1), and catalyst loading (2 – 20 % wt.) were evaluated using a Box-Benckhen experimental design. Subsequently, industrial calcium citrate was characterized to establish its molecular structure and morphology. A 1:1 calcium-to-citrate ratio was confirmed in the industrial salt, and its rough morphology and small particle size (∼0.37 μm) enabled fast acidification rates in solid–liquid reaction tests. Then, the simultaneous acidification-esterification tests were performed via H 2 SO 4 fed-batch addition into the solid–liquid reactive mixture. A simplified solid–liquid kinetic model was proposed, and it showed good agreement with experiments. The obtained model could be further used for process design, feasibility assessment, and scale-up
