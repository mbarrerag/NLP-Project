# EEG-based pain detection using Gaussian functional connectivity and willow deep learning with preserved interpretation

This thesis presents an innovative approach to the classification of pain based on EEG, addressing the persistent challenge of intra- and inter-subject variability. Taking advantage of Gaussian Functional Connectivity and shallow deep learning models, the study introduces a method of functional connectivity based on cores for the classification of pain in individual trials. The proposed model optimizes the spatial-frequency patterns through a spectral cross-distribution estimator, using the universal approximation properties of the Gaussian core to improve the extraction of characteristics. This approach is designed to improve the interpretation of the characteristics, which makes it particularly suitable for brain-machin interface applications. The research also explores the multimodal analysis, incorporating demographic stratification techniques based on factors such as gender, age and performance in trainings. These strategies significantly improve the generalization capacity of the model, achieving the most competitive performance metrics such as precision and AUC scores. The research also maintains a high level of transparency in the decision-making process.
