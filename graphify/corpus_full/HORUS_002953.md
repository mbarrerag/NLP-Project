# Addressing the nocturnal problem: the ecology of cryptic birds through bioacoustics in the neotropics

The study of the nocturnal animal populations has been neglected for a long time, which has led to what is known as the "night problem." However, advances in technology and analytical techniques have opened new possibilities, being the passive acoustic monitoring factor (PAM) a promising approach. The data obtained by PAM has become a valuable input for ecological models, including occupation models, which allows researchers to explore the factors that influence the use of the habitat of the species. In the bidiverse Cauca River Canyon, in Colombia, we research the specific responses of nocturnal bird species to environmental variables using PAM techniques and the semi-automatic workflow of the ARBIMON annotation. We could model the occupation of Megascops choliba, Nyctdromus albicolis and Pulsatrix perspicillata, and we discovered that the key variables for each species changed as the regional stations changed. Our findings highlighted the adequacy of the data acquired through PAM and supported by the assessment algorithms to model the occupation of the nocturnal bird species and their potential to address.
