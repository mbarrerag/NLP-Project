# EXPLORING OUR BIODIVERSITY TO UNDERSTAND TISSULARY REGENNERATION

One of the questions that motivates the work in regenerative biology is to understand why mammals possess a reduced regenerative capacity, while other organisms have an extraordinary capacity to regenerate complex tissues. The advent of mass sequencing tools is helping to answer this question. Studies of different biological processes in less studied species have increased over the last decades, allowing biological comparisons to be made between species established as model organisms and less studied, which allows a wider understanding of the molecular and cellular processes involved in tissue regeneration. In the field of regenerative biology, these tools have been widely used in the last decade to describe the genetic profile of the regenerative process of different species, including organisms that do not have a reference genome. Likewise, they have allowed us to understand in greater depth the key role that the immune system, microbiome and the extracellular matrix have, among others, in the regenerative capacity of the organisms that naturally have the capacity to regenerate tissues.
