# Impact of pollution from vehicle traffic on levels of carboxyhemoglobin and respiratory response in urban cyclists of the Universidad Nacional de Colombia – Bogotá.

Air pollution is considered a topic of global interest with important repercussions on toxicology and public health, since there is a high risk of increasing the burden of poisoning and morbidity in various populations due to exposure to short, medium and long term. The use of the bicycle as a means of transport has increased in Colombia, as well as the number of motor vehicles and with this the congestion of vehicles. One of the negative consequences of this is the increase in the exposure of the bike users to high concentrations of various air pollutants. In the present study of cross-cases carried out in 37 volunteers, bi-user, both men and women, of the National University of Colombia– Bogota, the changes in the levels of carboxyhemoglobobin (COHb) and the spirometry, in two scenarios of exposure to atmospheric pollutants, conditioned by the presence of the product of traffic in a cycle adjacent to the University and parallel to a high flow vehicle in the city of Bogotá were assessed, in the Avenida Carrera 30 or Avenida Norte-Quito-South (NQS). During each journey, the two possible vehicles were determined to the traffic and the speed and BC) in the low exposure scenario, a greater contribution of sources of emission parallel to the route was found, followed by meteorological variables and background concentrations. To explain the variability of respiratory parameters, the only statistically significant model was for the Maximum Respiratory Flow between 25% and 75% of the Forced Spiral Maneuvering Maneuver (MMEF25-75) taking as explanatory variables of the CO DEE model and the average PUF size. In conclusion, in the high exposure scenario, the bike users were exposed to concentrations of pollutants greater than in the low exposure scenario, except for the BC pollutant, for which no significant difference was found. Volunteers inhaled more pollutants in the high exposure scenario, on average 83% more PUF. The NO2 DEE on average was 1.3 times higher and for SO2 was 3 times higher than in the low exposure scenario. The change in COHb levels, before and after travel, correlated better with the high exposure mode of COUF.
