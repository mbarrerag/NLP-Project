# POINT OF ZERO CHARGE DETERMINATION IN CORN COBS BY IMMERSION AND MASS TITRATION FOR ITS POTENTIAL USE AS AN ADSORBENT.

Abstract Adsorption onto agricultural by-products is a widely evaluated methodology for removing of polar and ionic pollutants. Since this method is based on the interaction between the surface charges of the adsorbent material and the analyte, the evaluation of the point of zero charge (PZC) of the adsorbent is crucial as it allows to establish a range for the pH values at which the process should be efficiently carried out.[1] Many studies have shown that agricultural by-products, such as corn cob (CC), can be considered as potential adsorbents because of the presence of hydroxyl and carbonyl groups on their surface, which can be activated to generate anionic or cationic conditions that allow an adequate interaction with the analyte of interest.[2] In this work, the PZC of CC was determined via immersion and mass titration, obtaining the same value of 5.5 from both methodologies, confirming the reproducibility and effectiveness of these methodologies. Methodology The CC was acquired in a local market in Medellín, Colombia. It was milled and sieved to obtain particle size in the range 0,3-0,5 mm. Then, it was washed with deionized water and drought in a forced-convection oven at 80 °C for 36 h. The immersion technique was performed by preparing solutions at nine different pH values in the range of 3-11, which were adjusted by addition of HCl or NaOH as needed. The solutions were put into contact with CC at room temperature and a dosage of 10 gL of CC. After 24 h with a stirring of 130 rpm, the pH values were measured. The PZC is the value of pH at which this parameter remains constant after the contact. Mass titration was performed with solutions of distilled water at three pH values (3, 7, 11). For each solution, the change of pH after 24 h of contact was measured at six different dosages of CC in the range 10-120 gL. PZC is the value at which the final pH value approaches as the dosage increases. Results and discusión PZC of CC determined by immersion was 5.50 with a s of 0.07 while the PZC value of CC determined by mass titration was 5.5 with a s of 0.2. These results point out that the PZC values obtained via immersion technique and mass titration are not significantly different at the 95 % confidence level according to Tukey's Multiple Range Tests. Nevertheless, mass titration method also shows how the material modifies the pH of the medium.
