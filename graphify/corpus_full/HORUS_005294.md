# PREVALENCE AND CO-PREVALENCE OF IMMUNOGENETIC FACTORS IN PATIENTS WITH IDIOPATHIC INFLAMMATORY MYOPATHIES (IIM) IN A THIRD LEVEL OF COMPLEXITY

Autoantibodies and HLA alleles have been found to correlate with clinical phenotypes in patients with IIM across different regions globally. Our study investigates the immunogenic characteristics and its co-prevalence with clinical features in a specific Colombian population. Cross-sectional study conducted on patients diagnosed with IIM. The panel of fifteen autoantibodies specific to myositis and those associated with it. The HLA DQA1, DQB1, DR alleles were determined using real-time polymerase chain reaction technique. A total of 68 patients were included in the study, with a mean age of 52.2 years and predominantly female (73%). The most common myopathy was dermatomyositis (DM) (58%), followed by polymyositis (PM) (25%), antisynthetase syndrome and immune-mediated necrotizing myositis (5.8%) each and inclusion body myositis in 3 cases. Table 1 shows other clinical features. Autoantibodies were studied in 23 (33%) patients and HLA in 20 (50%); within the non antisynthetase Mi-2 was found (52%) related to DM (83%), skin manifestations Gottron (75%) and Heliotrope (58%) and HLA DQB1*03 (50%), Jo-1 antisynthetase antibody (30%) relating to PM (85%), pulmonary involvement (14%) and HLA DQB1*03 (28%); Ku associated antibodies (13%) relating to DM (66%), Gottron and Heliotrope (66%) pulmonary involvement (33%) and HLA-DQB1*03 (33%). This study is the first of its kind in Colombia, analyzing HLA haplotypes and myositis autoantibodies in IIM patients. The findings revealed varying co-prevalences between clinical features and the immunogenic profile, providing insight into the characteristics of the local population and supporting further research with larger sample sizes
