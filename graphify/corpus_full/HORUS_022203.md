# Brown algae from San Andrés Island, Colombian Caribbean: Metabolomic characterization.

Since the late 1970s, coral reefs in the Caribbean Sea have suffered a notable and progressive deterioration, caused by factors such as pollution, overfishing and climate change, this situation has generated that coral populations are replaced by other benthic organisms mainly by brown algae, which present rapid growth and the production of specialized metabolites which fulfil ecological functions. Due to their high abundance and bioactive potential, a study was made of the brown algae that inhabit the marine ecosystems of the shallow ones to the north of the island of San Andrés. In this study, from the analysis of the abundance, richness and metabolic profile of the species of brown algae more abundant, collected in 5 different collections, it was possible to determine that species such as Dictyoperis delicatula or Padina gymnospora produced the epidelic compounds mainly, while others like Stypodium zonale produced maraudipenes as specialized metabolites. In this species, the metabolic profiles were influenced by the substrate of collection.
