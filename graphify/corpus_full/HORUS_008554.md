# LIPOPOLYSACCHARIDE DEPROPIONIBACTERIUM ACNES AND E.COLI HEXOSAMINE AS IMMUNOTHERAPY ASSOCIATED WITH TREATMENT WITH VINCRISTINE IN CASE OF CANINE VENEREOTRANSMISSIBLE TUMOR WITH EXTRAGENITAL PRESENTATION

Anamnesis: Castrated male canine with a mass on the face in the zygomatic arch region that arose after an avulsive injury caused by another dog. Cytological and clinical findings: The cytology of the mass corresponded to a transmissible venereal tumor of the plasmacytoid type, T:3/N:0/M:0 with extragenital presentation.Furthermore, the canine presented hemoparasitic disease caused by Ehrlichia spp.Neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLN): 9.47. Additional exams: Cytopathological, histopathological and immunohistochemical study for interleukin six (IL-6) and TGFB-1 prior to treatment;hematological monitoring and assessment of the neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio weekly. Therapeutic approach: The patient received 10 mg/kg/24h of doxycycline for 21 days and immunomodulation with hexosamine from E. coli and Propinebacterium granulosum.Subsequently, 0.7 mg/mt2 of Vincristine was administered weekly. After controlling the hemoparasitosis and starting combined oncological therapy, the neoplasm had a complete response in the third week of treatment.Neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLN) at the end of treatment: 0.17 Conclusions: The combination of oncological therapy with immunomodulation in this case seemed to decrease the response time to treatment.The use of combined or targeted therapies is a current trend, very common in human oncology, which has opened doors for the study of mechanisms that could be therapeutic targets in the canine species, as well as for the study of prognostic biomarkers such as the neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio to evaluate the response to therapy
