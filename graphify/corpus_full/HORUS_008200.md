# Effectiveness of non-pharmacological treatments for the management of urinary symptoms of the low tract in patients with multiple sclerosis: systematic review of literature

The prevalence of urinary symptoms of the low tract in patients with multiple sclerosis varies in the literature between 32-96%. A negative effect on quality of life has been shown, in addition to its association with complications such as chronic renal disease, urinary infections to repeat and litiatic disease. Currently there are few therapeutic pharmacological options such as anticholinergics, beta-3 agonists and intravesical therapies such as the injection of intravesical botulinum toxin. However, these therapies are not harmless and may be associated with adverse effects, low effectiveness or decrease in their effectiveness with time. It is therefore important to evaluate the effectiveness of available non-pharmacologic therapies in order to improve the quality of life of these patients and to find alternatives or adjuvants to pharmacological therapy. Our objective was to determine the effectiveness of non-pharmacologic therapies for the management of the urinary symptoms of the low tract in patients with multiple sclerosis, based on a systematic review of the literature associated with the PRISMA guides for the publication of metaanalysis and systematic revisions of the registry of the management of urinary symptoms of the low tract in patients with MS. .
