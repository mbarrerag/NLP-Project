# GREEN SYNTHESIS OF CU AND/OR CUO NANOPARTICLES WITH PLANT VEGETAL EXTRACTS FROM TROPICAL DRY FOREST, CESAR, COLOMBIAN CARIBBEAN

Nanoparticles (NPs) are metallic structures with sizes less than or equal to 100 nm [1], this size confers them catalytic, antimicrobial and anticancer properties, which allows them to have applications in various fields. NPs can be obtained by green, economical and environmentally sustainable synthesis. Plant extracts (PE), with metabolites responsible for the reduction and stabilization of metal salts, are used to obtain NPs. In this study, copper and/or copper oxide NPs (Cu-NPs or CuO-NPs) were obtained by green synthesis, using aqueous PEs from Guazuma ulmifolia (guásimo), Caesalpinia coriaria (dividivi), and Cleome viscosa (rat tail), typical plants of the Tropical Dry Forest, T-dF, of the Colombian Caribbean. Initially, a 23 factorial experimental design was implemented to evaluate the effect of the variables (MC-metal concentration, temperature and PE concentration) on NPs yield, using H. sabdariffa PE as reducing agent. Then, the formation of NPs was evaluated with the PEs of C. viscosa, C. coriaria and G. ulmifolia. UV-Vis and FTIR spectroscopies were used to determine the characteristic signals of NPs. For PE from H. sabdariffa, the highest NPs yield was achieved at 80°C, 10% w/v PE, and 2mM for MC. Among the T-dF plant species, the highest NPs yield was achieved with C. coriaria, followed by C. viscosa and G. ulmifolia. By UV-Vis, absorption maxima, defined at 215 nm and 280 nm, were observed for the NPs synthesized with C. coriaria. In contrast, for the NPs obtained with PE from C. viscosa and G. ulmifolia, with less pronounced peaks at 317 nm and 315 nm, respectively. FTIR signals, related to metal (Cu-O) bond vibrations [2], were observed with higher intensity for NPs obtained from C. coriaria (577 cm-1) and C. viscosa (598 cm-1). It was concluded that, the formation of NPs was favored at high temperatures and reducing agent concentration
