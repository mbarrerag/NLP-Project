# GOAL ATTAINMENT SCALING AND ACHIEVING MEANINGFUL GOALS OCCUPATIONAL THERAPY

It is widely known the need to establish the measure of achievement of significant occupational therapy goals in individual care programs as a fundamental element for their management and for the recognition of professional action. to identify the perspectives and experiences of parents regarding the use of the Goal Attainment Scaling (GAS) as a form of parental participation in childcare processes. Qualitative descriptive study using a socio-demographic characterization, a questionnaire on the use of the GAS scale and a checklist on the technical quality of the goals. The questionnaire on the use of the GAS scale underwent a process of apparent validity by means of review by professionals and a student with experience in its use to identify its comprehension. The sample included 5 pairs of parents. The results show that the majority of parents agreed with the implementation of the GAS scale to measure goal achievement. Half of the parents agreed that therapeutic outcomes were related to goals taking into account the GAS scale. And most parents agreed that the GAS scale was implemented to make decisions about continuing or terminating therapy. The perspective of the parents' experience is that the use of the GAS scale was useful for evidencing therapeutic outcomes and for making decisions about the therapeutic process
