# Development of PEM membranes based on sulphonated polyethylene reterketone (PEEK-S) for stationary iron batteries

The energy transition is a necessity, and in some specific cases, a reality in Colombia, especially in remote and non-interconnected regions.In these areas of the country numerous photovoltaic solar energy installations have been carried out by the government and/or various non-governmental organizations [1] [2]. Hence a new challenge arises that is the economic- and environmentally sustainable storage of the generated energy. In the case of photovoltaic energy, this is generated in a period ranging from 6 to 10 hours of the day depending on the climatic conditions and must be stored so that it can be distributed during the rest of the day [3]. Today the stationary batteries of lead or lithium are the most used storage medium at microgeneration scale [4]. These provide a storage capacity that varies between 10 and some tens of materials that vary from 5 to 20 years of life and that it is considered to be a period of energy release of approximately 4 to 8 hours. Unlike solar panels, which have a duration of more than 20 years, the batteries, depending on the type and use given, have a period of life that varies between 5 and 20 years of time. However, the energy is also the most durable. The PEEK (polyetercetone) is an engineering plastic with high mechanical, thermal and chemical resistance. Additionally, since it does not have fluoride in its structure, it is an environmentally attractive candidate [8] [9]. Once sulphonated, this material has proven to be a good candidate for the production of PEM-type separators [10]. However, one difficulty that is present in the use of this material is in the production of fine sheets [11]. Currently, the processing by evaporation of solvent has been experienced, but this process is costly and environmentally unviable. In this project, separators will be produced by sulfonation and sintering of PEEK. Modifications in the preparation of membranes will result in changes in electrolyte permeability, water absorption, and cational exchange capacity of the same, or in other words, changes in their efficiency. These characteristics will be evaluated to select the most efficient and cost-effective membranes. Finally, with the selected membrane, a stationary battery prototype will be constructed to evaluate the separators and at the same time to generate 1 equipment (experticia tree)
