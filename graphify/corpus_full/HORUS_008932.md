# METABOLISM OF DOXORRUBICIN AND THE ROLE OF THE REDOX SYSTEM IN THE MYTOCONDRIAL DISFUNCTION DURING CARDIOTOOXICITY.

Doxorubicin (DOX) is a drug used in chemotherapy to treat various types of cancer, including breast cancer, ovarian cancer, leukemia, myeloma, among others (DAILYMED-NIH, 2021a, 2021b). Although it is effective in killing cancer cells, its use is limited due to adverse effects, such as cardiac damage and cardiomyopathy (van der Zanden et al., 2021). The balance between therapeutic benefits and toxic effects depends on the metabolism of DOX and the body's response to its metabolic derivatives. This article explores the possible molecular mechanisms related to the cardiotoxicity of DOX, focusing on its metabolism, physicochemical properties, cellular redox system, free radical generation and resulting mitochondrial dysfunction
