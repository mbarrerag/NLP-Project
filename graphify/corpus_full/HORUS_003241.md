# INSIGHTS INTO THE HYDRATION BEHAVIOR OF CALCINED KAOLINITIC CLAYS THROUGH THERMOGRAVIMETRIC AND RAMAN SPECTROSCOPIC ANALYSIS

Calcined kaolinitic clays are a promising supplementary cementitious material to reduce carbon dioxide emissions, as they allow for a partial reduction of the clinker factor in the cement mixture. Thermogravimetric methods based on the consumption of portlandite enable the comparison of their reactivity. However, determining the hydration products formed in the same thermogravimetric analysis is not easy, due to the broad overlap of peaks. This study compares calcined kaolinitic clays at different temperatures, allowing for obtaining different proportions of reactive silica and alumina. These raw materials are exposed to portlandite for 7 days to generate hydration products. In this way, by deconvoluting the first derivative thermograms (DTG) of raw materials and hydrated products, the produced phases can be determined. Once this is achieved, using a proposed reaction mechanism, the fractions of reactive silica and alumina that reacted are determined. Then, to verify the presence of coordination and chemical environments in both calcined and hydrated clays, Raman spectroscopy is used. Thus, it was determined the reactive silica and alumina from metakaolin calcined at 800 °C. Furthermore, when calcined at 1020 °C, the reactive alumina almost disappeared, leaving mainly reactive silica. Finally, when calcined at 530 °C, as the dehydroxylation was incomplete, the fractions of silica and alumina were lower, but the proportions of activated silica and alumina were not the same as at 800 °C. Thus, quantifying reactive silica and alumina in different samples, as well as the hydration products, facilitates the industrial-scale implementation of low-grade kaolinitic clays. This allows for a more precise inference of the development of hydration products for each type of clay, providing industry confidence in the cementitious material they will produce
