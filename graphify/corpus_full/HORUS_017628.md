# Local knowledge of YUCA VARIETIES IN MARY LOTS, COLOMBIA: FIRST STEP FOR THE CONSTRUCTION OF A PARTICIPATIVE FITHOMEJORATION PROGRAMME

Considering the importance of cassava (Manihot esculenta Crantz) as one of the main food crops in the Colombian Caribbean and the low level of adoption of improved varieties reported in the region, participatory phyto-improvement (FP) is presented as a strategy for obtaining varieties that meet the needs of those who produce cassava and at the same time value local knowledge, promoting the relationship between phyto-improvement processes and people. Through a co-research process involving ex-combatants of FARC-EP in the process of re-incorporation into civilian life, knowledge about the varieties of cassava preserved by 20 peasants living in the Montes de María was documented. As a result, it describes how crop management, uses and attributes associated with 25 reported common names are carried out. It is highlighted the need to recover the use of a greater number of varieties of casava, since, while knowledge exists about some of them, many are no longer cultivated. These results represent the primary input aimed at the development of new varieties that respond to the preferences of the peasant; the process that must be led by the communities themselves
