# Comparative study of mortality in patients with acute respiratory distress syndrome (APRS) by SARS-CoV2 (SDRA-C) and non-infection associated (SDRA-NC), in an institution of 4 levels of care.

The Acute Respiratory Difficulty Syndrome (SPRA) is characterized by bilateral radiographic opacity with severe hypoxemia due to non-cardiogenic lung oedema secondary to increase in excessive alveolocapilar permeability. The heterogeneity of its presentation and the absence of effective pharmacological treatment has put it as a medical challenge. The Pandemia by COVID 19 has caused an increase in its frequency, with an unacceptably high mortality that puts it back with a high increase in scientific medical interests. Type Study: Analytic Observation, Design: Retrospective cohort using propensity score with relation 1:3. Place UCI Hospital Universitario Nacional de Colombia, Population: Patients who enter the ICU with diagnosis of SDRA from January 1, 2018 to July 31, 2021. The patients were treated in the ICU by SDRA 50-CoV (incl. The survival rate at 30 days of patients in the COVID group was 24% and the non-COVID group was 49%. This difference was statistically significant with (P 0.0157). At the global level, there are few studies comparing mortality between C-SDRA and NC-SDRA. The present study found that mortality in the group with SDRA COVID is greater than in the non-COVID group, these results are far from the research of (Bain, et al.2021) and (Belonche, et al.2021). In the results There are differences in 30-day survival C-SDRA and NC-SDRA (24% and 49, respectively, log-rank test=0.0157)
