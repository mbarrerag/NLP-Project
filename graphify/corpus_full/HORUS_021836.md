# Biomarkers for occupational medical surveillance to formaldehyde: A systematic review of the literature.

The formaldehyde (FA) is a chemical substance classified by the International Agency for Research in Cancer (IARC), as a group I substance, which means that it has been confirmed that it is capable of causing cancer in humans. It has been identified as a cause of nasopharyngeal cancer, acute myeloid leukemia and sinusoidal cancer, additionally known as genotoxic, mutagenic and sensitising skin and pathways areas. This is a substance widely used in different working environments, mainly in hospital environments and in industry, by its properties as a preservative, biocide and intermediate product for the manufacture of sheets, resins and wood. Knowing its harmful effects to health it is imperative to carry out medical surveillance on workers through the control and monitoring of environmental and additional measurements, a medical examination accompanied by biomarkers that allow to closely monitor the exposure and also to alert possible adverse effects on health; however, although many efforts and research have been made to identify one or more biomarkers that allow to monitor exposed workers, the results of the generated evidence present wide differences, leading to a confusion between the results of the situation and the manual search. The initial search showed 108 records, however, only 30 articles met the inclusion and exclusion criteria established. Subsequently, a qualification of each study was performed by two researchers independently and the risk of bias was classified through the JBI tool. Some of the most frequently evaluated biomarkers were the micronucleus in peripheral blood, which showed an OR 3.21 (95% CI: 2.39-4.04) among those exposed to formaldehyde vs controls, however, the studies included were heterogeneous (I2=99 per cent) among themselves, which is why a random effects meta-analysis was performed. An intermediate to high risk of bias was found in all studies carried out, which hinders the analysis of the data and limits having a minimum of homogeneity in the data and generates high variability of each item evaluated in the different study groups.
