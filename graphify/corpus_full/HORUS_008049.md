# Design and construction of a fibre radio system (ROF) based on an optoelectronic oscillator

The increasing demand for wireless services that promise access at any time and place has generated an exhaustion of the electromagnetic spectrum, which raises the need to improve the existing communication systems. In this thesis work we propose a radio system on fiber based on the use of an optoelectronic oscillator as a possible solution to improve the use of spectrum in high frequencies and guarantee optimal transmission characteristics. We present the simulation of a prototype of transmission point to point that uses an optoelectronic oscillator of external modulation applied to a radio system on fiber, analyzing the results of the downward link. To this end, we characterized a radio system on fiber with the help of the software defined radio in the frequency band 5.8 GHz. Then, we developed an optoelectronic oscillator designed for this same frequency band. Finally we carried out a simulation where it integrates the system, where we contrasted the results obtained with the characteristics suggested by different standards and protocols, to evaluate the figures of merit of the system. The results obtained allowed to observe the direct relationship between the figures of the studied and their behavior under different conditions and schemes. A simulation of a point-to-to-point transmission prototype is present, employing an externally modulated optoelectronic oscillator applied to a ROF system, and analysis the results of the downward link link. To achieve this objective, a ROF system was characterized using software-defined radio in the 5.8 GHz frequency band. Subsequently, an optoelectronic Oscillator designed for this same frequency band was developed. Finally, a simulation was conducted to integrate the system, contrasting the obtained final results with characteristics suggestive standards and protocols, and then evaluating the system's performance metrics.
