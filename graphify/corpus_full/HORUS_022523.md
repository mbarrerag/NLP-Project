# Characterization of memory-specific T-CD4+ lymphocyte response for peptides derived from Leishmania proteins as potential vaccine candidates.

Leishmaniasis is a parasitic disease tropical, neglected and widely distributed in the world. It is part of the zoonotic diseases, transmitted by vectors that generates about 1 million cases annually, being the Leishmaniasis form skin, the most prevalent clinical presentation. Multiple fronts have been developed for the intervention of this disease such as vector control, development of therapeutic products and vaccines. However, the latter, although effective cost compared with the other strategies, if progress is very limited towards the licensing of vaccine prototypes for use in humans. This work focused on the identification of natural epitopes of Leishmania spp., i.e. those sequences that were recognized as antigens and that induced immune response in the natural infection of the parasite in humans (especially in those resistant to the disease) and its validation as vaccine candidates in a murine model. To achieve the objective, initially identified of peptides derived from immunogenic proteins of Leishmania spp. by means of a strategy insylic and after its synthesis, the human assessment by means of the Clutic cells and the (IL-10), the last. Subsequently, we identified that the HLA-DRβ1 04:05 allele could be associated with the susceptibility of individuals to leishmaniasis, in addition to those resistant to leishmaniasis, which generate TCM and Teff cells against parasite antigens and that the STI41 and STI46 peptides generate TCM and Teff cells in the same. Finally, we observed in a model of skin leishmaniasis by Leishmania panamensis (L. panamensis) in mice BALB/c that immunisation with STI41 peptide achieved the reduction of ulcers to smaller sizes compared to the control and production of IFNγ and TNFα mice in ex vivo splenocytes, as well as the STI43 peptide whose use generated smaller lesions, but to a lesser extent than STI41 peptide, together with the low regulation of IL-4 and IFNγ.
