# EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION OF THE MECHANICAL RESPONSE AND SAND PRODUCTION OF 3D PRINTED SAMPLES

The production of formation sand along with the produced fluids in a well is a phenomenon with occurrence in many reservoirs which can even cause the loss of a well or the damage to surface systems, and depends on many factors such as formations mechanical strength, in situ stresses among other factors. In this way, improving understanding of sanding mechanisms is crucial for the design of systems and the production plans of the reservoirs, this with an effective control/management of the level produced sand throughout the productive time. In order to improve the understanding of the sanding mechanisms, especially the relationship between the level of sand production, the mechanical behavior and the stresses acting on the medium, an experimental evaluation plan is designed using 3D printed samples and UCS (Uniaxial Compressive Strength), TXT (Triaxial tests), TWC (Thick Walled Cylinder) and sanding tests. The use of 3D printed samples for this study is very convenient, because it allows to have multiple cores of similar properties and evaluate their behavior in the desired experimental conditions. In this way, the samples are subjected to an experimental test plan consisting of four stages: 1. UCS tests for the analysis of the variability of properties. 2. Triaxial tests to evaluate the characteristic strain-stress behavior and calibration of mechanical parameters. 3. TWC (no fluid flow) tests to evaluate mechanical behavior under hollow cylindrical geometry. 4. Sanding tests with measurement of sand production, using a novel system, to evaluate the mechanical behavior of hollow cylinders under different conditions of stresses and flow. From TXT tests results, printed samples present a preferentially compacting behavior, this associated with their high porosity. Additionally, from the sanding tests, sand production occurs as a result of the mechanical failure of the samples, since, although the tests were designed to obtain different levels of sanding during the same test, only a single moment of sand production was recorded for each test, which coincides with the time of structural failure of the sample. This structural failure of the sample is reached when an important region of the sample has suffered internal failure due to shear and plasticity
