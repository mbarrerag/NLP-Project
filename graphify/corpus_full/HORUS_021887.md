# Characterizations of the march in Parkinson's disease.

Parkinson’s disease (EP) is a neurodegenerative disease that affects the motor control system responsible for the voluntary movements of the human body and cognitive functions. EP is the second most common neurodegenerative disease after Alzhaimer’s disease with a global population of approximately 6 million and with an estimated 18 million people by the year 2040. It is characterized by the death of dopaminergic neurons in an area known as compact nigra pars substance, which directly affects the function of the basal ganglia, affecting the motor control system. The main motor manifestations that occur due to EP are bradykinesia, hypokinesia, alteration of balance and march. In addition, the EP affects the ability to learn movements and repetitive tasks. Due to the functional limitations of the movements that occur during the progression of the disease, the current clinical strategies have been designed to determine the high-invasive (surgical) and non-invasive (sever) evolution to improve the quality of life of patients. The motor disorders in the EP show a high inter-individual variability that defiss the current clinical environment based on observation. The second part of this paper was intended to represent in a nonlinear way the cinemateca of the lower extremities, highlighting the differences between the stages of the EP. To this end, pose estimation based on deep learning was included to obtain the reference points of the body and its time series and, subsequently, to construct the phase space based on its derivatives. Then the largest exponent of Lyapunov was calculated, the correlation dimension and approximate entropy, resulting in statistically significant differences (Kruskal-Wallis range test, p < 0.05), particularly between healthy controls and stages 3, the most advanced stage, and when comparing stage 1 versus stage 3. These findings provide information on how complex patterns may be related to progression of the disease in the EP and can be easily implemented using affordable RGB video devices
