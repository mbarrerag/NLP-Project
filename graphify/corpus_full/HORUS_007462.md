# Design of Community Health Units (UCOS) in the Embera Chamí and Gitó Dokabú indigenous reserves of Risaralda

Access to basic sanitation restauins a critical and unmet need in numerous indigenous communities across Colombia. In the Embera Chamí and Gito Dokabú reserves, located in the department of Risaralda, the majority of inhabitants lake access to safe water and adequate sanitation infrastructure, resulting in significant public health risks and environmental degradation. This study presents the design of Community Sanitary Units (UCOS, by its Spanish acronym) as a contextual and socio-cultural characteristic of the target communities. The proposed intervention encompasses the architectural, hydralic, and health design of the UCOS, also the development of an integrated system for the target communities. The proposed intervention incompatibility, and treatment of water-use. This work presents the design of Community Health Units (UCOS) as a solution adaptable to the topographic and cultural conditions of these communities. The proposal includes the architectural, hydraulic and sanitary design of UCOS, as well as the implementation of systems for the collection, storage and treatment of wastewater. A modular palaphytic approach is considered to ensure structural stability on high mountain terrain and minimize environmental impact. Technical calculations, based on national and international regulations, ensure that these units are efficient and sustainable. In addition, a maintenance manual is incorporated so that the community can manage their operation in the long term. The implementation of UCOS is expected not only to improve the quality of life of these communities, but also to serve as a model for other rural populations in similar conditions
