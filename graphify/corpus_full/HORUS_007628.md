# Design and validation of stable suspensions of zirconic particles in photopolymerizable resin for use in the printing of hybrid dental parts through digital light processing (DLP)

With the growing demand for dental aesthetic restorations, dentistry has evolved towards a balance between functionality and beauty. Currently, replacement and tooth restoration require materials that are not only aesthetically pleasing but also functional and biocompatible. Traditionally, metallic materials such as titanium and gold have been used, but present problems of chemical stability, biocompatibility and high costs. This has spurred the search for more economical and effective alternatives. In this context, zircona (ZrO2) has stood out as a preferred option due to its excellent mechanical and optical properties.However, ceramic implants can cause abrasive wear on opposite teeth and their manufacturing methods are energy inefficient. Therefore, research has focused on developing dental resin compounds with ceramic loads, combining the advantages of both materials to improve the longevity and functionality of dental restorations. Nowadays it is very common that dental practitioners themselves want to produce the dental parts needed for restorations either temporary or permanent. However, the market still does not offer a professionally charged commercial resin to ceramics, will allow the production of functionally biocompatible parts
