# Diagnostic error in the era of the COVID-19 pandemic, a cohort study.

The infection with SARRS CoV-2 has very wide clinical manifestations; the possibilities of differential diagnosis and the need to identify patients correctly is fundamental to avoid problems associated with the diagnosis error. The objective of the study was to evaluate the impact of the diagnostic error in the context of pandemic, hospital stay and mortality. The retrospective cohort study of patients from the internal medicine service and ICU of the National University Hospital of Colombia, between May and August 2020, 2020. The exposure corresponds to patients with initial suspicion of COVID-19 rejected. The primary outcome was the impact of the diagnostic error on hospital stay and mortality. The secondary ones, the frequency of diagnostic error, the description of the diagnoses confused with COVID-19, the predictors of error and the description of the cognitive biases. The diagnosis error, the bias and the damage with the instruments “Safer Dx Dx Instrument” and the mortality rate, the frequency of diagnosis error, the description of the diagnoses confused Dx Process Breakdown IBIkdown Supupupupupuplement, the error, the predict variables, the multiple variables of the error and the error and description of the error and description of the cognitive bias 5 biases for each error case, the most frequent bias was that of anchoring in 34 (94.4%) cases. No association was found between the diagnostic error and hospital stay or mortality. There was association between the initial diagnosis of discarded COVID-19 and a longer hospital stay. Differential diagnoses were identified to the suspicion of COVID-19. 16.7% error frequency and a high frequency of cognitive biases were identified. Data could allow implementation of feedback processes to minimize risk
