# CHARACTERS AND CHARACTER STATES

From the prokaryote unicellular organisms to the most complex multicellular organisms, such as plants and animals, we can realize that all organisms are divided into parts. Even, until the hypothetical ancestral of all life on earth, a replicator RNA molecule, could have parts of its more specialized chain in the uptake of certain bases and other parts capable of assimilate other bases. Another important point is that the delimitation of parts in a given species often has a considerable equivalence with that of other species. The separation between root and stem of the species Zea mays as two distinct parts, for example, also exists in all other monocotyledonous species, eudycotyledonous, gymnosperms, among others. We can understand that correspondence as a homeostasis in the organization and unity of the parts of different organisms. Therefore, it is possible to try to recognize parts in organisms that are corresponding to others, that is, that is, that is, that are the same parts of other organisms. We call each recognized part and all their variation in the different organisms as a character.
