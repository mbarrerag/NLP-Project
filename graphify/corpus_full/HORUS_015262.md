# LACTIC FERMENTATION OF CARROT SUBSTRATES AS A STRATEGY FOR THE STABILITY AND PRESERVATION OF LACTIC ACID BACTERIA AND PHYTOCHEMICALS

A strategy for the preservation of tropical vegetables is controlled fermentation. Carrot (Daucus carota) is considered an ideal substrate for the growth of lactic acid bacteria (LAB) due to its bioactive composition. The aim of this study was to evaluate the behavior of two lactic acid bacteria: a mixed probiotic culture called VEGE 092 (10,3 ± 0,02 Log CFU/mL) from the HOWARU® commercial line and an autochthonous culture Pediococcus pentosaceus (10,2 ± 0,02 Log CFU/mL) inoculated individually in a carrot substratum (30% w/v) The biotechnological and physicochemical characteristics of the carrot inoculated under fermentation conditions (37°C-24h) and storage (4 °C-28 days) were evaluated. Before the inclusion of LAB in the vegetable substratum, the in vitro probiotic potential of the strains was determined. The viability of both stains maintains a cell density above 9 Log CFU/ml, at the end of storage, a value higher than that required by international regulations. Although fructose was consumed concomitantly with glucose, the latter is depleted first, leading to a discrepancy between the amount of glucose and fructose consumed during fermentation. Malic acid, glucose, and fructose consumption were determined for both treatments with respect to the control from hour 6 of fermentation, lactic acid was produced (VEGE = 250,7±0,01 mg/100 mL, P. pentosaceus = 101,6±0,02 mg/100 mL). The content of carotenoids varied significantly with respect to the control after 24 hours of fermentation for both treatments (VEGE = 5,06 ±0,03mg ß-carotene/100g, P. pentosaceus=5,24±0,02mg ß-carotene/100g). After 24 hours of fermentation, VEGE 092 presented a biomass of 10.7 ± 0,01 Log CFU/mL and P. pentosaceus of 11,2 ± 0,02 Log CFU/mL. These results indicate that commercial and autochthonous microorganisms inoculated in a carrot substratum carry potential probiotic properties and produce postbiotics (lactic acid), that allow them to transform the fermented product into functional foods
