# Reason and Nature in the Philosophy of History according to Kant

The present paper seeks to understand how the transit between the purpose of nature and the moral purpose in Kantian systematicity. From the theoretical reason, nature is determined by empirical laws by means of an efficient mechanical causality. For Kant, in nature there must be some form of causality according to purposes that gives account of some purpose in organisms. That life contains a purpose leads to the consideration of an objective in organisms that, in turn, as a whole of nature, will have a final end that cannot be considered as a means of any other purpose. Such a final end will be man. Such a final end will be the realm of the ends of humanity will then be a teleological project that imposes ends on the human species and nature itself which gives them rational capabilities to be human beings so that it raises them to their maximum degree.
