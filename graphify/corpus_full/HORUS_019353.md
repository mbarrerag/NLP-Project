# Risk-based resource allocation for management and pandemic response: The COVID-19 Case in Bogotá, Colombia.

In any major disaster, a gap between necessity and availability of resources develops. In a pandemic, this gap will be aggravated due to interruptions or delays in the global supply chain and the fact that governments and aid organizations are overwhelmed to respond to all those who need assistance. Then, determining the location of resources (e.g. budget for antivirals and preventive vaccines, Intensive Care Unit (ICU), fans, Non-Intensive Care Unit (no city), physicians) that will be used during a pandemic is a strategic decision that directly affects the success of response operations to a pandemic. The allocation of resources can be made using a risk management perspective, where a demand place has one (or more) associated risks (e.g. spread of virus, poverty, medical preconditions) and the objective is to choose the amount that will be invested in several interventions such that the risk exposed by the points of demand is minimized according to the budgetary limitations and benefits for health. Due to the randomness and uncertainty of the conditions, not only one, but a set of risks can negatively affect the situation (a geographical risk group of the risk is not affected by the situation)
