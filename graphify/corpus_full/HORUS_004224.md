# ASSOCIATION BETWEEN EATING PATTERNS AND BEHAVIORAL, PSYCHOLOGICAL, AND DEMOGRAPHIC VARIABLES DURING THE COVID-19 PANDEMIC IN LATIN-AMERICA

Sanitary measures were implemented during the COVID-19 pandemic to control the spread of the virus. This study was carried out to determine the association between eating patterns and behavioral, psychological, and demographic variables during confnement. Analytical cross-sectional study. The 18-item three-factor eating questionnaire (TFEQ-18) was used, and a self-perceived stress scale, also, weekly frequency of physical activity, type of confinement, age and sex were asked in an online questionnaire. Sample size: 273 participants (58 from Chile, 125 from Colombia, 90 from Mexico); a convenience sampling was conducted. Robust multiple linear regression models were performed in STATA MP version 17. A direct association was determined between stress level and the three eating factors: uncontrolled eating (ß=0.23; p=0.000), emotional eating (ß=0.28; p=0.000 and cognitive restraint (ß=0.18; p=0.005), female sex was associated with the uncontrolled eating (ß=-0. 23; p=0.001), frequency of physical activity was directly associated with the restrictive pattern (ß=0.25; p=0.000) and indirectly with emotional (ß=-0.20; p=0.001) and disinhibited (ß=- 0.16; p=0.004) patterns. Being in flexible confinement or without confinement was not associated with any eating patterns. A higher level of stress was associated with all eating factors. The frequency of physical activity was directly associated with cognitive restraint, while female sex was associated with uncontrolled eating.
