# STUDY OF VAPOR LIQUID EQUILIBRIUM FOR THE SYSTEM PROPANOL-PROPYL PROPANOATE-WATER

Propyl propanoate is an organic solvent widely used in the coating, paint, plastic, printing ink, and cosmetic industries, among others. Propyl propanoate is seen as a potential alternative to replace hazardous solvents, because it is a non-dangerous solvent for human health and biodegradable within weeks [1]. It is produced by the esterification reaction of propionic acid with propanol. The conventional propyl propanoate production process consists of a batch system that involves reaction and separation operations [2]. Accordingly, the thermodynamic information is of great importance to predict the behavior of the system and for the design of separation processes. The complexity of systems such as propanol-propionic acid-propyl propanoate-water is already reported in literature since there exists multiple heterogeneous and homogeneous azeotropes and a region of partial miscibility. Currently, in literature there is extensive information of vapor-liquid (VLE) and liquid-liquid equilibrium (LLE) of the system to produce propyl propanoate. However, there is no experimental information available about vapor-liquid-liquid equilibrium (VLLE). Nevertheless, it is paramount to know the behavior of this equilibrium because VLE and LLE can take place simultaneously. For this reason, VLLE equilibrium of the propanol-propyl propanoate-water system at two pressure was studied in this work. Measurements were done in a Fischer Labodest VLLE 602 equipment with an ultrasonic probe in the boiling chamber. The experimental data was validated with thermodynamic consistency test. The data set exhibits a heterogeneous ternary azeotrope. Besides, aiming to obtain a set of thermodynamic parameters to predict the behavior of the quaternary mixture, data from literature on VLE and LLE, along with data from VLLE from this study were used to correlate the set of binary parameters for the UNIQUAC-HOC and NRTL-HOC thermodynamic models. The obtained results for activity models depict good agreement with experiments, so that they can be used for process design
