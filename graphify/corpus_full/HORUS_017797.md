# Effect of oral contraceptives on risk aversion in young Colombian women

Behavioral economics analyzes the economic decisions of individuals according to certain principles and characteristics of people. In recent years, sex hormones (testosterone, estradiol, and progesterone) and the role they have shown in decision-making have become very important. Oral hormonal contraceptives change the balance of estradiol and progesterone in the usual menstrual cycle; raising progesterone and lowering estradiol, both at constant values during the planning cycle, which is why this effect on levels could modify the preferences of choices against risk. A two-arm study was carried out with young women who planned hormonally and those who did not consume this type of drug. A total of 60 women participated in the study, they were given a sociodemographic questionnaire and through lotteries with different risk coefficients, the participants were classified as lovers of risk, indifferent and averse to risk. It was found that there are no significant effects between the use of oral hormonal contraceptives and risk aversion between the two groups of participants
