# RESTAURATION OF FORESTS BASED ON ALTERNATIVE NUCLEAR-PURSUANT: ESTABLISHMENT, MICROHABITAT AND LANDSAGE

The dry tropical forest (BST) in Colombia is an embargoed and degraded ecosystems, so its ecological restoration is priority and urgent. This ecosystem presents degraded areas with high dominance of exotic pastures and compaction of the soil, in states of slow succession that hinder its recovery. Under this scenario, strategies are required to minimize in the short term the coverage of grasses and facilitate their substitution with native species. An alternative, is the applied nucleation, achieved by planting species ensambles, which mimics the natural recovery of the ecosystems through the progressive formation of patches, accelerating the appearance of natural regeneration niches and expanding the succession routes. The present proposal will be carried out in the BST of the high Magdalena valley (Huila, Colombia), within the area of compensation of the Hydroelectric Plant Quimbo, considered one of the largest restoration projects of the country, and the objective of the research, focuses on evaluating the effect of nuclear alternatives on the establishment of plant species and the formation of microhabitats. Currently, the two related studies of the structure and the structure factor, have been implemented, based on nine. To evaluate the growth rates and identify the differential yield of all species and their combinations from mixed performance indices. It is also necessary to evaluate the impact of species development on the structural attributes of microhabitats, based on new sampling of microenvironmental variables within the nuclei and clarify how the success of applied nucleation can be enhanced with implementation and management decisions that apart from considering local responses also involve the landscape. Finally, with this project scientific evidence will contribute to strengthening new efforts in the efficient use of strategies based on applied nucleation technique, maximizing results in scenarios with limitations such as water stress and grass dominance, and improving efficiency in investing resources in large restoration projects. Also, the project will be the basis for new research opportunities at the undergraduate and postgraduate levels associated with species responses to environmental filters and tolerance to drought in climate change-affected environments
