# Characterization of cocoa mucilage (Theobroma Cacao L., clone TSH 565) as a source of pectin and sugars for use in the food industry.

The mucilage or cocoa pulp is a byproduct obtained before the fermentation process. It has been used as a raw material for the manufacture of jams, alcoholic beverages, among others. However, although it is mainly constituted by sugar and dietary fibre, few studies have been carried out on the content and characterisation of the pectin extracted from the cocoa mucilage for use in the food industry. In this study pulp was obtained from the TSH 565 clone of San Vicente del Chucurí. The proximal analysis, the determination of total dietary fibre, has a neutralised relationship of pH, extraction time and the mass-solvent ratio (m/v). The cocoa mucilage has approximately 15,23 ± 0,08 % of total sugar. The uctose content is made by means of a muffilative acid solution as solvent, varying the pH, extraction time and the mass-solventus ratio (m/v). The cocoa mucilage has approximately 15,23 ± 0,08 % of total sugar. The uctose content is 6,32 ± 0,2 g/ 100 g/g/g/g/g/g/g/g/g/g/g cocoa growers. In addition, based on the sucrose content and its thermal degradation, a processing proposal can be made, in which degradation is avoided and the quality of the product is guaranteed
