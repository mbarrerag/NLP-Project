# Molecular characterization and early diagnosis of Lasiodiplodia sp. and Ceratocystis sp. in cocoa and avocado plants

The spiodiploditis sp. and Ceratocystis sp. are optional parasites that are characterized by staying in prolonged latency states and colonizing the vascular system of plants without producing symptoms in the early stages of infection. The inoculum can be dispersed through plant material of apparently healthy propagation, and therefore it is necessary to develop techniques to detect these pathogens early when the inoculum density is very low and the plant does not present symptomatology. The objective of this research was to characterize molecularly the isolates of a collection of Lasiodiplodia sp. and Ceratocystis sp. from samples of Theobroma cacao L. and Persea americana Mill. in order to identify specific markers and design a PCR for the early diagnosis of these pathogens from the stems of infected seedlings. To this, the regions of ITS and the genes β-tubulin and TEF-1α were amplitude. It was also demonstrated that the developed test detected pathogens in the stems from asymptomatic seedlings artificially inoculated from 7 dpi for Lasiodiplodia sp. and 9 dpi for Ceratocystis sp., so it is proposed to use them as a screening tool to verify that grafts to propagate plant material are free from inoculum .
