# Potentials evoked during movement discrimination in intermodal environment associated with ageing

In our day to day our brain is immersed in a dynamic interactive environment that demand more than an incoming sensory sense and the response of consequent movement, this requires a sophisticated capacity of synchronous and efficient simultaneous activation of different neural networks; the older adults are not alien to it and can even find greater challenges given modifications by aging. Being a clearly growing population, it is of interest to know better their adaptive capacities. Reason why the present project of thesis proposes as objective to describe the responses of potential evoked associated with aging in tasks of discrimination of movement within intermodal environments. To this end, an analysis of analytical factorial design of 2x2 was carried out in which they crossed at an age level (16 young adults (AJ) and 15 elderly adults (AM) and a level of audiovisual intermodal stimulation (congruent and incongruent), analyzing in its result a variable dependent of discrimination of movement by means of workstations validated as “Random Kinematic Dots” and its corresponding potentials evoked by means of electroencephalography (EEG). The analysis of data was performed in accordance with the above data (in the present case-mentioned data) a dynamic interactive environment that demands more than one internal sensory sense and the consequent movement response. This requires a sophisticated capacity for simultaneous, synchronous and efficient activation of different neural networks. Older adults are not Strangers to this and may even enter into larger challenges given the change caused by agriculture. As a clear growing population, it is of interest to understand their adaptation capacities. For this reason, the present these projects aim to describe the responses of evokement potentials associated with discrimination in mobility tasks with intermodalities. For this purpose, a 2x2 analytical factor which is designed and independent and objective. PN130 and PN 320. Lower efficiency was found on the part of AM with respect to AJ, presenting a lower number of correct answers in both congruent and inconsistent stimuli. This is reinforced by the ERP differences, where AMs present low wave amplitude and higher replication latency, especially in intermediate potentials such as PN130 and PN320 of occipito-temporal and frontal areas, with no significant differences for early potentials such as P80. This demonstrates the effects of addressing on strategies for intermodal audiovisual stimuli such as neural Oscillation or synchrony, with greater difficulties for AMs in motion discrimination
