# CANNABIDIOL CONCENTRATES BY MEANS OF MOLECULAR DISTILLATION: PILOT-SCALE CASE

There is growing interest in the chemical compounds that the Cannabis plant synthesizes naturally due to its pharmacological properties and in developing the cannabinoid-purification methods that allow these compounds to retain their properties to the greatest possible extent. Molecular distillation (MD) is one such method. It allows it to operate at reduced temperatures because it is carried out in a high vacuum, which makes it particularly efficient for purifying thermosensitive or highly oxidizable compounds, such as acids, fats, vitamins, and waxes [1]. This work aims to find the best-operating conditions for getting high cannabidiol (CBD) concentrations via MD. Consequently, as the boiling point is modified with pressure, and the vapor pressure change with the temperature, it was tested the efficiency of a two-stage MD process carried out under an evaporator temperature range (373-473 K), and two pressure (0.1-0.001 torr), also the wiped-speed was tested in a range (250-400 RPM) which is related with the thin-film homogenization. A mixture, known as Full-Spectrum (66% w/w in CBD) is the feed in the process, the residue of the first-stage distillation is used as input for the second-stage distillation, each of the original input samples resulted in four different output compounds. The CBD content of all residues and distillates of the MD processes carried out for this study was determined through the Waters methodology, i.e., by using HPLC and then validating the results of these measurements with the aid of the established calibration curves of the cannabinoids being evaluated. [2]. The distillate with the highest CBD content obtained had 84.40 % w/w of this cannabinoid. In order to achieve this CBD content, the following MD conditions were necessary at evaporator temperature 418 K, pressure of 0.1 Torr, and 350 RPM at first-stage distillation. On the other hand, to second-stage 438 K, 0.01 Torr, and 350 RPM. For future studies that aim to increase the efficiency of this separation method further, we recommend measuring the impact of different feeding rates on the cannabinoid content of the resulting compounds. Applying mathematical models and comparing this study's results could further optimize the concentrated CBD process
