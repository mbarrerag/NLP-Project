# Stress, depression, anxiety, and eating habits in people with irritable bowel syndrome

Abstract Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) is a disorder characterized by changes in the intestinal habit and affects 30% of the world population. Although a connection has been found between the brain-microbiota intestinal axis, the development of IBS and its association with the prevalence of mental disorders, the possible implications that have in the eating habit of people are not clear. This article aimed to explore the relationship between stress, depression, anxiety, mental disorders and eating habits in patients with IBS. A bibliographical examination was carried out in the search engines PubMed, ScienceDirect and BVS. It was found that people with IBS can present abnormalities in the brain microstructure and alterations in the brain-intestinal network associated with a longer duration of gastrointestinal symptoms and increased affective comorbidity. Also suggested is a relationship in different ways between stress, depression and anxiety, symptoms of IBS and changes in eating habits.
