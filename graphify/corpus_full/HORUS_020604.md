# José María Arguedas and the imagination of nature

This work is an exploration of the images of nature in three novels by José María Arguedas: Yawar fiesta (1941), The deep rivers (1958) and The fox above and the fox below (1971). The representation of the natural not only defines the pastoralism of Arguedas, which is consolidated in its latest novel, but is related to the constant search for an aconceptual and metaphorical language, capable of expressing reality. The the thesis tries to examine that relationship between nature, language and reality in its different chapters. In the first, on Yawar fiesta, we will see how nature is mitified and becomes a metaphor of the structure of indigenous thought, but also of capitalist progress; in the second chapter, on The deep rivers, nature, which is represented in the rumorous world, and is captured by the narrator in his moments of poetic state, it is a form of pastoral myth but not transcendent, and in the third chapter, on The fox above and the fox below, nature is totally degraded and his analogous language is used to describe the machine and the capitalism.
