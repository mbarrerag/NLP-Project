# Three-dimensional finite element program for prediction of fissure trajectory in fragile materials using a simplified discrete numerical model.

This thesis presents the formulation, implementation and validation of a simplified numerical model, which predicts the path of fissures in three-dimensional solids made of fragile material, preserving a low computational cost. In the methodology used, a mesh of linear tetrahedrons separated slightly by bond elements that connect the nodes of originally shared faces, these bonds have a nonlinear behavior that determines the appearance of fissures with total loss of cohesion and in fracture mode I. The developed model considers that the deformations are infinitesimal and that the applied loads are static. The algorithm was performed in a structured way in the programming language Matlab or Octave, preserving the routines in open code. The program developed PREFIS was used to analyze six examples of application, whose results satisfactorily represented the fissure path
