# ANIMAL WELFARE IN THE ACUICULTURE FRONT TO THE CHALLENGES OF THE FRAMEWORK

The approach to animal welfare, human well-being and environmental conservation are closely related and that by affecting one of them the other two are affected; the approach to health is defined as the clear relationship between ecosystem health, human health and animal health as a single health. Thus, there is a close relationship between welfare and health, not only of animals, but also of the human being and of the environment. A clear example of this is the recent pandemic by COVID-19, since an inadequate relationship of human with biodiversity (wet markets in Asia) created the perfect scenario for generating a global public health problem that up to the date of this writing (1 Sep. 2022) has reported 603 million cases around the world with about 6.5 million dead. This current global scenario requires human health to be aligned with the human model to alleviate problems such as hunger, war, climate crisis; protecting biodiversity, culture and natural resources. Thus, the 17 sustainable development goals have been set as a framework on which to align projects and to achieve several benefits of one activity. Many definitions can be found in the literature, but here the definition of Broom (1986) is to be used: is it well-being an individual's state in his attempts to adapt to the environment? Is it clear that well-being is something of the individual and therefore biologically depends on how that individual obtains information from the environment, processes it and acts in response? The degree of perception and sensory systems determine that all animals, whether vertebrates or invertebrates, have mechanisms of perception and adaptation and can therefore evaluate their well-being. In the case of aquatic organisms there are a great variety of highly complex and sensitive mechanisms of perception compared to birds or mammals, and that, being in an aquatic environment, greater knowledge is required of how changes in water conditions can affect them. Animal welfare should not be confused with the absence of pain or suffering, these latter are two important aspects within animal welfare but are not the only factors to be considered when assessing well-being.
