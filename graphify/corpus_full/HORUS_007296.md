# Research on water-based strategies for stabilization of ascorbic acid in aqueous solution

Water activity has been widely used in the pharmaceutical and cosmetic industries to reduce the risk of microbiological contamination of its products; however, few applications are used in the control of chemical degradation of active ingredients. As a contribution in this sense, the present research explores the use of different functional categories of excipients as strategies to reduce the oxidative degradation of ascorbic acid, a vitamin valued for its antioxidant properties. For this purpose, aqueous solutions of salts (NaCl and CaCl 2), viscosants (hypromellose and xantan gum), sweeteners (sacarosa and sorbitol) and cosolventants (glycerin and polyethylene glycol 200) are characterized with respect to their water activity behavior. NaCl, sorbitol and glycerine are the excipients that allow significant changes in water activity as a function of their concentration and therefore are investigated as alternatives to promote the stability of ascorbic acid.
