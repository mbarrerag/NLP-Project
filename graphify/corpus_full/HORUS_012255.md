# Effect of access to credit on the productivity of agricultural activities in Colombia

Given the importance of the agricultural sector in rural areas of the country as a source of income and a determinant of quality of life, there is a great need for this sector to improve its levels of productivity, which are very low in relation to its potential and other countries in the region. There are several characteristics that influence productivity from the merely physical ones related to geography to the public policy decisions of governments. This work focuses on the study of access to the credit market and investigates its effect on agricultural productivity, firstly identifying some particular characteristics that determine the participation and approval of financing requests by producers, and, secondly, the impact that this has on the different variables associated with productivity when you have a loan and when you don't. The results found, from the data of the third National Agricultural Census (CNA), allow us to identify that, during the year of data collection, the request for loans among the country's producers is very low, indicating that the use of credit for the development of agricultural activities is reduced. Likewise, the destination that was given to the resources was mainly focused on meeting immediate production expenses and not on the adoption of machinery and technological improvements that favored productivity. On the other hand, by estimating an endogenous commutation model (ESR) it was possible to identify that credit holders obtain a better result in productivity derived from the use of machinery, indicating that the use of credit can improve agricultural productivity. Likewise, the effect of credit on the productivity of some transitory crops such as green rice, cassava and potatoes was studied, finding that financing has a significant positive effect on rice, while on potatoes and cassava its impact is negative. This difference is attributed to the fact that the adoption of irrigation systems in rice cultivation is essential for increasing production, for which reason farmers with access to credit can more easily acquire this type of technology; while for other crops adequate fertilization is the main determinant of productivity. According to the results obtained, it is recommended to develop lines of credit focused on the adoption of machinery, especially for small farmers; as well as generate technical assistance programs based on the determinants of the productivity of each crop, in order to guarantee that credit resources are invested efficiently. Similarly, strengthening the presence of financial institutions in the most remote rural areas and providing advice to know and request loans is of vital importance in access to agricultural credit and the improvement of productivity
