# Legitimation and delegitimation of state violence on social media: the case of University of Cauca

This thesis examines the legitimation and delegitimization of state violence in social networks in the case of a student manifestation in the University of Cauca. To this end, it examines 8421 tweets with the aim of unraveling the specific cultural references and discursive strategies that can be used to legitimize or delegitimize state violence. It performs a qualitative analysis of all tweets with the use of the general categories of authorization, moral evaluation, rationalization and dehumanization. Located within the Critical Studies of the Discourse (ECD), this research focuses especially on the specific mechanisms of delegitimization of the social actors, in order to develop discursive strategies that allow their relegitimization. By conceptualizing the 'legitimacy' and the 'illegitimity' of violence as a fluid state rather than a fixed description, this the thesis suggests that legitimation and delegitimization of violence are continuous processes, which means that the legitimacy or illegitimacy of a violent act can be changed by making cultural references and using discursive strategies of violence appropriate to the context. qualitative analysis of all tweets by using the general categories of authorisation, moral evaluation, rationalization, and dehumanization.
