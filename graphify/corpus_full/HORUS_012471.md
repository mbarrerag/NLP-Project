# SUSTAINABLE MOBILITY AND MOBILITY FOR OVERVIEW: BEFORE, DURING AND AFTER THE COVID-19 PANDEMIA

Sustainable mobility is born during the second half of the 20th century, in high-income countries, thanks to the concern for environmental and social problems caused by the generalization of an urban transport model based on the particular car. The excessive use of the private car generated, mainly, great congestion and therefore a great loss of time for people. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) sustainable mobility is the one that promotes the use of public transport, walking and cycling; what can help to mitigate the climate change and improve the quality of the air by reducing carbon dioxide emissions from transport. Also says the WHO that increasing the safety of public transport, walking and cycling increases the physical activity of people and improves their quality of life and the ability to access to work and education. The WHO, based on the previous definition of sustainable mobility, raises the cycle of positive feedback, arguing that the increase of cyclists and pedestrians reduces the private vehicle travel and this, in turn, reduces the emissions and pollution of the air and also generates less accidents and dead. The above is good for high-income countries because they have largely resolved accidents and pollution. What is not true for low- and middle-income countries where accidents and pollution problems seem to be exacerbated year after year. With regard to accidents, it may be said that it is not entirely true that a reduction in private vehicle travel would lead to fewer deaths, as sustainable mobility does. What could occur in low- and middle-income countries is that by driving more people on the road, the rest of them remaining on the road, if they are driven by drivers with little road education, they could increase speed and accidents, which would lead to more deaths. What is happening in low- and middle-income countries is that by driving more people on the road and riding bicycles, without providing them with safety conditions, at least in road infrastructure and education, and without drivers having a road culture, education and respect for the lives of others, there is an increase in the number of deaths
