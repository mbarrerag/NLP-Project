# DESDE ACÁ PDFDE CAPTURA

Lip and oral cavity cancer represents 3.5 to 5% of all neoplasia and went from position 13 in 2007, to 12 -of 32 cancers studied-, in 2017. It has a higher incidence in men than in women with a 2:1 ratio. To analyze the difference in the global burden of disease (GBD) due to oral cancer for the Mortality, Years Lived on Disability (DALYS) and Years of Life Lost Due to Disability indicators, between the epidemiological regions of the World Health Organization (WHO) and the regions of income of the World Bank (WB) in the period 1990-2017. Retrospective descriptive study with information from the GBD database, for the 7 WHO regions and the 5 WB regions in the period 1990-2017, in ages 15-49 and 50-69 years, for both sexes Results: The most affected region is Asia Pacific, with a high prevalence (66.7/100,000 people) and mortality, while in Europe the incidence is lower (19.8/100,000 people). Conversely, DALYS are higher in Europe and lower in Africa. In the Upper Middle Income region for 2017, countries such as Croatia (4.47/100,000) and Belarus (4.63/100,000) register high mortality rates, while Iraq (0.31), Iran (0.58) and Colombia (1.04/100,000) present low values. The epidemiological profiles of lip and oral cavity cancer in the GBD database show great heterogeneity when comparing the WHO epidemiological or income regions, which is a reflection of the different exposures to risk factors, such as tobacco and alcohol consumption, socioeconomic position, lifestyles, access to screening, early diagnosis and timely care.
