# Differential Galois theory and algebraic dynamic system models

One of the most fruitful conceptual frameworks in geometry is the study of space through its transformations.In the framework of differential geometry, in a first stage that develops mainly until the middle of the twentieth century, this conceptual framework meant more than anything the study of the groups of Lie in differential varieties. One of the most notable applications is the study of the symmetries of differential equations, as well as the tera of Galois differential, both theories are related but are not equivalent. Although it has been managed to understand the relationship between these two theories is a particular context (Blázquez-Sanz et. al., 2015), there are still many loose ends. Even in the case of linear equations where there are equivalent systems that have different symmetries. The same precursors of the theory of groups of transformations, there is interested in structures of more general character. For example the groups of infinite dimension of Sophus Lie and Elie Cartán, which would later be formalized as Pseudogroups by some authors and finally as D-groups (2004) who used them to give a proposal of a theory of Galois for the equations These dynamic systems are known as varieties of translation. A surprising result of these authors, announced as Corolario B (p. 3), implies that if an algebraic dynamic system admits an invariant of n-points, where n is any positive number, then it necessarily admits an invariant of 2-points. Through this research we want to make some contributions to both the question of the relationship between symmetries and Galois for linear differential equations, and the problem of distinguishing when D-groupoids are algebraic groups
