# EFFECT OF INCORPORATION OF PHASE CHANGE MATERIALS IN TERNARY LOW-CO2 CEMENTS SYSTEMS

Calcium Sulfoaluminate (CSA) cements have emerged as a promising alternative to ordinary Portland cement, mainly due to their ability to reduce energy consumption during production, resulting in a significant reduction in CO2 emissions compared to ordinary Portland cements. In addition, the incorporation of substitute materials such as industrial by-products like fly ash and granulated blast furnace slag enhances the sustainability and environmental friendliness of these cement formulations. This study systematically investigates ternary systems containing CSA cements, fly ash, and ground granulated blast-furnace slag, with a particular focus on the incorporation of phase change materials (PCMs) to further improve energy efficiency. PCMs were intentionally introduced into the system at varying concentrations of 1%, 3% and 5%. Thermal conductivity measurements were performed to evaluate the effectiveness of the PCMs in improving the thermal properties of the cementitious blends. In addition, complementary techniques such as X-ray diffraction and scanning electron microscopy were used to study the microstructure and hydration products of the blends. Furthermore, the compressive strength of the cementitious blends was evaluated up to 90 days of curing. Overall, this research contributes to a better understanding of CSA-based ternary cementitious systems and their potential for improving energy efficiency in the construction industry. The results have implications for sustainable building practices, offering innovative solutions to reduce carbon emissions and improve the performance of cementitious materials
