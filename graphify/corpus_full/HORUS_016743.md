# INFLUENCE OF COMMUNITY STRUCTURE ON ARTISANAL FISHERY YIELDS IN A TROPICAL ESTUARY WITH MULTIPLE STRESSORS

Tropical estuaries are diverse ecosystems with important ecosystem services for fishing communities in coastal areas. However, these ecosystems are affected by multiple threats, which can impact diversity and have an effect on fishing yields. The community of fish and demersal macroinvertebrates of Buenaventura Bay estuary, was sampled during 5 years (2015 to 2019). To identify changes in the community structure, standardized sampling efforts with artisanal trawling were carried out. There was a significant decrease in fishing yields in this period, with a decrease from 21.7 ± 11.7 kg.h-1 in 2015, to 8.2 ± 5.5 kg.h-1 in 2019. This decline in the fishing yields was associated with lower species richness according to the GAM analysis. Although, the highest abundance of organisms was sampled in the middle of the study period (2017), this was not reflected in an increase in a fishing yield, because the species were not of commercial interest and the fishes were small in size, which are discarded in artisanal fishing. This decrease in species richness, could be an effect of dredging activities, reported in 2016 and 2018 for Buenaventura Bay. Dredging has been reported to modify and disturb the bottom habitats, and generating a succession where an entry of pioneer species occurs, of high abundance and low biomass. Additionally, for the area, there was an increase in water temperature of 2 ° C above the average, from December 2015 to March 2016. According to the ENSO events monitoring authorities, this increase has been the second highest event since 1950. These results are in accordance with global trends, in which the decrease in fishing yields are associated with the loss of diversity, influenced by both, anthropic and natural causes
