# Efficient removal of pharmaceutical contaminants using laccase immobilized on activated coal derived from cashew nut shells: Thermodynamic, kinetic and calorimetry study

This study investigates the removal of three pharmaceutical contaminants—diclofenac, amoxicillin, and carbamazepine—using activated carbons derived from cashew nut shells, which were impregnated with the enzyme laccase. The enzyme immobilization process was optimized under controlled conditions at pH 5 and 30 °C, achieving notable activities of 38.3, 32.1, and 29.2 U g−1 for the carbon-laccase composites LACK, LACN, and LACZ, respectively. A temperature range of 10 °C to 30 °C enhanced the immobilization efficiency, though performance decreased at higher temperatures. Adsorption isotherms were well-modeled by the Langmuir equation, and kinetic studies followed a first-order rate mechanism. Thermodynamic analyses indicated that the adsorption interactions were spontaneous and exothermic. The integration of laccase with bio-based activated carbons highlights a novel, eco-friendly approach for pharmaceutical removal, with potential for large-scale application in wastewater treatment
