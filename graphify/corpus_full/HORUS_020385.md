# Impacts of technological appropriation on habitat, by planning, management and territorial urban organization processes of the department of Cesar, 1960 - 2010

This thesis aims to assess in the perspective of technology, habitat and territory, the impact of the approaches to extractivist appropriation of Cesar: cotton production and mining exploitation, in planning processes and territorial organization. To this end, tools for production and cartographic analysis supported in the data ecosystems of NASA and institutions of the Colombian State are used; consultation of plans for the projection of the department; use of data produced by territorial actors and the exploration of the pilot of a technological platform for territorial projection with information from different territorial actors. In the development of the the thesis, the territorial changes that the department has had, the moments of territorial projection characterized by the decision making in isolation by some State institutions and the actors that make a presence in the territory such as multinational enterprises, organized community, academia, private enterprise and government. In addition, the gaps for the implementation of technologies that support the territorial decision-making and a methodology for remedying them are recognized. .
