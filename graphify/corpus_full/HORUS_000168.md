# Analysis of lindane in water using new “Lab-made” SPME fibers: Effect of parameters influencing determination

The efficiency of three lab-made solid-phase microextraction (SPME) fibers based on montmorillonite (MMT-13, MMT-CD, and MMT-Si) was evaluated for the determination of lindane in water samples spiked with this pesticide by GC-ECD. The study was designed to assess the effect of key extraction parameters, including analyte concentration in two ranges—high (1–40 μg mL−1) and low (0.01–1 μg mL−1); pH values of 3, 7, 9, and 12; presence of natural organic matter (glucose, isopropanol, and oxalic acid); and inorganic ions (bicarbonate and sulfate) to simulate natural water conditions and evaluate potential interferences. Results indicated that the high concentration range yielded superior analytical performance, with lower variability and greater reproducibility. Calibration curves exhibited good linearity (r2 ≥ 0.991) within the high concentration range. Limits of detection ranged from 0.046 μg mL−1 (MMT-Si) to 0.150 μg mL−1 (MMT-13), and limits of quantification from 0.230 μg mL−1 (MMT-Si) to 0.500 μg mL−1 (MMT-13). Reproducibility improved significantly at high concentrations: MMT-13 (6.26 % vs. 11.13 % RSD), MMT-CD (9.58 % vs. 17.00 % RSD), and MMT-Si (11.52 % vs. 22.64 % RSD). At neutral pH, all three fibers exhibited maximum lindane retention; MMT-CD performed best under acidic conditions, and MMT-Si under basic conditions, whereas efficiency decreased markedly at extreme pH values. The presence of inorganic ions enhanced extraction by up to 75 %. Organic matter reduced analyte retention, with glucose causing the least interference—up to 50 % higher extraction efficiency compared to isopropanol. The proposed methodology demonstrated high versatility and reliability at elevated concentrations, suggesting its optimal applicability for environmental monitoring of organochlorine pesticides in water
