# Voices that resonate: Social Work scholars and feminists who think about the family 2000-2010

The disciplinary construction in Social Work is an unfinished effort that requires constancy and permanence. Back to our bases implies critically analyzing what is written, what we read and what we share in the teaching-learning of our profession. This research focuses on the family seen from the Social Work with feminist perspective, in a documentary review of academic production and written works by four teachers of the Department of Social Work who belong to or have belonged to the National University of Colombia: María Himelda Ramírez, Yolanda Puyana, María Eugenia Martínez and Juanita Barreto, covering the decade from 2000 to 2010. With the aim of identifying and analyzing the conceptual theoretical contributions of these four feminist academics according to the definition of family that proposed Social Work in the mentioned decade. The main findings showed an extensive academic elaboration by the teachers, which contain explicit contributions according to the concept of family in Colombia, public policies, among others, the latter is a feminist perspective that included the gender approach in the historical, social, political and academic analysis, since this has allowed or influenced the creation of academic programs, the local observatories and public interests, among others, the latter is a feminist. written works by four faculty members from the Social Work Department who belong or have been taken to the National University of Colombia: María Himelda Ramírez, Yolanda Puyana, María Eugenia Martínez, and Juanita Barreto, covering the decade from 2000 to 2010, with the objective of identifying and analysing the theoretical-conceptual contributions of these four female students agreement to the definition of family proposed by Social Work in the mentioned decade. The main findings reviewed an extensive academic elaboration by the faculty, containing contributions, enabling the transformation of the concept of family in Colombia.
