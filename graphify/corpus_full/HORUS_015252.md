# DEVELOPMENT OF MICROPARTICLES BASED ON POLYSACCHARIDES AND COLOMBIAN PROPOLIS OIL EXTRACTS

Propolis oily extracts have been characterized as matrices with high antiradical, reducing and antimicrobial power. However, due to their susceptibility to oxidizing agents (e.g. O2 and light) that react with the environment and to the possibility of forming undesirable compounds that can negatively impact their bioactivity and sensory properties, their use remains limited. To preserve the protection of the active principles and allow the controlled release of their bioactive components, the microencapsulation of oily extract of propolis from the Colombian premontane rainforest (EOPC) was evaluated by Spray Drying. Maltodextrin and gum arabic were used as wall materials. The microencapsulated EOPCs (EOPCM) were analyzed by physicochemical and microbiological analyses. The results indicate that the EOPCMs presented high antioxidant, antiradical and antimicrobial potential. However, the loss of some metabolites such as polyphenols and carotenoids was confirmed. Additionally, formulations with higher EOPC concentration and lower drying temperature had higher bioactivity due to lower metabolite degradation and lower production of pro-oxidant substances. It is concluded that it is possible to elaborate EOPCM using mixtures of maltodextrin and gum arabic and more than 50% of the phenols and carotenoids in the EOPC sample are preserved
