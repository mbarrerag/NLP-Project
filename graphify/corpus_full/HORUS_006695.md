# Effectiveness of training with partial obstruction of blood flow in adults with pathologies of cardiovascular and musculoskeletal origin

Throughout the years, there has been a boom in the implementation of training with partial obstruction of blood flow (BFR) in potentially healthy people, however, there is little clarity regarding how effective this method is in people with a burden of morbidity associated with the disease compared to the processes of conventional physiotherapy. To determine the effectiveness of training with partial obstruction of BFR in adults with pathologies of cardiovascular and musculoskeletal origin. A systematic search was performed in the databases Science direct, Pubmed, Embase and Pedro. The results were selected for randomized clinical trials that related the training BFR and its application in people with cardiovascular pathologies (CV) or musculoskeletal (MSK). A final analysis matrix was performed whose content was evaluated by pairs and then through the software RevMan de Cochrane obtained the significance and heterogenity of the results, as well as the quality and risk of bias of the studies. 39 studies were identified in the analysis matrix of which 20 were selected for the quantitative analysis of 43 variables MSK and 4 variables. effect in favor of the BFR, as well as the functional tests and self-reported questionnaires, and in relation to the physiological response, the effects of chronic adaptation in favor of the BFR are shown similar to those recorded in the practice of high intensity exercise. The combination of conventional training with BFR shows a clinical significance in people with some pathology associated with variables such as pain, functionality and quality of life, as well as in the decrease of post-surgical atrophy despite not demonstrating a significant statistical difference in this systematic review, so that the BFR can be more effective compared to conventional physiotherapy; it is necessary to be able to determine specific prescription parameters in terms of recommendations of the AOP (blood occlusion pressure) and the percentage of intensity generally given in RM to generate expected anatomical and physiological modifications and the replication necessary for intervention and research in the area .
