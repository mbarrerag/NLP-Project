# Influence of Deposition Conditions on Morphological, Optical and Structural Properties of Rb 3 Bi 2 I 9 Perovskites Prepared by Sequential Evaporation

Despite the enormous progress in fabricating hybrid organic-inorganic lead halide perovskite solar cells, their toxicity and poor stability remain as major limiting towards large scale production. The presence of toxic lead and solvents during the fabrication process, make that the large-scale manufacturing of Pb based perovskite solar cells be a big obstacle from the environmental point of view. Tin and germanium based perovskites turned out to be unsuitable to be viable replacements of Pb due to the instability of their +2 oxidation states; however, taking into account the isoelectronic nature of lead (II) and bismuth (III) ions, possible stable and non-toxic alternatives for the development of photovoltaic devices, could be found among bismuth-based halide compounds. Since conventional solution-grown inorganic perovskite films suffer unavoidable pinhole defects and poor surface morphology, limiting the device performance, in this work, a dual-source thermal sequential evaporation approach is explored to achieve a uniform and pin-hole-free morphology Rb3Bi2I9 polycrystaUine thin film. The structural, optical and morphological characteristics of the resulting thin films are studied by X-ray diffraction (XRD), optical spectroscopy and scanning electron microscopy (SEM)
