# TOWARDS PARTICIPATIVE GOVERNANCE IN THE MANAGEMENT OF THE ECOSYSTEMS OF THE CULTURAL PARTRIME: THE CULTUREL CAFETERO PAISAGE OF COLOMBIA

The implementation of participatory governance models, which distance themselves significantly from traditional approaches to heritage management, in which the responsibility for conservation rests exclusively with public administrations, has been a significant challenge in the field of cultural policies. In this work we analyze the tendency to engage in the sustainability and improvement of a cultural heritage ecosystem from a demand perspective. As a case study, we analyze the Coffee Cultural Landscape of Colombia, declared a cultural heritage of humanity in 2011. Based on information from a survey conducted on visitors and residents of the area, we apply in a novel and integrated way two methodologies for the classification of publics: Latent Classes Models and Multiple Correspondence Analysis. We find that it is possible to identify three segments differentiated according to the degrees of propensity to participation and intensity of valuations; moreover, that the perceptions that each group has of governments have significantly influence on their decision to become involved. The findings of this study are useful as an input to the formulation of policies and as a mechanism for evaluating public management on strategies for sustainability of tourism, heritage and development
