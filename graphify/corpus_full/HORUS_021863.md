# The Constitutionalization of Occupational Safety and Health.

The right to safety and health at work is not a discipline of prevention, protection and promotion as limited by Colombian legislation. On the one hand, it constitutes a human right in accordance with the constitutional right to life, integrity, health and decent work and, on the other hand, constitutes an unnamed right integrated into domestic legislation by block of constitutionality. This is based on the principle of human dignity, when we recognize the inherent dignity of the person engaged in work, when we recognize that the worker does not lose his humanity from doors to economic unity, but on the contrary, economic units open the door to giving entry to constitutional rights and duties in the private arena. Thus, providing safety and health at work is not an option, but a sine qua non for developing a work activity in conditions that guarantee the life and integrity of the worker. Thus, the Constitutional Court has understood that the right to health and safety at work has been protected by direct violation of other rights recognized in the first chapter of the Political Charter as fundamental, but rather as the concept of health, in the face of safety and unanimity
