# CAMPISIN ASSOCIATIVENESS IN FOOD AND FOOD AND NUTRITIONAL SECURITY POLICIES IN THE CENTRAL REGION OF COLOMBIA.

Although most of the food consumed in Colombia comes from peasant production, only until the beginning of this century is the formulation of public policies aimed at improving the articulation of small-scale producers with the supply of food in cities. A pioneering experience was established by the Master Plan for Food Supply and Food Security of Bogotá (PMASAB) (2006-2019), followed by similar policies in other cities and departments of the country. These policies have been faced with logistical and commercial difficulties involving the production of food in small and dispersed productive units, agreeing to identify peasant associations (or small agricultural producers) to ensure that food distribution is done efficiently. This chapter analyses how these challenges have been faced from the promotion of agriculture by MMSAB from 2006, to the current proposals for the redesign of food supply in the Central Region of Colombia
