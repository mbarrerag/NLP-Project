# Sound-based interventions in the field of ophthalmological surgery: systematic review of randomized clinical trials.

75% of patients facing a surgical procedure experience anxiety. Under a 40-procedure in a state of consciousness, such as ophthalmological surgeries, considerable levels of anxiety can occur and consequently states of insomnia, pain and physiological stress.The sounds designed to improve the patient's experience can help not only distract, but also isolate the annoying sounds or the conversations in the operating room.In the context of plastic surgery, the presence of music has been shown to improve the quality of surgical closure and patient's anxiety.There is interest in evaluating interventions that stimulate psychological and physiological relaxation in patients.The purpose of this project was to evaluate sound-based interventions in the context of ophthalmological surgery, being useful to indicate or reject its potential use.OBJECTS: Assessing the safety and effectiveness of sound-based interventions for patients undergoing ophthalmological surgery.METOLOGY: A systematic, explicit search was performed through the strategy of high sensitivity in Medline (Ovid), CENTRAL LILACS, Cochrane Central Register of Controls (ENTSE) and CENTRAL. The general risk of bias was "low risk" in two trials and high risk in six trials. The effect in favor of sound intervention was identified on the outcome of anxiety (- 0.73 95% CI: -1.17 to -0.29) and on some secondary results (heart rate, diastolic blood pressure, systolic blood pressure).With the GRADE system we qualified the certainty of evidence from low to very low for the results. The results of this study could suggest the potential use of sound-based interventions to reduce anxiety in patients undergoing ophthalmic surgery
