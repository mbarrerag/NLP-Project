# Manufacture of magnetically-property scaffolds using magnetite microparticles from alluvial mining waste

The tissue regenerative medicine offers new solutions to recover function and structure of damaged tissues. One of the methods consists in implanting cells in artificial extracellular or scaffold matrices, in order to grow there until they form functional tissues. Such scaffolds can be improved with growth factors and magnetic properties. In this work magnetite was used from alluvial mining of gold and polylactic acid to manufacture magnetic scaffolds for bone regeneration by means of the technique of deposition by molten filament. Scaffolds were printed in the form of a TPMS giroid printer and were subjected to four types of characteristics: a) morphological effect by means of electron microscopy of sweep, b) magnetic by means of agregulity, c) mechanical by means of resistance tests to compression, and d) cytotoxicity and cellular proliferation. Also a mathematical modeling of the obtained hysteresis curves was performed.
