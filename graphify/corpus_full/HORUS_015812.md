# Mechanical and thermal CHARACTERIZATION OF A PEG-modified EPOXIC RESIN

The epoxy materials are used as matrices for high-performance composite materials, surface coatings and adhesives. Due to their high reticulation, these materials have low impact resistance, low resistance to crack propagation and low deformation to breakage. To improve the material’s tenacity, one route is to mix with a low molecular weight modifier, where the additive presents separation of phases in curing. The modified epoxy often has excellent fracture properties. However, the presence of another phase can decrease the module, the thermal stability of the material and loss of other properties at high temperatures. While for the adhesive applications such decreases in the module and temperature resistance are normally of no importance. This approach has been based on mixing the thermofix resin with a thermoplastic polymer with phases that separate when curing the resin, but where the thermoplastic phase has relatively good thermal stability and a low water absorption compared to the rich phase in thermofixed material.
