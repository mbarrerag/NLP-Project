# RECYCLING OF GALVANIC SLUDGE FOR THE PRODUCTION OF MATERIALS FOR THE CERAMIC INDUSTRY

The accumulation of heavy metals originating from the galvanic industry in the form of sludge constitutes a significant source of environmental contamination. Therefore, the utilization of heavy metals present in these hazardous residues represents a viable option for recycling and mitigating environmental pollution. In the current research endeavor, a solid waste derived from the galvanic industry was employed as a raw material for the industrial-scale production of ceramic tablets designed for heavy-duty traffic, along with the pigments necessary to bring them to the finalized product. Technical characteristics, including apparent density, linear shrinkage, water absorption capacity, porosity, and flexural strength, were systematically assessed. Structural properties were analyzed through X-ray diffraction (XRD), morphology was scrutinized via scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and leaching tests (TLC) were conducted. The outcomes reveal that the percentage of sludge added to the clay utilized in the manufacturing of tablets for heavy traffic has a modifying effect on the technical attributes of the ceramic. Furthermore, these attributes are influenced by the specific shaping process to which the material is subjected. Specimens formed through extrusion exhibit greater shrinkage in comparison to those obtained through dry pressing. The incorporation of sludge results in an increased overall porosity of the ceramic, directly correlating with its concentration.
