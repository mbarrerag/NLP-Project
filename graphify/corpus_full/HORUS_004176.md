# USING BLACK SOLDIER FLY TO SUPPORT FOOD SECURITY/SOVEREIGNTY IN LOCAL COMMUNITIES

Food insecurity in Colombia remains a critical challenge. Approximately 28% of the population experiences moderate food insecurity, while 4.9% face severe insecurity, resulting in skipped meals and hunger. Factors such as armed conflict and climate change exacerbate this issue. The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United nations suggests that strategies to mitigate the harsh prediction on food security globally, should include increasing agricultural biodiversity and increasing sustainable livestock management. In both scenarios, insects may play a crucial role since they act as pollinators, herbivores, predators, and decomposers in ecosystems. For example, some insects are efficient converters of organic waste into high-value protein and biofertilizer, making them vital for sustainable food production. The Black Soldier Fly (BSF), in particular, has been identified as a key insect in addressing food insecurity since it efficiently transforms organic waste into protein-rich larvae and nutrient-dense biofertilizer. The program "Insects for Peace" focuses on utilizing BSF in circular agriculture to support food sovereignty and sustainable farming practices. This initiative primarily aids vulnerable communities, including those affected by the armed conflict in Colombia. At Universidad Nacional de Colombia sede de La Paz (UNALP) we established a collaboration with Insects for Peace and the Arhuaco ethnic community in Pueblo Bello, to support their initiatives to develop protein-rich fish feed from BSF larvae, enhancing the resilience of local aquaculture systems. This model fosters circular economy practices by transforming waste into valuable agricultural inputs. The initiative extends its impact through ¿Ecoaulas de Paz¿ at UNALP, pedagogical spaces that demonstrate the practical applications of BSF farming in sustainable agriculture. The EcoAulas connect academic knowledge with community needs, fostering the transfer of expertise in agro-aquaculture and insect-based practices. By leveraging native insects like BSF, the EcoAulas de Paz initiative bridges gaps in food security, supports biodiversity, and promotes sustainable practices. This collaborative approach exemplifies the potential of circular agriculture in empowering communities and addressing climate challenges
