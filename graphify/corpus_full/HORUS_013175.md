# Development of a model to quantify intermittance in stochastic processes

One of the characteristics of precipitation that has been studied is intermitence, understood as the alternation of periods of intense and reduced activity. Intermitence has consequences for agriculture and the generation of hydroelectric energy and favours the occurrence of droughts and floods. Chapter 1 of this thesis proposes a methodology to quantify the intermittance of conglomerates of extremes, equivalent to the groupings of values that equal or exceed the P90 of the series. This consists in the use of a coefficient of dispersion, Burstiness, and of an extreme grouping measure, the Extreme index. Chapter 2 quantifies the intermittance of rainfall in Colombia by applying the methodology of chapter 1 in daily series of precipitation of CHIRPS of spatial resolution 0.05° between 1981 and 2025. According to the results, the Amazon and Caribbean regions are the regions with the lowest and greatest dispersion of the parameters that quantify the intermittance, respectively. resolution between 1981 and 2022.
