# DIET AND MICROPLASTIC ENGINE IN BAGRES OF THE CAUCA VALLE VALLE

The presence of microplastics in fish has been widely reported, this contamination occurs by direct ingestion or by trophic transfer, generating effects on fish health as well as toxicological effects. Similarly, these microplastics can be transferred to humans by consumption of fish, representing a risk to health. Many species of catfish are of commercial value due to their great size, abundance and quality of their meat, in the same way, they are an important fishing resource for the support and consumption of fish dependent populations, however, since they are generalistic predators (generalistic predators). Therefore, the aim of this study was to determine the trophic habits of Barbinche (Bagre panamensis), Ñato (the notarius kensleri) and Canchimala (Ariopsis silsi). The most abundant microplastics were fibers (91%) of size less than 1 mm, the predominant color (55%), this type of microplastics are usually associated with the textile industry and the fishing gears. In addition, an individual of N. kessleri showed consumption of a macroplastic (136x70 mm) corresponding to a embutid wrapper (Poliamide), this is possibly related to his feeding strategy associated with estuarine sediment. Finally, these results allow us to conclude, on the one hand, that the intake of microplastics was not associated with any particular dam, however, as has been reported in other studies, the catfishes when presenting omnivorous generalistic diets are susceptible to be affected by the environmental concentration of microplastics
