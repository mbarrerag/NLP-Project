# Shrinkage crack induction for the study of repair agents in rigid pavements

In the construction of rigid pavements, shrinkage cracking may occur at early stages, affecting the durability, strength, and aesthetic appearance of concrete surface layers. When studying the effectiveness of treatments to repair this type of cracking, resorting to reduced-scale physical models in the laboratory is quite relevant. However, simulating the dimensions and characteristics of shrinkage cracks is not an easy task. In this work, a successful protocol is proposed, based on the design and construction of an experimental assembly in a wind tunnel, to induce plastic shrinkage cracks in cement-based materials. Thus, ideal experimental conditions for crack generation were established: temperature, wind speed and relative humidity of the environment. Also, the dimensions and characteristics of moulds for sample preparation were defined. As a result, the system allowed to generate typical plastic shrinkage of 2 cm depth and 1 mm opening, whose geometry was defined by non-destructive tomographic images technique. Induced cracks are suitable for testing any remedial agent, including biological or chemical ones
