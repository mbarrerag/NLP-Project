# Hydroelectrics, Hydroenergy Policy and Environmental Conflicts over Dams in Colombia

From a critical and reflective reading, this special issue seeks to approach, on the one hand, the forms of national hydroenergy production, its seat on the management of basins that are heavily—in turn—turned over to the hydroelectricity and the normative framework that supports it, and, on the other hand, its relationship with the CAs and the alternatives proposed in local environments for the understanding of water or energy. This contributes to the necessary reflection of making changes and adjustments to this predominant energy model in Colombia. Since the 1930s, the construction of hydroelectric power plants was seen as synonymous with development and economic progress, which prompted their expansion throughout the world, effectively bringing important benefits to societies in terms of the provision of electricity. However, today these projects are questioned by the immense social and ecological costs that they generate, hence we can affirm that the dams are a source of environmental conflicts and technology in obsolescence
