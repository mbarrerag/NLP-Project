# Quantumness of the photonic blockade effect.

This thesis aims to investigate the effect of blocking photons, understood as a phenomenon in which the presence of a single photon inhibits the emission of more photons, effectively transforming a system into one that emits a photon at a time. This effect can be classified into two categories: the blocking of conventional photons, which is based on the nonlinearities of a system, and the blocking of non-conventional photons, which employs the quantum interference between two trajectories to cancel the probability of accessing a particular state. In order to investigate the underlying physical mechanisms of these two forms of blocking, this thesis uses numerical solutions of master equations, complemented by the application of analytical techniques to determine the optimal conditions for each type of blocking. Specifically, the study finds that the model of Jaynes-Cummings pumped and dissipative provides an ideal model in which both mechanisms are exhibited simultaneously. This allows the analysis of the mechanism of blocking photons in a unique and experimentally feasible configuration, as a cavity-QED composed by a semiconduciental quantum point within a micro-mould mechanism
