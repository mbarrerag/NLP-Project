# WOMEN

This article analyzes the discourse that the indigenous women Kankuamas of the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta, Colombia, have used to defend and position their territory in the so-called process of reetnization or reborn Kankuamo, which began to materialize in 1994 by initiatives of community leaders and some academics, with the aim of preserving their territory, claiming their indigenous, political, ethnic and cultural identity, in the context of the implementation of the Political Constitution of 1991. The research questions that guided our research were: (PI 1) What are the practices of communication and action used? (PI 2) What are the discursive cores employed? (PI 3) How do the discourses and practices of the Law of Origin and/or Law are connected with the main view of the indigenous communities / prophan law, with the western discourses and practices of positive law? (PI 4) How have these discourses changed and reflect changes and fissures in the practices used since 1990 to the present moment? Because our case of study, it is the only event registered in Colombia, as a process of indigenous reetnativa, we use them, we have to achieve the details.
