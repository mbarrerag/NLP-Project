# Effects of aerobic and isometric dynamic exercise on the treatment of primary arterial hypertension: Systematic review and meta-analysis.

Primary or essential arterial hypertension is one of the types of chronic disease of high blood pressure, referenced as heterogeneous and polygenic origin; on which various acquired and environmental factors exert effect. Following the Cochrane methodological development and the requirements of the PRISMA declaration, a systematic review and metaanalysis were performed quantifying the effects of aerobic dynamic exercise and isometric exercise on arterial pressure in adults diagnosed with primary arterial hypertension. Randomized clinical trials were included that investigated the effect of intervention with isometric exercise greater than 3 weeks and aerobic dynamical exercise greater than 6 weeks. 9 studies were finally grouped and synthesized by the inverse statistical method of variance, with effect of mean difference and a model of analysis for random effects with 95% confidence interval. 8 weeks of exposure demonstrated the significant reduction of systolic arterial pressure (SAP) with the two exposures, as opposed to diastolic arterial pressure (DBP) and cardiac frequency (FC) were expected to have an effect of isometric effects with 95% confidence interval. 8 weeks of exposure showed the significant reduction of systolic arterial pressure (SAP) with the upper arterial pressure, while the lower arterial pressure was used, unlike the cardiac pressure (AD)
