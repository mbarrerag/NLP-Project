# COMPASION AND CULTURE COMPETITION IN THREE HOSPITALS OF BOGOTA,COLOMBIA

Cultural competence, Compassion, Health professionals Introduction: Compassion (CS) allows health professionals to recognize and do something to alleviate the suffering of other people, while cultural competence (CC) makes it refencia to the ability to provide care in culturally consistent health.1-3 Overall, the cultural competence and compassion of the professional contributes to a greater confidence of the subject of care in this, as well as to greater satisfaction with care. To identify the level of compassion and cultural competence in health professionals of three hospitals in Bogotá. Cross-sectional descriptive study, in which health professionals participated (93 nurses, 216 physicians, 29 therapists) of three hospitals in Bogotá, which signed the informed and diligent sentiment: 1) Questionnaire of sociodemographic data, 2) Cultural Competence Assessment (CCA-S)3, and 3) Compassion Scale (CS) in its versions validated in Spanish and with permission of the authors. The data were analyzed with descriptive and comparative statistics according to type of variable. The level of compassion and cultural competence was moderate and high. Compassion has physiological effects that favor emotional regulation, and with this the sensitivity to the needs and suffering of the care subjects, so that it motivates action for their relief. A study conducted in Turkey reflects slightly higher levels of compassion among health professionals (3.71; DE: 0.31).4 However, these are levels that can be improved. The joint analysis of the constructs compassion and cultural competence is fundamental to understanding the cultural connotations of human suffering, which allows health professionals to provide culturally competent and compassionate care.5 The above requires the implementation and evaluation of interventions. Limitations: The results are descriptive of three hospitals, are not representative of the professionals in Colombia, nor establishes associations among the constructs under study, which requires additional analysis. The extension of the instruments negatively influenced participation, due to the priority of the care activities
