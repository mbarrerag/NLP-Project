# Roots of educational experience: an exploration of early philosophical developments behind John Dewey's philosophy of education

This thesis starts from the perspective according to which the concept of educational experience, presented for the first time in Dewey's last educational work, constitutes the beginning of a process of reconstruction of the philosophy of the education of the pragmatist thinker. As a contribution to this process of reconceptualization, the thesis is directed towards early philosophical developments of the author not directly educational, which will contribute to the manifesting of the connections between his early philosophy and the mature, and non-educational philosophy with the educational. In order to contribute to the enrichment in the understanding of the concept of educational experience, the the the thesis is realized in four chapters. Chapter I presents the concept of educational experience in general, in its two principles (continuity and interactivity) and the criterion of differentiation of experiences (growth). Chapter II deepens in the criterion of growth of experience, starting from the theoretical differences from the concept of Dewey with Thomas Henry Huxley, which shows a clear decantation of the thinker of Vermont towards a social evolutionary reading.
