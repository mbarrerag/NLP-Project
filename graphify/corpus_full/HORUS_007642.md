# Subalternities and gender citizenships in the Anti-Ocaquean Urabá. Reflections from political theory.

This study focuses on the subaltern masculinities and gender citizenships in the Anti-Oqueño Urabá, exploring how these masculinities contribute to the construction of a more equitable gender citizenship. To this end, it is based on an interdisciplinary approach that combines political theory and gender studies, examining the experiences and perspectives of men who challenge the hegemonic male norms in the region. The research is based on qualitative methodologies, including in-depth interviews and focal groups with selected participants from different identities within the Urabá, such as young people, peasants and members of religious groups, using fundamental theories such as the hegemonic masculinity of Connell and the contributions of Latin American studies on masculinities. The study aims to identify the barriers and opportunities that face subalternal masculinities in their daily life and in their civic participation, as well as to propose specific routes to reform policies and social programs that support new forms of masculinity in the region.
