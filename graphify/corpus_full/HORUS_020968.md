# Transcribomic Analysis of Thevetia peruviana cells grown in suspension and elicized with Methyl Jasmonate and Salicyl Acid.

It has long been known that plants are a valuable source of a wide variety of metabolites of pharmaceutical and agricultural interest; however, one of the first stumbling blocks to take advantage of these metabolites is the low concentration in which they are synthesized by most plants. Moreover, since they are molecules that occur only under stress conditions it is difficult to activate these cascades of reactions in a live plant so that the response is repeatable and specific for the production of a group of metabolites of interest. On the other hand, although there is extensive knowledge of central metabolism, the understanding of secondary metabolic cells is still incomplete, as there are many unknown intermediate reactions. Similarly, knowledge of routes of one species is not extrapolable to other plants since the response to environmental stress is different in each plant. Thevetia peruviana is a promissive plant species due to the fact that it produces secondary metabolites with a wide variety of biological activities such as, antimicrobial, antifungal, antiviral, antioxidant and cardiovascular action, some of which have been quantified. The aim is to study the response of the cells of T. peruviana grown in suspension against the treatment with organic elicitors such as Methyl Jasmonate and Salicyl Acid for induction of production of Secondary Metabolites.
