# TROMBOPROPHILAXIS DURING MANAGEMENT, PART AND POURPERIUM.

Venous thromboembolic disease constitutes by excellence a multicausal pathology, since it involves the complex interaction between acquired or inherited predisposed factors (v.g. thrombophilia) and environmental exposures (clinical risk factors) that combine to interrupt the delicate hemostatic balance, thus generating a prothrombolistic state. In a broad sense, venous thromboembolic disease then encompasses deep venous thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary thromboembolism (PTE) and refers to the formation of a fibrin and red blood deposit in the deep venous system, usually at the level of the leg, thigh or pelvis. On the other hand, it is understood by PTE when such material housed in the lower body segments, is released and migrates through the bloodstream finally reaching pulmonary circulation. Multiple studies support the predominant role that exhibits this entity in terms of morbidity, not only in the general population but especially in the obstetric area.
