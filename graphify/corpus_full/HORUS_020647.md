# FACTURAS OF HIGHER MEMBERS

The most frequent fractures of the children's population are the third most frequent fractures of the radium and ulna, representing 13-40% of the total fractures (1). Historically, most of these fractures have been treated conservatively or orthopedically. Recently, there has been a tendency towards surgical treatment in an effort to improve results (2). Treatment depends on age and type of displacement of the fracture, among others. Considering the potential for a child's physical growth, some degrees of angulation can be accepted based on age and remodeling capacity (3). The immobilization of the forearm with circular splints or casts remains an option for treatment for many fractures within acceptable alignment parameters (4). For fracture traces that do not meet these parameters, surgical treatment is recommended. Surgery options include fixing with plates or with intramedular nails. Recently there is a growing interest in determining which method provides superior results, but the supercelsus treatment remains controversial (5, 6)
