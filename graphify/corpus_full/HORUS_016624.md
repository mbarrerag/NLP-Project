# Evaluation of possible anti-Parkinsonian effects of the etanolic extract of Nertera granadensis in mice.

Evaluation of the possible anti-Parkinsonian effects of the etanolic extract of Nertera granadensis in mice Introduction: Parkinson's disease (EP) is a neurodegenerative disorder of motor character, progressive evolution and long course; caused mainly by the decrease of dopamine at the level of the brain which is intimately involved in motor activity, although today there is a wide range of medicines for the control and management of symptoms in EP, no drug is yet available that slows the progression of the disease or has neuroprotection characteristics.The therapeutic is focused and adapted to the treatment of symptoms, time of evolution, phase of disease and the disability that entails. Currently the treatment of choice for the management of motor symptoms associated with EP is the L-Dopa, precursor of dopamine, but as time lapses the treatment is losing effectiveness, due to the evolution of the neuronal deterioration, with which the dose must be adjusted and increased, and after the chronic use of the L-Dopa can present important adverse effects. The etanolytic extract of Nertera granadensis did not present significant anti-Parkinsonian effects. However, when associated with levodopa-carbidopa, a tendency to improve the performance of the animal is observed
