Colombia, ORCID 0000-000-434-7065, 0000-0002-4358-3600. * Corresponding autón autón: daradang@unal.edu.co Introduction: The regrouping of pigons is a common practice after the period of wean, this affects the hierarchy established during the nursing period and generates a pre- and generates a pre-employment. The analysis of the images showed that the lesions were most frequently presented the first 5 days and were decreasing the second week, but reaching the last week there was an increase in type 1 lesions, going from 3 pigs with lesions to 8, this could be due to the size of the pigs, which is why, probably increased the competition. It was also observed that in the group of males the fights persisted until the third week compared to the females and it was also observed that pigs with greater grade 2 lesions lost between 200 and 300 grams the first 3 days. the first 5 days after weaning are the most important to reduce the fights and avoid injuries between the animals, there were no differences between lesions according to sex, but it was more persistent in males than in females.
