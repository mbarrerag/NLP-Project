# On arithmetic equivalent and Iwasawa invariants

This thesis explores the connections between two fundamental areas of algebraic number theory: the Iwasawa theory and arithmetical equivalence. On the one hand, it studies how ideal classes groups grow in cyclotomy extensions of pádic integers, starting with the pioneering works of Kenkichi Iwasawa, who introduced pádic methods to describe this growth by means of Iwasawa modules. On the other hand, it analyzes the zeta function of Dedekind, a central object that encodes arithmetic information of a numerical body, and the concept of arithmetic equivalence, developed by Ronald Perlis, which identifies non-isomorphic numerical bodies but with equal zeta functions, using the notion of equivalence of Gassmann. The thesis highlights how certain modules of Iwasawa can help to determine the zeta functions of fully real bodies and to identify relationships between Iwasawa modules of arithmetically equivalent bodies.
