# Early prediction of frosts in high-rise crops, using machine learning methods.

In Colombia, many crops are located in the highlands of the Andean regions, at altitudes above 2,500 m above sea level, where the greatest susceptibility to the occurrence of frost events is concentrated. The aim of this study was to propose an early prediction model of frosts based on the relationship between these events and climatic variables, by implementing machine learning algorithms. The climatic variables were obtained from thirteen weather stations distributed in nine municipalities of the department of Cundinamarca. The variables recorded were temperature, relative humidity, dew point, photosynthetically active radiation and precipitation, these constituted the explanatory variables of the events of frost. The metrics used for the predictive evaluation of the performance of the five machine learning methods examined were precision, rate of true positives, rate of true negatives, accuracy and F1 score. It was identified that the hours prior to the occurrence of an event of frost were characterized by low humidity, low dew point and high radiation. Four of the five trained models were performed satisfactorily, with average time of evaluation above 91%. The cross-validation and statistical analysis showed the error of low humidity, low humidity and high radiation.
