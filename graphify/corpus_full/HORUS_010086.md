# Intraseasonal variability of precipitation over northern South America: diagnosis and connections.

Understanding precipitation at intra-seasonal scale is fundamental to establish forecasting systems that allow for the prediction of different events that condition the availability of water resources and extreme events involving floods and droughts. In this sense, a study is carried out of the main precipitation patterns on northern South America, by means of analysis of main components, in three intra-seasonal bands: 1-10 days, 10-30 days and 30-90 days. Subsequently, these patterns are related to the waves coupled with convection (CCW), low-level jets (LLJ) CHOCO, Caribbean and Orinoquia, and the initial-vegetation-atmosphere interaction represented by evapotranspiration (ET). The results show significant links between the first PCs of the 1-10 day band with Kelvin waves, tropical depressions (TD), inercio-gravitative (iner-er, exercise to the east (EIG) and the mixed-vegetation-atmosphere interaction represented by evapotran-gravity (MRG).
