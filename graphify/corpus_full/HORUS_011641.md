# • Operational relationships between the sole and the PLANETARY HYDROSISTEMS

Soils have many vital ecological functions or only for ecosystems, but also for humans and some Earth dynamics. For example, Hydrosystems, which correspond to the dynamics of Earth's water, its distribution, its physical and chemical properties, and its relationship with living beings, include the understanding of the interactions of biogeochemical cycles. Soil, stores largely biogeochemical cycles, which interrelate the geosphere, the hydrosphere, the atmosphere, the biosphere and even the anthropospheric. Soils and planetary hydrosystems also perform fundamental functions of the Earth for the sustainability of life on our planet. The planetary hydrosystems then refer to all water systems on Earth, including oceans and seas that cover about 70% of the earth's surface and absorb carbon dioxide; rivers and lakes, are both sources of freshwater and species, which serve for agro-industrial and hydropower; aquifers store water from the rapid surface pollution; and glaciers and polar caps, store the natural water and contain a good amount of wealth for scientific research, and environmental information. These aspects can be affected by soil degradation, through inadequate agricultural practices, urbanization and deforestation. Soil as an organism is functional on a global, regional and local scale. HYDROSISTEMAS are the energy-stimulating and balancing of the biogeochemical cycles of the Planet, the Geosystem, the Biosystem, the Atmospheric System and the Anthroposystem. On the other hand, GEODERMA is the protective layer of the Earth system. It is the protective PHILTRO of the water reserves of the Planet. The largest tissue of the PLANETA EARTH. And as well as the skin, it is the largest tissue of the human body, the soil, it is the Earth's and protects microorganisms, the meso and macroorganisms, therefore, biodiversity.
