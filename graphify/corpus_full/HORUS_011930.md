# THE OCEAN AS CLIMATE'S REGULATOR

Based on the knowledge on the irregular incidence of solar rays on the earth's surface, and the distribution of heat through the general circulation of the atmosphere, one could say that the behavior of the climate is easily understandable. However, this is not because there is an important factor to take into account in the climate's behavior and is the water vapor. In order to exist the vertical and horizontal movements of the air masses, it is required of the warm air with steam, being the motor that acts to general circulation of the atmosphere.The steam comes from the terrestrial surface formed by 71% by the ocean, which, moreover, accumulates great amount of heat, rather than the same atmosphere, making the ocean of supreme importance for the regulation of the climate through its movements.With the ocean's circulation the heat transport is carried out, not only towards the continents and the atmosphere, but also towards the depths of the oceans, are called the horizontal movement of the atmospheric circulation (ffz). South, where atmospheric pressure is high, subtropical ocean turns are generated, occupying in each ocean an area between 30° and 45° latitude with a movement opposed to the rotation of the earth in the northern hemisphere (anticyclonic) and opposite in the south (cyclonic). Subtropical turns begin with a cold current, which is generated at 30° by the effect of the high pressure of the Hadley cell. Cold currents are formed because high pressures, in the form of Alisios winds, at the time they exert force from the respective coasts, move warm surface water masses to the other side of the ocean, and leaving a space that is compensated by water from the cold bottom and rich in nutrients that ascends and generates to the coastal areas of surgency. These vertical movements of ascending water also relate to waters that recirculate by the thermocline, which is a layer of water column, where the vertical gradients of temperature are significant.
