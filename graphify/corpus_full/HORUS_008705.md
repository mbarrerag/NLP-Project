# Design and analysis of a multi-stage control for power multi-converters in a DC microgrid

The micronetworks are designed to connect different types of AC and CC loads that require robust power controllers to achieve efficient energy transfer. However, the effects of AC and CC disturbances on a single type of controller make achieving such stability on a micronet a design challenge. In addition, in multi-stage systems and loads where disturbances affect both upstream and downstream of the micronet these controllers require greater robustness. This thesis presents an analysis of a slider mode control (SMC) applied to a multi-stage microgrid with DC power converters and AC. The objective was to implement drivers from upstream to downstream to converters that feed constant power loads CC-CC and CC-CA. The controller was tested considering a single sliding surface that faces external disturbances, such as variations in AC converter frequency, sudden changes in the voltages upstream and constant power charges (CPL). Initially the driver was analyzed from first simple order, then with washout filter and later validated experimentally. Then, a second order driver was analyzed, and the output was found. k was also analyzed from the current voltage and constant power (CPL)
