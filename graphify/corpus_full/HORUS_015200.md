# CONJUGAL VIOLENCE IN THE NEOGRANADIN CARIBBEAN, 1750 AND 1811.

From a series of criminal trials for insults, ill treatment, severance, conjugation, abandonment, separation and divorce, which took place in New Granada, this chapter draws attention to the couple's conflicts in the Caribbean (Chagre, Portobelo, Caracas, Cartagena, Malambo, Mompox, San Benito Abad and San José de la Victoria de Majagual) in the transition from the 18th to the 19th century and the struggle of women, especially the elite, for their individuation. The voices of married persons and their legal representatives tell us of a tension between the practices of male domination and the ideas that postulated gender equality by arguing the recognition of the capacity of female reasoning. The text reconstructs the illustrated debate that took place in Spain in the second half of the 18th century around the role of women in the family and tries to make visible the appropriation of it in New Granada
