# Characterization of pH and compressive resistance of experimental bioceramic QCP5 compared to a commercial bioceramic

The vital pulper therapies (TPV) are clinical treatments that seek to promote an environment to restore the pulp health of teeth affected by pathologies associated with different etiological factors. This is achieved through the use of bioceramic materials that must comply with certain physical properties (compressive resistance, dimensional stability, working time and fencing), chemical (pH adequate) and biological (biocompatibility, bioactivity, bioinergy) that guarantee their effectiveness and maintain the quality standards. Currently the most used bioceramic materials are the calcium di-trisilicates, but one of the main disadvantages are: their time of prolonged fencing which can increase the total duration of treatment, difficult manipulation, presence of toxic ions and cations and high costs which make it difficult to access to all the population, specifically the Colombian one. Therefore, in the National University of Colombia, a bioceramic experimental QCP5 from the egg shell for TPV has been designed.
