# Improvement of digital lens holographic microscope for the visualization of biosamples

The micro-samplistic micro-scopic digital holographic without Lenses (DLHC) is an image technique that has been used for the visualization of micrometric size samples. Simplification in the required hardware, adaptability in digital processing and the need for markers have positioned it as an attractive, portable and cost efficient alternative for the visualization of micrometric biological samples. Despite the simplicity in its implementation, the hardware components used to capture digital holograms have limitations that directly affect the correct display of samples. In the present master thesis in Physical Engineering, limitations in the hardware of DLHM and their impact for the visualization of microscopic objects are studied. In turn, improvements to these limitations are proposed by means of the implementation of opto-numerical methods which are validated by the visualization of biological samples. Given the importance of numerical opening (NA) for the performance in DLHM, a method for the characterization and validation of the NA in light beams is proposed.
