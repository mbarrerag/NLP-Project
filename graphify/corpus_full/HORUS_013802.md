# Sweet water mimicry: determination of eco-evolutionary mechanisms in Corydoras (Siluriformes: Callichthyidae)

Mimetism is a mechanism where the species that coexist tend to look like each other, often in terms of color patterns. A specific type of mimetism is the Mülleriano, which occurs when the form and coloration is shared by species that present generally poison and/or bad taste, avoiding the attack of their predators. Various studies of mimetism in terrestrial environments have identified different ecological and evolutionary mechanisms that generate similar phenotypes which allow the coexistence of species that could be competitive. However, in aquatic environments the visual identification is different from that of terrestrial environments and can be affected by the color of water and other physical-chemical processes that could interfere with the patterns of color methamphetamines observed. A group that can be used as a model for elucidating the mechanisms that promote mimetism in sweet water is the genus of fish Coryadoras. This is one of the most diverse genera of the Neotropics (~160 species) composed of nine widely distributed color lines in all South America. The genus presents different mimetics if they are in different basins, with groups of species that converge in patterns of the same and contain similar spots and that contain similar patterns of the Neotrops. The information obtained will not only contribute to the knowledge of the processes that generate the formation of mimetism in freshwater fish, but will also allow us to know in a broader context the ecological and evolutionary processes that differ between terrestrial and aquatic environments
