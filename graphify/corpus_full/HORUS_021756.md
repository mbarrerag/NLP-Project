# Seismofacies Interpretation and Implications for the Production Geological Model: Examples from the Buzios Field

Buzios field is part of the Brazilian Pre-salt petroleum province located in the northeast portion of Santos Basin. Reservoir rocks are formed by lacustrine carbonates that reflect a great variety of facies e diagenetics processes. The seismofacies described in the present work are the most evident features observed in the available 3D seismic data and they are here denominated as follows: carbonate platform backstepping, deep lake siltstones, onlaps filling relatively low structures, platform edge clinoforms, coastal ridges, shelf edge collapse deposits, clay-rich minibasins, and carbonate mounds. These seismofacies were defined through classic criteria already known in the literature, allied with reflector stacking patterns and acoustic impedance attributes. Denominations proposed for these seismofacies show their interpretive character, which is supported by well data. Each seismofacies represents a set of characteristic rocks formed in specific moments inside of depositional environments and may reflect distinctive permoporous features. Therefore, proper identification and characterization of geological features in seismic data are important inputs to the spatial distribution of the different depositional elements and their lithofacies in the reservoir model, and directly affect volume calculations, field exploitation strategy, and drainage plan optimizations
