# STUDY OF SEXUAL DIFFERENCES IN DEGENERATIVE ARTICULATE DISEASE AND THE ENTIAL CHANGES OF FOUR OAST EASTEARCHEOLOGICAL COLLECTIONS OF THE NORTH OF SURAMERIC (2350-450 AP)

In this study, the degenerative joint disease (EDD) and the changes in the body (EC) were studied in four osteoarcheological collections from the Colombian Andes (2350-450 AP) and the Peruvian coast (1350-950 AP) to achieve an approximation to the levels of activity and the possible sexual division of labour. A total of 334 individuals over the age of 15 were systematically observed and the frequencies of EAD and CE analyzed and compared using Chi Cuadrado and Fisher's test. In evaluating the frequencies by sex, heterogeneous profiles were obtained, suggesting the execution of some different activities by male and female individuals. Differences were also found according to the environment in which these communities lived. Osteological evidence, added to archaeological and ethnohistorical information, allows us to suggest that, despite the fact that there was a certain sexual division of work, female individuals appear to have participated in physically demanding activities that have traditionally been linked to male sex
