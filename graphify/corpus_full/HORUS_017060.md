# Phosphate-solubilizing bacteria that form biofilms on extraradical hyphae of mycorrhizal forming fungi and their relationship in the plant nutrition of phosphorus.

The potato is the largest non-grain crop in the world, the fourth largest crop of importance for global food security, and today, one of the most expensive products in the family basket due to the high production costs due in part to the prices of fertilizers. Phosphorus (P) is an essential element and one of the most used in the potato crop, representing a high percentage of the cost of fertilisation. It has been proposed the use of soil microorganisms that can potentially interact synergistically for the benefit of P, is the case of fungi forming mycorticorrhizas abuscular (HFMA) and bacteria solubilizadoras de phosphor (BSF), however, its interactions should be studied. This work evaluated the interaction of strains of BSF Pseudomonas fluorescens, and the presence of the foliar potato plants, under two scenarios, in vitro and in vivo.
