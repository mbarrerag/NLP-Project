# Mechanical-thermal modelling oriented to the design of a compacting chamber for densification of residual biomass

Summary The energy transition requires multi-directional efforts to sustainably exploit the largest number of renewable sources available. Of particular interest to countries located in warm regions and with favourable soil conditions results in the use of residual biomass from agricultural crops. This renewable energy source presents the drawback of its low apparent density when it leaves the post-harvest processing systems, requiring densification to achieve an energy density that is attractive in economic terms. This article presents the mechanical-thermal modelling of a briquette compactor shirt for experimental prototype design purposes. The internal and external diameters of the compacting chamber are considered as independent variables; and, the heating time to achieve a temperature in the biomass that produces the activation of the lignin. The simulations were carried out by maintaining constant heating electrical power, the working length of the shirt, the compacting pressure, and the materials, among other geometrical factors. Finally, geometric optimization for the compaction shirt subject to a condition of mechanical resistance and of electrical power of fixed. As a result of the study, the compacting of the best design of the shirt can be achieved
