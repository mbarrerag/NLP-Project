# WITCHAFT AND WEATHER: THE PROBLEM OF MAGICAL CONTROL OF THE WEATHER

The chapter "Witchcraft and Weather: The Problem of Magical Control of the Weather" explores medieval beliefs about the possibility of controlling the climate by magic. The author examines variable attitudes towards this idea, ranging from acceptance to outright condemnation, influenced by the evolution of social structures and belief systems. The concept of climate manipulation is closely linked to demon belief and its supposed ability to influence climatic patterns. The chapter analyzes historical texts, including biblical and written references from early ecclesiastical authorities such as Isidoro of Seville and Augustine, to discuss how these beliefs and the theological implications of attributing such powers to demons. The article also considers the skepticism and criticism of figures such as Agobardo of Lyon, who opposed the idea that humans or demons could control the climate, emphasizing that only God possessed such power. Through this exploration, the article highlights the complexities and changes in medieval perceptions of magic, demons and natural phenomena
