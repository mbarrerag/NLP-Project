# Measurement of the individual benefits of medical treatment from longitudinal hospital data with missing responses not unknowable caused by discharge: Application to study the benefits of pain treatment after spinal fusion

The electronic health records (EHRs) provide valuable resources for longitudinal studies and to understand the risk factors associated with poor clinical outcomes. However, these may not contain complete follow-up, and missing data may not be random, because hospital discharge may depend in part on expected but not recorded clinical outcomes that occur after discharge. This absence of non-ignored data requires appropriate methods of analysis. Here we are interested in measuring and analyzing individual benefits of medical treatment in patients reported in CSR databases. We propose a method for predicting individual benefits which manages missing data due to hospital discharge. The longitudinal clinical response of interest is modelled along with the time spent in the hospital using a joint model of mixed effects, and the individual benefits are predicted by means of a frequency-providing approach: the Bayesian empirical approach. Our approach is illustrated by evaluating the individual benefits of treating pain only in patients who were subjected to spinal fusion surgery. Here we examine the evolution of individual benefits over time by calculating the individual percentiles of the empirical percentiles outcomes
