# QUANTICAL TECHNOLOGIES AND THEIR APPLICATIONS TO METROLOGY

The Colombian nation has the challenge of incorporating quantum technologies into the development of the country, that allows the relationship between academy, productive sector, state and society, is why the initiative of a Center of Excellence in quantum technologies and its applications to metrology seeks to concentrate efforts so that the country can incorporate to the second quantum revolution through the interdisciplinary work between researchers of the National University and national as international entities. The premise is to articulate the academic, research and collaborative work with the sustainable development of the country. The project of Center of Excellence proposes to strengthen a quantum optics laboratory for the generation of compressed states and its use with metrological purposes, developments at the level of research and applications to the quality of water. This objective exhibits a strong synergy between the different actors of the proposal, from the theoretical, experimental and applied research, as well as from social appropriation. The Center seeks to work in a transversal sense for the optimization of the use of the resources, and to try to ensure that beyond the particular interests of the country the Metro. It also seeks to target the educational sector at all levels, the productive sector with the intentionality of generating the quantum context as a favorable scenario for the solution of companies. The aim of this project is to create a network of national and international work, as well as to optimize the computational and experimental capacities of the groups and to seek a strategy of support of 3-6 years. As academic products, it aims to publish at least 5 articles in international journals, 3 works in national and international events, generate a document of the articulation of the Centre with teaching, research and outreach at the University, incorporate the center into a new network of work, creation and consolidation of research seedbeds and make at least 6 social appropriation activities in the quality of water and quantum technologies. Finally, we would like to mention that not having in the Centre can involve more years of lag in the proposed strategic lines and continue promoting a panorama of individual weakened efforts. We hope that the proposal of this Centre will allow the University to be a leader and give guidelines and push to the joint and networked work on these issues and that will contribute and give viability to the country to achieve incorporation into the second quantum revolution
