# ASSESSMENT OF THE WORLD PATRIMONY ECOSYSTEMS: THE CULTUREL COFFEE FIELD OF COLOMBIA

The economic valuation studies of non-market goods applied to the field of culture have a wide and recognised trajectory. These studies have focused mainly on the evaluation of prototypes or well defined cultural events; remaining as an important challenge and less explored the valuation of complex cultural groups that combine elements of material and intangible heritage, public and private goods, referred not to a specific enclave, but to a wider geographical area that works as an ecosystem. On the basis of these premises, we propose the valuation of a particular case study; the Coffee Cultural Landscape (PCC) in Colombia, which was declared a World Heritage in 2011 by UNESCO and is internationally recognized as a tourist destination, thanks to the richness of its landscape; modeled by the human activity and the combination of different elements of tangible cultural heritage (hacids and buildings), as well as the various uses, technologies and idiosyncrasia coffee that belong to the intangible area and therefore, they present a combination of public and private goods on a well defined ecosystem that presents a great tourist attraction of international appeal. The main objective of our work is, therefore, to establish a technical and economical evaluation of the PCC and more innovative design. The second is that they demonstrate the importance of tourism services as a means of enhancing material and intangible heritage; and thirdly, they contribute to the formulation and evaluation of cultural and tourism policy, especially with regard to emerging economies, in the sense that arguments are being made in favour of public subsidies and the promotion of strategies for heritage management and sustainable tourism, among others
