# BIRD SPECIES RICHNESS AND TAXONOMIC COMPOSITION ACROSS THREE COLOMBIAN CITIES: UNDERSTANDING ECOLOGICAL PATTERNS FOR IMPROVING LANDSCAPE MANAGEMENT

Major cities across Northwestern South America represent one of the most densely urbanized landscapes overlapping with one of the most biodiverse regions on Earth. However, ecological studies in this region are limited, making it difficult to improve urban landscape and management practices. In this study, we analyzed changes in species richness and taxonomic composition of bird assemblages across three Colombian cities: Valledupar (0.5 million people, 170 m. a.s.l), Medellín (2.6 million people, 1500 m. a.s.l), and Bogotá (7.4 million people, 2600 m. a.s.l). Surveys were conducted in urban landscapes and surrounding areas, including non-urban systems (i.e., native forests, tree or palm plantations, open areas) and urban systems characterized by different levels of urbanization (i.e., 0'33 %, 34'66 %, 67'100 %). Across all cities, areas with the highest urbanization levels exhibited the lowest bird species richness, while those with the lowest urbanization level exhibited higher or similar values compared to native forests and other non-urban systems. However, the relationship between urbanization and bird taxonomic composition varied among cities. In Medellín, urban areas showed low similarity with native forests, but were similar to open areas. In contrast, Valledupar and Bogotá exhibited relatively high similarity between urban areas and native forests, as well as other non-urban systems. These findings suggest that high urbanization levels represent high environmental constraints for bird diversity within cities, but the coarseness of these constraints varies across other urban and non-urban systems depending on the city. While, in Medellín, urbanization might represent a high limitation for most birds inhabiting forests and some birds inhabiting other non-urban systems, in Valledupar and Bogotá urbanization might represent less limitation for all birds, at least in lower to intermediate urbanization levels. Understanding the role of landscape heterogeneity in bird species assembly is crucial for informing targeted and effective urban planning and management strategies. Thus, our study underscores the importance of contextspecific ecological knowledge for developing focalized solutions to biodiversity conservation in highly heterogeneous urban landscapes
