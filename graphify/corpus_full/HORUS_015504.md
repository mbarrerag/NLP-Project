# CONCEPTUAL DESIGN OF A BIOCHAR MINERAL COMPOSITE FOR IMPROVEMENTS OF SOILS WITH DOLOMITE AND COFFEE HUSK FROM HUILA

Between 30 and 60% of the world's arable soil has a pH below 5.5, characteristic of acidic soils common in tropical areas [1]¿[3]. Excessive soil acidity indicates chemical infertility that accentuates soil weathering [4], affecting crop quality and food security [1]. Since ancient times, the acidity of the soil has been corrected through liming techniques using different simple or compound amendments derived from basic salts to increase the pH [4]¿[8]. Additionally, there exist several lignocellulosic wastes that can be used as raw materials for the obtaining of biochars, one of them is the coffee husk, a material with a high lignin content (41.86%), low humidity content (11,45%), pH between 5 and 6, 90 % organic matter and a high percentages of C, H, O, N and mineral presence of Ca, Mg, P and K, etc, favorable characteristics to soil amendments. Nowadays Huila Department is the main coffee productor in Colombia with 144.895 he of land cultivated, and 74% of the rural population dedicated to coffee growing. 53% of department exportations are from coffee, being Pitalito the principal productor. For that reason, there is a high availability of coffee grain and its residual components such as the husk or parchment. An alternative that allows obtaining soil improvers, taking advantage of the availability and characteristics of coffee husk are the biochar-mineral composite materials (BMC), which positively influence soil properties such as stability, carbon content, and nutrient retention. The incorporation of additives like minerals, lignite, wood ashes [9], or composting materials helps to use nutrients more efficiently, improve nitrogen fixation and reduce nutrient losses [10]¿[12]. The addition of these materials to the biochar can be carried out before, during, or after the pyrolytic process of the biomass [13]¿[15]. It consists of the heat treatment of biomass in absence of oxygen at elevated temperatures. These operating conditions produce a rich carbon material with high alkalinity, a large specific surface area, and physical-biological stability [16]¿[20]. The department of Huila has the largest deposits of dolomitic, marbleized, and white Cretaceous limestone between Neiva and Palermo, with MgO contents up to 34% [3]. Calcareous minerals are exploited in 17% of the 228 mining units of the department, located mainly in the municipalities of Palermo, Santa María, and Neiva [21], [22].No research works have been reported aimed to simulate the process of obtaining BMC from coffee husks and dolomite to establish operating conditions such as the pyrolysis temperature and the proportion of calcined dolomite that generate less environmental impact and greater profitability. Initially, 20 samples of calcareous minerals were taken in seven open-pit quarries located in Palermo, Santa Maria (Huila), and a sample of coffee husk was collected in Neiva (Huila). The minerals were analyzed by geochemical characterization (Inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS)), physiochemical characterization (humidity, density, pH, EC, ash, organic matter), and mineralogic characterization (petrography). While the coffee husk was analyzed by physiochemical characterization (humidity, density, pH, EC, ash, organic matter). Mineral Samples M3 and M6 with dolomitic characteristics were selected to avoid the presence of phytotoxic elements. Their characteristics allowed to propose and simulate a process for the obtention of BMC, using Aspen Plus V10, including grinding and calcination (600-1000°C) of dolomite, coffee husk drying (100°C) and pyrolysis (300- 800°C), and their mixing. Under several scenarios varying pyrolysis temperature and biochar: calcinated dolomite ratio, the simulated process viability was evaluated with environmental indicators applying LCA (gate to gate) using the ReCiPe Midpoint H impact method in openLCA software. Also, financial indicators were assessed (Net present Value NPV, Return on investment ROI)
