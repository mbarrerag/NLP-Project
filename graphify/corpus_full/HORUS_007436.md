# Application of computational modelling and simulation for prediction and cost-time optimization in building projects and construction processes

Traditionally the construction industry has been characterized by its reluctance to use digital technologies and adoption of practices that allow to advance to a culture of innovation and reinvent itself against its poor results, especially in view of its ability to estimate deadlines and budgets. Its recurrent delays and cost-effectiveness of implementing projects, as well as particularly a lag in the adoption of new technologies have not allowed it to capitalize on advances in science and technology applicable to the sector to raise its productivity (WEF, 2016) (Rahman et al., 2020). Another worrying aspect is the way in which the life cycle of the projects is conceived, since they reflect fragmentation (Mossman, 2020) which conditions the development of learning by experience, that is to achieve adaptive changes to the inputs of its digital environment (Witteman, 1997) and to take advantage of the evaluation of data that can serve as a basis for forecasting (CII, 2013 a). s well as a particular lag in the adoption of new technologies, have not allowed it to capitalize on advances in science and technology applied to the sector to increase its productivity (WEF, 2016) (Rahman et al., 2020).
