# Isolated Baseries of the Holy Father's Basements in Medellin-Colombia with capacity to reduce the HEXAVALENT CROMO

In the last decades, however, we have been actively working to reduce the environmental impact generated by the anthropic activities that constantly release toxic components into the environment, generating instability and damage to the health of biological communities. Among the different pollutants, heavy metals are important because of their properties, which make it difficult to degrade or transform into other less toxic compounds. Chromium is one of the metals of the highest interest in the global level because of its use in many industries. Conventional methods that use chromed materials in their processes, not only throw considerable amounts of residues into the environment, but give little account of the fraction of Cr6+ present in certain ecosystems. Bioremediation has been proposed as an economically viable and environmentally sustainable alternative. The purpose of this work was to evaluate the capacity of chromium reduction by bacteria, isolated from a matrix of biosolids from the Waste Water Treatment Plant (PTAR) San Fernando in the city of Medellín-Colombia. Bioremedies were cultivated in Agar Nutritive enriched with different concentrations of Cr6+.
