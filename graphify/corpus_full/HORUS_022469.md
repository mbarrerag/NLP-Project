# Route allocation model in urban transport systems considering user behaviour from a decision-making perspective.

In any city of the world, taxi routes constitute a great source of data for urban studies, given their ability to capture a large proportion of travel between different origins and destinations. This thesis proposes a methodology of real-time behavior data collection in situ of public service drivers (Taxis). A questionnaire was designed with two components: i) Survey of revealed preferences and personality aspects through direct observation of drivers and ii) route information via GPS devices measuring distance and time during travel. Data collection focused on the reaction to travel information and during it, the willingness of drivers to receive information about the network, their behavior when driving and their influence on the choice of a route. Additionally they are proposed and estimated; a hybrid model of discreet choice that integrates the latent variable in relation to the way of driving; prospective models and models of route choice repentance that capture the behavior of drivers in real traffic conditions. The route choice models used are the MNL, C-Logit, PSL and PSCL. The sample of the performance models of the city is performed by the local drivers
