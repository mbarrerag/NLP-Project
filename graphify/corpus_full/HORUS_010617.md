# Determination of allelic and genotypic frequencies of 8 variants in the genes DCK, CDA, ABCB1, GSTT1 and GSTM1 in a sample of Colombian population of three ethnic origins (descendent, Native Americans and mixed Colombian population).

Colombia is a multi-ethnic country where the population with a European mixture (AE) predominated after the discovery of America in 1942. However, a large proportion of the population are Afro-descendent (AD) with 6.8% and inhabit the peaceful and Caribbean coasts of Colombia, plus Amerindians (Am) with 4.4% of the population with more than 80 tribes distributed throughout the territory. In this country, leukaemia is the 5th most prevalent cancer and acute myeloid leukemia has a general incidence of 2 – 3 cases per 100,000 inhabitants. The mortality rate in young patients has also fallen in recent years, but intensive chemotherapy has been associated with higher mortality rates associated with therapy (RM) and relapses. For this condition the treatment is based on cytarabine and anthracyclines and for this reason we wanted to know the distribution of 8 variants that have been associated with changes in the response to these treatments, within the three main populations of our country. We analyze the alleletic and genotypic frequencies of the genes ABCB1, rs104642 (35C>) Allelic and genotypic frequencies of these variants (P=0.05) in all three population groups. Overall, high frequencies for risk genotypes were found among the Afro-descendant population, followed by the Colombian population with mixture and amerindia, establishing different pharmacogenetic profiles for each population in our country. Our results highlight the importance of establishing the pattern of ancestorry of populations analyzed in pharmacogenetic studies, at the same time, reflect the relevance of characterizing the Colombian population characterized by a high level of mixture, to correlate genotypes associated with toxicity when these individuals require some pharmacological therapy
