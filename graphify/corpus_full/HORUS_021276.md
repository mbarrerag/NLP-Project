# Metal-organic structures as solid catalysts for the recovery of essential oils

One of the biggest challenges in materials engineering and green chemistry is the design and preparation of environmentally friendly catalytic systems, which allow the development of clean processes, working at atmospheric pressure, ambient temperature, and which are highly selective[13]. The solid catalysts formed by active metals heterogenized in photoactive solids constitute an interesting field of research, framed within the search for new catalytic materials that lead to the renewal of existing processes, which allow the easy separation of the catalyst, its reuse and that, as far as possible, work under environmental conditions of pressure and temperature or use alternative energy sources such as sunlight [3]. In the search for new hybrid materials applied to oxidation processes, progress has been achieved in the covalent binding of different dioxo-molibden complexes (MoO2) on commercial titanium oxide P-25 and on the medium-term structure (Tio2 mesoporoso by an original route of synthesis [4,5]. Key words: Valorization of essential oils, Organic Metal Structures (MOF), catalysts for epoxidation
