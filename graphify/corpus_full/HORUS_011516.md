# EVALUATION OF THE EFFICIENCY OF THE USE OF COAGULANTS FOR THE REMOVAL OF PHOSPHORUS IN EFFLUENTS.

Phosphorus in wastewater is an important macronutrient, and its discharge in large quantities can favor the eutrophication of lakes and streams.Thus, specific treatments can be used to remove them from effluents.The use of coagulation and flocculation to remove phosphorus has been widely used due to its flexibility and high efficiency, and for this purpose, there are several products available.Therefore, the objective of the present study was to evaluate the efficiency of using aluminum sulfate, polyaluminum chloride (PAC), ferric chloride and tannin coagulants in removing phosphorus from domestic and industrial effluents, using data available in literature.When ferric chloride and aluminum sulfate were used, the median was approximately 90% and the PAC was 80%.The use of tannin resulted in greater variation in efficiency values ​​and lower phosphorus removal (<75%).The average coagulant dosage (mg coagulant.(initial mg TP)-1) was 9.33 mg.mg-1 for PAC;8.09 mg.mg-1 and 7.96 mg.mg-1 for ferric chloride and aluminum sulfate, respectively
