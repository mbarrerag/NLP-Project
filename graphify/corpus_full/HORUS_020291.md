# Impact of financial openness on the duration of banking crises: a survival analysis for the period from 1976 to 2017

During the last decades the world has suffered banking crises that, although less frequent, are each time much more prolonged. At the same time, financial openness has become more important in many of the economies around the world. For its part, within economic research, numerous studies have been developed that seek to identify the determinants of the probability of occurrence of banking crises and their costs, but little attention has been paid to another key dimension of banking crises, namely, their duration. Therefore, this paper aims to assess the impact of financial openness on the duration of banking crises. For this, a survival analysis is perfomed using the Cox semiparametric proportional hazard model taking a database that records 103 episodes of banking crises that took place in 80 countries during the period from 1976 to 2017. The results obtained constitute empirical support for the hypothesis proposed in this document, since the survival analysis carried out concludes that higher levels of financial openness are associated with longer banking crises. Additionally, it is found that this relationship between financial openness and duration of banking crises holds for both developed and developing countries
