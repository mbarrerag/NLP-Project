# Influence of the Indian Ocean on the evolution of ENSO.

Although the main aspects that give rise to El Niño-South Oscillation (ENSO), the dominant mode of climate variability in the year-on-year time scale, are reasonably well understood, predicting the state of ENSO before the boreal spring continues to represent a challenge. Recent research suggests that the interannual variability of the surface temperature of the sea over the Indian Ocean can influence the development of the following year's ENSO as well as its phase transitions, representing a possible additional source of predictability. This research aims to analyze the coupled interaction between the Indian and Pacific oceans that could provide information beyond the spring predictibility barrier. First, an empirical approach is used to study the covariability of the temperature of the subsurface in the two oceans to evaluate the role of the Indian Ocean as precursor to the ENSO. In addition, two possible mechanisms of coupling are investigated. Then, we evaluate the representation of the historical implications of the CMIP6 of the covariability between the two oceans and compare the performance with the metrics of the simple ENSO
