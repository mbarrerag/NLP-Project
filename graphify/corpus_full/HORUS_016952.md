# Morphological and molecular characterization of toxicogenic fungi associated with corn cob in Valle del Cauca, Colombia.

Maize is an important source in human and animal nutrition, and in the production of industrial raw materials. However, the yield and quality of the crop are severely reduced due to various diseases caused by fungi of the genera Fusarium, Aspergillus and Penicillium, which are the main source of contamination of this cereal before and after the harvest, but their importance lies in the ability to produce various mycotoxins that generate a series of biochemical, physiological and pathological changes in humans and animals. The aim of this research was to characterize morphological and molecularly the toxin fungi associated with the macorca of maize in the Valley of Cauca. Visits were made to 25 locations producing maize, collecting the dry and asymptomatic macorcas. The following 50 isolates were obtained, being the most frequent Fusarium (42%), producing the toxigenic fungicose fungi (28%), Penicillium (18%) and Aspergillus (12%). The identification of the isolates was carried out by means of taxonomic and asymptomatic diseases, the inclusion of the region of the ITS, TEF- 18%, and FEF-1 %, and FUS. The results revealed that, of the 50 isolates, 24 amplified by PCR to the detection of AFL, DON, FUM and ZEA, which showed high concentrations, especially of fumonisins, exceeding the maximum levels established by the US FDA, the European Commission of the European Union and the Ministry of Health and Social Protection of Colombia. Likewise, the first report on the presence and production of DON and ZEA by species F. sororula and AFL by A. sydowii in maize is released worldwide. The identification and characterization of these toxogenic fungi and the timely evaluation of mycotoxins allow the development and strengthening of the different strategies for the prevention, control and monitoring of these pollutants that guarantee the quality and safety of maize and derived products intended for human and animal consumption
