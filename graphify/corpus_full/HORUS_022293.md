# The Janus States, the Two Faces of the Constitutional States: A Characterization of Legal Normality and Legal Exceptionality from Foucault's Theory

This article seeks to understand the way in which the process of transformation of constitutional States into Janus States has operated, that is, into States that guarantee rights and freedoms while repressing and exercising violence against the population, as is the case of the Colombian State. It also analyzes the way in which the indiscriminate use of the state of exception influences the repressive drift of States. In that sense, this article seeks to establish the nature of the Janus States and their relationship with the state of exception. To achieve the answer, a characterization of legal normality and exceptionality is elaborated based on Foucault's theory. In the end, it is established that the legal normality of the Janus States is altered by a reduction of freedoms within the power relations that corresponds to the process of exceptionalization of the law
