# MICROBIAN DIVERSITY DURING THE BIOFILTRATION OF H2S AND NH3 IN TRANSITIONAL CONCENTRATION CONDITIONS

Biofiltration is a biotechnology of high efficiency and low cost for the removal of hydrogen sulfide (H2S), ammonia (NH3) and volatile organic compounds emitted in the treatment of waste water. However, in the simultaneous biofiltration of H2S and NH3 it has been reported that, when there are drastic changes in concentration, the removal efficiency of gases decreases due to the inhibition of the biological oxidation of these compounds. Therefore, the objective of this work was to evaluate changes in the microbial community and the oxidation of gases during the biofiltration of H2S and NH3 also decreased under transient conditions of concentration using biofilters packaged with pollinacy compost and cane bagasse.
