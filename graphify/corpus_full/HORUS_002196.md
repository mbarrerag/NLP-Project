# Techno-Economic Analysis of Low-Dimensional Perovskite Solar Cells A Cradle-to-Gate Perspective

The integration of low-dimensional (LD) materials into perovskite solar cell (PSC) architecture has emerged as a strategy to enhance interfacial properties, mitigate degradation, and introduce functional tunability. This study presents a techno-economic analysis of LD-PSCs from a cradle-to-gate perspective, following ISO 15686-5:2017 and International Energy Agency's Task12 report. Using 1 m2 as the functional unit, we evaluate five LD materials: graphene, reduced graphene oxide (rGO), graphene quantum dots (GQDs), molybdenum disulfide (MoS2), and black phosphorus (BP), across different PSC layers of two promising alternative architectures. Inventories are based on experimental synthesis routes, updated with industrial-scale prices. Results show how production costs of LD-materials are affected by raw material and processing requirements, with BP-based LDs, around $15 per gram, and Graphene-based LD-materials remain below $1.17 per gram. Total manufacturing costs of the enhanced LD-PSCs for the two alternatives range from $11.40 to $18.77 per m2, which is slightly higher than PSCs without LDs
