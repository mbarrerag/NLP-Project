# CHANGES IN EQUITY BY PANDEMIA COVID-19, IN STUDENTS OF ODENTHOLOGY AND THEIR FAMILIES

BACKGROUND The pandemic by COVID-19 has challenged the social order, widening gaps of inequality between groups and affecting more virulence to those who have been historically excluded.In this context, to resume activities in the field of study has meant to deal with a huge complexity, which combines psycho-social impacts with difficult situations of social inequality; challenges of remote training; and new requirements of biosecurity. Understanding the inequalities that deepen the pandemic in students and their households, It becomes a challenge to face to implement appropriate measures to mitigate possible inequalities, to face psychosocial impacts and create an environment favorable for the execution of new routines that require professional training. OBJECTIVE Identifying from a perspective of equity changes generated by the COVID-19 pandemic, in psycho-social conditions of students, the remote training and social, cultural and economic capacities of their families in a faculty of dentistry MÉTODOS Exploratory, transversal, quantitative study. Retoma approach to the theoretical capacity (2000). The theoretical sample involved 254 students of the undergraduate, of both sexes and the university and of the regular admission. 64% considered that the training was better before the pandemic. Being a woman, over 23 years of age and having more students is associated with better perception of academic performance. Under worse economic conditions and deficiencies in family network, household obligations make it difficult to fulfill academic activities. 89% of students have a risk of depression (PHQ-9 Scale): 55% moderate risk and 34% high risk. The higher number of enrolments increases, but decreases with strong family network and group membership. Special Admission students worsen economic capacities, risk of overcrowding and need for support. Partnerships with cultural capacities were not found. CONCLUSIONS Affections on economic capacities generated or exacerbated by pandemic increase risk of decreasing number of meals or experiencing home obligations hindering academic activities. Affection in social capacities increases self-perception of poor academic performance, increase risk of experiencing depression and also that home obligations hinder the fulfilment of academic activities
