# INDIGENOUS PLANTS AND PEOPLES OF NOMODE TRANSLATION OF THE AMOUNT NOROSE IN HIGH BLACK RIUM VALUE, COLOMBIA FRONTER AND BRAZIL

The Amazonian region is a privileged scenario for the study of the relationship between humans and plants. Far from being a purely material element, the use of plants is linked to multiple symbolic aspects. In the Amazonian Northwest region, bordering Colombia and Brazil, six indigenous peoples of nomadic tradition have their territories. Botanical records on them cover fifty years. This article offers for the first time a comprehensive review of the data to the present, including the inventory of species, their uses and detailing some symbolic associations. The forms of use of some of these species have introduced slight modifications in the forest, evidence that supports the approaches to the impact of societies on nature. Likewise, they challenge the perception of the nomads of the Amazon forest as societies lacking agriculture
