# BUILDING THE LIVING OF OUR OUR EMPHASE LEARNING FROM MONTHS VAN DER ROHE.

At the beginning of the third decade of the twentieth century, Mies van der Rohe sought to have a positive impact on the anachronism of architecture by referring to the habitation. He considered that all the effort made so far in the construction of housing was a failure, since the buildings did not correspond to the level of the technique nor to the pace of life imposed by his age. Besides emphasizing that great problem that attacks the foundations of architecture as a discipline, also demanded a mission: to realize a contemporary housing. That task would occupy a very important place in his intellectual production during the rest of his life, as demonstrated by the projects carried out and the theoretical postulates legacies. It seems that with the passing of the twentieth century the wise lessons generated by modern architectural culture, whose final thought was more important was precisely the action of inhabiting. We should take more advantage of the so hard reflection on the subject, realized by Mies van der Rohe and his colleagues in the same way. It is necessary to review those lessons, at least consider which of them would apply today; all this with the purpose of bringing to build the housing of our historical age, and not to be. In speaking and writing, we find that he constantly and repetitively put forward his thought and his intellectual referents. The interest of this analysis is to synthesize the postulates of Mies van der Rohe found in his writings and to relate them to the theme of living. To find, after his words, lessons on housing. To understand, between lines, some contributions that could be applied today to obtain a better living. The present analysis is structured in two parts: the first is the first about the lessons of Mies van der Rohe, traced in his writings and in which the conditions imposed to realize the architecture of the time are set forth: to organize living and the new technical means; to find themselves in each of these conditions, different aspects that make up it. And the second part refers to history as a possibility of application in the present. It is a reflection of how Mies' postulates might have some validity and, possibly, some degree of utility to make architecture today
