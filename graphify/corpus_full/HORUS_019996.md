# INTERROGATING THE RACIAL GRAMATIC OF WHITE: TOWARDS A ANALYTICAL WHITE-WARNING IN COLOMBIAN RACIAL ORDER

This article studies the perception of individuals about the color of their skin as an ideological dimension of whitening. Using binary logistic regression models in a national representative sample of the Barometer of the Americas in Colombia, we ask: Is there any association between ethnic-racial categories, skin colour, social status and individual desire to have a lighter skin color? What explains the desire to have a lighter skin color within a racialized society? This study finds that people who self-identify as white are more likely to want to have a lighter skin color than black people, after controlling for skin color. In addition, those with an intermediate skin tone also have a greater desire for whitening. The results show that, for people who self-identify as black in Colombia, bleaching does not seem to constitute a window of escape from historical and structural processes of social marginalization, nor does it constitute a strategy of evasion from practices of racial discrimination
