# DIVERSITY, STRUCTURING AND ENDOGAMIA BY HOMOCIGOSITY SERIES OF COLOMBIAN HAIR OVINS

The populations of Colombian hair sheep (OPC) were founded from cattle brought from the west of Africa by the Spaniards. During their expansion, they were differentiated and adapted to different tropical climatic conditions. For this reason, different lines of OPC (Etiope, Sudan, Wayú and Pelibuey). Despite the importance of these genetic resources, poor management of production records is unknown, the diversity, structuring and levels of endogamy are unknown for current livestock, which makes it difficult to conserve and genetic improvement. OBJETIVEs: To determine some parameters of diversity, structuring and endogamy in the OPCs using middle density genotyping. Blood samples of 205 animals were used in the illumina animal (Ethipes n=49, Sudan n=72, Wayú n=24, Pelibuey n=60) from different regions of the country were used. From the samples DNA extraction was performed, for the genotyping with the ilumin chip Ovine OvineSNPNP50k. The analysis and edition of the genomic information was performed and edition of the software (72, and edition of the analysis and edition of the data was 16149 SNPs were conserved and H observed from 0.35 to 0.37, and H expected from 0.35 to 0.37 were found. In the PCA analysis, the first component explained 10.35 per cent of the variation and the second 6.02 per cent. The first component separated Pelibuey from other sheep of Pelo Criollo, the second separated Sudan from Ethiopians, while Wayú were observed as an intermediate group. The average FST estimated was 0.05, showing a small structuring. Of all NPSs, 16 presented FST values>0.25, of which 47% were intrones, 29% in intergenic regions and 24% in regulatory regions. The genes identified were mostly related to ontologys of biological processes such as tissue morphogenesis
