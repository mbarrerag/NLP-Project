# Seropositivity for bovine viral diarrhea virus and enzootic bovine leukemia in Blanco Orejinegro cattle in Colombia and factors associated with infection

Serological controls for diseases of viral origin in animal production systems and the identification of factors associated with infection are decisive elements for establishing prevention and control measures.The objective of this study was to establish the serological status of Bovine Viral Diarrhea (BVD) and Enzootic Bovine Leukemia (EBL) viruses in Blanco Orejinegro (BON) cattle from Colombia and identify factors associated with seropositivity.498 animals of all age groups and physiological states of the BON breed were selected, from 14 herds located in six regions of Colombia, in which research was carried out to identify possible factors associated with seropositivity against the two viruses.A 4 ml blood sample was taken from each animal to extract plasma and perform indirect ELISA tests to detect antibodies against the two pathogens.A general seropositivity of 27.1% for EBL was obtained, finding factors associated with seropositivity to be the inadequate disposition of placental tissues after the cows gave birth and the failure to perform serological tests on new animals that entered the herd.For BVD, the seropositivity obtained was 50.6%, and the factors associated with seropositivity identified were: history of disease in the herd and use of semen from bulls that are not recognized as free of the infection.We suggest the establishment of control measures considering the factors associated with each viral infection to limit its expansion in BON cattle production systems in Colombia
