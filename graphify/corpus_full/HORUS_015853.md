# SUPERCONDUCTIVITY IN BI2SR1.4(PB)0.6CA2-XYXCU3O10 AND APPLICATIONS OF MAGNETITE IN THE DESIGN OF MAGNETORHEOLOGICAL FLUIDS

One of the discoveries that aroused most interest in the last century was that of superconductivity, found by Kamerlingh Onnes in 1911, three years after he succeeded in liquefying helium for the first time. this new state of matter could be achieved thanks to the fact that temperatures very close to absolute zero could be reached. in our research, The magnetic and electrical properties of the Bi2(Sr1.4Pb0.6)(Ca2-xYx)Cu3O10+d system pellets were studied with x = 0.3, 0.7, and 0.9. Zero field cooling (ZFC) and field cooling (FC) measurements were made, the applied field was 100 Oe on the substitution of Y for Ca, a degradation of the superconducting properties was observed. From the X-ray diffractograms, reflection directions corresponding to the low Tc Bi-2212 phase and the high Tc Bi-2223 phase were observed, since yttrium is a hole acceptor, it contributes additional electrons, which in turn generates a decrease in the concentration of hole carriers, leading to a decrease in Tc and other superconducting properties. It should be clarified that the compound worked with in this article Bi2 (Sr1.4Pb0.6)(Ca2-xYx)Cu3O10+d is a compound with a stoichiometry different from that found in the literature, although the results found are comparable to those found by other authors in similar systems, additionally it is demonstrated that the substitution of Y is responsible for the change in the magnetic behavior of the Bi2(Sr1.4Pb0.6)(Ca2-xYx)Cu3O10+d system. In this way, we can estimate that the magnetic nature of the rare earth ions play an important role in the degradation of the superconducting properties. on the other hand, we have studied magnetorhelogical fluids. A magnetorheological fluid (MRF) is defined as a dispersion of magnetizable particles, which shows a transition from a liquid behavior to a solid behavior through the application of a magnetic field, thanks to this, MRFs are used in the manufacture of shock absorbers, brakes, clutches, valves, among other applications. These fluids act as energy dissipators or storers depending on the applied magnetic field, achieving a variation between energy dissipating or storing behavior in a time of milliseconds, thus FMRs are considered as smart materials. our project aims to design an FMR from a Colombian mining waste mineral, as is the case of magnetite, performing a physicochemical characterization and an in-depth study of the rheological properties that can be obtained from this material when suspended in commercial synthetic oils and by varying the intensity of an external magnetic field, parameters such as particle size, volumetric concentration, deformation, stress, are also modified seeking to meet the necessary properties to be subjected to damping applications especially in motorcycles
