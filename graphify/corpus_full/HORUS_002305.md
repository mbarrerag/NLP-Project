# Slum fictions: how to build a nightcap in the air

This project is an intimate journey towards the streets, sounds and textures of the neighborhood El Perdomo, the place where I grew up and where I learned what it means to live. Through a neighborhood cartography built not with rigid maps, but with affective memories and sensitive traces, I gather the traces that my neighborhood has left in me and in those who inhabit it. In this journey, the cracks of a territory in transformation are present: the silent gentrification that pushes, the farewells of neighbors that leave, the walls that rise while the social fabric is undone, and the growing individual concern that goes away leaving the courtyards and empty corners. In the middle of this landscape that moves, I have understood that perhaps this is also my time to leave. But, what do I take with me? How does a place that begins to stay behind in this transit, embraces me to the idea of the Cambuche, that improvised and self-constructed architecture that sustains the neighborhoods of southern Bogota, made with old doors, broken roofs and affections become a place?
