# USE OF THE THEORY OF DISEASE IN RESEARCH: TRANSLATING THE REQUIREMENTS OF THE THEORIC FRAMEWORK.

ABSTRACT Introduction: Nursing is an emerging science that has been thickening the knowledge of itself with the passage of time. The interest of producing theories that explained the phenomena of interest in the last decades of the twentieth century, was a successful step in the path of becoming science. Nevertheless, fifty (50) years later, the use of the own theories to support the practice and research remains scarce. However, the production of theories that explain more and more phenomena of interest of the discipline grows. The aim of the presentation is to reflect on the importance and benefits of integrating nursing theories for the understanding of the specific phenomena in the research processes. Relevant points The change in the construction of the theory of Nursing passes from the reflections of the own experience, the inspiration in theories of other disciplines and currently includes the generation of theory derived from the research(1). One of the tools that allows to harmonize the theory with the practice or research, is the conceptual system of the Empirical Theoretical theory (S-CTE). This tool makes visible the ability of the theory to increase the understanding of the phenomena of interest of the discipline. The training programmes require learning strategies that offer the possibility of providing true understanding of the meaning and true scope of the various theoretical frameworks that explain nursing phenomena. It is essential that the use of own theory or of any discipline, allows the expansion of the understanding of the phenomenon of interest, integrates itself into the process in a reflective and analytical way and directs the generation of knowledge, thus transcending the mere requirement of proposing a theoretical framework. The example given in the paper demonstrates that the existing Conceptual Models can be a guiding framework even in experimental studies, so that they can be compared to what some authors propose; methodologies that provide a great level of evidence can be developed in conjunction with the testing and expansion of disciplinary theory(3).
