# COMES TO HEAT, A GUMBIAN INVITATION TO BE PART OF THE HEART OF THE HOUSE

This work is framed in the consequences that capitalism and the neoliberal system have generated in humanity and nature, especially in the indigenous communities that live and care for it. In this context, a series of problems are presented about the identities, practices and knowledges that permeate a community of the Misak People or Guambiano within the La María shelter, problems that respond to the productive and destructive forces of the capital. After living and living together four years with several families of this shelter, and moving away from the colonial and western tradition that characterizes anthropology, the author offers us a sense of the social, cultural, economic, educational and daily struggle and resistance of the Guambianas within Colombian society, thus proposing a closer understanding of the ways in which indigenous policies are constructed and apprehended in the heat of the nakchak and the rhythm of the words of their women
