# Flower Induction of Avocado (Persea Americana. Mill) Var. Lorena and its relationship with the water content in the soil, in Palmira, Valle del Cauca, Colombia.

The avocado (American Persian. Mill) is a perennial fruit species cultivated in tropical regions. In perennial fruit trees flowering is the most important stage of the cultivation, in the subtropics temperature is the most determining environmental factor in the induction to flowering, in the tropics where changes in temperature are minimal other environmental factors such as the availability of water have been studied as an induction factor in fruit species. The aim of this study was to determine the relationship between the availability of water in the soil with the floral induction of var avocado. Lorraine. Two experiments were developed in the research center Palmira of Agrosavia, Colombia in semi-controlled conditions using as plant material avocado var. Lorraine. The quantity of latent buds that gave way to flowering and the phenological development of the floral stage at macroscopic level. Two differences were made in the gaseous exchange, curves of response to the light and fluidescence of chlorophyll.
