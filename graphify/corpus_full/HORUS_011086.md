# Decision-making system for the location, dimensioning and optimal dispatch of charging stations for the energy supply of a multimodal electric transport system.

This thesis proposes a decision making system for location, dimensioning and optimal dispatch of charging stations for energy supply of a multimodal electric transport system, which positively impacts mobility, transport and the environment. The system is composed of hybrid motorcycles, electric boats and charging stations with photovoltaic power generation, battery energy backup and connection to the electrical network. The location and dimensioning of charging stations is based on mixed whole linear programming (MILP) which integrates investment costs, maintenance and energy not supplied, demand profiles, energy prices, radiation and binary variables that describe the load and discharge of the energy support. In addition, for the development of the energy dispatch in the charging station, a discrete model of prediction was used and is fixed by means of the quadratic scheduling method (QP for its respective voltages) for the maximumization of the profit of the charging station operator. Such implementation required a simulation software of urban mobility to validate the multimodal electric transport system.
