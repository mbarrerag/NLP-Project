# The ecology of cultural pavilions in Colombia: case studies and recommendations for the design of removable structures with circular focus

The pavilions are temporary constructions designed for the celebration of mass events, such as trade fairs, industrial or cultural, and that are defined by having a definite expiration date. From the middle of the nineteenth century, their ephemeral nature has turned them into spaces of experimentation where historically they have generated technical developments and representations of the culture of each epoch. However, due to their short life cycles, and the entrenched linear model of production, consumption and discard in which they are framed, the dismantling of the pavilions usually leads to the waste of tons of materials and their potential for re-use in contexts with limited access to cultural infrastructure. In the Colombian context, the pavilions are recognized as an opportunity to expand the cultural presence in different contexts, in an affordable and adaptable way.Based on this premise, this study aims to identify strategies aligned with the circular economy, considering their aesthetic aspects and focusing on a deeply local approach, to extend the life cycle of temporary projects.
