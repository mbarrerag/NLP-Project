# Learning to be Indian: Ethnoeducation and the Renewed Forms of Being Indigenous

This document invites reflection on the renewed indigenous subjectivities that flourished in the country within the framework of the multiculturalist turn that the State assumed with the signing of the Political Constitution of 1991. The ethnic claims of the indigenous movements have stressed the homogeneous national project that Colombia wants to build and, consequently, the question for education for ancestral peoples questions the idea of national identity that we have imagined in our recent history. Race, ethnicity and nation triangulate a form of understanding of hegemony. Identity, school and state explain a historical relationship of subordination in which the idea of Indianity is confronted with the imaginary of a mixed country, allowing the hatching of multiple forms of being the other of the nation. The research proposes a historical and ethnographic approach to the spaces where inter-ethnic encounters occur in attention to the development of ethno-educational programmes in the department of Nariño (Colombia); spaces in which the various actors of a society that still believes in the cultural diversity of the country, despite its obstinate fixation to historical forms of exclusion, are described the different forms of being the other forms that have been built in the country (Colombia); spaces in which the various actors of an interpretation are involved in an interpretation of a society that still creates an interpretation rich relationship of subordination in which the idea of being an Indian borders the imaginary of a mixed country, allowing the multiple ways to be the others of the nation. This research was made with an historical and ethical scope to the places where the inter-ethnic partners October around the development of educational programs for indigenous peoples, especially in the department of Nariño (southwestern Colombia). In those places are involved all the actors of a society that still believe in the cultural diversity as an opportunity but, at the same time, practical ways of exclusion since the colonial time.
