# EXPLORATION OF THE GENETIC RESOURCES OF TACSONIAL SUPERSECTION (PASSIFLORA L.) IN PERU ANDES

The Tacsonia supersection (Passiflora, subgenus Passiflora) with 65 species inventiated presents a high potential for its nutritional, medicinal and ornamental use. The species are distributed in the tropical Andes from Bolivia to Venezuela and most of them are characterized by having pendulous flowers with hipantius up to 14 cm of length. The Peruvian Andes is one of the main zones of distribution and richness of the Tacsonia species, and despite this they have been little studied and documented, OBJECTIVE: Study the diversity, distribution and conservation status of the Tacsonia supersection in Peru with information of herbarium, literature and field. The conservation status was established following the criteria and categories of the IUCN in the GeoCAT platform. The geographical distribution and climatic modeling (Bioclima) was represented by the Pacin, Pacin, Inkea. Other ethnobotanical uses are related as a medicinal plant (17%, infusions of leaves) and ornamental due to the striking colour of its flowers (17%). On the other hand, 100% of the species present some degree of threat with 8% Vulnerable (VU), 71% Endangered (EN) and 21% Critically Endangered (CR). The climate modeling established that the average temperature and precipitation of 831 mm/year and 12,3 °C are the preferential parameters for Tacsonia species. The species P. amazonica, P. huamachucoensis and P. trifoliata present atypical climatic ranges according to classification analysis and ACP. Potential climatic distribution allowed to establish three ultra diverse zones (hotspot) in the departments of Amazonas, La Libertad, Cajamarca, Huánuco, Pasco, Junín, Huancavelica and Cusco, with a range of probability of occurrence between 8 and 13 species
