# VACUNATION IN CHILD CANCER: SCOPING REVIEW

The immunosuppression caused by oncological treatments in childhood cancer represents a high risk for infection that are immunopreventable, therefore the most effective strategy known is vaccination. The objective of this review is to integrate the available scientific literature on vaccination in childhood cancer to identify vaccines, types of vaccines, adjustments to vaccination schemes, therapeutic and secondary effects of these. Likewise, to identify trends in research on vaccination in childhood cancer; and to generate recommendations for the practice of care of children with cancer in the vaccination area. Searches were carried out in databases such as PUBMED, Scopus, among others; and search filters were applied that identified articles published in journals indexed in the English, Spanish and Portuguese languages, between the years 2016 and 2021, the articles were read in full text and verified that they met the minimum requirements of report according to the checklists available in the platform THE EQUATOR NETWORK and a flow chart PRISMA with objectives of identification, selection, eligibility and inclusion of the articles, results of the studies, which were verified in accordance with the minimum requirements of report according to the checklists available in the platform THE EQUATOR NETWORK and the platform.
