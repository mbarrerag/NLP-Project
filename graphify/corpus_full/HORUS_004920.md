# Neurosciences and criminal law. Epistemological approach to the evaluation of novel evidence in the criminal procedure system

This paper addresses the discussion regarding the relationship between neurosciences and criminal law, for this purpose the birth of neuroscience as scientific discipline is put in context, identifying its contributions and its relationship -tormentosa- with criminal law, emphasizing the dissertation in guilt as a dogmatic category. Secondly, the discussion is moved to the criminal procedural field, highlighting the case law of the United States in the matter of admissibility of novel evidence, to proceed to critically analyze the criteria set in the Daubert doctrine, and in this way to establish the epistemic and extra epistemic criteria of admissibility under the screening principle of probatory relevance. Finally, the discussion regarding the probative assessment of neuroimágenes according to the theory of probative reasoning, to elaborate criteria, which will serve as epistemic tools of this type of evidence, will be addressed. ry of evidentiary listening, to develop criteria, which will serve as epistemic tools for Judges to carry out an adaptation assessment of this type of evidence
