# SEX, POWER AND SPIRITUALITY IN A COLONIAL MONASTERY. THE FUNK OF THE FACE, POPAYAN, A.S. XVII

Few facts could cause more scandal in colonial society than rumors about the sinful conduct of the cloistered nuns. Because, contrary to what may be thought, there were nuns who, in many ways and in various cloisters in Indias, led acts of indiscipline, disobeyed bishops and judges, and had illicit relations as a challenge to male authority and to professed religious vows. This is the case of the Augustinian nuns of the convent of Our Lady of the Incarnation of Popayán, who were accused of various acts of disabling against themselves and against the cloister, causing obvious tension between the estates, an attempt to murder the person of the bishop, and the execution in patibule of a foreigner. The interrogations and judgments that make up the process show how the rumors about what was happening in the convent exalted the spirits of relatives of the religious, authorities and neighbors, who between 1608 and 1613, became ensconquered in a strong dispute for condemning or defending the Augustinians, and whose outcome was that they were banished to be banished and Quito
