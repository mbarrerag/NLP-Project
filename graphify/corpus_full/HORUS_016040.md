# Clinical-pathological characterization of a series of cases of squamocellular carcinoma of the oral cavity and oropharynx, p16 and p53 determination.

Introduction The squamocellular carcinoma of head and neck is a malignant neoplasia of intermediate prognosis with an increase in the number of cases in recent years. In the oropharyngeal cavity two types are described: those related to the infection of the human papillomavirus (HPV) related to the histological variant baseline and those that do not, which in turn are related to other risk factors such as tobacco use and alcohol consumption, in addition to the conventional histological variant. The carcinoma related to HPV usually has a better prognosis even in advanced stages because it initially does not possess the mutation for the TP53 gene as opposed to the non-PHV that has it. Materials and methods The objective of this project is to find the differences in the expression by immunohistochemistry of a subbrogated marker for infection by VPH (p16) and of the expression of p53, using accepted scales, as well as its association with risk factors and histological variables, in patients with squamocellular carcinoma of the oral cavity and oropharynge of the National University Hospital between the years 2017-2021. Results 50 patients with squashing of scamocellular patients were found in men and in the heterogeneous with some unexpected data such as double marking (p16 and p53)
