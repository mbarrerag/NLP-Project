# Monitoring urban air pollution using low-cost sensor devices

The study of urban air pollution is of fundamental importance in the fields of environmental science and public health. Urban areas are epicentres of various human activities, industrial operations and vehicle traffic, contributing collectively to high concentrations of air pollutants. As the use of LCS devices becomes more frequent in citizen science initiatives, for educational purposes, increasing information and awareness, it is crucial to establish their performance characteristics and metrics of assessment for air pollution monitoring. This thesis focuses on evaluating the performance of low cost sensors (LCS) in monitoring PM2.5 concentrations in urban outdoor environments in Colombia through data mining and machine learning models. The results show that polynomic regression models and artificial neural networks improve the accuracy and accuracy of measurements of the different LCS models used in this study compared to simple linear regression and other machine learning models. Finally, the project aims to demonstrate the applicability of LCS devices to monitor PM2.5 concentration in various modes of transport within the city of Medellín. This research contributes to a broader understanding of the quality of LCS devices’s energy performance and the potential for improving LCS devices
