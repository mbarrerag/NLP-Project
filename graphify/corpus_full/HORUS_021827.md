# Analysis of factors that condition sand production in wells producing hydrocarbons by applying the reliability assessment.

Sand production is a complex geomechanical problem in the hydrocarbon industry that involves rock properties, efforts, change in the pores pressure of the deposit and operational changes in wells. Multiple analytical and numerical deterministic models have been developed to investigate what are the conditions that trigger the beginning of sand production. Based on the reliability assessment, we seek to identify what are the factors that condition the beginning of sand production in hydrocarbon production wells. To this end, through the simulation techniques of Hasofer Lind and Monte Carlo we model as random variables the parameters and geomechanical properties that represent the resistance and the loads of the function of the safety margin derived from analytical models proposed in scientific literature. Our results show that resistance to uncontained compression of the rock, the maximum horizontal effort, the exhaustion of the deposit pressure and drawdown have the greatest influence on the beginning of sand production. The analysis by reliability in wells with production and without sand production suggests that values of the reliability index lower or equal to 2.6 represent a threat of starting sand, greater values 3.0 and greater values at low values
