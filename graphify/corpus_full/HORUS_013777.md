# Porphyromonas gingivalis and Apolipoprotein E genotypes in patients with Alzheimer's disease (first phase)

Alzheimer's disease (EA) is considered the most common form of dementia in old age, whose prevalence is doubled approximately every 5 years after 65 years. Periodontitis is a multifactorial chronic inflammatory disorder that can lead to irreversible damage of the supporting tissues with consequent dental loss. Roles of vascular damage, oxidative stress and neuroinflammation in the pathogeny of EA have been identified, the components found in the brain tissues show similarity with those found in periodontal infections, the infection caused by P. gingivalis seems to induce the appearance of brain lesions. ApoE is a protein of importance for EA and is expressed in hepatocytes, monocytes/macrophages, adiocytes, astrocytes and renal cells; it is encoded by the APOE gene, which has three common alleles, epsilon 2, and 4. APOE4 is considered the most significant genetic risk factor for the development of EA, which causes damage in the hematoencephal barrier facilitating the passage of periodontal microorganisms.
