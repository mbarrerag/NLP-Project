# VISIBILIZING WOMEN. GENDER BODIES IN THE ECONOMIC STORY COURSES OF COLOMBIA

Is there gender bias in teaching? Faced with the growing academic interest in focusing on gender biases in university curricula - syllabos - we analyze the participation of references (literature) written by women in the mandatory courses of Economic History of Colombia to investigate the existence of gender biases in teaching these courses. We address gender biases in two categories: relevance and participation through a methodology that includes the review and elaboration of two databases, one characterizing the courses of economic history of Colombia (profesors, training, topics, etc.) and another that contains the disaggregated history of all the readings that are included in the programs (author(a); year, title, topic, etc.). This information follows the methodology of Hardt et al. (2019) and Colgan (2017) work. From the analysis we conclude that teaching and research in economic history of Colombia reveals biases specific to the economy (Nelson, 1993) and that the change to the awareness on these topics is still slow.
