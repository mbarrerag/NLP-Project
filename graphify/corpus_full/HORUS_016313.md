# REACTION AND PERFORMANCE OF TOMATE MICROINJERTS (SOLANUM SP.) INOCULATED WITH FUSARIUM OXYSPORUM F. SP. LYCOPERSICI (SACC.) SNYDER & HANSEN CAUSESING THE VASCULAR MARCHITATION

The vascular wilting of tomato cultivation (Solanum lycopersicum) caused by Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. lycopersici (Fol), whose habitat is the soil, is a devastating disease that can cause losses up to 100% of fruit production. Among the alternatives of disease management is grafting. In our study the reaction to the disease was determined by measuring the percentage of the incidence and yield of micrografts with the use of three patterns of the type Cherry, IAC412, IAC426 and IAC391, and cups of the commercial varieties Calima and Carguero inoculated with an isolation of F. oxysporum f. sp. lycopersici, breed 2. The results indicated that the combinations of the heterografts IAC391/CAR and IAC412/ Hecoculatomy production were the most promising in terms of average fruit weight, with 59 and 79 g.
