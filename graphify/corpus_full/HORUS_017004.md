# CHAPTER 3 THE TERRENCE OF THE MEAT

From the point of view of the consumer, the degree of satisfaction and therefore the greater or lesser acceptability of a meat cut or meat product is assessed by means of factors such as appearance, purchase price, aroma and taste, ease of preparation, ternity, juice and perceived nutritional value.Although the term palatability is not precise, it can be stated that those characteristics of the meats mentioned above, which are attractive to the eye, to the olfactory and to the palate, contribute equally to that characteristic (Hedrick et al., 1994; Purslow, 2017).The eventual commissioning of the payment of an added value by the plants mentioned, on each animal according to the composition and quality of its channel, will benefit the producers that produce animals with high genetic values for characteristics of economic importance.In addition, among the many factors to compete in the international market of the meat, it is necessary to demonstrate the quality of these meat products, defined by parameters of better food efficiency, better weight and a better quality of the channel. For the consumer, the greater or lower acceptability of a beef cut is considered to be a more competitive. The lack of consistency in the ternity is due not only to the inability to routinely produce properly tender meat, but also to the inability to identify those cattle that have a harder meat and to classify them properly, which would be of great importance. To solve this problem, researchers have directed their efforts to the genetic improvement of cattle destined for consumption, which could solve to some extent the problem of ternity in the cold industry. However, current knowledge indicates that both genetic factors and environmental factors contribute equally to variation in the ternity (Lawrie and Ledward, 2006). Within the same breed or in cross-breeding, genetics control about 30% of the variation of ternity in bovine meats, representing the percentage of heredity of this characteristic. Therefore, around 70% of the variation is also explained by the environmental factors or conditions of breeding, lifting, feeding and manufacturing.
