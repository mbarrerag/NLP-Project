# A REFORM TO JUSTICE TO PROFOUND THE STATE OF LAW AND DEMOCRACY

The commemoration of the thirty years of the Constitution is an ideal scenario to elaborate and formulate the recounts and balances on its advances and failures.In this order of ideas, it is possible to affirm that the institutions and practices developed after the constituent process in 1991 have not ceased to be perceived as novel advances in the framework of constitutionalism in Latin America. However, despite these achievements evidenced from a comparative perspective, there are still many tasks to be done. To mention only some that constantly call attention, it is worth highlighting the problem of political reform, the permanently postponed reform to justice and the irresolved counter-departure of institutional decentralization (Rodríguez and Ruano, 2018, p. 12).Now, however paradoxical it seems, the reform to the justice was one of the factors that motivated the convening of a Constituent Assembly in 1991 (Lemaitre, 2016, p. 8).For this reason, there were not few measures that were ordered in that field of the Constitution that year, like the creation of the Attorney General Office of the Nation or of the Constitutional Court and the establishment of mechanisms of guarantee of rights, in particular the actions, p. 8). The first thesis is political in nature: judicial reform must not be a matter of purely experts, but also of the general public. To paraphrase Clemenceau’s famous phrase, which would have said that war is too serious a matter to be left to the military, we consider judicial reform to be too serious a matter to be left to the judges and lawyers alone. Reflection on the judicial system and its reform is the responsibility of the citizenry, since, although it is a complex, and sometimes technical and boring, issue is also a political issue of utmost importance for democracy and fundamental rights. In the judicial system, not only do people play their rights, but there is also important evidence that without an effective and independent judicial system, it is impossible to consolidate a solid State of law or achieve inclusive economic development.
