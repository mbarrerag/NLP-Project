# Artificial intelligence in engineering education: empowering the human, not replacing it

The emergence of artificial intelligence (AI) in higher education — especially its generative forms, i.e., models capable of producing texts, images or code — has rapidly transformed pedagogical, administrative and evaluative practices in our institutions. We are facing a reconfiguration of the university space that cannot be reduced to the adoption of tools, but requires a critical examination of its epistemic, ethical and pedagogical implications. The generational AI has established a series of practices that, if not constantly monitored, could jeopardize the very essence of education in engineering and other disciplines [1]
