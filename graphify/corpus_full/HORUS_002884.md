# Evaluation of microbial inoculums in the process of fermentation of special cocoas for the mitigation of the cadmium content in grain

Colombian cocoa is recognised by international markets as fine and aroma, a characteristic that has increased its commercial value. Thus, cocoa cultivation has become an increasingly attractive option for farmers and of greater economic importance for the country. However, in some cocoa areas of Colombia the limits of cadmium established in the regulation of the European Union are exceeded, negatively impacting exports. Although different methodologies exist for the extraction of heavy metals, most are not compatible with the cocoa production system or are highly expensive. In response to this problem, this research evaluated the capacity of microbial inoculums to decrease the cadmium content in cocoa grains during the fermentation process. For this purpose, micro-organisms were isolated from the fermentation process and two strains of bacteria and yeast were selected as a process of individual fermentation under field conditions. In addition, the selected products included the appropriate conditions of the production of the organic acid (influent) and the design of the initial pH (3.5; 4.5 and 5.5). In addition, it was observed that at this pH the capture of the metal is favored both for individual strands and for those in mixed culture. However, these strands did not have the capacity to decrease cadmium levels in cocoa beans during the fermentation process. Although the microorganisms obtained and identified in this work provided to be potentially used for the bioremediation of heavy metals, it is necessary to carry out the subsequent studies to define substitutions for their implementation
