# ACCESS TO MEDICINAL PRODUCTS: BETWEEN LOCAL PHARMACEUTICAL SECURITY AND THE CONSTRUCTION OF GLOBAL PUBLIC GOODS

The pandemic has shed the gaps that globalization has created on a model of biomedical innovation and research focused on promoting patent exploitation monopolies, but it also opened the way to discussing a more inclusive and supportive global health agenda, focused on building public goods. This document presents data that support the problem posed, the political and conceptual approaches for its solution, and the elements of a national and international agenda to promote alternatives to biomedical innovation focused on health needs, rather than on expectations of profits from pharmaceutical companies. Among the elements of the agenda, it includes the promotion of the consideration of vaccines and new essentials as a means of building up a global economy, and of building up a new technology, as well as a new technology, a focus on health needs, rather than on the expectations of pharmaceutical companies
