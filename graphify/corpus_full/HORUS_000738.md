# LONG- AND SHORT-TERM REPLENISHING OF MAGMA CHAMBER IN NEVADO DEL RUIZ VOLCANO, COLOMBIA

The Nevado del Ruiz Volcano (NRV, northern Andes, Colombia) displays calc-alkaline affinity from medium to high potassium content. Porphyritic dacitic'andesitic lavas from NRV have diverse plagioclase populations, amphibole breakdown rims, pyroxene corroded cores and magmatic enclaves reflecting magma chamber instability owing to abrupt melt composition changes during crystallization related to fractionation and magma mixing. Analysis of lava flows samples of the NRV eruptive periods shows that crystallization of pyroxene occurs at 1022°C for Pre-Ruiz (1.8 to c. 0.97 Ma), 955°C for the second eruptive period (66 ka to recent) and 930°C for Cisne Volcano Lavas. Newly reported 40Ar/ 39Ar ages together with published data show a moderately good correlation with interpreted NRV evolution stages and indicate a quiescence in magmatic activity from c. 550 to 680 ka. The geochemical, petrographic and isotopic data for NRV indicate that magmatic petrogenesis was controlled by partial melting of a metasomatized mantle wedge with hydrous fluids from the subducted slab. Magmatic evolution was controlled by fractional crystallization and local crustal contamination. The evolution of the recent lava flows was controlled by periodic replenishing of magma producing geochemical variation at the centimetre scale
