# REFORM TO ORDER, ORDER TO FLOW. LONG-TERM LAND POLICY ENFORCEMENT FOR STRUCTURAL CHANGE

In Colombia, land policy has been long-standing: since independence, the delivery of wasteland has been used and, subsequently, the recognition of the extension of the agricultural frontier as a fiscal policy and as a productive policy in agriculture. In the twentieth century, land policy has sought to address, in addition to rural property, some structural issues in the Colombian countryside, such as concentration of land, informality in tenure and poverty associated with land production and use.This work analyses the policy since 1936, when it has addressed the issue of access and distribution of land, as a public policy, seeking to resolve the process of de facto access and clarify title to property.In 1961, the policy of agrarian social reform was strengthened, with Law 135, which, in addition to the objectives of Law 200 of 1936, adds institutional designs3 and mechanisms for the expansion of land owners.
