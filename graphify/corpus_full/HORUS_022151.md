# March patterns and their relationship with basic mobility in children with Charcot-Marie-Tooth.

March patterns and their relationship with the basic mobility in Children with Charcot-Marie-Tooth The disease of Charcot Marie Tooth (CMT) is also known as motor sensory hereditary neuropathy and is the most prevalent neuromuscular disease in children. The CMT equals 30% of pediatric neuromuscular diseases.(1,2) The march is an important activity for the interrelation of the people with whom the basic needs can be performed independently.When the loss of the march is presented, limitations in the activities of daily life are generated and the quality of life of the patients is impacted. In this study we aim to describe the march patterns in patients under 18 years with CMT and determine their relationship with measures of capacity and performance Methodogy: Patient descriptive study of retrospective type, in patients under 18 years with diagnosis of CMT, with computational analysis of march between August 2014 and July 2022, without previous surgeries, with temporary parameters of left limb, with patient measures and performance as are the walking test in 6 minutes and PODCI, performed in the Roosevelt Institute of March and March 2022, the patient's.
