# SPARK GAP BEHAVIOUR IN A COMPACT ELECTROSTATIC FAST IMPULSE CURRENT GENERATOR21ST-25TH NOVEMBER 2005 ¿ SÃO PAULO, BRAZIL

Current impulses produced by lightning are usually divert to ground by surge protective devices (SPD's). Most SPD's are tested with normalized current impulses 8/20μs. However, the effect of high current derivative events such as 100kA/μs or more is not considered. The reason is that such generators are not commercially available. The second reason is that such impulses have a low probability to be present in electrical installations. Nevertheless, nowadays is more and more possible to have such type of events in commercial electric and electronic installations. The reason is that lightning flash is a source of very steeped current impulses. Additionally, man made impulses can be applied to valuable equipment. In this paper, a new version of a very fast impulse generator based on the floating electrodes ¿ FE ¿ principle is presented. This improved version of this novel type of electrostatic generator, called ¿Roman Generator¿, increases the electrostatic energy stored on the complex capacitance established between the floating- and earth electrodes. The focus of present work was to analyse the behaviour of a special FE discharge spark-gap, in which the insulating spark gap properties such as pressure and length can be modified
