# Four Decades of Government's Discourses on Document Quality: An Analysis from National Development Plans (1974-2018)

From a historical perspective, this article studies the evolution of the political discourse on teaching quality and the factors that explain this transformation, based on a critical analysis of the National Development Plans of Colombia produced in the period between 1974 and 2018. This article shows, first, that the political discourse of the NDPs uses the notion of teaching quality as an argument to explain the low quality of education in Colombia. To this extent, in the last 44 years the concept of teaching quality has been transformed in such a way that it has been aligned with the ideas of the free market, that is, the low quality of teachers responds to the lack of incentives, which prevents the market from adjusting and attracting the best candidates to the teaching profession. The analysis finds that the concept of free market, in the teaching labour market, does not solve the problem of quality, given the monopoly that the State of this market has and the social discredit of the profession; moreover, the monopoly of the teaching labour market is not recognized by the State, and therefore, it is not adequately addressed. Consequently, it concludes that there is a tendency for the National Development Plans to position teachers as teachers as responsible for teaching quality
