# Construction of a gene regulation network in response to environmental pollution from the integration of omic data and identification of related biomarkers potentials

The World Health Organization reported in 2021 that air pollution is one of the greatest health risks, mainly due to exposure to fine particulate material (PM2.5.5). This type of pollutant is associated with lung diseases and millions of premature deaths. In Bogotá, peaks of PM2.5 concentration have been recorded in recent years, which are hazardous to health. In this work, the effects of PM2.5 exposure on gene regulation are evaluated. The methodology used consisted of: 1) Differential union analysis in data from ChIP-secq, 2) Differential expression analysis in data from RNA-secq and 3) Differential expression analysis in data from RNA-secq and 3) Construction and analysis of regulatory networks. In addition, a novel approach was used in the construction of regulatory networks based on data from ChIP-secq. With the information obtained, it was possible to identify possible metafirms of genes and biomarkers transcriptional and epigenetics, as well as evidence of the potential of the methodology used. The findings enabled the identification of potential gene meta-signatures and transcriptional and epigenetic biomarkers, highlighting the potential of the proposed methodology.
