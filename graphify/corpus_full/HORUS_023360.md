# DEVELOPMENT OF AN ADULTERANT DETECTION MODEL (STARCH AND CHALK) FOR QUALITY CONTROL OF GARLIC POWDER

Food fraud is considered a global problem that affects the agri-food economy, as well as consumer health and confidence. Reports of spice and condiments fraud have tripled in recent years, since these products are one of the most vulnerable to adulteration at each stage of the production chain. Their general powder form makes the identification of adulterants a more complex process, compared to other foods where this aspect can be easily verified through their organoleptic properties. Garlic powder is a common spice due to its culinary and medicinal properties, which is susceptible to adulteration with compounds such as chalk and starch, since they have physical characteristics similar to this product. Therefore, to ensure the safety of this product, more precise methods are required, such as near infrared spectroscopy (NIR), which allows the components of a product to be determined efficiently compared to conventional methods. The objective of this research was to develop a predictive model based on NIR spectroscopy that identifies the presence of corn starch and white chalk as adulterants in garlic powder. The model was performed by applying partial least squares regression (PLSR) for which 725 samples of garlic powder adulterated at concentrations between 0 and 30% (w/w), 500 samples for calibration and 125 samples for validation were used, coefficient of determination (R2) and root mean square error (RMSE) were established as statistical criteria for model selection. The developed model allows identifying the presence of corn starch and chalk as adulterants in garlic powder (R2= 0.99, RMSE=1.06), which can be useful in the food industry as a quality control mechanism, mainly in the reception of imported products
