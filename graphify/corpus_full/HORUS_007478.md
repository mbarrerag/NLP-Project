# Analysis of the relationship between the Special Indigenous Jurisdiction (JEI) and the Ordinary Jurisdiction in the due process of indigenous communities in Colombia: case of Ethnic Wayuu

This research explores the interaction between indigenous and ordinary jurisdictions in judicial processes involving members of indigenous communities, focusing on how and to what extent the right to due process is guaranteed for these groups. In particular, it focuses on the Wayuu community and highlights the threats to guarantee due process in indigenous contexts. Through the analysis of relevant judgments and the revision of international regulations such as ILO Convention 169 (1989) and the UN Declaration on Indigenous Rights, the urgent need to establish robust regulatory frameworks that protect the fundamental rights of these communities is underlined. This socio-legal research, anchored in the Wayuu worldview and its ancestral law, contributes to the recognition of Colombia as a multi-ethnic and multilingual State that respects cultural diversity. The importance of ensuring compliance with international conventions and treaties is emphasized, promoting inclusion and respect for the rights of ethnic minorities.
