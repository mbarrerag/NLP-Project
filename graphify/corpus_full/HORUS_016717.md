# Prevalence and infection status of endosimbiotes Wolbachia spp. and Rickettsia spp. in Atta cephalotes populations.

The symbion bacteria inhabit a large part of the arthropods in the world, including the ants (Hymenoptera: Formicidae). Some of these endosimbiotes manipulate reproduction, such as the genera Rickettsia and Wolbachia, highlighting themselves in many aspects of the host’s life. Although they have a biotechnological potential in the integrated management of pests, the status of infection and prevalence in the life cycle of high economic impact species such as the Atta cephalotes ant atrium was unknown. A set of specific primers for Rickettsia, Rb gene, and Wolbachia, wsp gene, using an an ant nested PCR with the purpose of detecting these levels of infection in the larva, pupa and adult stages of samples collected in different nests of this ant was evaluated in the Valley of Cauca. We found for a band of 900 pb that infection by Rickettsia (27.7% of the nests) is relatively common compared to Wolbachia’s physiological effect who was not detected in this study.
