# RETICULATED ACANTOMA WITH SEBACEA DIFFERENCE

The reticulated acantoma with sebaceous differentiation (RASD) is a benign anexial tumor described for the first time by Ackerman et al. in 1998. The frequency of occurrence is unknown and usually present in the seventh decade of life, with a slight male predominance and a preferred location in the trunk, head and neck. Clinically it is presented as papules or yellowish, brownish or reddish plaques. Dermatoscopy is usually observed a multicomponent pattern with yellowish and bright curvilinear structures that are anastomous, or yellowish structures with a predominantly squalled appearance, with or without reticular lines of pigment, whiteish reticular lines and atypical vessels. Histologically, a benign tumor lesion characterized by a proliferation of skin cancer was observed with predominantly squamous or with more basaloid focis that fusion of the network of crests associated with mature sebocytes
