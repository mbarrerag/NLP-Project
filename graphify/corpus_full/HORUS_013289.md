# Study of thefts in Pereira through the detection and dynamics of patterns in temporary space networks

This paper presents a spatial-temporal analysis of crime in the city of Pereira, Colombia, through a modeling through complex networks. A network of criminal events is constructed considering each crime as a node and spatial and temporal relations between them as edges. The data used for this study consist of larctos reported to the local police department between 2018 and 2021. The structure of the network is studied by identifying recurring patterns, known as motifs, that describe emerging crime behaviors. To improve the computational efficiency for the detection of motifs, a methodology is proposed that uses the structure of these subgraphs that optimizes the counting within the algorithm. The results are used to identify the areas that have similar spatial-temporal patterns of crime. It was observed that a log-normal distribution of the hurt of people was adequately adjusted to the extent of the network of events, allowing to define the distance in which two events can be related in the space. The results show that the proposed methodology is effective in the identification of motifs that capture the crime-temporal patterns of the hurt. ed that you use the structure of these subgraphs to optimize the count within the algorithm. Clustering algorithms are used on the data to identify areas that have similar spatio-temporal patterns of crime. A log-normal distribution was found to additionally fit the event network breakdown, allowing to define the appropriate distance in which two events can be related in space. The results show that the proposed methodology is effective in identifying motifs that capture spatio-temporal patterns of crime. This study demonstrate the usefulness of network analysis in crime modelling and producing insights that can inform prevention strategies
