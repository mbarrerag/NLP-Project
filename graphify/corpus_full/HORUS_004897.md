# A COMMENTARY ON ¿BREAST-CONSERVING SURGERY IS ASSOCIATED WITH A LOWER INCIDENCE OF SUICIDE AMONG FEMALES WITH BREAST CANCER IN THE UNITED STATES: A POPULATION-BASED RETROSPECTIVE COHORT STUDY¿

titled ¿Breast conserving surgery is associated with a lower incidence of suicide among females with breast cancer in the United States: a population-based retrospective cohort study¿ (1). Using a population-based retrospective cohort, the authors assessed the risk of dying by suicide among breast cancer survivors, comparing breast-conserving surgery (BCS) and mastectomy. This study, which included more than 1 million women, 56.5% with BCS and 36.1% with mastectomy, revealed that patients that had mastectomy showed a significantly higher suicide mortality compared to the general population: standardized mortality rate (SMR) of 1.18 (95% CI 1.05-1.33), while there was no significant difference in the suicide rate between patients with BCS and the general population: SMR 0.92 (95% CI 0.83-1.02). Multivariate Cox analysis revealed that BCS, compared with mastectomy, was associated with a significantly lower risk of suicide in women with breast cancer (95% CI 0.68-0.95; p=0.009)
