# Optimization of antigen-presenting cells for detection and analysis of specific antigen T lymphocytes for immunotherapy purposes.

The immunotherapy of cancer is based on the study of the immunobiology of the disease using cells and molecules of the immune system in order to direct, improve or restore the immunovigilance of the tumors [1]. Based on this, different types of immunotherapy have been developed and among them the therapeutic vaccines against cancer and the adoptive therapy of cells that seek to expand T (LT) antitumourous lymphocytes specific of immunogenic tumor antigens able to control or even eliminate the tumor cells, are promising immunotherapy strategies [2, 3]. An essential aspect for the expansion of antitumorous LT is the correct presentation of the antigen. Despite a long trajectory of research in immunotherapy around how to improve performance, stimulate and efficiently press with antigen the antigen to the antigen presenting cells (PCs) with the aim of expanding efficient antitumorous LT [anti-tumor] in the cancer patient, no consensus has been established on the protocols of obtaining and intervention of the PCA compartment both in vivo and in vitro. Histórico has been explored different types of the PCA [4, 5], types of the PTAs and PTAs have been given and PTAs [incts]. These studies evaluate two types of PCAs: professional and artificial, different viral and/or tumor antigens and various antigen delivery systems to these PCAs in order to examine their ability to expand specific antigen LT for immunotherapy purposes
