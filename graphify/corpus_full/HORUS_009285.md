# ESTABLISHMENT OF AN EFFECTIVE METHOD FOR TRANSITARY TRANSFER OF SIMILARY CELLS TO HTERT-DONTOBLISHMENT

The odontoblasts are known as the most representative cells of the pulp-dentine complex, are highly specialized postmitotic cells, which perform various functions including production, maturation and mineralization of dentin, constitute a first defense and play an important role in the physiopathology of dentinal pain. Therefore, the understanding of biochemical mechanisms related to odontoblasts is key in the research in dentistry. Unfortunately, due to their postmitotic characteristics, their invitro culture is complex and therefore it has been proposed to make use of cells similar to odontoblasts (OLC), cells originated thanks to the differentiation of stem cells of the pulp. After a limit of divisions, these cells will enter into a state of replicative senescence, which can only be evaluated by the process known as pulmonization, caused by the overexpression of the catalytic subunit of the telomerase (hTERT). The number of transfected cells was evaluated again by fluorescence for PFM and, in the case of hTERT, by immunofluorescence using the expressed fusion hemagglutinin label. After transfection with lipofectamine, the expression phenotype was confirmed again by immunofluorescence for PSF and DMP-1. Three independent experiments were performed with 3 replicas each. PEI produced low transfection rates (about 30%) in PLCs but induced severe cytotoxicity. On the other hand, lipofectamine had an efficient cellular transfection with the two plasmids (about 60%), but a large loss of cells with antibiotic selection was found.
