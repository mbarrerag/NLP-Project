# HOW TO REDUCE INLAND EMPTY CONTAINER MOVEMENTS? AN ASSESSMENT OF SHIPPING LINES COLLABORATION AND EMPTY CONTAINER REPOSITIONING STRATEGIES IN A DRY PORT IN SWEDEN.

More than two thirds of the world's trade value is transported by sea in containers. This fact has made international trade highly dependent on container availability, which has lately experienced shortages due to fluctuations in demand and disruptions in global chains. The use of containers means that an empty container (EC) needs to be moved from the place where it was stripped to the place it would be stuffed, so-called empty containers repositioning (ECR). Trade imbalances, global infrastructure disturbances and containers shortages generate mismatches in the supply and demand of containers that augment the need of ECR. Previous research pointed at dry ports as a possible solution to mitigate inland containers imbalances, minimizing EC movements and thus reducing the impacts on cost, energy consumption and emissions. This paper aims at assessing the environmental and economic effects of dry ports under three ECR strategies, i.e., dry-port inland depot, street-turns, and ownership container substitution. Data collection took place at a Swedish dry port case that provided container movements between 2019'2021. A multi-paradigm simulation combined agent-based and discrete-event modelling to represent flows and estimate kilometerstravelled, CO2 emissions and costs resulted from seven ECR strategies combinations. Results showed that ownership container substitution combined with dry-port inland depot has the highest impact in improving ECR metrics, although implementation challenges need to be overcome.
