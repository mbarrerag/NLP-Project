# INTEGRATED LEARNING OF CONTENT AND FOREIGN LANGUAGES WITH APPROACH BASED ON PROJECTS FOR IMPROVING COMMUNICATIVE COMPETITION IN ENGINEERING

The development of certain transversal competences related to effective communication is crucial in higher education, especially in certain areas, such as engineering. In this sense, communicative competence in a foreign language constitutes a basic pillar of higher education in engineering. In these contexts it is very relevant to the implementation, very in vogue today, of active methodologies, which oppose a traditional educational paradigm based on the mere transmission of knowledge. Engineering training requires the construction of a complex know-how, so that competence-based education represents a totally coherent and authentic approach, understood authenticity as the suitability of future engineering professionals also has a prominent place in the real world, once they finish their study period. Currently, graduates in engineering need to have developed an increasingly wide range of competences to carry out their professional work (Riemer, 2007). Among these, the competences related to communication have been developed in general, especially in terms of English language (Kovalyova et al., 2016). One of the most relevant active methodologies for engineering and their training is the learning of Projects (ABP), or PBL). Among many others, effective communication is highlighted in the case of this study, a crucial cross-cutting competence in engineering, especially in a world as globalized as we live in. Another teaching methodology considered active, in this case in the field of language teaching, is the Integrated Learning of Foreign Content and Languages (AICLE), better known for its English acronym, CLIL (Content and Language Integrated Learning), in which theoretical and practical content is taught through a foreign language, and therefore it provides an ideal framework for promoting a fully contextualized cross-cutting and multidisciplinary learning, at a double level, as progress is made in the domain of the discipline field concerned, on the one hand, and, on the other, the corresponding foreign language. And in this globalized world to which has been alluded, the lingua franca is English, so the mastery of this language becomes a competitive requirement for engineering students
