# Evaluation of a strategy for the recovery of fatty acids for the production of diazotropic microbial communities with biofertilizing potential

The current agriculture uses about 100 million tons of nitrogen fertilizers per year to sustain global food demand; the production of these fertilizers and their application in field has caused considerable environmental effects. Moreover, food production is also associated with the production of millions of tons of annual agro-industrial waste. Alternatives of solution for these problems have been proposed based on: the ecological theory of microbial communities, the engineering of bioprocesses and the valorization of agro-industrial waste in order to produce biofertilizers. In this study, our objective was to use the targeted bioprospecting focused on microbiomes, to grow the microbial communities diazotropes. This was achieved by enriching soil samples in bioreactors that supply nitrogen by pumping air and are fed with volatile fatty acids as a source of carbon. The results show that through a process of micro-biobial selection, we can obtain a more microbial community of soil. diazotropha capable of fixing 2.7 times more nitrogen than the starting inoculum. This community is able to grow using volatile fatty acids from residues as a source of carbon. The results show that through a process of micro-based selection can be obtained. The study aim to use direct bioprospecting focused on microbiomes to cultivate diazotrophic microbial communities, bioprocess microbial communities, bioprocess engineering, and valorization of agro-industrial waste to produce biofertilizers. This study aim to use direct bioprospecting focused on microbiomes to cultivate diazotrophic microbial communities. This was acquired by enriching soil synthesized nitrogen fertilizers. The potential of this methodology to cultivate a diazotrophic microbial community with biofertiliizer potential is recent. The simplicity of reactor operation and the ability to operate with carbon sources from agro-industrial waste treatment make its application promising for technologically underdeveloped countries
