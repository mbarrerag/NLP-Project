# MICROFORMS IN DOMINICAN FAMILIES WITH AND WITHOUT OROFACIAL CLEFTS

Cleft lip and/or palate (CLP) is a congenital defect caused by failures originatedduring the fusion of the craniofacial processes that form the primary and secondary palate.The genetic variant can come from the mother, the father or both, and is expressed ormanifested in them with diff erent distinctive facial phenotypic characteristics or anatomicaldiff erences. The microforms are the result of variable genetic expressivity of the orofacialclefts and may result in alterations in various underlying tissues. The only way to determinewhether these phenotypic characteristics are distinctive in this population is to compare itto a population of families unaff ected with the malformation. This case-control study aimsto characterize microforms in Dominican families of children with and without non-syndromic cleft lip and/or palate. Observational case-control cross-sectional study. A total of 145 participantsgrouped in 47 families were evaluated, corresponding to 28 families of children with non-syndromic cleft lip and/or palate and 19 families without a history of CLP. To collect thedata, home visits were made, and the patients underwent an intraoral and extraoralevaluation, a questionnaire about their medical and dental history, intraoral and extraoralphotographic images, and a record of the family descendants through a family tree. Forhypothesis testing, a Chi-square test was performed in IBM SPSS Statistics, version 23. There is a greater number of microforms present in the relatives of children withHLP compared to the control group. The statistically signifi cant microforms were nasalasymmetry (31.7%), deep palatal vault (47.6%) and V-shaped maxillary arch (17.2%),obtaining a p-value of less than 0.05. Microforms are related to the prevalence of CLP and could be a phenotypicindicator of the disease
