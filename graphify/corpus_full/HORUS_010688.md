# Aerosol size distribution and size resolved composition in urban areas of Colombia.

The National Air Quality Strategy of Colombia has established that the actions of prevention, reduction and control of emissions must be focused mainly on emissions of particulate material, PM10 and PM2.5, due to the excesses regarding the air quality guidelines suggested by the World Health Organization and the National Air Quality Standard. In addition to the harmful effects on public health of the population exposed to particles dispersed in the air. Hence, one of the objectives of the Strategy is to promote scientific knowledge to improve the management of air quality at national level. The objective of this thesis is to contribute to the knowledge of the particle size distribution (PSD) of aerosols in urban areas of Colombia, linking local and regional sources of air pollutants and local weather conditions. This study was carried out in two places of Colombia: Bogotá and Palmira (Valle del Cauca). The most active aerosols in the air were investigated using cascade impactors to measure the size of the particles inhaled. The concentration of the number of particles and the distribution of the number of particles was measured in the size of 17m and 10m. In this study, the urban background area showed higher concentrations than the area affected by traffic emissions in Bogotá, and other similar environments reported in the scientific literature. Submicrometric PM1 particles, which can enter the alveolar region of the human respiratory system, were 20.8 ug/m3 in Bogotá and 13.8 ug/m3 in Palmira. The mass size distribution exhibits a bimodal distribution that is equally centered between 0.43 and 1.1 um, and 4.7 and 9.0 um. According to the chemical composition separated by size, the elemental carbon was accumulated in the fine PM2.1 mode in Bogotá at a rate of 72%, compared to 57% in Palmira.
