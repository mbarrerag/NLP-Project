# Cognitive Capitalism and Transhumanism: Political Economy of Human Bioimprovement and the Search for Immortality

Over the last 500 years and, in particular, after the full irruption of capitalism more or less 250 years ago, the scientific, political and economic forces have been given a positive boost to five technological revolutions.In fact, capitalism has provided the society with the necessary conditions to assist the industrial revolution (year 1771), the steam (1829), the electric energy (1875), the oil (1908) and the microchip (1970).Today it offers the necessary conditions to trigger a sixth revolution driven by the transhumanist project.Transhumanism is a philosophical, ideological and political project that defends the overcoming of the biological condition of people through the use of technology.The advocates and promoters of the transhumanist project consider that this sixth revolution would take place in the next two decades (2020 and 2030).In the framework of the proposed research, and after identifying the historical conditions common to the technological revolutions that have occurred during the last 250 years, will be considered the two economic forces that drive the emergence of biotechnological capitalism in the present: the "cognitive capitalism" and the "finance". The result of the loss of reliability of scientific advances and the impossibility for all those who are affected by technological innovations to fully exercise their right to participate in the discussion of their risks and benefits is the "fast science", subject to the impatience of capitalist accumulation. The so-called "industrial secret" - which is fundamental to stimulating and accelerating technological changes - prevents "scientific collectives" from objecting to or testing the scientific benefits and foundations of innovation. Moreover, it leads those who drive such innovations to ignore the ethical, social and political aspects of their work and to move away from public discussion on how their actions contribute to the general interest and the common good. All this because such questions are considered "non-scientific". Ultimately, the valorization of science ceases to be scientific and social, and becomes explained by their simple promise of profitability. What will be the condition and role of public university in this context?
