# HYDROGEOCHEMICAL AND ISOTOPIC CHARACTERIZATION OF THE MIDDLE MAGDALENA VALLEY BASIN

The Middle Magdalena Valley Basin is an important economic development band where a significant percentage of the Colombian GDP is produced. Activities such as agriculture, livestock, oil and gas industry are developed there. Water requirements of these activities are provided by surface water and groundwater. The pollution and unavailability during dry seasons of surface water have turned the groundwater as a reliable source for more than five decades. From the geological point of view the north part of Middle Magdalena basin is a sedimentary sequence that endured drastic environmental transitions: from continental to marine during the Mesozoic and then back to continental in the early Cenozoic. Continental Cenozoic rocks have particular importance due these capacity to storage fresh water, where water withdrawal come from the first meters. Nevertheless, there is not a proper management of the aquifers because the technical and scientific knowledge of this resource is limited. Even though, in this study a huge amount of information from different sources such as private environmental studies, governmental entities and individual owners was compilated and analyzed focused in hydrogeochemical and isotopic records. Also, during the dry and wet seasons in 2020 about 280 water samples were collected (rain, springs, rivers, wetlands and wells from 10 to 350 m depth). Major ions (Ca2+, Mg2+, K+, Na+, Cl¿, ¿ NO3-, NO2- and SO42-) and stables isotopes (2H, 18O) and CFC were analyzed. Compositional Data Analysis (CoDA) techniques were implemented on the hydrochemical data before a multivariate statistical analysis (MSA) based on a hierarchical cluster and a principal component analysis (HCA and PCA). As result, two main clusters were grouped. Those are spatially distributed displaying on the characteristic Stiff diagram (average of the concentrations on each cluster). Less mineralization at the southern zone (where the Cenozoic formation outcrops) with Na+ - HCO3- water type compared with the northern tendency (in the alluvial quaternary formation) with Ca2+ - HCO3- water type. The CoDA-PCA grouped southern cluster to the Cl+, probably as influence of the precipitation which is about twice than in the north. The northern cluster is associated to the bicarbonate dissolution from fragments of igneous rocks transported in the alluvium fan close the eastern Colombian range. Stable isotopes were grouped close to the local meteoric line (built with rainy samples) even the deepest wells. It suggests that recharge by precipitation is the main inlet on the whole Valley. It was confirmed through the age estimation based on CFC-11 and CFC-13 concentrations in water samples collected in the shallow wells (less than 60 m depth) which was less than 30 years
