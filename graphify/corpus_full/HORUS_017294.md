# DETERMINATION OF THE PREFERENCES OF TEMPERATURE AND ENDOSIMBIONTE WOLBACHIA IN A POPULATION OF ALBOPICTUS AEDES OF COLOMBIA

The temperature as an abiotic factor is a parameter that has a considerable impact on the development of the vector insects by its implications at metabolic level, etological and in their vectorial competence. In the last decade, the role of the microbiota and some endosimbiotes involved in the survival or replication of pathogens has also been demonstrated. Aedes albopictus is a recognized vector of several arboviruses such as Chikunguña, ika, yellow fever and dengue in tropical and subtropical regions. In this study the preference of temperature was evaluated and the presence of the Wolbachia endosimbiont in a natural population of A. albopictus from the municipality of Bucaramanga, department of Santander, Colombia. For the development of the study, 400 females of A. albopictus were subjected to tests of temperature preference in a thermocline prototype using 50 individuals per replica. The device is divided into nine cabins with stable temperatures between 21 °C and 34 °C. For the presence of 1 hour in each trial, the Sanbopicus were separated and each patient was associated with a total temperature. The bioinformatics analysis showed that the detected WSP gene haplotypes are related to the wAlb strain belonging to the endosimbiont’s supergroup B. A. albopictus strain. More females infected with the endosimbiont were found for the temperature cabin corresponding to 21-23 °C. This study provides evidence of Wolbachia’s natural infection in A. albopictus from Santander and a preference of the species at specific temperatures for its development. It has been reported in other studies that the putatively detected wAlb strain may decrease the host’s vector capacity for the transmission of arboviruses such as dengue and Chikungunuña. Knowledge of the temperature preferences of this species may serve to make predictions in the ability to adapt to the transformation of its ecosystems and the effect of climate change
