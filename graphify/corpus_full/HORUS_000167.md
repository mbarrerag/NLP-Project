# Are continuous Fenton processes a sustainable alternative for addressing complex wastewater matrices? An environmental study using a life cycle perspective

This study aims to comprehensively understand the environmental issues associated with the continuous treatment of a synthetic dye bath using the Fenton reagent. The bath included Remazol Brilliant Orange 3R dye and chemical auxiliaries typically found in textile effluents (NaCl: 30g/L, Na2CO3: 5g/L, CH3COOH: 1g/L). Chemical auxiliaries increased reagent consumption (iron: +500%, peroxide: +135%) and extended treatment time (+700%). Life cycle analysis from cradle-to-gate concluded that electricity and reagents (NaOH, H2SO4, and H2O2) use were the environmental hotspots of the process due to their significant contribution (95%) to the relative impact across 15 out of 18 midpoint categories evaluated using the ReCiPe method. As the treatment capacity increases from 1.0 to 150m3/day, the carbon footprint of the process decreases from 10 to 3kg CO2-eqv./m3 (70%), while treatment cost falls from 5.9 to 2.1 US$/m3 (64%). At 100m3/day, the process reaches its minimum efficient scale; beyond this capacity, no further environmental improvements occur, and ecological loads may slightly increase. Improvements in the efficiency of electric devices at larger scales decrease electricity's contribution to the carbon footprint from 81% to 49%. The characteristics of the electricity source also impact two critical parameters for the sustainability of the textile industry: carbon and water footprint. A shift to hydropower reduces the carbon footprint by 19% at the expense of a 20% higher water footprint; in contrast, electricity from gas and coal boosts the carbon footprint by 27% and 87%, respectively, and cuts water use by 46%
