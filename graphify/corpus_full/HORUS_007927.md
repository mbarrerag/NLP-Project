# LABOUR LINGUISTIC FAMILY CORRESPONDENCES

The barbecue language family is made up of four languages: the namtrik, spoken in the department of Cauca-Colombia, the awapit, spoken in the southwestern Colombia and the northwestern Ecuador, the tsafiki, spoken in the Ecuadorian Pacíico and the spoken chapala language in the north-western Ecuador. Although there are synchronic works of these languages, there are very few diachronic studies. In 1998, Curnow and Liddicoat conducted the first comparative study of this family in a contemporary typological framework, which manages to establish the existence of the family; however, their study was based on a very limited corpus, composed mainly of partial studies. In this study, we used the comparative method to establish the percentages of cognates shared between the languages of the barbecue family, based on recent data, first hand; we also compared these percentages with those cognados shared between the barbecue languages and other languages of the region, with the aim of revisiting the findings of Curnow and Liddicat (1998) and the internal classification of the barbecue family, in light of new findings
