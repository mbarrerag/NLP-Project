# Depression, anxiety, insomnia, excessive daytime sleepiness and quality of life in subjects with obstructive sleep apnea.

To evaluate the frequency of symptoms of anxiety, depression, insomnia, excessive daytime drowsing and quality of life in men and women who attend the baseline polysomnography and are diagnosed with obstructive sleep apnea (OSA). Descriptive study, cross-section, observational, prospective, including men and women adults over 18 years of age who attended the baseline polysomnography and were diagnosed with AOS. The variables considered were the presence of anxiety, depression, insomnia, excessive daytime drowsiness and quality of life, determined by the following instruments: Zung scale for anxiety, the Athens Scale for insomnia, the scale for depression PHQ-9, the FOS scale10-SV to measure the impact of the quality of life in the daily activities and the Scale of Somnolence of Epworth locally validated. Scenario: It was carried out in a group of adults men and women, diagnosed with AOS by means of baseline polysomography that attended this procedure in the Fundación Suño Vigilana Colombian IPS. Population of study: Sample of the first and study population of men and baseline: to the application of scales it was found that the mean scale of ZUNG was 17 (CI95% 16-18) (Cut value: 10-22 normal, 23-30 mild/moderate, 31-37 severe, >38 extreme), the mean scale of PHQ9 was 5 with (IC95% 4-6) (Cut value: 0-10 normal, 11-14 mild, 15-19 moderate, 20- 27 severe), the mean scale of Athens was 7 with (IC95% 6-8) (Cut value: without insomnia  6), the mean scale of EPWORT was 6 (IC95%5-7) (Cut value: 0-10: normal, 11-15 mild/moderate, 16-24 severe), the mean scale of FOSQ 10 was 18 (IC95% (17.6-18.3), the average of the cut-off value:  19 life quality compromised). The relationship between insomnia in all its forms of presentation and the psychiatric symptoms of the patient as depression, the anxiety, insomnia and quality of life has been demonstrated
