# Partial funding of master's thesis project: Peasant Innovations in Silvipastorile Systems as a strategy for rural development in Montes de María, Colombian Caribbean

The region of Montes de María historically has lived a constant dispute for the territory between illegal actors, conventional livestock, agro-industrial crops and peasant communities, where conflicts of environmental character have been configured, to put at risk the balance of a fragile ecosystem as is the Tropical Dry Forest (Bs-T). Aspects such as the devastating effects of the armed conflict, the ecosystem vulnerability and the low impacts of the presence of the State, have led the peasant communities to generate resistance actions based on their systems of local knowledge. Thus, the Silvipastorile systems (SSP) are configured as a productive alternative that opens the space to generate innovations based on local experiences and the ancestral knowledge of agricultural production and animal husbandry. These exercises of dialogue of collective knowledge, allow to obtain an alternative of rural development of a participatory nature, from below and in an endogenous way. The general objective of this project is to analyze the main innovations of the peasant society in the Montes de María subregion, based on the characterization of these subschemies of the region and to determine these socio-technical innovations. The development of sustainable production projects, a category within which the SSPs are integrated, has been implemented. The leverage of this type of sustainable productive systems by institutionality, allows the participation and appropriation of peasants in the processes of building local and territorial rural development, re-valuing the multiple functions and contributions of the peasant economy in the territory
