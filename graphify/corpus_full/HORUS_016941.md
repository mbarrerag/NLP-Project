# Characterization, conservation and use of Maco [Pouteria lusuma (Ruiz & Pav.) Kuntze] in the Inga and Kamëntšá indigenous communities of Alto Putumayo in Colombia.

The aim of this research was to characterize the morphanatomomy of the tree, fruit and seed of Pouteria lulucuma (maco) in the 22 chagras of the families of the Kamëntšá and Inga indigenous communities with high Putumayo, analyze their distribution in the study area, evaluate knowledge and use in the communities and obtain plants as conservation and promotion strategy. A semistructured survey was carried out on the knowledge of the species and characterization of the communities. The trees have 3 types of cups and branches; height between 6 and 12 meters; slow growth. Leaves with undulating margin and oblanceolated forms, oblong, oblong; acute apex attenuated, obtuse and acutely acuminated and attenuated base. The forms of the floral buttons are of solitary, par and yellowish form. The fruits are a slightly achatada, epicarpous, fleshy and endocarpous. The colour of the mature fruits is considered as yellow-anaran and yellowish. It is important to emphasize its fruit-growing potential in the use of food and medicine through the evaluation as a genetic and ancestral resource that is part of food sovereignty
