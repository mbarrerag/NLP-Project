# Identification of viral sequences and detection of natural infection by Leishmania from meta-transcribed data in a natural population of Lutzomyia longipalpis present in a historical focus of leishmaniasis in Colombia.

Lutzomyia longipalpis is a vector insect widely studied for its relevant role in the transmission of visceral leishmaniasis in America. However, there is little information about viral diversity that can host this species in Colombia. In this study we carried out a metatranscribic analysis to identify viral sequences present in groups of Lu. longipalpis females from the alley Callejón (Ricararte, Cundinamarca), considered a historical focus of leishmaniasis in Colombia. By means of the taxonomic allocation of readings and contigits assembled, we identified readings associated with eight viral families, where the analysis of the protein with RdRp activity allowed to confirm two contigs related to the families of Mononegavirals and Hepelivials. Additionally, analysis of the sequenced data allowed the directed search of transcribes to perform taxonomic identification of the host using the Citochrome taxidas I gene and the detection of the parasite infection by Leishmania infantum by means of the analysis of the kDNA marker in one of the groups studied RAP, which demonstrates technical advantages of the RAT
