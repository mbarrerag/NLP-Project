# CHARACTERIZATION OF THE BACTERIUM MICROBIUM OF RESIDUAL WATER IN VALLEDUPAR CITY, FOR EPIDEMIOLOGICAL FOLLOW-UP AND POOTENTIAL BIOTECHNOLOGIC USE

The residual water plays an important role in the spread of bacteria and antibiotic resistance genes (AMR), mainly due to the selective pressure of antibiotic residues that can spread in natural ecosystems and areas of anthropogenic activity. Currently, there are no measures to effectively reduce microbial and antibiotic emerging pollutants in water bodies and there is not enough information or supervision on the presence of these pollutants at environmental level, which limits the understanding of the evolutionary and ecological processes that lead to the emergence, propagation and transfer of resistance genes between humans, animals and the environment. In this research, we seek to analyze the microbiome and bacterial resistance in samples of wastewater in the city of Valledupar with relevance in health and potential in biotechnology, through microbiological studies such as membrane filtration, cultivation and isolation in selective and differential media, and the determination of the most likely number of fecals, as well as the study of physiochemical parameters, the determination of antibiotics, analysis of metragnomic data and comparative genomics.
