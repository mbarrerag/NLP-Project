# CONSTITUTIONAL AND LEGAL FRAMEWORK FOR THE APPLICATION OF THE TERRITORIC AS AFIRMATIVE ACTION

The term meritocracy contains a paradox: on the one hand, it refers to a criterion for distributing burdens and benefits that is considered legitimate because it is based on the qualities and effort of each person; however, on the other hand, its application implies a certain form of differentiation between the members of a society, since it supposes that some are better than others, and, what is worse, does not seem to correct this situation but rather deepens and perpetuates it. In this order of ideas, meritocracy would imply a tension between two values: on the one hand, the recognition of individual effort as expression of freedom and, on the other, the value of equal treatment vis-à-vis all persons regardless of their status. This tension is reflected in the eventual conflict between two principles recognized in various constitutions of the real world since the end of the eighteenth century. As a historical reference, it can be cited article 6 of the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen promulgated in the framework of the French Revolution in 1789. If, in any way, the application of the meritocratic principle succeeds in taking into account the material living conditions of those who seek to gain access to a benefit or a position, it could also respond to the challenges posed by the application of material equality. One of the mechanisms through which this objective could be achieved is the so-called affirmative action, whose aim is to give effect to the principle of equality in its material dimension and thus eradicate the consequences of social discrimination. Affirmative actions are measures that give preferential treatment in the distribution of certain basic resources to members of groups that have been subjected in the past to a discriminatory regime, and that are concretely implemented in programmes specifically designed to compensate such individuals, as well as to eliminate those effects derived from this disadvantaged situation that would otherwise be perpetuated. (Velasco, 2007: 142)
