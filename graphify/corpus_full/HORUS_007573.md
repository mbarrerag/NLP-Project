# MITIGATION OF CHANGE IN THEOBROMA CACO L. MEASUREMENT OF UREOLITIC MICROBIOTA

The need to take advantage of the country's biodiversity has been highlighted in different scenarios at national level both in academic and political stays. Within this context, the use of microorganisms has proven to be an attractive biotechnology strategy for treating soils contaminated with toxic metals such as cadmium (Cd). The application of ureolytic bacteria that induce the precipitation of carbonates has shown promising results for the bioremediation of the Cd and the decrease of its absorption by different plants. Particularly, the Cd is associated with problems in the cocoa production chain, because its absorption and translocation to cocoa beans, have resulted in the presence of this metal in chocolate samples exceeding the maximum permissible levels. This has generated a negative socioeconomic effect on the cacaoculture plants, mainly from Latin America and the Caribbean, and represents a threat to human health. Thus, it is necessary to develop a strategy that allows the availability of the Cd in cocoa soils to be reduced to the maximum permissible levels.
