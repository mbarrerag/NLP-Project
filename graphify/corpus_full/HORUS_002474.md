# EDAPHIC CHARACTERISTICS AND ORGANIC CARBON IN THE COLOMBIAN ANDES CATENA. ¿WHAT CAN SOIL MICROMORPHOLOGY GIVE US?

Changes in land use dynamics condition its physical and chemical composition, finding significant differences in soil quality between natural covers, those in recovery, and highly anthropized soils (Gonzalez et al, 2024). This research proposes to study edaphic changes through a soil catena, where the geomorphological position and land uses have a close relationship; ¿how this relationship affects the carbon dynamics within the soil profile? For this purpose, four sites located in the mountainous area of the eastern mountain range of the Colombian Andes were selected; the precipitation is 4000 mm/year and mean temperature 15oC, under premontane forest conditions (Espinal, 1992). The soil profiles were classified according to the criteria of the Soil taxonomy (SSS, 2022a) and the IUSS Working Group WRB. (2022). For these soils, a complete morphological description was carried out, the samples from each horizon were subjected to determinations of soil organic carbon (SOC), and physical - chemical properties in accordance with the Soil Survey Laboratory Methods (SSS, 2022b). Preliminary results show a higher SOC content in the lower parts of the slope compared to the higher mountainous areas. These results lead us to consider the use of soil micromorphology to study the processes associated with the accumulation of organic matter in these soils, and to identify the most important pedogenetic processes. The question in this part of the research is ¿What can soil micromorphology give us?
