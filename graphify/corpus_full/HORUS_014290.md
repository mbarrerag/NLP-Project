# Decomposition and respiration in high-andean forest soils at different successal stages.

Respiration and decomposition in soils were evaluated by comparing between two successive stages in high Andean forests. These processes are an important part of the global carbon balance as they are directly linked to carbon emission and sequestration in the soil. However, it is not clear how these processes can be affected by the successional stage due to vegetation properties, such as forest structure and functional composition. We measure respiration with an infrared gas analyzer (IRGA) and use the tea bag index (TBI) to measure the potential decomposition of leaf litter and the stabilization factor. We also measure forest structure and functional composition at community level, as well as moisture, temperature and soil pH. We then use a structural equation model approach to evaluate the cascade of effects of succession to soil processes, which plays the role of succession through the attributes of forest and microclimate.
