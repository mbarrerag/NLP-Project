# Collective social innovation in the protection and recovery of the strategic marine-coster ecosystems of the Biosphere Reserve - Seaflower through community-academy knowledge exchange and social mapping.

The tangible and intangible benefits provided by strategic ecosystems (EE) exceed any economic valuation or monetization. Example of this are sandy beaches, which provide multiple ecosystem services (SE) and are recognized at a global level for their importance for tourism, economy, coastal protection with energy attenuation of waves, storage and transport of sediments and biodiversity habitat (Schuhman and Mahon, 2015).The sand beaches are of vital importance for the communities of the Island of San Andrés, since they are one of the main tourist attractions driving the economy in multiple sectors both formal and informal and community (Prato and Newball, 2016). Identifying the community's knowledge about their ecosystems is extremely important to exercise greater sensitivity and citizen control over the territory and the environmental management decisions.This way, the present project seeks to define through the exchange of knowledge, to generate a mechanism of social innovation that empowers different social sectors of the island of San Andrés to improve knowledge and recognition of its territory.This empowerment is necessary given the high vulnerability of the ocean islands both to extreme climate events and to the impacts generated by the economy and by the economy. The community of the islands of the archipelago, immediate and direct beneficiaries of ecosystem services that provide mangrove forests, grasslands and beaches, are also the main knowledgeable of their dynamics and their historical relationship with them. This is why implementing collective social innovation tools will allow a more comprehensive understanding of the territory, essential for the joint formulation of strategies for the protection and recovery of these ecosystems. The exchange of knowledge, skills and experiences becomes an opportunity to create a network of knowledge about the island territory, involving the vision and perception of different social sectors to build a holistic representation of its true complexity and allows timely decision-making based on scientific, social, economic and cultural information.
