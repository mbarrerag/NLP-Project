# A decent and adequate housing for the recycling population of the city of Bogotá case study: Association of Environmental Waste Pickers A Better Living (ARAUMV)

The access to social housing, in any Latin American country, is given by different plans, programmes and projects that are developed in order to guarantee a fundamental right, the right to decent and adequate housing. The previous ones, are created precisely as a response to a social, economic and cultural problem that prevails throughout the region and is the housing deficit. However, the magnitude is such that no government is able to have a total coverage of decent and adequate housing for each of its inhabitants. The precarious conditions in which millions of people live in the region, are given to some extent, as a result of globalization and a neoliberal economic model. Within all the people who are in a state of vulnerability, there are the waste pickers of their own. The rest have minimal opportunities to live in a decent and adequate housing, nor is it possible to access to a housing solution within the state entities. In Bogotá, only 11.4% of the waste pickers live in a home, which most of the cases, are in a precarious condition. The precarious living conditions experienced by millions of people in the region are, to some extent, a result of globalization and a neoliberal economic model. Along the vulnerable populations are the recycling informal workers. The recycling informal workforce, for the most part, cannot access housing solutions offered by government entities. In Bogotá, only 11.4% of recycling informal workers live in their own holding, which is often in a precarious condition. The majority of those have minimal opportunities to reside in decade and additional housing, and they also face farms in accessing logistics or subsidies, together other things. This is an essential to understand the dependent conditions of the dependent upon occupation, and their also dependent upon labour to take into account the consequences of the occupational disease
