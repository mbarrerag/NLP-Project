# Development of Quorum Sensing inhibitors in Pseudomonas aeruginosa based on chemical constituents of Piper asperiusculum

Pseudomonas aeruginosa is an opportunistic pathogen and causal agent of a wide range of infections. At the clinical level, it is one of the most common gram negative pathogens in infections of intensive care units (ICUs) and is one of the main microorganisms causing nosocomial infections with predisposition to be multi-resistant in immunocompromised patients. The multiresistance of P. aeruginosa has gained importance in the last decade due to the high mortality rates it causes and the high costs associated with medical care. Considering that the problem of resistance to antimicrobial agents (RAMs) continues to spread globally, it is necessary to implement new therapeutic alternatives. Among the control alternatives to reduce the ADR is the inhibition of the system quarum sensing (QS), a mechanism of cellular communication that controls factors of the plants, which control the factors of the flueating and the production of biopellicoses, which favours the survival of the bacteria in the host. The importance of controlling this system lies in controlling the factors of virulence associated with QS, and reducing the evolutionary pressure that are generated by the bacteria. P. asperiusculum species, for which it has been determined that it has the potential to inhibit QS by inhibiting by more than 50% the formation of biofilm in P. aeruginosa and significantly reduce the production of violacein in Chromobacterium violaceum. The present proposal is aimed at seeking QS inhibitors over P. aeruginosa by studying the structure- qualitative activity relationship of chemical constituents from Piper asperiusculum and determining possible mechanisms of action. The first stage of this research includes the phytochemical study directed on P. asperiusculum to isolate and identify chemical constituents with QS inhibitory potential. In the second stage, the effect of bioactive molecules on the formation of biofilm, production of virulence factors and/or in the concentration of autoinductive molecules in P. aeruginosa.
