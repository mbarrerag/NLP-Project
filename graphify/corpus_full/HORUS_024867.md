# THE TUBS OF LAB AND ITS IMPORTANCE IN PLANETARY EXPLORATION.

One of the greatest challenges for manned exploration missions and the establishment of permanent colonies on near-Earth asteroids on the Moon and, in the long term, on Mars is the intense cosmic radiation that will be faced by the first crew members or settlers (During & Cucinetta, 2011). These difficulties are compounded by the intense variations in temperature and the almost constant bombardments of meteorites in these rocky bodies. Thus, research is carried out in search of areas that may be considered as potential shelters for the first settlers and that function as a shield or attenuator to these potential risks. This is why, tubes or caves in lava are considered as a highly viable option. In the land, some of these caves are known to have a history as primitive areas inhabited by humans, some examples are found in Hawaii (Allred & Allred, 1997), the Canary Islands (Aranega, 1995), Kenya (Simons, 1998) or the United States (Waters, 1990 & Roger)
