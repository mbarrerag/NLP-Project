# Evaluation of a low-cost Smart Sensors system for surface runoff monitoring

The monitoring of the superficial runoff, especially in relation to the structures that manage this phenomenon i.e. urban systems of sustainable drainage (SUDS), has experienced a notable interest in the last decade. Low cost sensors have emerged as essential tools for water monitoring and the SWS, being employed by various researchers. These tools, have had a limited application in Colombian research, specifically in the monitoring of SUDS. This is why the research question arises: what are the advantages and disadvantages of the Smart Sensors in the monitoring of the superficial runoff and its applicability in SUDS in almost real time? To address this question, the present research evaluates a Smart sensor system that includes measurements of pH, conductivity and temperature in scenarios such as in laboratory, in-situ sampling and in continuous sampling. The results obtained reveal that the performance (in terms of accuracy and accuracy) of the Smart sensor is comparable to that of a multiparometer commercial in point measurements, with an 80% savings of the cost. However, challenges are identified in the transmission of data in environments with limited connectivity for continuous monitoring scenarios.
