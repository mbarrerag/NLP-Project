# Exploration of hydrogen cyanide variation in yucca leaves and effective disposal strategies

The cassava leaves have significant nutritional potential; however, their high content in cyanogenic glycosides, which are synthesized as a mechanism for defending the plant, release hydrogen cyanide (HCN). This situation poses a challenge for use as a potential food source. This study assessed the variation in HCN content in yucca leaves of seven and five cultivars in two stages of post-siembra development, respectively (seven and nine months). HCN levels varied between 1800 and 2761 mg kg−1 (ppm) at seven months, observing a significant biological variation within cultivars. A reduction in HCN content was observed at nine months, with a maximum decrease of 74% in one cultivars. In addition, treatments involving crushed leaves followed by boiling with NaHCO3 resulted in a 90 % reduction in HCN content. This research highlights the importance of understanding the variability of HCN between cultivars and plant ages, and suggests practical methods to reduce their content, which could convert to the more safe and nutritional leaves
