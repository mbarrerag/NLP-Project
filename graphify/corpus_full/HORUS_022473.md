# Latin America and Postcolonial Theory: Critical Review of a Theoretical Transplant

This article proposes a critical debate on post-colonial thinking that has become widely popular in Latin America over the past few years. On the one hand, it asks whether a worldview that argues based on historical visions really has a consistent geohistorical and comparative foundation; on the other hand, it examines a variety of contradictions of political-ideological nature, reviewing whether, within the framework of an ambiguous postmodernism, a kind of right-wing sister of the classic theory of dependencies that does not hesitate to promote a neo-völkisch ethnocentrism has not emerged. Two proposals are also presented to emerge from the theoretical-methodological dilemma: the world perspective and understanding of the hegemonic-transnational structuring of the post-1940 era and its imperial way of life, without speculating on linear continuities since Columbus. Finally, it aims to contextualize the most recent and drastic manifestation of this last system of power, in the form of the corona-autocratic transnationalism of 2020
