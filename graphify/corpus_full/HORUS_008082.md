# Contributions from the exercise of popular and traditional music in violinists from Cuba and Colombia

This work was born with the idea of finding the musical benefits that the inclusion of popular/traditional music brings with it the process of formation of violinists in Cuba and Colombia, the majority of violinists in these countries who have belonged to institutions or conservatories of academic music have a high technical and musical level so I have always found curious the fact that why so few are those who decide to explore other musical genres different to the academic environment. In this qualitative research we used the semi-structured interview as a method of data collection, counting with the testimonies of Cuban and Colombian violinists, who not only showed us the answer to the main question of the research; but also gave us answers to several incognitas that accompany us to almost all Latin American musicians in our musical diary, as why do we not learn to sell us as artists in our process of formation from the initial and emotional advantages of the violin depending on the genre and the interpreter? why the methodology to teach popular/traditional musical genres in the violin is so scarce? The results found show the improvation, the rhythm, the audative development and the emotional value of the violin? not only provided insights into the main question of the research also shell light on several uncertainties that accompany almost all Latin American musicians in their musical journey. Questions like, “Why don’t we learn to market ourselfs as artists in our training process?” or “Why does the role of the violin differ depending on the gene and the performer?” were added. Additionally, the research explored why the methodology for training popular/traditional musical genders on the violin is so discard. The results recent improvisation, rhythm, audit development, and emotional and personal value as the main advantages observed with the practical of popular/traditional music among Cuban and Colombian violin
