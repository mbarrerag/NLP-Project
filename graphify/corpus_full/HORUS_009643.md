# NEW AND ANTIGUATES

This chapter deals with the geographical dimension of inequalities, in their various facets, dimensions and scales: both interregional and intra-urban inequalities. It addresses issues such as living conditions, situations of poverty, socio-spatial segregation and new territorial expressions of inequality. It is essential to reflect on the genesis of inequalities and to try to understand how inequalities have historically occurred: tensions between inherited situations and contemporary processes (global, national or local). Inequality has been theoretically addressed by multiple authors from various disciplinary fields and social sciences. Tilly (2000) highlights two key concepts for presenting categorical inequalities, that of Weber's "social closure", a concept that describes the form of groups or individuals with power that exclude through various mechanisms, with less power, from the benefits that are generated in society, groups or companies; and Marx's concept of "social exploitation", which basically refers to the lesser value of sale by the work performed by each individual
