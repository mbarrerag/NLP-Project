# Practical reason, criminal law and nonhuman animals. On the legitimacy of the criminalization of animal abuse

This paper offers a partial answer to the following question: is it legitimate to criminalize conduct that causes harm to nonhuman animals? The partial answer is that, if only moral reasons can justify actions and decisions, then only such reasons can account for the justification of the legitimacy of criminalization and punishment of animal abuse in a legal system. The argumentation to arrive at this answer consists of two parts. In the first, two arguments are considered. On the one hand, the semantics and the ontology behind the categories used to formulate the question. And, on the other hand, the ontology and semantics of the categories used around that question in legal systems like the Colombian one. To this, three arguments are followed. (1) A historical conception of the biological taxons is defended. (2) The notions of animal welfare, sentience and mistreatment are examined. And (3) the fundamental status of non-human animals, and animal abuse is examined as a fundamental distinction, as well as a fundamental distinction of the Constitutional Court of Colombia. The argument to arrive at this answer, which is not the alternative, which is the only alternative, which is the only way of achieving the objective of the objective of the Union. a necessary justification relationship between law and morality. As a consequence of that, the justification or legitimacy of legal norms such as those through which a behavior is criminalized is found exclusively on moral reasons
