# Selective photo-Fenton oxidation of glycerol to dihydroxyacetone over magnetite–maghemite catalyst

A magnetic iron oxide was prepared via coprecipitation under controlled pH. Characterization confirmed the formation of magnetite with a spinel structure, which partially transformed into maghemite upon ambient exposure due to surface passivation. The catalytic performance of this material was evaluated in the heterogeneous photo-Fenton reaction for glycerol oxidation using H 2 O 2 in a 0.1 M aqueous glycerol solution at room temperature. Key reaction parameters including glycerol:H 2 O 2 molar ratio, catalyst loading, visible irradiation, and reaction time were assessed. Fenton assays under optimal conditions (1:5 glycerol:H 2 O 2 molar ratio and 5 g/L catalyst loading) yielded a glycerol conversion of 48 % and selectivity of 86 % to dihydroxyacetone after 4 h. Visible-light irradiation increased conversion to 94 % while maintaining 86 % selectivity to dihydroxyacetone, indicating a photocatalytic effect. A color change in the catalyst from brown to black suggested a redox mechanism involving glyceroxy species, reducing maghemite to magnetite. The reaction pathway for dihydroxyacetone selectivity was investigated using in situ DRIFT analysis, employing isopropanol and acetone as probe molecules. The catalyst exhibited low iron leaching (0.23 mg/kg) and stable performance over five consecutive cycles, with magnetic properties enabling complete post-reaction recovery. Under solar irradiation, the system achieved complete conversion of pure glycerol and 66 % conversion of crude glycerol, with selectivity of 94 % and 77 % to dihydroxyacetone, respectively. These findings demonstrate the potential of the photo-Fenton mechanism for glycerol valorization, using a simple, cost-effective, stable, and magnetically recoverable catalyst under low-intensity visible light (1.8 W)
