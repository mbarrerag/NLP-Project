# SURFACE INTEGRITY ASSESSMENT IN RAIL GRINDING FOR DIFFERENT RAIL GRADES AND GENERATED FACETS

Rail grinding is essential for managing rail defects and optimizing wheel-rail interactions. This technique employs strategically positioned grinding stones to remove material from the rail head, creating facets that collectively form the rail head profile. However, process limitations, lack of lubrication, and stone dressing lead to a white etching layer (WEL) and increased surface roughness, potentially impairing post-grinding surface performance. While WEL formation has been well investigated, the impact of rail grinding on facet surface integrity remains unclear. This work characterizes surface integrity across seven facets, including rail gauge corners and part of the rail crown, in three rail grades during field grinding under low traffic conditions and identical parameters. The evaluation includes surface roughness and subsurface microstructure related to WEL. Findings reveal rougher surfaces on softer materials, particularly within rail gauge corner facets. WEL consistently appears in all grades, with a thicker layer in harder materials and within rail gauge corner facets. These insights can enhance bespoke rail grinding processes for various rail grades
