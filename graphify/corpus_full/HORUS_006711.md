# Physics-reported neural networks-based optimization for gas-powered energy systems

The global transition to a sustainable energy paradigm highlights both the persistence of fossil fuels and the boom of renewable energy sources.In this context, the adoption of technologies based on gas turbines emerges as a viable option that minimizes the environmental impact by reducing emissions and operational costs.Although the demand for fossil fuels for electricity generation is expected to increase until 2030, parallel to this event, a considerable investment in renewable energy is foreseen, especially in the solar and wind sectors.Latin America, with a remarkable hydropower capacity of 340,332 MW, is at the forefront of the efforts to mitigate carbon emissions.However, the region faces challenges, including dependence on imported fuels and fragility of its energy infrastructure.In Colombia, climate variability drives energy diversification. The Commission of Regulation of Energy and Gas (CREG) aims to optimize gas transport, aware of the temporary limitations of current reserves, estimated in only seven years. The linear model traditionally used by CREG, although effective for certain purposes, does not consider critical factors such as the variations of pressure and the role of the compression stations, revealing the need for a non-linear optimisation model (NOPT) and/NPO.
