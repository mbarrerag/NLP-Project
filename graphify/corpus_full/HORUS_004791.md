# Public policy narratives on education and disability in a territory that is affected by armed conflict

This thesis is a personal, collective, ethical, political and sense effort that seeks to claim the voices of seventeen people related to the education of dislocated children in Caguán (Colombia), to generate mobilization processes that manage to liberate and transform sentipensaries in this regard. My purpose as author of this study was to explore and re-construct, from their hand, the narratives and experiences that they have on education and disability in the municipality of San Vicente del Caguán, always trying to put in the center the voices of children and youth, mothers, fathers, teachers and teachers who cohabit the territory. From a critical approach that draws from a territorial and decolonial perspective, I wanted to recognize their stories deeply based on a primarily narrative methodology where, starting from the conversational interactionism, we walk, appropriate, we challenge and re-signify the senses and conceptions about disability and education that circulate in sanvicent lands (especially, with the intention of producing and sustaining reflections that contribute to the spaces of decision on education and disability that take place in San Vicente del Caguán, mothers, the fathers, and the teachers who cohabitate in San Vicente del Caguán. Assuming a situation that is based on, both, a territorial and decolonial perspective, I wanted to fully collect their accounts on that matter using the approach of conversational interactionism, with which we walked, applied, questioned, and re-designated the means and understandings about disability and education that tend to circulate within the region. All with the intent to where critical reflections that can contribute to decision-making scenarios in San Vicente del Caguán, especially in political, historical, social, and culturally re-launching spaces
