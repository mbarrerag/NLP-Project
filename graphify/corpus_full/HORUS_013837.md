# Participatory construction of aquaponic systems for food security and sovereignty in the Kogui Maruámake reserve of San Jose, Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta

The Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta, known as the heart of the world, is the ancestral territory of the indigenous communities Kogui, Arahuaca, Wiwa and Kankuamo. Food autonomy to self-management the systems of local production and social welfare is one of the main characteristics of these communities; however, the loss of their territories as a result of the armed conflict in the country, the global climate change and the loss of biodiversity have affected significantly the production of food in these communities.For example, the Kogui ethnic group has used verticality in the ecosystems to plant a variety of crops, diversifying their diet. Therefore, the monocultures of cane, coffee, yuca and banana dominate them today, being the main basis of the diet. The protein in the diet of the Kogui is scarce, who despite having woolly produced, porcine and birds, not native, they all use them mainly for sale and trade and not for consumption. The aim is to ensure that through this initiative a dialogue of knowledge can be conducted between academia and the community through the technicalization of these systems and the ancestral knowledge of the communities regarding their preferences, culture, traditions and existing food systems of each community, respecting their food autonomy, self-management of local production systems and social welfare
