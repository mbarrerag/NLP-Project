# Visual art and alterity in Triptych of infamy, by Pablo Montoya

This thesis aims to reveal the way in which in Tríptico de la infamia, by Pablo Montoya, the visual art and the problem of alterity are articulated in the reflection on a certain seductive conception of the role of historical fiction. This study is organized in three chapters, which correspond to each part of the Triptych: “Le Moyne”, “Dubois” and “De Bry”, and the analysis revolves around three questions: How does the problem of alterity present itself? How does the problem of alterity arise in the visual art in each part of the novel? And why is the role of the artist in the construction of historical memory in the three stories that make up the novel? The reading here on the image of the infamy is that it is a novel in which the artist (paintor, etching, writer, etc.) puts in the position of the historical processes of his time in which he is victim and witness, plays a fundamental role in the construction of the art of the other facts that, in which the infamy, is in danger of fading, the history of the history. is the problem of otherness presented? How is the problem of otherness posed in visual art in each of the parts of the novel? and Why is the role of the artist important in the construction of historical memory in the three stories that make up the novel? The reading we propose here of Triptych of Infamy is that it is a novel in which the artist (painter, engraver, write), as a subject towards the historical processes of his time—of which he is both a victim and a Witness—plays a fundamental role in the construction of memory not that, otherwise, run the risk of manufacturing under the weight of official history.
