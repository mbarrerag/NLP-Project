# Inhabiting modes: community-based adaptations as a climate governance tool for urban resilience

The effects of climate change are undeniable and increasingly evident. In the face of them, numerous summits and meetings have been carried out and have resulted in the formulation and adoption of climate policies and strategies in the countries participating in them. However, it has also been proven that these measures are not usually effective in the short term and many remain unimplemented. Despite this - and perhaps even because of this - different communities of very diverse contexts have taken action on their own to adapt their habitat to the new conditions that face climate change as a survival strategy. Linked to this, it has also been determined that climate change does not affect in the same way all geographical and socio-economic contexts, so that they are usually the most precarious habitats socio-economically the most vulnerable to the consequences of climate change because they are generally located in places exposed to the phenomena that it generates. The present research work addresses the problem of adapting to climate change from this perspective based on specific contexts and community solutions, so it takes as a case study an example that brings together these factors and from where the concern arises to get the actions undertaken in the informal way is based
