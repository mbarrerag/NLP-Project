# Optimization of the Operation of the Photovoltaic Generation System and Demand of the Departmental Hospital of San Andrés Islas

This work focuses on optimizing the operation of the photovoltaic generation system and meeting the energy demand of the Departmental Hospital of San Andrés Islas. Initially, an energy audit was carried out to evaluate the installed capacity and energy demand of the hospital, then with a clustering approach in order to identify consumption patterns, correlations, and efficient consumption. A correlation model was also used to forecast the hospital's future energy demand. Pilot designs and improved proposals for photovoltaic systems were developed to meet the hospital's energy needs, as well as suggested actions to improve energy efficiency, including efficient consumption, non-intrusive meters and the implementation of an Energy Management System (EMS). The San Andrés Hospital has a considerable annual energy demand of 171,520 kWh, which motivates the installation of a photovoltaic system to reduce its dependence on fossil fuels. The generation potential was evaluated through simulations, concluding in a capacity of 278.6 kWp of photovoltaic energy. The results showed high consumption during the day with peaks at peak times. In addition, it has identified areas for improvement in lighting, air-conditioning, and medical equipment. The implementation of the photovoltaic system is presented as a viable solution to reduce dependence on fossil fuels and meet the hospital's energy demand. In addition, the consideration of further actions, such as the implementation of an EMS, could maximize energy savings and reduce the hospital's carbon footprint
