# Hydrological and environmental assessment of Sustainable Urban Drainage Systems (SuDS) case of study campus of Universidad Nacional de Colombia-Bogotá

The increase of impermeable surfaces on urban areas deteriorates urban runoff quality and quantity. In the last decades, the use of sustainable urban drainage systems (SuDS) has been proposed to mitigate these issues in Colombia for public areas. However, there is not a guideline for selecting the location/typology of SuDS in private areas and their impact on the sewer system. The Bogotá campus of Universidad Nacional de Colombia is selected as a representative area of the city given its similarities in land uses and coverages to assess the hydrological and environmental impact of SuDS using urban runoff models and monitoring data. The methodology is divided in four stages [1] Identify SuDS typologies that could be implemented in the campus based on physical characteristics (e.g. available space for the structures, soil type, runoff quality). [2] An automated monitoring system is designed for measuring hydrometeorological parameters on a real-time basis using internet of things (IoT). [3] An urban rainfall-runoff model is developed to simulate flow rate by a catchment within the campus. [4] Finally, the hydrological and environmental impacts of the implementing SuDS are estimated creating different SuDS scenarios on the campus through the urban rainfall-runoff model. The results show that the use of geographic information systems and remote sensing products produce a good set of input parameters of rainfall-runoff models based on physical characteristics in the campus. SWMM estimates the rain events nicely and reproduces the attenuation effect of implementing SuDS typologies such as green roofs
