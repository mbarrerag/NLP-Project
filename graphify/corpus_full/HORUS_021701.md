# Impulsive nonlinear MPC with application to oncolytic virus therapy

Oncolytic virus therapy is an experimental cancer therapy that uses genetically modified viruses to target tumor cells. Recently, mathematical models have been developed to describe the interactions between oncolytic viruses and tumor cells. These models advance our understanding of the outcomes of therapy and provide an in silico platform to design candidate protocols. Motivated by the urgent need to improve the outcomes of this promising therapy, this study applies nonlinear predictive control theory to tune viral doses. The proposed control strategy is designed using a published model of oncolytic adenovirus therapy where the model parameters have been identified by fitting the model to experimental data from five nude mice. The proposed model predictive controller (MPC) determines the optimal viral loads to reduce the number of tumor cells to a therapeutic target within a sensible time. Moreover, this MPC delivers personalized therapy which exhibits some robustness to parameter uncertainty
