# THE MASCULINIC DEBER-DERECTION OF CASTIGATION AND THE HOUSE OF RECOGIDS AND THE CARTER OF INDIA CARDAGE IN 1789

This paper aims to outline one of the many civil initiatives to build prisons in the Viceroyalty of the New Kingdom of Granada at the end of the 18th century and the beginning of the 19th century, which reveals the response of the neighbours and even of the ecclesiastical authorities to the worrying security conditions in their territories. Focused on the house of pick-ups and prison of the divorce of Cartagena de Indias in 1789, it addresses the continuous pressures that the locals, as vassals of the king, exerted towards the Crown to build spaces of imprisonment and punishment for the vagrants, malevolents and women disorganized, as well as to appoint mayors of the pedanaries. The documents analyzed were generated at a key moment of implementation of the Borbonic reforms, which sought to monopolize the use of force, civilize interpersonal violence and restore to the Crown the power delegated to the corporations during the period of the Austrians
