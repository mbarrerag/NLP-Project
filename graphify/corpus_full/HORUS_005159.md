# Adolescent Citizenship and Participation Practices: Mediation of Parental Support for Self-Government and Life Skills

Abstract The general objective of the research was to establish the relationship between parenting practices and citizen participation in adolescents, mediated by parental support for autonomy and life skills. Previous research indicates that parental support for autonomy is related to the development of political behaviors in adolescents and that life skills can predict civic participation. In Cundinamarca, studies of citizen participation in youth have been carried out, but they do not investigate family aspects, such as parenting practices. A mixed study was proposed, through the modeling of structural equations and in-depth interviews. 298 parents and their adolescent children, inhabitants of three municipalities of Cundinamarca, participated. The results show that there is a significant relationship between the promotion of life skills (assertive communication, critical thinking and empathy) and parenting practices (communication), with greater adolescent citizen participation, especially in community impact actions. This allows us to point out the relevance of family variables in the study of civic behaviors
