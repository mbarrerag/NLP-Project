# An integrative look at untechnified pig farming in the Colombian Caribbean: health, wildlife and human communities.

The origin of the domestic pig that is known today has been discussed widely, however, up to the moment they refer to its oldest ancestors as His fereus scrofa coming from the north of the continent European towards the Baltic Sea, Its mediterraneus, coming from genetic variations of the previous one, as it was internalized towards the Mediterranean and finally His Vitatus native to Asia, South China and India. Since then, the species has been transformed by the Eurasian history, nourishing and molding the feudo-vasaltatic relations, until reaching the American continent through the colonizing yoke of an empire in frank decline. However, in the subsequent centuries, this animal has become a colonial symbol to become a means of subsistence within the growing rural population that develops outside the urban centers. The use of these animals took a course towards the technification, the economic activity and the above mentioned. The health effects of this economic activity are explored in addition to the potential zoonotic risks of these animals on some protected areas and wildlife through an integrative methodology that also seeks to generate analysis tools limited to geographical space. Finally, through the compilation of a series of associated literature, the design of a strategy is proposed that seeks, through social work and the construction of community bonds, to provide tools for the adoption of productive strategies that reduce the effects of the untechnified breeding of pigs on the environment and the human communities found in the previous chapters. Thus, this work presents a series of results and theoretical elements that, when integrated converge and become important tools for the decision-making on the intervention of customs and forms of subsistence that support the life of the Colombian Caribbean population
