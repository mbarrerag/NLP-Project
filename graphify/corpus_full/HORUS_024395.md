# THE LAB TUNELS REGISTRATED IN MARTH AND THE LONE AS REFUGIAL POTENTIALS FOR FUTURE SPACE MISSIONS.

The lava tunnels are defined as underground ducts formed during effusive volcanic eruptions, with the presence of lava, very hot and fluid, on predominantly soft slopes. Although these structures are almost exclusive of basaltic lavas, they have also been recorded in vesicular lava characteristics of ancesitic-basaltic composition in Costa Rica. Although in some areas of Colombia old lavas of basaltic and and anesitic basaltic composition have been documented, there are no records of hollows of lava. Studies have determined that lava tubes can be formed as a response to five main mechanisms: (1) formation of a solid roof on the lava flow; (2) progressive enlargement of marginal portions of lava solidified that allow the development of arched roofs on the lava flow; (3) aggregation of mobile fragments (usually created, or otherwise, of a few structures) of bark that are found in the ducts, which are found to be mainly solidified. smaller scale, with structures of the Galapagos Islands on Earth. The similarity between these two volcanic zones offers the possibility of addressing the study of Martian lava tunnels in terms of stability and favorability as potential refuges, from a terrestrial perspective
