# Emotion, representations and fiction

Does fictional works, such as novels and films, have philosophical value? And if so, how is it that something seemingly false can give us real knowledge about the real world? I tend to think that good fiction, like good philosophy, has relevant and true things to say about reality. However, it is common to find in philosophical discussions about fiction an analytical correspondence between this and falsehood, and if this is the starting point, to deny philosophical value to fiction is, as the least, reasonable, and in the worst of cases, inevitable, since, under this premise, if we give the benefit of doubt to fiction we risk to allow it to deceive us. However, I consider that this correspondence between fiction and the false is questionable, not to say wrong. I would defend that association between falsehood and fiction is a problem inherent to our concept of fiction itself, a concept deeply linked to the way in which part of analytical philosophy understands cognition and its relationship with reality, and an insufficient concept to understand the nature of the narrative arts at the philosophical level.
