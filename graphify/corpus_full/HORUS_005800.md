# What has happened to the Pope of the broken CEMETRY? BIOARCHAEOLOGICAL EVIDENCE OF VIOLENCE DURING THE TRANSITION BETWEEN THE MIDDLE AND LATE FORMATIVE (500-400 BC) IN THE PERUVIAN NORTH-CENTRAL COST.

This study addresses violence during the transition between the Middle and Late Formation in the Central Andes, a critical period of political disintegration at the regional level, of hypothetical population pressure and reorganization of belief systems, poorly known from a bioarcheological point of view. Our aim is to understand the nature of violence and its associated factors, based on a detailed analysis of skeletal traumas in 67 well-preserved individuals (20 adolescents and adults and 47 subadults) recovered from the Quebrada Chupacigarro cemetery (500-400 BC, Supe Middle Valley, Northern Coast of Peru).The prevalence of trauma was recorded according to sex, age, anatomical location, mechanism (contusus, cutting, mixed, etc.), temporality (before death and perimortem) and manner (inflicted or accidental), to detect patterns and potential causes.The results show a high prevalence of traumatism, approximately 80% of adolescents and adults (16/20) perished as a result of trauma inflicted, with injury patterns that suggest repetitive interpersonal violence events.The perimortem injuries in the bones of the skull are the face and the body
