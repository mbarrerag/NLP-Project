# Determination of the Dietary Habits of Anopheles Darlingi from Two Indigenous Communities of the Colombian Amazon

Malaria is a disease caused by parasites of the genus Plasmodium, transmitted to the human being through the bite of female mosquitoes of the genus Anopheles. It is considered the disease of the greatest impact on public health among vector-borne diseases (ETV). Tropical and subtropical countries have environmental conditions conducive to the proliferation of the vector and efficient transmission of the infection, which makes some of these regions endemic to malaria. This situation, coupled with limited resources for the development of public policies for the control of infectious diseases in developing countries, represents an important challenge at the level of health policies. Although the implementation of therapeutic strategies, such as the development of medicines, has contributed significantly to reducing the burden of malaria, these initiatives have not been sufficient to stop the transmission of parasites in humans. It is essential to focus the efforts on prevention schemes during the asymptomatic stage of the disease, with the aim of interrupting critical phases of the parasite life cycle, such as the sexual phase in the definitive host (insect).
