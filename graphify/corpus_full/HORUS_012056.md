# • A LOT OR A LITTLE STATE?

This article examines the characteristics of institutional spatiality in the peripheries of Colombia and its impact on the development of armed conflict, recognizing that there are certain social, political and economic realities that determine the geography of the conflict in the country. For this purpose, a bibliographic review of conceptual aspects is made, followed by some theses that have emerged around the idea of institutions as an instrument of `conquering the State in peripheral spaces, to end with a look at the actions of the Colombian government in response to this theory; all of this accompanied by a cartography of the institutional presence and the Colombian armed conflict. Finally, it is found that the institutional presence does not represent a support of the political system in the fulfilment of rights, but there are no guarantees for officials in the performance of their functions, among other conclusions.
