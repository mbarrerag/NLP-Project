# Evaluation of cloud-based real-time simulation of smart grids

The so-called power transition faces major challenges in terms of decentralization, decarbonisation, digitisation and democratization of electrical systems. To address them, industry and academia have proposed solutions that generally require optimal coordination between the electrical network and information and communications technology systems. Research of these solutions depends on realistic test environments, so real-time digital simulators have become very popular in laboratories. On the other hand, cloud computing has become a technological enabler for the electrical sector. However, cloud technologies are not usually used for workloads with real-time restrictions. This thesis aims to evaluate a proposed cloud infrastructure, as a compute tool for serving digital power systems twins. In this thesis is implemented a private cloud based on a Kubernetes cluster, which serves as a laboratory for cloud native applications that interact with the simulation systems of a real-time simulation framework.
