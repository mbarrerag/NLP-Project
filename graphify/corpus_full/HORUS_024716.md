# Production and physical properties of new complex perovskits of the type

The crystalline structures of the new complex perovskits that make up the RAMOx family (R = La, Nd, Sm, Eu; A = Sr, Bi; M = Ti, Mn, Fe) differ in their properties because the elements that make them, when coupled, create bonds that despite crystallizing in the same spatial group Pnma or Pbnm (# 62) present slight changes originating from the magnetic, electrical, field, and ion radio characteristics and from the ion or covalent bond of the different chemical elements that compose them. In addition, small differences in the atomic positions and reticular parameters in the structure with majority phase found (+98% of purity), which leads to differences given by distortions or inclination of their octaedron B-O6, which have been exposed to different mechanical, electrical or thermal processes applied. During each of the seven stoichiometric formulas synthesized and then analyzed in this document, the polycryline samples were produced by means of the synthesis method of the thermal reaction. Paramagnetic, ferromagnetic and ferrimagnetic behaviours in several compositions. The techniques of electrical characterization of current-voltage (I-V) and resistivity in a wide temperature range (from 60 K to 400 K approximately) revealed behaviors of semiconductor type (high resistivity to ambient temperature), semiconductor-thermistor (low resistivity to ambient temperature), electrical transport mechanisms involving polarions, type Mott (VRH) and type Efros-Shklovskii (ES) and which provided the calculation of the activation energy of each mechanism in some samples. Measures of enablement according to temperature with constant frequencies corroborated the results of electrical transport and showed relaxations type Debye and Maxwell-Wagner in the samples with higher resistivity, with which also from the Arrhenius curves the activation energy was obtained and the probability of transitions of an electrical or magnetic nature was deduced. These type of materials perovskita exposed a wide range of magnetic resistances, correlated with the optical energy gap obtained in some samples.
