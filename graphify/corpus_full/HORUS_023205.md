# CACOO CHANGE (THEOBROMA CACAO L.)

The accumulation of Cadmium in cocoa almonds is a subject of crucial interest to the community that produces, transforms and consumes it, both in exporting countries and consumers, for this reason the cadmium relationship soil cocoa chocolate plant and the legislation associated with the topic, both in Colombia and in other countries of the world. The soil is the fundamental resource for agricultural production, especially when it comes to obtaining food for the well-being of the communities. However, it can become source and reservoir of toxic substances, which could affect both plants and animals consuming them (Adriano, 2001: Bronick and Lal, 2005). Cadmium (Cd) is found among the transition metals belonging to the group ll-B of the periodic table and can be toxic for plants that absorb it from the soil (Adriano., 2001; Eutropio et al., 2016). The soil is recognized as a carcinogen for human beings, because of its bioaccumulation, generating risk when the amounts available in the soil are taken by the plants, affecting the yield of the crops and the soil
