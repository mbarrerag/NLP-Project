# Effect of nitrogen fertilization, maturity and altitude on the quality and profile of high-Colombian tropic pasture fatty acids.

The pastoral systems in the upper tropics of Colombia base their feeding on polyphytic pastures whose main species is the Kikuyo (Cenchrus clandestinus). The maturity of the plant, the climatic conditions, the height above the sea level and the management practices (fertilisation, rest days, grazing intensity) influence the composition of species and the nutritional quality of the pasture. Scientific evidence suggests that fertilization with nitrogen (N), the altitude and rest period (maturity of the pasture) modifies the quality of the pastures, particularly the proportions of lipids in the fodder. Additionally, some works indicate that the composition of the fatty acids (AG) could be influenced by the environmental temperature that varies with the height on the level of the martic. The present research aims to evaluate the effect of the nitrogen fertilization, the rest period and the altitude on the quality and the impact of the altiplano cundijaenses and explore some aspects of the quality of the different strata (super, medium and inferior) of these pastures with emphasis in the kikuyo. In the province of Ubaté, distributed in three altitude zones: high zone (2,820-3,200), medium zone (2,600-2,820) and low area (2,500-2,600). In each farm, subsamples were collected that will be used to study the kikuyo performance, in order to analyze its nutritional quality and AG profile. The third experiment will determine the nutritional value, including the ethereal extract (total lipids) of the vegetative structures of kikuyo (stalles and leaves) in different strata (superior, medium, inferior and stoloniferous), according to the regrowth age. The ANOVA will be applied with 2 treatments corresponding to different stages of growth of kikuyo (5 and 7 leaves per rebreed) and 3 repetitions per treatment. In each plot, the kikuyo stolons will be collected, which will be classified according to the number of leaves present with its stem. Each sheet will be analyzed separately to evaluate its influence on the distribution of lipids within each stolon.
