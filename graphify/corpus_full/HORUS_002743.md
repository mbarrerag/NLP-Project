# ANOTHER CASE OF HETEROSPECIFIC AMPLEXUS: RHINELLA HORRIBILIS (WIEGMANN, 1833) AND LITHOBATES PALMIPES (SPIX, 1824) IN NORTHEASTERN COLOMBIA

n frogs, reproductive behaviour is shaped by numerous factors, including ambient temperature and humidity, rainy season, photoperiod signals, species¿ intrinsic physiological events, and the timing in reproductive cycles (Wells, 2007; Woodley, 2011). During the breeding season, males start or intensify vocalisations mainly to attract females, signalling their size, vigour, and readiness for mating (Bowcock et al., 2008). These calls, however, might be disrupted by sounds emitted by other sympatric species, potentially leading to confusion and unusual behaviours or interspecifc amplexus. Males of some groups, such as toads, are very visual and can engage any moving object vaguely similar to a female toad (Marco and Lizana, 2002). Such occurrences may stem from sex-ratio imbalances and/or proportional sex loss, which can trigger atypical reproductive behaviours (Flores-Hernández and Martínez-Coronel, 2014). Despite their relative rarity, mistakes in amplexus have been documented across diferent frog groups. Among other deviations from typical reproductive behaviour, these mating errors range from amplexus between individuals of the same sex to interspecifc combinations of amplexus (Vásquez-Cruz, 2020).
