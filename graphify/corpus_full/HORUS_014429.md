# Narrow bipolar events study based on broadband observations.

This study determines the characteristics of the narrow bipolar pulses (NBEs) in Bogotá, Colombia, providing information on the NBEs in a tropical region observed from an Andean high plateau. The instrumentation and the ray software were created specifically for this study and consist of an electrical field measurement module, a magnetic field measurement module and a wideband beam interferometer. For data acquisition, the instrumentation of rays uses two digital oscilloscopes that together provide eight channels. The ray instruments were deployed in the roof of a building in the center of Bogotá. The measurement campaign collected data from 14 November 2019 to 9 April 2020 and included the second rainy season of 2019 and part of the first rainy season of 2020. The observation period lasted exactly 148 days and recorded 1,316 events related to rays. The identification of the NBEs involved four stages: the first stage had the objective to eliminate the noise of the acquired signal, the second stage involved the identification of the NBEs, the third stage of the individual forms and the classification of the NBEs, the In addition, there are 22 groups of NBEs composed of two or three NBEs temporarily close to each other. The rare presence of NBEs in regular ray activity suggests that NBEs are not the cause of the onset of lightning. This statement is supported in this study by their presence in only one sixth of all recorded ray activity. The data collected suggest that NBEs are not an isolated event, but rather, it follows that NBEs are a phenomenon that takes place as a related effect of some GC or as part of the development of subsequent intranubes electrical discharges. The average value of the total pulse duration suggests that NBEs should originate from some energy and short-lived electrical discharge. This result agrees with the hypothesis that rapid electric discharges are the phenomenon responsible for the production of NBEs. When comparing the rates of occurrence of NBEs, the result reveals an apparent relationship with latitude, meaning that NBEs are more frequent at lower latitudes and also show a greater proportion of NBEs of negative polarity.
