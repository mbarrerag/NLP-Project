# `I saw the things of my ancestors and I was cured: violence and memorise in an ethno-grafic collection of the British museum

In 1905, the British Museum received about a hundred objects collected by French explorer Eugène Robuchon among the Murui villages (formerly known as uitoto)1 and Bora, from the Caquetá-Putumayo region (Northwestern Amazon).This collection was added to eight artifacts exchanged with the Munich Ethnographic Museum in 1860, and to a dozen objects deposited by George Lomas in 1904; lastly, it was supplemented by some additions in the next two decades. All the objects, with the exception of those coming from the Munich Ethnographic Museum, were collected during the boom of rubber exploitation between 1900 and 1920.
