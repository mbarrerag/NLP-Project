# LABOUR INFORMATION AND VENEZOLANIA MIGRATION IN THE COLOMBIAN CARIBBEAN. PERCEPTIONS OF INDISA CARDAGE VENDERS AND SAINT MARTA

This article analyzes the political situation that the Venezuelan migratory movement to Colombia and other countries of the region produced and how the socio-economic effects of its insertion into the informal economy are perceived by the unformalized Colombian local workers of the cities of Cartagena and Santa Marta, in the Colombian Caribbean. This article is descriptive in nature and has methodologically resorted to the revision of documents and statistics from the official archives, which reflect the dynamics of Venezuelan migration. Questionnaires were also applied, through the survey technique, to a representative sample of informal vendors, whether stationary, semi-stationary or mobile, in order to investigate their socio-economic characteristics, their knowledge of the Ilegate of new vendors due to the economic crisis in Venezuela and their perceptions of the impact that occurred on the development of their commercial activities. Likewise, the actions or strategies that these vendors have turned into to mitigate the effects of the insertion of migrants are determined
