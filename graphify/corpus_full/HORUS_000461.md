# Mining and geochemistry of stratoligated copper and silver deposits hosted by the La Quinta Formation, northern Colombia

The Formation “La Quinta” is a volcano-sedimentary unit formed by continental red layers, oxidized and interstratified with subaerical lava flows of fesica to mafica composition. These lithostrategic elements that emerge in the Serrania del Perijá (north of Colombia), host two different styles of stratoligada mineralization Cu (±Ag): Hosted in basalts and hosted in sediments. The first style of mineralization includes native copper and silver, together with smaller quantities of Cu sulphides hosted in mafica lava flows. On the other hand, the second style of mineralization consists mainly of calcopirita, Bornita, and digenita hosted in sedimentary strata. Microanalysis with electronic probe (EMPM) indicate that Cu sulphides present in both styles of mineralization host silver. On the other hand, the evaluation of mineral ensambles suggests a low temperature and epigenetic origin for mineralization Cudiment (±Ag) in the following phases of the basaltic flows and sedimental strata. In spite of the above, most major elements and trace evidence a wide range of variation, which implies that alteration reactions were mainly controlled by litological factors. In addition, the subergency oxidation of hypogenic ores of mena resulted in the formation of spionkopita, anilite and covelite, in addition to other secondary minerals of Cu. In general, the characteristics of the Cu deposits (±Ag) hosted in the “La Quinta” Formation are similar to those described for cuprifer deposits type Chilean Manto and Volcanic Redbed
