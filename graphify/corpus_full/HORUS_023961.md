# PHYSICAL ACTIVITY REGULS SUGAR BEVERAGE INGRY AND SPEED AT EAT IN YOUTH ADULTS OF A HIGHER EDUCATION INSTITUTION IN BOGOTA-COLOMBIA

Overweight is a growing problem worldwide, as part of the risk factors identified are two behaviors related to food consumption: the intake of sugary drinks and the speed of eating. Physical activity has been proposed as a useful strategy for the prevention and treatment of excess weight and comorbidities associated with eating. To identify the relationship of physical activity with the intake of sugary beverages and the speed of eating in young adults. A cross-sectional analytical study was conducted with 243 students from a University of Bogotá, aged between 18 and 29 years. The International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ) was used for physical activity, as well as questions about the frequency of intake of sugary drinks and the speed of eating. A slight inverse correlation was found between the duration of daily physical activity and the frequency of consumption of sugary beverages, especially in the male sex; and the weekly frequency of vigorous physical activity was related to the frequency of intake of sugary drinks and the speed of eating.
