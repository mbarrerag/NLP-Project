# DEMOGRAPHICAL, CLINICAL AND HISTOLOGICAL FACTORS ASSOCIATED TO MAJOR NOVO METASTASIC DISEASE RISK IN PATIENTS WITH MOTHER CANCER

To describe the sociodemographic, clinical and histopathological characteristics and the times of opportunity for diagnosis and beginning of management of patients with novo metastatic breast cancer in a specialized cancer center. A retrospective observational study of historical cohort was conducted, which included patients with diagnosis of breast cancer who entered with initial clinical staging between I and IIIC, who ended up classified as stage IV with extension studies. 270 patients were included; 77% of them took more than 60 days between the onset of symptoms and the date of the positive biopsy report for cancer and 35.2% took more than 60 days between the biopsy result and the first consultation in the INC. Most of them were initially diagnosed in stage IIIB (59.3%; n=160) and the predominant molecular subtype was Luminal B HER2 negative in 41.4% (n=112) of the cases. Delay in diagnosis and remission to a histological entity are factors associated with the presentation of breast cancer in stage IV
