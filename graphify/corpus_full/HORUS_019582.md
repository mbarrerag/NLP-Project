# MODELING OF FREIGHT TRIP GENERATION FOR LARGE FREIGHT TRAFFIC GENERATORS IN URBAN AREAS

This study focused on estimating the number of daily freight trips made in large freight traffic generators (LFTGs), where the receipt and delivery of a large amount of freight takes place, using the ordinary least squares technique. The variables used depended on the type of establishment, such as the number of employees, area, and extent of the commercial premises, and in the case of hotels and universities, of the number of rooms and students, respectively. Freight trip generation models were estimated by analyzing the variables that are associated with the production and attraction of freight trips generated in a LFTG. The information obtained from the 2018 Freight Transportation Study of the Medellin Metropolitan Area (Colombia) was used to estimate 108 models that would explain freight trip generation in large facilities coming under the following seven categories: universities, hotels, firms, shopping centers, hospitals, buildings, and markets and slaughterhouses. The results can help in the planning of LFTG infrastructure in cities; they can also facilitate vehicle emission analysis by determining the number of trips arriving and leaving the sites, as well as the analysis of sustainable freight transport practices
