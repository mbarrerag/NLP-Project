# PRESERVING A LANDMARK: REINFORCEMENT, REHABILITATION, AND RESTORATION OF THE CATHEDRAL OF MANIZALES

The Cathedral Basilica of Manizales stands as one of the most emblematic buildings in the renowned Republican architecture style, which experienced a distinctive revival after the devastating fires that swept through Manizales, Colombia in the 1920s. Designed in a unique eclectic neogothic style, the architectural plans were created in Paris as a response to the 1926 fire that destroyed the city's former cathedral. Celebrating its ninety-fifth anniversary in 2023, this Colombian architectural masterpiece began with the laying of its foundation stone in 1928. Reinforced cement was used in its construction, a few decades after the introduction of reinforced concrete. Over its ninety-five years of existence, the cathedral endured several high-intensity earthquakes in 1938, 1962, and 1979, which significantly compromised its structural integrity. As a result, seismic retrofit studies were conducted in the late 1990s to preserve this nationally recognized monument, which was declared as such in 1984. Given the complexity, these studies included various modeling strategies of the structural performance to evaluate and select the possible interventions to enhance stiffness, resistance, and energy dissipation capacity. An examination of the state and pathology of the materials was also carried out. This comprehensive approach encompassed geotechnical studies on seismic amplification, dynamic behavior analysis using ambient vibrations, 3D virtual modeling, and the final structural analysis using finite elements. The proposed interventions carried out between 2002 and 2004, focused on reinforcing the base of the central spire, ensuring the stability of the four corner spires, and strategically constructing new structural walls connected at upper levels to facilitate diaphragm action and improve seismic behavior. These efforts aimed to enhance the cathedral's seismic resistance. Subsequently, between 2016 and 2017, attention turned to the treatment and preservation of the concrete, as well as other necessary interventions concerning sculptures and images. This paper outlines how a new generation of local specialists has approached seismic modeling, reinforcement, material treatment, and preservation interventions for this 113-meter-high cathedral. The proposed measures align with state-of-the-art earthquake engineering modeling, design, and construction principles of reinforced concrete while also addressing the imperative of protecting historical heritage
