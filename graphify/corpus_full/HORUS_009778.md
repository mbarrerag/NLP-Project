# COMPONENT OF THE ATMOSFERICAL WATER VAPOR COLUMN ON TWO ENTARTICAL GNSS STATIONS, AND ITS POSSIBLE RELATIONSHIP TO GLOBAL CALENTATION

The water vapour content in the atmosphere is determining for the climate of our planet, since it is the main responsible for the natural greenhouse effect. Although the human being has very little direct control over the water vapour content in the atmosphere, the current global warming caused mainly by the anthropogenic emissions produced by the burning of fossil fuels, causes an indirect increase in the content of water vapour. This is due to the exponential relationship of the saturation vapour pressure with the temperature, described by the equation of Clausius-Clapeyron. This constitutes one of the strongest feedback processes within the terrestrial climate system, since the increase in the water vapour content amplifies the initial warming caused by the anthropogenic emissions. Obtaining precise measurements of the increase in the overall concentrations of atmospheric water vapour is a great challenge due to its high spatial and temporal variability, but very important to evaluate the evolution of the terrestrial climate to global scale. One of the most accurate methods to estimate the water vapour content in an atmospheric column, is from the delay that this causes in the signal that the GNSS stations receive so associated, some of these stations are in operation since several years and even decades ago
