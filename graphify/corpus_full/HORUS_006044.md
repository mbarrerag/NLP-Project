# Evaluation of immunogenicity of pulsed dendritic cells with different antigen formulations for immunotherapy

The dendritic cell (DC) based vaccines have been projected as an important alternative for cancer immunotherapy. Their design is typically based on the ex vivo stimulation of monocyte-derived DCs (MoDCs) using different formulations of antigens and maturation conditions. However, there is no consensus regarding the optimal formulation of the antigen when designing DC-based vaccines. On the other hand, it has been described that the maturation stimulus in the MoDCs could influence their ability to present certain formats of the antigen. Thus, this work was proposed to explore the implications of various formulations of the antigen in the development of anti-tumoural immune responses by LT CD8+ grown with MoDCs treated under different conditions. To this end, healthy donors with the CD8+ specific anti-epittop model were identified, using the cytometric method based on tetramer. Mopedral and co-constituted lines were derived from these antigens, under the formulations of short peptide, long peptide, recombinant protein and tumour cell, all of the epitheminal agents of the epicycle. As has been reported classically in the literature, simple and direct formulations based on peptides promoted robust cytokine activation and production responses. On the contrary, complex formulations such as recombinant protein and treated tumor cells showed less obvious effective responses. Activation and exhaustion phenotypes exhibited an expression dependent mainly on the format of the antigen used, although heterogeneity was observed among individuals. In addition, the LAG-3 marker was influenced by the maturation cocktail used in the MoDCs. Finally, it was possible to induce the expression of CRL in the treated tumor lines and its phagocytosis by iDCs, although it was not shown cross presentation of the antigen.
