# Pluralizing Peace. Global and local conceptual approaches in times of crisis - II

The Center of Thought Pluralize Peace: conceptual approaches global and local in times of crisis (CPPP) is a proposal of articulation between professors researchers of Bogotá, Medellín and Amazonia of the National University of Colombia, integrating in turn the research groups Imaginary, practices and representations, History, environment and politics and Critical Museumology and studies of Cultural Heritage. The institutional network that supports it is composed of the Amazon Research Institute (IMANI), the Master's Degree in Museumology and Heritage Management (UN-Bogotá), the University of Caen (France), the Institut pour la Paix (France), the Institut des Amériques_x0096_Pôle Bogotá, the French Institute of Andean Studies, the Cultural Subgerence of the Banco de la República and the Centre for Memory, peace and reconciliation of Bogotá. The Iberoamericana Network in Conceptual History (IBERCONCEPTOS), of which the Centre of thought is part, enriching the lines of research theoretically. The aim of these actors is to generate and strengthen alliances with public decision makers, and take place in the CPber-based practices. The new stage of the Thought Center will strengthen the team’s activities in four lines of action with the following specific objectives. On the one hand, it will propose a long-term vision of peace through the recovery of disputes, variations and semantic structures of long duration of the sociopolitical concept of peace, its nature, its way of building it, its complicity in social injustice as well as its liberating potential, and the obstacles it has faced. On the other hand, it will promote didactic-pedagogical perspectives on peace, understood as coexistence, inclusion and recognition in democratic settings, through routes that expand and deepen the understanding that we have about peace and insist on its status as a scenario of work and mediation of social conflict. Thirdly, it will promote intercultural dialogues around local histories and memories through the construction of collaborative methodologies that appeal to the symbolic, aesthetic and embodied strategies, in local contexts of root to generate transformative narratives. On the other hand, the CPPP will set up a fourth line of work with the mandate to explore the relationship between the social struggles and to give rise to understanding of the social relationships
