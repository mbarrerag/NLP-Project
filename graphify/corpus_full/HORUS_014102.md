# --Geochemistry of endolytic microorganisms in high radiation zones in Colombia

--The microbial endolithic colonisations are common in various litologies throughout the world, with cyanobacterials, as frequent protagonists. These colonisations in semi-transparent rocks and minerals are especially notable due to their location under surfaces exposed to intense UV radiation.Cyanobacteria are predominant in these habitats, which makes them ideal biomarkers for astrobiology. The studied rocks include limestone, quartz, plaster and halita. In evaporite environments where these translucent minerals are formed, the microorganisms face challenges such as high osmotic pressure, desiccation and intense UV radiation. As a response, they produce protective pigments visible in coloured areas of the rocks. Common pigments include scitonemin and gloeocapsin, which participate in photosynthesis and act as antioxidants. The chlorophyll and carotenoids are also produced, with a secondary role in protecting against UV radiation. These pigments not only indicate the presence of bacteria in the endolithic niches, but also provide information about their mechanisms of adaptation. The main objective is to evaluate how the structure of the plaster and the environmental conditions related to altitude influence pigments developed by endolytic microbial communities. In addition, the potential of these pigments as biomarkers or life indicators in translucent minerals will be explored. This approach will contribute to the understanding of microbial ecology in lythic substrates and the exploration of life in other celestial bodies
