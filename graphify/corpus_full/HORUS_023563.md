# IDENTIFICATION OF A CITOPLASSATIC PEROXIDATE TRIPAREDOXIN IN LEISHMANIA Braziliensis

The anti-oxidant defense systems used by the intracellular parasite Leishmania braziliensis during the infection process allow the elimination of reactive oxygen and nitrogen species at the expense of equivalent reducers derived from tripanotiona, avoiding cellular damage of the pathogen. With the aim of identifying molecular white potentials for the development of drugs against this parasite, the detection of the cytoplasmic triparedoxin peroxidase enzyme of L. braziliensis (Lb TXNPxII), which is essential to reduce toxic concentrations of hydrogen peroxide in the context of infection, was carried out. Polyclonal antibodies were generated in the avian model, starting from cloning, expression and purification of the recombinant protein 6xHis-SUMO-Lb TXNPxII (37kDa) in the heterologist system Escherichia coli.
