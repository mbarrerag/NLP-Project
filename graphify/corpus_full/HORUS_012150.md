# Extruder for 3D bioprinting with composed bioink oriented to the cellular viability evaluation in the generation of tissue

The 3D bioprint is an emerging biomanufacture strategy that employs biotins and models generated with CAD-type tools for automated manufacture of tissue scaffolding and organ-like constructs. Despite recent advances in materials and techniques with great potential to achieve the manufacture of tissues relevant for clinical and in vitro applications, several aspects such as tissue vascularization and the prolonged functionality of cells are limited to advances in this field. Among the various 3D bioprinting techniques, the bioprint based on extrusion (EBB) has been conceived as the most promising to achieve this objective, due to its versatility and availability. In this document we report the development of three and four layer extrusion systems in axial layers with high degree of cellular viability and versatility for the current limitations faced when trying to manufacture vascularized tissues and stable and perfusible vascular structures. We combine insilicon simulations with in vitro experiments to accurately design multiple tissue extrusion systems in axial layers with high degree of cellular viability and versatility for bioprint 3D applications. In addition, we report the modifications of hardware and software performed with lifting for clinical and in vitro applications, various aspects, such as tissue vascularization and prolonged cell functionality, are limited by the advances in this field.
