# The 1991 Constitution: the ideal in the midst of the storm

In general terms, the works that are part of the collective work The 1991 Constitution: the ideal in the midst of the storm question the acritical and idealizing vision of our Political Charter, typical of the recurring narratives of our recent bibliography. On the one hand, they call into question the dominant perception according to which we have witnessed a linear and progressive development of the discourse of rights, in order to show, in contrast, contexts of crises and ruptures that also reflect important setbacks in the guarantee and protection of rights, and in the constitutional jurisprudence itself. On the other hand, they also relativize those dominant approaches that attribute the advances achieved exclusively to the work of the Constitutional Court, for which they claim the role of social movements and civil society organizations in the transformative and reformist dynamics that have allowed significant social gains in the field of rights during these three decades.-- Contracovered
