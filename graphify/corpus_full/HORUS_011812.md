# THE VIENTES OF LIBERALISSM IN CARIBBEAN........................................................................................................................

The Atlantic revolutions, whose key moment spans between the end of the eighteenth century and the beginning of the nineteenth century, provoked social and political upheavals throughout the Americas. As a result, transformations took place that demolished in part the old monarchical political structures, giving way to a complex process of formation of state projects with republican political systems. In fact, in recent years, given the commemoration of the bicentenaries of independences in Latin America, the achievements of political emancipation and of the republican systems became important subjects of reflection; in parallel, a profuse historiography flourished in different countries that tried to re-read these transformation processes in relation to the advances of the new political history, sometimes, with limited progress by the institutional influence and the interference of the governments that imposed nationalist dyes on these commemorations, reducing the possibility of a renewal in the historical interpretations. This publication seeks to make a turn to this vision of the nationalistic accounts to advance on the knowledge of the Caribbean region since the analysis of the reformist winds that blew in the Caribbean during the second half of the eighteenth century, linked to the economic and economic transformations of the epicenters. The idea and the effort that many of the chapters in this publication guide is to divert attention from the interpretations that they see in institutionalized reformism the main engine of American and Caribbean revolutions and transformations. The influence of enlightened thinking, imperial rivalries and the need for them to drive transformations with a view to achieving more effective control over territories and their resources cannot continue to be the only explanations for understanding the changes and transformations that occurred in the Caribbean. It is a question of putting the gaze on the fabric of social, economic and political relations that intensified the changes that took place in the territories, below and in parallel to the Caribbean
