# Financing of non-financial enterprises and the expansion of intangible assets in the balance sheet structure

The financing of large non-financial corporations has emphasized two facts that have been identified since the 1980s, to which the literature attributes negative effects on investment. These facts are represented by the increase in payments to shareholders and the accumulation of financial assets. This study analyses the evolution in the composition of assets of large non-financial companies using the financial information available from the Computat North America base for the period 1980-2018. From the exploration of data, it is identified that there is an additional characteristic that consists in the increasing participation of intangibles that is observed in the financial balance structures. This evidence allows to discuss how this type of assets can account for the way in which the financing of large companies is implemented. This also has to do with the regulatory frameworks of financial information and its provisions for the recognition of intangibles in financial reports. As a differential aspect, it is added to the current literature that the strengthening of a tendency of accumulation of financial and intangible assets is characterizing the above-mentioned process of financing in the field of non-financial firms
