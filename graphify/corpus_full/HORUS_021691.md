# Complementarity beyond correlation

Complementarity becomes important in electricity markets as the generation mix diversifies, especially when the system's storage capacity is not enough to smooth the time natural variability of the primary source or when there occur control or dispatch problems. Unfortunately, a formal and standard definition of complementarity between energy sources is not available up to date. Consequently, authors have often, tacitly or explicitly, adopted the idea that complementarity is a synonym of “anticorrelation”, or negative correlation. Most authors that measure complementarity by anticorrelation use the Pearson's correlation coefficient to assess anticorrelation, while some use Spearman's and a few use Kendall's correlation coefficients. In a recent chapter, we argue that a complementarity metric should measure the uniformity of the sum of all available resources, and correlation coefficients fail to do that. Later on in this chapter, the measure of regularity will be precisely defined by means of the mathematical concept of total variation
