# Bayesian Constraints on observable properties of exoplanetary systems using planet population synthesis simulations.

Recent advances in exoplanet observations have led to the discovery of almost 4400 planets in more than 3000 planetary systems. However, many physical properties of these systems remain largely unknown due to observational biases and stochasticity in their formation processes.We address the current limitations of the classification of exoplanets by means of the formulation of a new and robust classification scheme based on the first moment of the planet mass versus the distribution of the semi-larger axis using the models of the Gaussian mixture (GMM).We validate our method by means of approximate Bayesian information and calculation criteria, which demonstrates that it is robust with the measurement of uncertainties and adaptable to new observations.Our scheme produces four kinds of planetary systems: Sub-Mercurian, the Venusian systems, the similar to the solar systems and the peripheral systems. We also propose a new method to compensate the observational biases by addressing the stowaticity in the formation of stars and planets. To this end, we develop a probabilisticalistic pre-obvious format in which we take pre-distribution of the observed planetary systems and the marginalisation of the variables. We also propose a new method of the functional functions. It contributes significantly to the total number of planets, so we hope to find more rocky planets in future observations. Our formalism allows us to obtain probability distributions of unobserved exoplanetary systems or with biased properties. This formalism will help direct future astronomical studies and motivate more questions about observed planetary systems
