# CORPORATE GOVERNMENT AND ORGANIZATIONAL SOCIAL RESPONSIBILITY.

This book is subscribed within the broad body of organizational theory and administrative theory subdivision. In general, it is about contents referring to the GC, the RSO, the multi-latinas and the relationship between GC and RSO in a multi-latin company. The text proposes that a majority owner block exercising a very broad power minimizes the conflict of interests with the manager. Therefore, the relationship between GC and RSO in the multi-latinas would be little affected by the degenisation process and by the classic agency problem of the GC. Consequently, the multi-latinas companies are likely to minimize the conflict between the board and the manager when: a) they have the presence of a solid family block of majority owners, b) they act in a market of unstable capitals with little development and c) they are strongly imbued in an organizational culture defending the concept of family (Arora and Dharvandkar, 2011); the contents of the book constitute a contribution to the organizational theory. In this disciplinary body, the present paper joins the group of research that focus the relationship between GC and embargo RSO in the internal environment of the GC and in the context of the ME (Arora and Dharwandkar, 2011; 2011; 2011; The relationship between this organizational figure and the RSO. Secondly, corporate governance is addressed. This section addresses: (a) the dimensions in which it is developed, (b) the problem of the agency, (c) the relationship between the GC and the governance of the organization and (d) the possible differences or variations that the GC has in each institutional context. Thirdly, organizational social responsibility appears. In this section, the conceptual principles of organizational social responsibility are addressed. This section mainly discusses the semantic field referred to its denomination and the conceptual bases that consolidate the RSO as a concept of greater breadth and that is suggested as a cross-cutting theme to all the activities of the organization. Chapter 2 relates corporate governance to organizational social responsibility.
