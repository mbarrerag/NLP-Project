# Semiparametric smoothing spline to join mean and variance models with responses from the biparametric exponential family: a Bayesian perspective.

Statistical applications should address an increasing complexity due to new data emerging from recent technologies, new phenomena and various sources of uncertainty. Demand for methods with non-standard data structures, high-dimensional real-time estimation and adequate latent models has caused semiparametric models to play a crucial role in recent statistical analysis. This thesis implements flexible Bayesian methods to infer together the functions of mean, variance and asymmetry when the response variable comes from the biparametric exponential family or asymmetrical distributions. The approximation is obtained by methods based on Monte Carlo simulation techniques with markov chains and deterministic variable learning algorithms. In these scenarios, each submodel includes variables in parametric and non-parametric form to analyze the effect of predictors on moments. The modelling scenarios also benefit from the fusion of graphical models and semi-parametric regressions quickly using generalized linear model architecture. The importance and implications of our strategy lie in their potential to contribute to both a unified computational methodology, which provides complex modelling, and analytical models that quickly contribute to the wide-ranging Bayesian mode
