# from Social Justice to Compasive Justice: The Contributions of MARTHA NUSSBAUM to Political Philosophy

Martha Nussbaum is fundamentally a political and moral philosopher who, based on the Aristotelian tradition, has managed to integrate into a theoretical position a vision of social justice with a perspective on nature and the cultivation of certain moral emotions that are appropriate to build a new democratic citizenship. For our author, justice cannot be detached from the actions of people in everyday life, therefore, she considers that this bond can materialize through the cultivation of emotions, which are understood as key elements of moral life that sharpen our perception of the world and guide our choices. Guided by this notion, in this article we present some fundamental aspects of Nussbaum's political thinking, showing the transition that the author experiences in her theories from the positions of Rawls' liberalism to consolidating her own vision of compassionate justice, which is a concrete way of underpinning the public norm that defends how all people deserve equal respect and dignity. In this journey we will also reflect on the influence of communitarian positions of the late twentieth century on the ideals of citizenship of the Chicago philosopher
