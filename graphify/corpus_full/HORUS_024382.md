# EFFECT OF SOCIAL CONFLICT IN THE ACCUMULATION OF THE HUMAN CAPITAL: EMPLOYMENT FOR PERU

Social conflicts, which differ significantly from armed conflicts, are understood as a complex process between several social actors that pursue opposing interests, which can result in protests and social mobilizations affecting the normality of communities. This study studies the effect of social conflicts on the accumulation of human capital. To identify this effect, the geographic and temporal variation of the number of social conflicts reported in Peru is used. This study uses a novel and rich source of data for Peru that reports the number and type of conflict per populated center, which allows the construction of a set of data panel per school belonging to the populated centers between 2008 and 2016. Using a methodology of regression of data panel with fixed effects of school and year, it is found that social conflicts are associated with a statistically significant reduction of educational achievement in mathematics (0.088 DE) and readership (0.062 DE). Furthermore, no effect is found on school dropout, nor on reprobation. These results are consistent with different model specifications
