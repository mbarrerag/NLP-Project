# Assessment of simultaneous mechanisms and physicochemical and biological indicators during the immobilization of toxic metals by microbiologically induced precipitation of carbonates by ureolytic bacteria.

Colombia is a country with a great potential for development considering its wealth and biodiversity. Within the different ecosystems, the biota present represents a niche that can be used to develop and promote the biotechnology of the country, with an emphasis on environmentally friendly and economically viable strategies. In particular, the use of microorganisms has been shown as a promising alternative to treat environmental problems related to bioremediation of matrices, such as soils, contaminated with potentially toxic metals. More specifically, in view of the presence of these metals in agricultural soils, promising results have been reported from the application of MICP technology (Microbially Induced Carbonate Precipitation), where the precipitation of metallic carbonate mediated by bacteria decreases the bioavailability of the pollutant and prevents them from reaching the plant tissues of crops destined to human consumption.
