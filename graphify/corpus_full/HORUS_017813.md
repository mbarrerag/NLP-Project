# Notes on the nature of philosophy and its teaching

Abstract The popular definitions of philosophy (including some that can be described as traditional) in terms of (i) +8220; love of knowledge+8221; or +8220; love of wisdom+8221;, (ii) +8220; mother of all sciences+8221;, (iii) +8220; opium to relieve penalties+8221; or +8220; therapy+8221; (iv) or +8220; research into why things =8221; are clearly unsatisfactory. I have found these and other similar definitions in auditoriums constituted mainly by non-philosophers. In addition, I consider that the vocational imperative imposed on all of us who devote our efforts to philosophical knowledge requires us to seek an adequate characterization of that knowledge, i.e., the minimum that any philosopher should be able to respond to is what is the nature of philosophical knowledge. In this article I will offer the reasons why such definitions are inadequate and I will formulate a definition of the term +8220; philosophy will not be a conceptual exercise that is identified in the above definitions
