# Assessment of the regression methods of soil use in the estimation of concentrations of PM10 and PM2,5

The estimation of the concentrations of particulate material (PM10 and PM2.5) is essential for the management of air quality and the formulation of public health policies. This study focused on the Bucaramanga Metropolitan Area (AMB), using Soil Use Regression Models (LUR) based on remote sensing data to estimate such concentrations between 2018 and 2023. Satellite images of Landsat 8 and Air Quality Monitoring System data were used, with predictors such as the reflective bands and several environmental indices (NDVI, SAVI, NDWI, NDSI). Three predictive techniques were compared: step-by-step regression, regression of partial squares and artificial neural networks (ANN-MLP). The results showed that the ANN-ML was the most accurate, with a determination coefficient of R2 = 0.57 for PM10 and R2 of the M2.5, and mid-range quadratic errors (RMSE) of 13.13 and 6.61 respectively. The results showed that the ANN-ML was the most accurate, with a determination of R2 = 0.5 for PM10 and R2 of the R2 of the M2 of the M2.5, and M2 of the M2 of the M2 of the M5 networks (ANN-MLP). The results showed that ANN-MLP was the most accurate, with a coefficient of determination of R2 = 0.57 for MP10 and R2 = 0.71 for MP2.5, and root mean square errors (RMSE) of 13.13 and 6.61, respectfully. Using the best models, daily concentration maps of MP10 and MP2.5 were generated, providing a valuable tool for managing air quality in areas with limited monitoring
