# Functional structure of termite assemblies associated with productive rubber crop systems in the northwestern Colombian Amazon.

The extensive livestock in the Amazon has caused deforestation and degradation in the soil, with negative consequences on biodiversity and environmental services.While the establishment of rubber plantations has emerged as a productive alternative and restoration for soils degraded by the livestock. This study evaluated the differences in the assemblies and the functional structure of the termites in three different rubber crops as an indirect way of evaluating the restoration of the diversity of the soil. Three systems of rubber crops were sampled: clonal fields (CF), commercial plantations (CR) and mixed plantations (MX). In addition, livestock pastures (PA) and natural forests (NF) served as reference ecosystems. The termites were sampled using the transect method. Of the 80 species collected, 3.8% were the families of the Rhinetermitidae and the 96.2% of the total of the species found in the natural forests. The natural forest was the use of the land with greater richness (54 species) and occurrence of species (437 occurrences). In the rubber cultivation systems it was 39% greater than in the soils and included the variables of the soil of the species of the Termitidae.
