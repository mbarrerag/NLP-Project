# THE NATURE OF THE BEAUTIFUL AND THE ART

This article aims to offer clues to think of a Hegelian notion of nature that is not exclusively constructed with resources to the concepts of 'Logic', but rather results from an examination of the processes of the spirit that are correlative to it. Therefore, we seek an enriched notion of nature that is obtained here from an analysis of art. Therefore, after exposing the general traits of the relationship between nature and spirit, the article addresses the notion of the beautiful as a sensitive manifestation of the idea, and its declines in the natural beauty and the artistic beauty. The first one makes evident to us a nature that remains external, an obstinate finitude that is not allowed to blur in the realm of the spirit. On the other hand, art idealizes nature and surpasses it, but it is shown here that Hegel would have glimpsed a form of art that does not sublimate the natural on the way to the idea, but that it returns to the natural world to exhibit it in its own contingency and finitude
