# THE ROADS TOWARDS WATER SUSTAINABILITY AND POVERTY ALLEVIATION: A POLICY SCENARIO ANALYSIS IN COLOMBIA

Poorer countries often face a severe trade-off: the need to improve socio-economic conditions is hard to balance with the maintenance of key ecological processes. Most of the available literature shows a paucity of studies that combine these issues. We aim at filling this gap by analysing the complex relations among several economic (e.g., GDP, poverty, informal labour) and environmental variables (water and land use) at a low-spatial scale (i.e., 33 administrative departments) under the threat of climate change. Both groups of variables are highly relevant for sustainable development processes. To this purpose, we select Colombia, a Latin American country with almost 10% of its inhabitants living in extreme poverty without access to potable water. Based on EUROGREEN model (D'Alessandro et al., 2020), this study develops an Integrated Assessment Model that combines system dynamics with sub-national Environmentally Extended Input'Output (EEIO) tables to capture the spatial and temporal interdependencies across economic sectors, including detailed disaggregation of the food production sectors, besides agriculture. The linkage between water sustainability and poverty alleviation passes through the food production sectors that ¿ other than being water- and land-intensive ¿ show high-income inequality and a high share of informal workers (i.e., without legal contracts and with high-income volatility). The main novelties with respect to the available literature are manifold: i) the model sheds a light on the complex relations between the water-food-energy nexus with the economic and social structure, ii) it simulates the dynamics of multiple socio-economic and environmental indicators in the long-run, iii) it provides a coherent quantitative framework to test the impacts of multiple-policy packages, and iv) it analyses the role of the food production sectors in alleviating income inequality and improving water management. Preliminary results show that departments that are net exporters of virtual water suffer from water stress that might be exacerbated by future high rainfall variability due to climate change that hit Colombian regions in a heterogeneous way. Most of the rural departments that provide food (and ``virtual water¿¿) to the other departments might be exposed to water stress conditions and exacerbation of poverty conditions. Beyond these outcomes, we expect to provide some policy recommendations on what Colombia's government should reconsider to achieve several of the UN's sustainable development goals
