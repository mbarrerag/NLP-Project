# The mutations of the legal-spatial border of the Center-Oriental area of Medellín

The city of Medellín, given its geographical conditions, has no soil to expand. This problem is reflected in the edges of the city, which have had an uncontrolled growth for decades and which has been aggravated by the phenomenon of informal settlements in areas that were not suitable for construction. Taking into account the classifications of the soil present in Law 388 of 1997 and that are inscribed in the Plans of Territorial Ordenamiento as fundamental to urban activities, in Medellín it has been impossible to delimit, at least in its physical-space reality, the urban perimeter, and every year more settlements appear that colonize the rural soil (and also the protective soil) to generate housing that, both in its material and urban conditions, present high levels of precarization of the habitat. Considering also that the mitigation of the risk is one of the determinants of the territorial order, as well as the provision of public services and the construction of road infrastructure, the edges today suffer from almost everything that the norm has as a safe place to live, and hundreds of thousands of people live in extremely precarious conditions without access to the public housing, without public services. This project aims to carry out a review of the proposed city model from the institutional point of view and to propose new methodologies of analysis that will allow overcoming the dichotomy between administrative decisions and what takes place on the edge. To put forward a definition of the border from the legal-spatial point of view, it presupposes contrasting the realities and mediating between what happens and what could happen, allowing reflection on the power of space despite the shortcomings it presents. In short, this project presents not only an opportunity to generate knowledge about the specific edge of the central-eastern area of the city, but also to conceptualize around the border problem in its general terms and to propose another perspective that links the right to the city from the notions of territorial order and minimizes the effects of urban segregation
