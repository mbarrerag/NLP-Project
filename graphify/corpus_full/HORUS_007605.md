# Generation of value-added products from photooxidation reactions of essential oils catalyzed by active dioxomolybdenum (VI) centers

One of the biggest challenges in materials engineering and green chemistry is the design and preparation of environmentally friendly catalytic systems, which allow the development of clean processes, working at atmospheric pressure, ambient temperature, and which are highly selective[1], [2]. The solid molecules formed by active metals heterogenized in photoactive solids constitute an interesting field of research, framed within the search for new catalytic materials that lead to the renewal of existing processes, which allow the easy separation of the catalyst, its reuse and that work as far as possible under environmental conditions of pressure and temperature or use alternative energy sources such as sunlight [3]. In the search for new hybrid materials applied to oxidation processes, it has been possible to advance in the covalent union of different dioxin-molybdenum complexes (Mo2) on commercial titanium oxide P-25 and on high-temperatures using a route of synthesis [4], [5]. These materials have shown excellent activity and stability in the selective oxidation of phosphins, alquilbenzenes [6], [7], in the degradation of pollutants (Mo2), [7], [8], and in the photo-lower structures. its modification in oxygenated products of commercial interest, which is a topic of research of current interest [15]_x0096_[17] In this context, this research proposal is aimed at synthesizing a new solid catalyst MOF post-functionalized with the MoO2Br2 unit called MoO2Br2@COMOC-4 and studying its catalytic activity in photoepoxidation reactions of a molecule of commercial interest extracted from an essential oil (trans-anethol) using different oxidating agents (O2 molecular and TBHP) in the presence of visible light. From this proposal it is expected to obtain: 1- A new active catalytic heterogeneous system for epoxidation reactions of essential oils sufficiently stable to be reused, 2- Advance in the design of materials that by emulation of active natural centers work with green oxidant agents such as molecular O2; 3- Advance in the development of new materials that by their stability and working conditions in the presence of visible light allow in the future the development of new technologies of synthesis for obtaining environmental products of commercial and pharmaceutical interest with better selectivity
