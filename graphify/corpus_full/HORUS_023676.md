# On the use of random sets in geotechnical engineering

The geotechnical is subject to two great types of uncertainty: random and epistemic. Random uncertainty is manifested through the inherent variability of materials and their external agents, while epistemic uncertainty is manifested through the scarcity of information and lack of knowledge. An integral geotechnical model should take into account these two types of uncertainty in the light of a reliability analysis. However, the reliability analyses that are developed conventionally in geotechnical engineering have a series of gaps and approximations that can generate that the results of models distend from reality. First, the reliability analyses have been commonly based on the probability theory, which is able to model the random uncertainty but not epistemic, so that the latter is usually overlooked. Second, assumptions about the dependence between the basic variables of the models, which are not validated and in most cases lack of foundation. Third, obtaining precise results requires an excessive computational cost, which in many cases can be proposed to be unviable.
