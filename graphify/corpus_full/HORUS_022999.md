# ASSESSMENT OF THE SOLUBILITY OF CANNABINOIDS AND TERMINES CONTENTS IN SUPERCRITICAL CO2 CANNABI OIL USING GROUP CONTRIBUTION METHODS FOR THE ESTIMATION OF THEIR PROPERTIES

This study presents the estimation of critical properties of two terpenes (Mircene and a-Pineno) and three cannabinoids (9-THC, CBD, CBN) using group contribution methods. The supercritical CO2 solubility of cannabinoids is also modeled by fusion properties. Three group contribution methods were evaluated to determine critical properties and used in the modelling of supercritical CO2 solubility. The most effective group contribution method was Marrero-Gani's by presenting %0.74 of deviation against literature. The estimation of binary interaction parameters (kij) was performed by using a theoretical model that depends on only one parameter (critical volume) to compare with those reported in literature, since this is an intrinsic parameter in solubility, being one of the most accurate methods of comparing experimental solubility versus modeling. It is observed that at lower temperatures the estimation of binary interactions is approximate to the experimental, but at high temperatures the deviation of each group.
