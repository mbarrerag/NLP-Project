# Contribution to the prevention of cross-contamination of medicines by the development and standardization of a Cleaning Validation Matrix.

In this research work, a review of the current national and international normivity and of guidelines of union organizations oriented to control and prevention of cross-contamination in pharmaceutical manufacturing environments was carried out, which focused on the search and comparison of criteria for the evaluation of worst potential cases, finding that there are important differences in approaches for the determination of the worst case. Subsequently, a risk assessment was carried out to the criteria using the risk management tool called the Matrimony Analysis of Modes of Failures and Effects (FMEA), the result of this evaluation revealed that the great majority of the criteria were located in a high risk area, another small portion in the medium risk area and finally a single criterion, the Daily Permited Exposure (PDE) was located in the low risk area. Additionally, the criterion of difficulty of cleaning was calculated by two methods, calculated and by interviews, finding that the results obtained are not comparable.
