# Computational study of the reactivity of copper complexes as catalysts and dismutase superoxide

The radical species involved in oxidative stress are several, a phenomenon that has been related to several diseases that represent a great risk to health such as cancer, diabetes, neurodegenerative diseases, among others. One of them is superoxide anion, which occurs continuously in normal metabolic processes of cells and can result in chain reactions, forming other reactive species. Therefore, interest has been generated in finding molecules that are able to mimic the antioxidant activity of enzymes such as dismutase and catalase superoxide, combining efforts and computational and experimental strategies. In this study, mechanisms of reaction for 9 copper complexes as possible micro-moistizers of the enzyme superoxide dismutase (SOD) the catalytic enzyme (CAT) by calculating the free energies of reaction for the mechanisms plausible of the dismutation radical and superoxide and decomposition of the hydrogen peroxide were evaluated as the functional density theory with the functional M06-2X and the model of SMD. Subsequently, a topological analysis of the electronic density was performed (using mechanisms of the superoxide and the organic mechanism) according to the theory of the organic gases and to the, training other reactive species.
