# FREIGHT TRANSPORTATION ZONING SCHEME FOR LAND USE PLANNING PROCESSES

In urban contexts, freight movements are associated with an increase of negative social, environmental, and economic externalities. As freight demand has significantly increased in urban areas, planners must account for freight in their analyses to align their decision-making with more realistic urban growth and transportation realities. However, such planning processes have been conducted using traditional traffic analysis zones (TAZs) which are mainly developed for passenger transportation. In this paper, the authors present a methodology for the spatial aggregation of urban areas considering microscopic freight movement data to create freight transportation analysis zones (FTAZs). The methodology is based on unsupervised machine learning algorithms and implemented in a case study in Colombia. The resulting clusters were aggregated based on the current TAZs to compare both schemes and, based on Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) method, to get a continuous representation of zones. As a result, 257 polygons of TAZs were merged into 100 with a good performance for planning use, considering natural and artificial boundaries. KDE presents better goodness of fit and more compactness in clusters than aggregation based on TAZs. The methodology contributes to freight-efficient land-use planning and supports policymakers in proposing programs and initiatives to mitigate the negative impacts of the cargo movement in urban areas, especially in developing economies
