# Mature retroperitoneal teratoma with somatic malignant transformation to a non-rhabdomyosarcomatous sarcoma: a case report

Germ cell tumors (GCTs) have the capacity for totipotential differentiation and, in rare cases, may undergo malignant somatic transformation. The most common histologic subtypes include rhabdomyosarcoma, primitive neuroectodermal tumor, squamous cell carcinoma, and acute myeloid leukemia. These cases are infrequent but well-documented in adults, while similar cases in children are rare. We present the case of a primary retroperitoneal germ cell tumor with malignant somatic transformation manifesting as a non-rhabdomyosarcoma sarcoma. This manuscript was prepared following the CARE guidelines (https://www.care-statement.org) Case presentation: A 10-year-old male patient presented with abdominal and costal margin pain. Contrast-enhanced abdominal computed tomography revealed a retroperitoneal mass measuring 120 × 121 × 116 mm. Serum alpha-fetoprotein and human chorionic gonadotropin levels were undetectable. Complete resection of the mass was performed via open surgery, identifying a 14 cm solid-cystic tumor adjacent to the body and tail of the pancreas. Histopathological analysis confirmed a mature teratoma with somatic-type malignancy manifesting as a non-rhabdomyosarcoma sarcoma. The tumor was classified as stage III, T2BN0M0. Oncologic treatment was directed at the somatic component, consisting of adjuvant chemotherapy for non-rhabdomyosarcoma sarcoma and radiotherapy to the primary site. At the end of treatment, a new lesion was detected, which was biopsied, ruling out tumor recurrence. One year after completing treatment, the patient remains asymptomatic with no evidence of disease recurrence. We present the case of a pediatric patient with a primary retroperitoneal teratoma that underwent malignant somatic transformation into a non-rhabdomyosarcoma sarcoma. This type of transformation is rare in pediatrics, and the identified histologic subtype has been infrequently reported. We emphasize the importance of appropriate surgical management with complete tumor resection. The optimal oncologic treatment remains undefined; in this case, therapy was directed at the identified sarcomatous component, yielding a favorable oncologic outcome
