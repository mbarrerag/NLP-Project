# Assessment of blue carbon storage in the mangrove ecosystem of the Nariñense Pacific.

Mangroves represent large carbon reserves, especially in soil, however, at local scales it is necessary to improve the accuracy of their estimates with field sampling, allowing adequate management of the ecosystem. Thus, the objective was to evaluate the total carbon stored in the mangrove ecosystem of the Nariñense Pacific. Forest inventory information was used at 10 sites to determine carbon stored in the aerial (AGB) and underground biomass (BWG) by allometric equations and biomass to carbon conversion factors. For carbon stored in the soil (COS) a Random Forest (RF) model was constructed with 28 profiles taken at two meters depth and 18 predictor variables. A good adjustment of the RF model (R2 of 0.82) was found. Total carbon stored presented an average of 359.05 ±71.29 t ha-1, where the greatest contribution was made by soil (75.51%), followed by aerial biomass (17.24%) and underground biomass (7.25%).
