# ALTROPOLOGIES OF CHRISTIANIS. PERSPECTIVES STATUS FROM SOUTH CHAPTER: BETWEEN RUPTURA AND CONTINUITY; CONVERSION POLICIES IN GUANIA

This book aims to give a wide range of anthropological studies on the sociocultural dimensions of Latin American Christianity. In particular, we are interested in critically rethinking the theoretical proposal of an anthropology of Christianity to understand Latin American religious configurations, as well as problematizing conceptualizations on conversion, culture, modernity and social change, among the main ones. The work offers a set of works located in different geographic, historical and cultural scenarios of South America. Three central parts define it: (1) the critical analysis on the contributions and conceptual problems of the aspect of self-defined studies as anthropology of Christianity, taking as key axes the categories of modernity, culture and Christianity within the framework of postcolonial criticism, (2) ethnographic and historical research on Catholic and evangelical missionaryization processes among indigenous peoples of the South American lowlands, problematizing theoretical tropes such as conversion, cultural mediation and social imaginarys; (3) the inquiry on dissimilar religious configurations of Catholicism in urban and rural contexts, exploring their devotional forms, their religious aesthetics and their national and national meanings; (3) the study of dissimilar religious configurations of Catholicism in urban and rural contexts, exploring their religious designs and their national designs;
