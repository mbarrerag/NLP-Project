# THE CONCEPT OF IMPRONTA AND ITS USE IN THE LITERATURA OF CARE AND REHABILITATION OF FAUNA SILVESTRE

The parrots of the Amazona genus are among the birds most affected by the illegal trafficking of wildlife. The research line that supports this article aims to understand the various ways in which the behavior of the parrots is affected by trafficking, which usually occurs when specimens are in early stages of development and are exposed to people who can produce a response of habitation to humans. In order to analyze the learning mechanisms that mediate this response, a review of the phenomenon of imprint was carried out, finding that the term is used in an inaccurate way in literature and in contexts of management in captivity and rehabilitation of wildlife. The origin and nature of the imprint is discussed and its most characteristic elements are described, presenting its theoretical bases and taxonomy of this phenomenon. Multiple examples of the incorrect use of the term in academic and non-academic literature related to the management of wildlife are presented. Finally, some examples of the inaccurate use of the concept are analyzed, the most frequent errors of interpretation are discussed, and the most appropriate terms are suggested to refer to animals that are used as human. The use of the term is impreclusive, the term is widespread in several areas of wildlife, and the best use of the concept is considered to be of wild fauna
