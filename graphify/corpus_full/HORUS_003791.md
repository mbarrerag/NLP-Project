# Integrative Taxonomy of the Neotropical and Endemic Genus of Colombia Argopleura Eigenmann, 1913 (Characiformes: Characidae: Stevardiinae)

The genus Argopleura currently has four valid species, endemic of the basins Cauca-Magdalena, San Juan-Atrato and Baudó. Delimiting their species has represented a great problem, due to the use of highly polymorphic characteristics. This study addressed these difficulties by an integrative approach that combines morphological and genetic analysis based on the cytochrome oxidase I (COI). Three valid species were identified, A. magdalenensis, A. conventus and A. chocoensis, being final the brother group of their congeners. Furthermore, it is proposed to consider A. diquensis to be a junior synonym of A. conventus, due to the overlap of the characters originally proposed in the description and the phylogenetic results that support it, when placing the individuals of both species in the same class. For A. magdalenensis, three clados, A. B and C were identified in the system in the basins of Cauca and Magdalena. Although A. magdalenensis has a geographical structure, morphologically they were not. conventus, and A. chocoensis, with the latter being the sister group of its congeners. Furthermore, it is proposed to consider A. dichensis as a junior synonym of A. conventus, due to the overlap of the characters originally proposed in the description and the phylogenetic results plastic individuals of both species in the same clade. For A. magdalenensis, three clades were identified, designated as clade A, B, and C in the Cauca and Magdalena bases system. Although A. magdalenensis shows geographical construction, morphologically no diagnostic differences were found to consider them as distinguished species.
