# PEDAGOGAGES TO LIVE: CARE PRACTICES OF THE TERRITORY IN THE ADDED CONFLICT ARMED IN BENNAVENTURA, COLOMBIA

In this article, I analyze the practices of defense of the body-territory that teachers work in schools affected by the armed conflict in the city of Buenaventura, in the Colombian Pacific. From an ethnographic work, I highlight how the armed conflict and capitalist predation of the ethnic territory of Buenaventura have affected schools and the reproduction of the lives of the black communities that live there. In the face of the practices of terror, dispossession and steritorialization of the armed actors, the Buenos Aires black teachers have responded with pedagogy to grasp life. These pedagogy articulate strategies of teaching, care relations and ancestral knowledge that allow to sustain the bodies and the school territories in despair. It is concluded that care is a form of political-pedagogical agency to recover and heal the territory, but it is also a ambivalent practice, populated by pain, inequalities and contradictions. In this sense, care is highlighted as a central analytical key to understanding the possible relations between education, resistance and defense of the Afro-Colombian ancestral territories
