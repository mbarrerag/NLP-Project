# Governance, agricultural production practices and contributions of nature to people in coffee socio-ecological systems

The productive coffee landscapes (PPC) constitute a source of food, cultural identity, social life, aesthetic value and rural life.However, the complex dynamics in which coffee growing is immersed have generated a great pressure on the CFPs, which compromises the potential supply of other contributions of nature to the people (NPC) and undermines the natural bases of the same coffee growing. The intensification of the coffee growing contributes to the creation of landscapes with a high supply of coffee at the expense of other NCPs (trade-off) while other productive alternatives can favor the provision of various contributions. These productive alternatives are viabilitated, among other forms, through the governance since the latter can orient, through the governance interventions, the decisions of agricultural management towards certain production models. In order to contribute to the understanding of the governance modes and their impact on agricultural production practices and therefore, in the potential provision of other NCPs in a socio-ecological system (SSE) coffee, in this the thesis was proposed, by means of a qualitative approach research, to analyze the typology of governance and the practices of the practices of the have generated great pressure on PPC, which concludes the potential provision of other national contributions to people (NCP) and undermine the natural foundations of coffee farming itself. The intensification of coffee farming contributions to the creation of landscapes with a high supply of coffee at the expense of other NCP (trade-off), while other productive alternatives can favour the provision of various contributions. These productive alternatives are made viable, together other ways, through governance since the impact on agriculture, then governance interventions, agricultural management decisions to be binding production models. which highlights market participation; and a trend towards the adoption of intensive agricultural practices. Likewise, the results reveal the complexity of interactions of framework conditions for coffee growing that can influence the decision making of coffee growers
