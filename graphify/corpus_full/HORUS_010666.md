# Effect of the application of resistance inducers in carnation (Dianthus caryophyllus L) on the in vivo expression of encoding genes for candidates for virulence factors of the pathogen Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. dianthi.

Thiamin has been postulated as an inducing potential for resistance to vascular wilting caused by Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. dianthi (Fod), since its foliar spraying in carnation plants significantly reduces the severity of the disease.However, until now the biochemical phenomena involved in the induction of resistance in this model are unknown, for example, the effect that this type of substances can have on the pathogen is not known.This study investigated the effect of thiamine on mycelial growth and the transcriptional levels of the sod1, sed4, six7 and six10 genes which encode for possible virulence factors of this key pathogen of the carnation.For this purpose, the in vitro cultivation of the pathogen was first performed and the presence of thiamin was determined on growth and transcription levels of sod1 and sed.4.
