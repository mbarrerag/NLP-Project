# The resolution of disputes among indigenous peoples in the Caribbean region over alternative conflict resolution mechanisms (MASC) and restorative justice in Colombia.

The issue of resolving conflicts in Colombia's history has had a hard time moving beyond legal life, however, in the context of indigenous communities, especially in the areas analysed here, it is a subject that has always been present, since under its uses, traditions and customs the resolution of conflicts has marked courses that are closely linked with the general interest and the right to peace, under the perspective of the Wayuu and the individual and collective peace that must reign within them from the Arhuaca world view, through the figures of the Pütchip and Mamos who, under their principles, cosmogonía, cosmovision and teachings, are the suitable people and called to intervene in these processes. Given these considerations, the present research focuses on comparing the mechanisms for resolving conflicts of the indigenous communities of the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta (Arhuacos) and the peninsula of La Guajira (Wayuu) with the alternative mechanisms of conflict resolution that are included in the Colombian domestic regulatory system, with which we can observe the direct practice in both the above-mentioned communities
