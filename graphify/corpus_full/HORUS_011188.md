# IN SEARCH OF ALTERNATIVES TO THE LITHIUM: THE CHALLENGES OF THE PRODUCTION OF BATTERIES,

Batteries are one of the most commercially available energy storage systems and have been widely used to power portable electronic devices [1,2,3,4]. This widespread use of batteries has transformed our daily lives and is leading the future of energy-dependent, interconnected and multi-functional devices [5]. For example, the Internet of Things (IoT) integrates devices that function not only as sensors, but also as transmitters of detection signals, and these devices require batteries with a higher level of performance (higher energy density and long life cycle) to power their operations [5,6]. To meet the requirements of these applications (size, portability and flexibility), rapid progress has been made in exploring new materials for battery components, cathode [7,8,9], anode [10,11,12), electrolyte [13,14,15] and separators [16,17]. Any decision on the next generation of batteries will have to go beyond testing and error towards material-based selection.
