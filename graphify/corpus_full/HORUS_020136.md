# Pattern representations for classifying (non-)metric (non-)vectorial data with applications in Structural Health Monitoring and geotechnical/natural-hazard engineering

Currently, the Recognition of Statistical Patterns and Machine Learning provide powerful and versatile tools for predictive modelling of problems of civil, mechanical and geo-engineering structures. Unlike the main trends in the state of art in substitutes based on data on structural and geo-engineering problems, this thesis focuses on the representation of data. First, for vector data of slope/slip, vector spaces based on characteristics are enriched and improved according to the principle of Occam's knife or parsimony, which is achieved by three simple but powerful variants already existing from the nearest neighboring qualifier. Second, for non-vectorial data belonging to Structural Health Monitoring, they are constructed, powerful and highly discriminating, disimilitude spaces using spectral/time-frequency information, taking a proximity-based learning scheme. Second, for non-vectorial data from Structural Health Monitoring, they are constructed, powerful and highly discriminating, spaces using spectral/time-frequency information, taking a proximity-based learning scheme. In both cases, the results demonstrate the importance of appropriate data representation and their influence on the incremental design of substitute models
