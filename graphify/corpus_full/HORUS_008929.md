# CLINICAL SIMULATION FROM INSURANCE STUDENTS: A STRATEGY FOR IMPROVING INSURANCE CARE IN A HOSPITALIZATION SERVICE

Hospital services require the innovation of continuous education for Nursing, in search of improving quality care and creating an interactive environment between students and healthcare staff. To evaluate the effect of training based on clinical simulation in nursing care in hospitalization of a University hospital in Bogotá, Colombia. Simulation was used as a pedagogical strategy to provide a realistic and safe learning environment, in what was called the Care Fair, intervening 49 professionals and 89 nursing assistants of the hospitalization service. Through interactive stations led by undergraduate nursing students, clinical scenarios were recreated in three areas of interest: prevention of falls, care with the bladder catheter-associated infections and prevention of vascular catheter-associated infections. Participants received prior theoretical instruction. The level of knowledge before and after the intervention was evaluated, as well as adherence to the protocol in each area. An increase in the level of knowledge in prevention of falls from 93.1% to 94.4%, in the evening study from 81.7% to 98%, and prevention of infections associated with peripheral vascular catheters from 57% to 70% and central protocols from 50% to 95%
