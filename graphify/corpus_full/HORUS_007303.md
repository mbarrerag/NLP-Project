# Interaction of Rhizophagus irregularis and phosphorus-solubilizing microorganisms and their effect on the growth of Gmelina tree in nursery

Gmelina arborea is an important species for forest development, due to its rapid growth, ease of management and commercialization. However, its production faces limitations associated with nutritional requirements, particularly phosphorus (P), an essential nutrient that limits growth in more than 50% of forest production. To improve the availability of P and reduce the use of chemical fertilizers in its productive stage, the use of solubilizing microorganisms (MSF) and phosphorus mobilizers has been proposed, whose interactions are not yet fully understood. Therefore, this study evaluated the interaction between (MSF) (Pseudomonas orientalis, Paenibacillus taichungensis, Penicillium chrysogenum and Penicillium citrinum) and the Mushroom Forming of Arbuscular Micorrizases (HFMA) Rhizophage irregularis in Gmelina arborea in living conditions under different levels of phosphatated fertilization was performed with 3 factors, 18 treatments and 5 replicates during 120 days. The results of height, diameter, diameter and percentage of the soil were analyzed. It recommends that future studies include experiments with marked phosphorus to better understand their distribution in the plant and the interactions between inoculated micro-organisms and native microbial communities .
