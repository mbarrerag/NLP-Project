# INDUCTED COMPACTATION DURING THE MECHANICAL PRESENTATION OF SACCHArum SP. AND THEIR RELATIONSHIP TO STRUCTURAL SOIL PROPERTIES

Characterizing the compaction induced during the Saccharum spp. harvest with autovolteer wagons, in terms of mechanical resistance to penetration and apparent wet density considering its spatial variability. In two batches of sugar cane cultivation, located in the department of Valle del Cauca, Colombia, the monitoring was carried out to the changes in the physical variables of the soil caused by different intensity of machinery traffic during the mechanized harvest; the variables of interest were subjected to statistical and geostatistical analysis. The apparent density in initial humid influenced the compaction of the soils subject to study, while, when its magnitude was greater (1.67 Mg/m 3 ), the susceptibility to compaction was lower, finding a mechanical resistance to the maximum average penetration of 1.83 MPa; compared to the occurrence, when its magnitude was lower (1.56 Mg/m 3 ) the compaction was greater, finding a mechanical resistance to the maximum penetration of 2.46 MPa.
