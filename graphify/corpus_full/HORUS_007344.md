# Analysis of the availability and access of food for banana-producing families located in the village of La Arabia in the municipality of Tame, Arauca

Food and nutrition security is one of the fundamental pillars to ensure the well-being and sustainable development of the communities. In Colombia, 63.8% of households in the Orinoquía-Amazonía region have food insecurity, which is greater by 10 percentage points than the national prevalence. In the department of Arauca, the country's first banana producer and key crop in the local economy and diet, 90.3% of families live in food insecurity. In a context of high food insecurity, this research focuses on identifying the availability and access of food in banana producing families in the area of the province of Arabia, Tame, Arauca, assessing how productive and socio-economic dynamics affect their food security. A qualitative methodology was used that included semi-structured interviews of 20 families and systematic analysis. The results reveal that 75% of the income are used for agricultural inputs and food, reflecting dependence on banana monoculture (60 per cent of local production) and the existence of limitations for diversifying crops, although 70% of the producers are land owners.
