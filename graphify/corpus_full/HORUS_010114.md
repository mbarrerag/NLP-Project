# An integrated approach to the prioritization of adaptation strategies for the artisanal fishermen of Tumaco Nariño against climate and non-climate stressors.

Scientific evidence indicates that climate change is responsible for increasing frequency, intensity and duration of extreme climate events, increasing land and marine temperature, variation of marine currents, among other effects; which have an impact especially on artisanal fishing activity, as a result of the adaptive response of marine species. In coastal regions such as Tumaco, fishing communities are considered highly sensitive in terms of multiple stressors – additional to the climate challenges that arise for systems and livelihoods – such as marine pollution and poverty. This research paper proposes an integrated approach to identify and prioritize the climatic and non-climatic stressors that affect artisanal fisheries, as well as adaptation strategies in response to key stressors. The approach includes both qualitative and quantitative elements and involves community interaction mechanisms. Network analysis techniques were implemented to understand synergies and prioritize the “climate and non-climatic factors” factors (Tuc) affecting the San Andrés de Tumaco Economic Units.
