# URBANIZATION AND REVOLUTION IN LATIN AMERICA. SANTIAGO DE CHILE, GOOD AIRES AND CITY OF MEXICO, 1950-1980

In this book he studies from the historical point of view the classic problem of social sciences about the political meaning of the urban masses, that is, whether the social changes brought about by the urbanization of Latin America and the transfer of millions of people from the countryside to the city implied a threat capable of subverting the capitalist social order or could be employed to ensure the continuity of the system in a situation of accelerated transformation of society. It is a story about the social technologies that were tested in Santiago de Chile, Buenos Aires and Mexico City, in the face of the dilemmas posed by the emergence of the urban masses in the second half of the twentieth century. It is a social history of the social sciences, but in the sense of knowledge applied, tested, reconstructed and questioned when they are put into play, in a contingent way, with the historical knowledge of urban struggles. Thus, this is not a history of science and technique or of the formation of relatively autonomous fields of knowledge that are concerned with cities in a strict sense, but of how knowledge occurs, reorganized and questioned when it becomes a method of managing social change set out in the urban neighborhoods, this decade
