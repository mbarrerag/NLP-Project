# HYDROCLIMATIC IMPACTS OF AMAZONIAN DEFORESTATION OVER SOUTH AMERICA

We discusses the hydrological, climatic and biogeochemical impacts of deforestation in the Amazon River basin, including: (1) imbalance and breakdown of water, energy and carbon balances; (2) severe alteration of various mechanisms and processes of atmospheric and oceanic dynamics on a planetary scale; (3) increase in air temperature and reduction of precipitation and evapotranspiration, aggravating climate change due to global warming; (4) causing longer dry seasons and greater water deficits; (5) loss of recycled precipitation; (6) weakening and collapse of the moisture circulation cascade in the soil-vegetation-atmosphere system; (7) high risk of forest collapse and permanent "savanization" of the Amazon; (8) weakening of moisture transport by aerial rivers and surface wind jets in South America; (9) decreased moisture transport to the Andes, jeopardizing the water supply for cities such as Bogota, Quito, Lima and La Paz, and contributing to the melting and retreat of glaciers and tropical páramos; (10) affected air quality in South America due to forest fires associated with deforestation; (11) altered air quality in South America due to forest fires associated with deforestation; (11) alteration of the South American surface stream, threatening water supply and air quality in the La Plata river basin; (12) loss of the "forest reservoir" and intensification of extreme hydrological events; (13) collapse of the biotic pumping mechanism of atmospheric humidity exerted by forests. Finally, the work of the Scientific Panel for Amazonia is presented
