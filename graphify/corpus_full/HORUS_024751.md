# DIVERSIFICATION OF THE MANAGEMENT AND FINANCING MECHANISMS FOR URBAN DEVELOPMENT AS A NEOLIBERAL STRATEGY

The recent structuring of urban planning in Medellín has involved a diversification of urban planning and management mechanisms gradually incorporated since the 1980s as a neoliberal strategy to compensate for the State’s withdrawal in spatial regulation functions; to understand this, a framework of interpretation is used on the concept of the rescaled urban regime and the form of neoliberal state that has produced it. Recently, Medellin has been the subject of large urban interventions including social urbanism, positioning it internationally as a city model; however, these have occurred within a neoliberal urban regime that seeks to open new spaces to involve them in the capital market: renewal, redensification, expansion and multinucleation of urban-regional functions, with a tendency towards the reduction of developmental policies to promote self-construction and the promotion of the formalization of informal habitat with impacts on urban income distribution; this has been achieved through the structuring of so-called urban treatments as a strategy at the polygon or fragmental level of city that has allowed, on the one hand, to consolidate the pre-built model of development, and on the other hand, to favour the formation of an associated model of a coordinated one
