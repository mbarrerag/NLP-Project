# Classification of patients with suspected acute coronary syndrome according to the universal definition

Summary Objective: To describe the clinical, imaging and laboratory characteristics of patients taken to coronary arteriography with a presumptive diagnosis of acute coronary syndrome, in the period between January 2107 and December 2019; as well as the frequencies of the types heart attack according to the universal definition. a cross-sectional observational study of patients over 18 years of age taken for coronary angiography, at a university hospital in Bogotá. Using the STATA software, statistical analysis was performed, with calculation of absolute frequencies, proportions, measures of central tendency and dispersion according to the behavior of the variables. The classification was made of patients according to the fourth universal definition of infarction, identifying each of the respective frequencies. 714 medical records were analyzed, corresponding to 459 men and 255 women, with arterial hypertension, dyslipidemia, obesity, diabetes and previous coronary heart disease as the most frequent cardiovascular risk factors. 68.9% of patients had an electrocardiogram interpreted as abnormal and only 43.8% of patients had obstructive lesions, of the which 33.2% corresponded to patients with type I infarction. A prevalence of 12.2% for type II infarction and 17% for the MINOCA category. In-hospital mortality was of 2.5%, mainly due to cardiogenic shock. The male population with cardiovascular risk factors, predominated in the presentation of acute coronary syndrome. For this study, the majority of patients did not have obstructive lesions on arteriography. However, within the types of heart attack; the type heart attack I was the most frequent.
