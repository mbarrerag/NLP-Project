# Molecular evolution of zoonotic Betacoronavirus associated with Acute Respiratory Distristion Syndrome (APRS).

The Betacoronaviruses have caused their passage deadly epidemics, such as the outbreak of SARS-CoV of 2002 and the continuous prevalence of the MERS-CoV which was first detected in 2012. At the end of 2019, the PDVI19 pandemic was initiated, which led scientists from around the world to apply their respective knowledge to address how SARS-CoV-2 infects humans. The main strategy has been the implementation of conventional health surveillance systems to identify, intervene and control viral infections caused by these emerging viruses. Although the monitoring of the genetic evolution of the virus has been of great importance, the ZVI and SAVI of the VVI has been unknown to what extent zoonotic transmission between susceptible and non-a susceptible animal species is possible, as well as the possible functionality of the structural architecture of the RNA of the Betacoronavirus in the physiopathology, mainly for OSA-CoV, MS-CoV of the reported viral sequences from the intermediate data and to the functional data. To fill this gap and facilitate the development of effective treatments, a comprehensive study of the genomes of the Betacoronavirus was carried out by the analysis of 1,252,95,952 and 5 Small viral RNAs, with a possible function associated with pathogenesis in the selection process. More than 87.58% of these RNA structures indicate that 12 regions carry small RNAs in Betacoronaviruses, suggesting the possibility of modulating the transcriptional reprogramming of the new host after infection. The findings of this study provide a series of significant molecular patterns that contribute to expanding the boundaries of human therapy in the context of the current global health crisis
