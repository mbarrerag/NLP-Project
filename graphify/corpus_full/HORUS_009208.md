# Cross-world border: the indigenous habitat of the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta in the face of a context of touristisation

The present study address the indigenous habitat of the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta. It specifically addresses that expansion of tourism in the last 20 years has for the habitat. Tourism is seen as a way of inhabiting and the tourist as an inhabitant, so it can be applied as part of the field of habitat. Tourism has the potential to transform the habitat, a process that has been called “tourism”. It poses tourism to the autonomy of indigenous peoples and their possibilities of applying their habitat. Tourism has the potential to transform the habitat through a phenomenon that has been called “touristification”. This poses challenges to the autonomy of the indigenous peoples and the possibilities of appropriation of their habitat. Thus, an ethnographic and historical approach was established to the indigenous habitat of the Sierra Nevada and in particular to the Ikğ population located in the limits of the Arhuaco Reserve of the Sierra Nevada with the urban centre of the municipality of Pueblo Bello, Cesar. It was evidenced that a habitat of edge has occurred historically where the indigenous and the external converge, which today favors turistification. The indigenous community, for its part, presents resistances, but also negotiations with the dynamics of edge and the mentioned turistification.
