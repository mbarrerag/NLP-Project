# ASSESSMENT AND INVENTORY OF THE SILVE SPECIES FOR CULTIVATES

The part of biodiversity recognized for its direct and immediate utility is the agrobiodiversity or diversity of plants, animals and microorganisms for food and agriculture. Within agricultural or crop agrobiodiversity, the wild species related to crops, known internationally as Crop Wild Relatives (CWR) are also a component essential for the future of agriculture, as the largest source of genes and features of interest.CWR species have characteristics that differentiate them in a strong way from their relatives cultivated, but maintain certain possibilities of transferring by hybridization (natural or induced by man) traits that the cultivated forms have lost or never had and that could provide to the crops the recognized resilience or ability to adapt to the changing environmental conditions that are expected in agriculture in the present and future. There is also another group of wild plants of interest and those of wild plants of food use, of low value, of genetic value not suffered by all forms of domestication, but that humans extract from natural ecosystems or disturbated and use for their food
