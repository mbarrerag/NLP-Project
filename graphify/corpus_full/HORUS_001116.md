# Determination of the immersion enthalpy of graphene oxide in aqueous solutions of emerging organic compounds with pharmaceutical activity

The immersion enthalpy of graphene oxide (GO) in aqueous solutions of phenol, salicylic acid, acetaminophen, and methylparaben—emerging contaminants—was determined to understand the interaction process between the solid and the solution. The immersion enthalpy of GO in water and benzene was also measured, revealing that the adsorbent solid has a hydrophobic nature. The results are exothermic, with values for the change in immersion enthalpy, ΔHimm, in the aqueous solutions ranging from −9.32 to −11.9 Jg−1. These experimental results indicate that different systems require energy to facilitate the displacement of the solvent during the adsorption process. It is calculated from the experimental results of the change in the immersion enthalpy, ΔHimm, the change in the enthalpy of interaction between the solid and the organic compound, which leads to the fact that the different systems require energy to be able to carry out the displacement of the solvent in the adsorption process
