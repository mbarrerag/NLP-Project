# On the structure of the transition bark goes down to the mantle in an Andean margin with an accretion heritage, North-West Andes.

The cortex-manto transition under the North-West-nor-west Andes is expected to be complex, given its tectonic history involving block accre. Taking into account that research on this subject remains scarce, especially in the Colombian region, this thesis presents new knowledge on the structure and nature of the cortex-mant transition in several parts of the orogen. Four chapters are presented, in which we analyze: (1) the variations in the depth of the Moho along the orogen by inverting gravity data; (2) the latitutial heterogeneity in the upper mant under the modern arc by means of estimates of the speed of the waves Pn and Sn, anisotropy and thermo-compoposical modeling; (3) the nature of the root of the arc below the modern arch by means of the analysis of the receiving function; and (4) the intra-continental deformation under the plateau of the Eastern Cordillera, by the Eastern COR, by the joint investment of the times of arrival of local earthquakess and by the severity data. The integrated results suggest three main features of the in the grossing of the bark of the bark along the middle-in-low-in The latitudinal anisotropy and temperature dissimilarities are probably influenced by the lithospheric tear of Caldas to the north, which causes the entrance of hot mantle, and the interaction of the Carnegie dorsal to the south, which allows a less profound subduction. Additionally, research on the arc region, using the technique of telesysmic receptor function, which seeks conversions from phase P to S, suggests that the cortical root under the modern arc shows high speeds with a variable thickness in latitude, which, together with documented xenolytes, support an arclegitic nature. This high speed and density layer suggests a lag between the seismic Moho and the manth cortex boundary of about 8.5-14 km. Finally, under the Eastern Cordillera plateau, a heptom is identified towards a well constricted anomaly at depths of 40-60 km under the western flank of the plateau, at a latitude of ~5.7°N.
