# Effect of wetting and drying cycles on suction, of a residual amphibolitic soil from western Medellín in a natural and compacted state.

The stability of the slopes is affected by the diversity of physical and chemical phenomena, the climate is one of the most important factors in this aspect. In the tropical zones there are great meteorological variations throughout the year, and it is the superficial soils the most susceptible to change of humidity and temperature that impact on their behavior and resistance. To evaluate the influence of these variations, several cycles of wetting and drying were developed for the soil of a road slope located to the west of the Aburra Valley. Laboratory tests were initially carried out in unaltered and compacted samples and subsequently in samples subjected to wetting and drying to the furnace at 30°C. The physical and chemical characterization of the soil is performed initially and the suction curve is measured by the filter paper method, the permeability and resistance parameters for each cycle of wetting and drying.
