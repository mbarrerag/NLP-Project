# Interpretations on the territory from different actors related to rurality

The territory has been interpreted in various ways from different scientific disciplines and under the influence of multiple historical and social contexts. Therefore, it is a concept that changes in time and space, according to the interpretation of the researcher and the actors, who understand it, compose and transform it. Currently, the dominant interpretation, which governs the public policies of Latin American States, is the result of the Technical Approach to Rural Development, which implemented a development model based on modernization, neoliberalism, and more recently, under the so-called Territorial Approach to Rural Development. This model, understands the territory as a physical space, clearly delimited, where the State exercises sovereignty. In its implementation, it generates hunger, energy insustainability, environmental deterioration, poverty and inequality, and introduces this concept as a discursive element, away from reality, that ignores the social mobilizations and expressions of resistance and confrontation, product of the negative effects that it motivates. In order to overcome this approach, this work will analyze how the territory is interpreted by academic, institutional and rural actors. To achieve this objective, a methodology of qualitative analysis and inductive reasoning will be implemented
