# Non-linear valuation of financial derivatives in iliquidity markets through extensions of Feynman-Kac theorem and machine learning algorithms

This research proposes and develops four models of unliquid financial markets, in which the dynamics of the price of the risky assets and the emerging relationship between that dynamic and the trading strategies of the agents are characterized. In addition, the corresponding partial differential equations for the valuation of contingent assets are deduced. Specifically, they are presented: 1. A market model with an iliquidity factor proportional to the price of the asset; 2. A model in which the iliquidity is a function of the asset price; 3. A model that includes proportional iliquidity, with the presence of noisy agents; 4. A model in which the iliquidity is stochastic and is described by means of a process of reversion to the mean of square root type. The valuation equations obtained are nonlinear extensions of the partial differential equation of Black-Scholes, where the nonlinearity results from the feedback effect associated with the iliquidity of the market. A model in which liquidity is static and described by a means-reverting square-root processes. The resulting assessment equations are nonlinear extensions of the Black-Scholes partial differential equation, where the nonlinearity risks from the feedback effect associated with market illiquidity.
