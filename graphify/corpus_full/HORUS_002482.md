# ANFIFILIC COPOLIMERS SYNTHESIS AS PREcursors OF MICELLARY VEHICLES IN ANALGESIC FORMULATIONS BASED ON CANNABI DERIVATIVES

In recent years, Cannabis sativa has been recognized as a valuable source of drugs due to its multiple biological activities. Compounds such as THC and CBD have emerged as promising to treat diseases such as cancer, arthritis and chronic pain, modulating CB1 and CB2 receptors [1]. Despite scientific interest and evidence, the low oral bioavailability of these compounds remains a challenge [2]. This study was synthesized copolymers in block conjugated with fatty acids as precursors of mycelar vehicles for cannabis extracts rich in CBD and THC, in order to improve their analgesic effect. The synthesis of the copolymers involved the copolymerization by opening e-caprolactone ring and a cyclic carbonate with alquine groups initiated by polyethylene glycol, followed by a click-al-based/azilized approach with fatty acid derivatives. The resulting copolymers efficiently encapsulated the extracts, presented nanometric size and high colloidal stability in the simulated gastrine fluids.
