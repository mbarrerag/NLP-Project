# Skeletal dysplasias in patients diagnosed with Achondroplasia: morphological and social implications

Title: Skeletal dysplasias in patients diagnosed with achondroplasia: morphological and social implications. Statement of the problem: skeletal dysplasias entail physical and neurodevelopmental characteristics that can put mental health at risk and cause the disease to worsen. The existence of areas of study that require observational and experimental research to provide the patient with holistic therapy is undeniable. to describe, from a morphological point of view, the different skeletal dysplasias in patients diagnosed with achondroplasia, members of the Little Giants Association of Colombia, and their relationship with the personal and social context, in a documentary audiovisual project. exploratory/descriptive research was carried out, adopting a combination of qualitative and quantitative methods. The information was obtained using the semi-structured interview as a research technique and a survey as a data collection instrument. the quality of life for people with achondroplasia is diminished over the years, conditions such as short stature, chronic pain, limited mobility, difficulty walking and a greater predisposition to gain weight; It represents a brake on carrying out daily activities (individual and collective), the difficulty in structural and architectural access and dealing with stigmatizing issues at a social level. these conditions have an absolute impact on all aspects of the lives of those affected, which goes beyond what current evidence addresses. Therefore, it is imperative to prioritize the study of this condition through a project aimed at reducing the existing knowledge gap. achondroplasia, FGFR3, skeletal dysplasia, dwarfism, morphology, quality of life
