# CO2 EMISSION MODELING FOR INTER-CITY TRUCKING DEMAND IN COLOMBIA

Trucking is one of the most significant actors in supply chain pollution generation in developing countries like Colombia as it serves as the major transportation means for commodities. Pollution associated with trucking and its externalities over humans and ecosystems are studied in public health, environmental, planning, and economic scenarios. Using data acquired from the Colombian Department of Transportation (CDOT) information systems, the authors examined carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions produced by intercity trips of the most representative movement in Colombia. Using the explanatory variables traveled distance, roadway slope, and cargo movement, the Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) technique estimated econometric CO2 emission generation models (EGMs) between any Origin-Destination (O'D) pair. This research presents the first available EGMs at a country level, aiming to complement and generate information about pollution caused by freight transportation for future forecasting and policies focused on sustainable transportation. Freight policy outcomes and analyses are provided
