# SOCIAL NETWORKS.

Several studies show that the Internet and networks are increasingly used by large companies to disseminate social and environmental information, increasing accountability and transparency. However, there is still no clarity about the pros and cons of this process. This paper raises the need for a critical reinterpretation of transparency and social and environmental accountability through social networks. To do this, it takes up some arguments from the dialogical perspective of accountability, as well as approaches by the Korean philosopher Byung-Chul Han. The case of the mining company Vale is being studied, characterizing the way in which corporations use social networks arguing greater transparency and social and environmental accountability in the face of serious socio-environmental catastrophes. However, the case shows that such disclosure does not make visible the intrinsic conflicts of this activity, nor does it incorporate the visions, expectations and claims of those affected
