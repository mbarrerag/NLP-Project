# BEST CONTROL STRATEGY IN AN AGROFORESTAL SYSTEM WITH MINIMUM RESERVE

Agriculture and forests are essential resources for human beings, providing food and purifying the air, which is crucial for the health of the planet and people. However, intensive agriculture can lead to land degradation and deforestation in search of more arable land. Therefore, it is essential to allocate the workforce in an optimal manner to maximize the usefulness of production, improve quality of life and maintain ecological balance. This study focuses on maximizing the usefulness of production of a population engaged in both agriculture and forest management, using as control the proportion of people working in the forest sector. Two scenarios are analysed: one requiring a minimum forest reserve and the other where this restriction is not active. The results of the research indicate that when the restriction of the minimum reserve is active, greater utility is generated, underlining the importance of policies for the conservation and sustainable management of natural resources. Preserving part of the forest resource ensures a minimum basis of sustenance for the economic activities and needs of local communities
