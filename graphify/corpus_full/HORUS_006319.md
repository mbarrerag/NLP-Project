# Crossing borders: a dialogue between narrative practices and indigenous stories and traditional Western literature in the perspective of coloniale: challenging Western conceptions of literary authorship in the cultural transition of Iku students, from their territories to the universities of the city

In this work of memory, we explore how indigenous literature and more precisely the Iku literature especially during a period of cultural transition, through a decolonial perspective, defies the norms and traditional conceptions of the role of the author which is established in Western literature. We examine the dynamics of power that historically marginalized indigenous voices, thus influencing the production and legitimacy of knowledge. By integrating elements of the oral and visual traditions, the Iku literature not only preserves its cultural identity but also expands the understanding of what might be the literature by opposing the forms of colonialist and neocolonialist oppression.
