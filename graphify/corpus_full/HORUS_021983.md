# Study of the method of neutronic capture with Boro-10 for use in radiation therapy.

The neutronic capture therapy with boron (BNCT) is a type of radiation therapy, which aims to deliver a high and localized dose of radiation to a tumor tissue, while significantly minimizing the dose in the healthy tissue. The NCBT is performed with the concentration of low-energy neutrons, whose sources are nuclear reactors and particle accelerators, by 10B nuclei that are incorporated earlier into the tumor. The doses are obtained from secondary particles resulting from the nuclear reaction 10B(n, α)7Li, 7Li nuclei and alpha (α) particles, which deposit high energies in their trajectory, so that they attenuate the rapidly emitted radiation and penetrate little tissue around them (high linear energy transfer). In this work, the dosimetry of a neutral beam of energies interacting with a homogeneous mixture of 10B and water is studied. The result was that the dose ratio of the healthy tissue was changed according to the depth of the lesion, demonstrating that the localized dose deposit for this type of therapy is better in surface tumors, because the ratio of the dose between tissues is higher, meaning that the healthy tissue is radiated in a smaller amount. Finally, a radiation treatment plan was simulated for a tumor lesion located in the brain region, whose source of irradiation was the neutron spectrum of the nuclear research reactor IAN-R1 located in the Colombian Geological Service, after quantifying the dose received in risk structures, it was found that the ratio values between the dose of tumor tissue and that of healthy tissue was 8.3:1.
