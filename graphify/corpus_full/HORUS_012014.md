# A PARALYLE OF OPTISMATION BASED ON A METAHEURISTICS OF POPULATION AND TRAYECTORY TO REsolve THE PROBLEM OF TRAVEL LABOR ON GPUS.

In the work we consider the Problem of the Traveller Ledger (TTP: Traveling Thief Problem, by its acronym in English), a problem of combinatorial optimization NP-difficult. We will attack it through a modeling in parallel computing by means of an evolutionary algorithm improved by a local search heuristic, for problems of significant size. The problem has different possibilities to analyze and transform it into a problem of equivalent parallel nature, to such degree that it can be implemented its algorithmic solution in a parallel processing device such as a GPGPU (GPGPU: General Purpose Graphics Processing Unit, by its acronym in English). This work achieves the implementation of a metaheuristic based on population as a parallel algorithm of coarse grain and fine grain and improved with a metaheuristic based on trajectory as a heuristic of local search (to find an optimal solution or close to the optimal TTP). The model of fine grain combines the most representative characteristics of the population in the evolutionary algorithm (Tomassini, 1995), the model of coarse grain combines the most representative characteristics of populations (to be based on the spatially the domain of the mobile) and the functional problem (to the functional). Do you (or can you) if the traveler thief meets the following conditions: Do you visit all cities exactly once you start in the first city and return to it at the end? Can you select any object in any city as long as the total weight of the collected objects does not exceed the specified capacity? Will you pay an income rate for each unit of time taken to complete the trip? Will we notice with what, and what, the maximum and minimum speeds that the thief can take when traveling. The problem is solved by finding a route, along with a package plan of the objects, which results in the maximum benefit; that is, ensuring that the total accumulated gain in the backpack is maximized minus the rent, while ensuring that the capacity of the backpack is not exceeded. The performance of the algorithm is measured by executing some instances of different sizes (benchmark problems) referenced in the literature
