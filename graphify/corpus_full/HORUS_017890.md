# Morphological and molecular study in a model of Zika infection during embryonal neurogenesis.

The disease of the Zika virus (ZIbenV) has generated serious problems in public health since its appearance in the outbreaks of the Americas of 2015-2016. This disease can cause disorders in the neurodevelopment as microcephalus; a condition that generates, among others, motor, visual, auditory and cognitive dysfunctions.Despite a large number of publications on the pathogenesis of the ZIKV the mechanisms associated with the infection of the nervous system cells are still unknown, therefore, the objective of this work consisted in evaluating the morphological changes and the participation of markers of the neurodevelopment in the infection of the Zika during the embryonal neurogenesis in a murine model.To achieve this objective five female mice were inoculated BALB/c in the early stage of gestation E6,5 by the intraperitoneal route, with a dose of interferon and the following day (E7.5) with a dose of 9,4 x 105 PFU of the ZIKV. The euthasia of the females was performed in the embarinal state E14,5; at the moment of the constitus of the constituation of the contituation of the constituation of molecular coupling tests were performed between the selected proteins for the project (DCX, MCPH1, CEP152, RELN, TBR2, GFAP and NES) with ZIKV replication proteins NS4A and NS5 and pro-coupling energies were found for MCPH1, RELN, DCX and TBR2 with ZIKV NS4A. This study concludes that Zika virus generates morphological alterations such as the appearance of picnotic nuclei highly associated with its presence and viral replication. Changes in the expression of cortical and cerebelal neurodevelopment markers associated with ZIKV infection such as MCPH1, CEP-152, RELN, TBR2, are reported for the first time, in addition, this study is the first report in which molecular interaction between the neurodevelopment proteins MCPH1, DCX, RELN and TBR2 with viral replication protein NS4A are evidenced by the ZIKV and allow for future infection mechanisms
