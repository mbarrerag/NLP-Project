# Legal anti-formalism in the Council of State ' s interpretative practice

Although the application of formalist convictions has not been completely abandoned in the interpretation of the Council of State, it is true that the application of anti-formalist elements has gained relevance in recent time, so much so that it has meant a shift in the interpretive paradigm in administrative litigation. Therefore, it is pertinent to investigate the reasons why this change of theoretical perspective has occurred. This paper is concerned with examining how this change is due to the receipt of anti-formalist thesis provided by authors such as Hart, Dworkin and Alexy, together with the influence of the Political Constitution of 91. To glimpse this change, decisions issued by the Plena Chamber and the Third Section of our highest court of administrative litigation, particularly in matters of State responsibility and electoral matters, are observed how decisions that, connected with social and economic reality, are based on interpretations that consult the (constitutional) principles (stance of argumentation) and in the argumentation of a moral and political nature, show this change of a theoretical perspective in terms of interpretation leading to anti-formalism (Text of the source of the opinion). matters of State responsibility and electoral matters.
