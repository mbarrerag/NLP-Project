# Effect of the geometric parameters of the unit cell TPMS and the photopolymerisation printing parameters on the surface roughness of scaffolds for bone tissue culture in vitro

The main objective of this research is to determine the effect of the geometric parameters of the unitary cell TPMS and the printing parameters by photopolymerization processes in the surface roughness of scaffolds that can be used in culture of bone tissue in vitro. The methodology followed included the definition of the fundamental requirements for the scaffolds used in culture of bone tissue in vitro, the determination of the geometric and dimensional parameters of the unit cells studied; this by means of calculated manufacturing maps, considering the two ways of constructing the cells TPMS (Walled and Offset), the adequate poro size, porosity and the conditions of manufacture given by permeability and the construction of thin thicknesses by means of the photopolymerital process MSLA. Subsequently, the manufacture and measurement of the surface roughness was performed in accordance with the experimental design of Taguchi defined with a Giv arrangement.9. These results showed that there is a change in the value of the roughness between treatments; that is, there is influence of some of the factors. In addition, treatments can be grouped according to layer thickness, that is, those that share the same layer thickness show very close results. In general, it was observed that the variability of the data increases with layer thickness. The applied statistical tests showed that layer thickness is the only factor that significantly affects the variables Sa and RaL. However, for RAT, both the unit cell size and the layer thickness are significant. In conclusion, it is established that layer thickness is the most influential factor in the superficial roughness, regardless of the direction of measurement.
