# Analysis of tonal consonance in musical works from harmonic structures

The consonance is a property of the sounds subject to human perception and which is associated to how pleasant or unpleasant these can be for those who listen to them. From the approach of the tonal consonance, this phenomenon can be analyzed taking into account the physical properties of the sounds. In the case of sounds emitted by musical instruments, these are known as complex tones and are characterized by being described as composed by pure tones, each with a proper harmonic frequency. Each musical instrument has a specific timbre or pattern of overlap of pure tones that distinguishes it, and the characteristics of that pattern depend on the properties of consonance of the sounds emitted. The level of consonance of a complex tone, or the overlap of several of them, depends on the pure tones that make up it, and can be estimated from all possible combinations of these. In this work we will study the consonance properties associated with musical chords, for this purpose as a reference the dissonance value of each of three chords arranged in the 7 octaves of the piano, major chords, minor and their respective investments, with these results we will perform the analysis of the academic music pieces of different periods of the years of the consonance can be obtained from parameters used to describe the physical properties of sound, mainly the frequency of the pure tones, in this paper we study the consistency properties of chords, the progress of consistency in two works of different musical periods, and how the timbre and the use of the instrument's register effect this progress
