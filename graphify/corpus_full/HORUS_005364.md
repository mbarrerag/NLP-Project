# Coordination of the distribution of humanitarian aid with the restoration of transitory road disruptions in areas affected by sudden natural disasters with accessibility constraints

As a result of sudden natural disasters presented in the world, it has been demonstrated that the survival rate of the population is directly related to the delivery of supplies.The 72 hours following the disruptive event can be considered the period after which the probability of survival of the population can decrease drastically, when the degree of difficulty that people or communities have to meet their basic social and economic needs tends to increase by limitations of accessibility in the road network.In the literature two approaches have been identified to address the problem of delivery of humanitarian aid in areas with limitations of accessibility called distribution of aid and restoration of access.The first is to find transitable paths for the aid teams to reach the population, and the second generates a repair schedule to improve access to the areas of refugees or points of demand.In the literature both problems have been addressed in isolation form, through the development of models of decision-making that ignores the interrelationship between the processes and their impact on performance measures related to efficiency (minimization of operational costs) and efficiency (minimization of response time) and social welfare (minimization of the affected or cost of the outcome). The study has been carried out in the context of the implementation of the social assistance strategy, which has been overlooked in the literature. As a general contribution, the research has designed a proposal for coordination between the distribution of humanitarian aid and the transitional restoration of roads, based on the joint and collaborative decision in the development of activities framed at the operational level during the response phase; considering multiple performance measures related to the dimensions of efficiency, efficiency and social welfare under a multi-objective approach. Likewise, as an additional contribution, the relationships of interdependence of limited resources and the establishment of times of arrival and departure of vehicles at the points of demand, considering the completion times of the repair operations, representing the relationship between resources in the distribution and repair processes, were considered as a minimization of the maximum delay in the delivery of humanitarian aid as a measure of performance equivalent to the minimization of the time of deprivation within the social welfare dimension.
