# Development of a methodology for characterizing the genetic variability of sugarcane access (Saccharum officinarum L.) stress-tolerant salts

The increase in yield and productivity of sugar cane cultivation in areas with salt problems is of interest for phyto-improvement programmes, however, it is necessary to implement a method that allows to find sources of genes that contribute to salinity tolerance. The aim of this research was to adjust a method that would allow characterising the genetic variability in sugar cane by its level of tolerance to salinity conditions. For the development of the methodology, an experiment was established in a design of complete blocks at random according to three factors organized in subdivided plots, six varieties (main factor) were evaluated in four concentrations of sodium chloride (secondary factor) for four weeks (tertiary factor). Subsequently, 235 accessions from the work collection of CENICAÑA were evaluated with the purpose of characterizing them by their tolerance to stress by salinity in an increased block design.
