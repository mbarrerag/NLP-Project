# Heterogeneous methodology for automatic visual inspection based on inaccurately supervised learning techniques

The purpose of the automatic visual inspection is to detect and locate defects in different types of objects and surfaces. Traditionally, these processes were carried out manually by human experts. However, manual inspection techniques are usually slow and inefficient, finding that in many cases, they do not adequately meet the demands of production in various areas. Over time, different solutions have been employed to address this problem, focused mainly on image processing and classical techniques for the extraction of characteristics, recognition of patterns, and the use of sorters as support vector machines, the closest neighbour rule and decision trees, among others. Recently, it has been managed to solve this problem by means of deep learning techniques, yielding very promising results. However, certain limitations have been identified, such as the need to have a comprehensive set of data for training, the high computational limitations and the lack of clarity in the interpretation of the results. In this the thesis explores the use of various techniques that incorporate the automatic visual inspection problems in the production of different objects, such as glass sheets, sweets, fabrics and synthetic sets, in addition to the results. as the ability to work with weakly labeled images, adaptability for data sets with few or unbalanced images, multi-resolution graphical detection of defects, the implementation of a sliding window for bag generation and, finally, the ability to interpret graphically the results obtained. As for computational analysis, it is relevant to highlight that convolutional neural networks (CNN) represent the most significant computational load, whether in model training, in extracting characteristics or in predicting the label of a test object. However, the temporal performance analyses indicate that the methodology can be effectively applied in various contexts .
