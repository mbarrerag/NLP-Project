# Estimate of the impact on carbon flow and accumulation, from the change of land use from native savannah to transient crops, in the Colombian altillanura.

The natural and restored ecosystems of the Colombian basin of the Orinoco river occupy an officially protected area of more than 2.5 million hectares (Mha). More than half of the natural areas, protected or not, are native savannas. The ecosystemic services provided by these natural areas are little known. The objective of this research was to estimate the impact of the change of land use on the historical variation of the flow and accumulation of carbon in two ecosystems in the Colombian Altillanura. For this purpose, in this study, the biogeochemical model DNDC was used in conjunction with observations and databases of meteorological and CO2 flow, the latter measured by covariance of whirls. The correlation between the daily flows measured and simulated was good, but with a lower bias that leads to the underestimation of the accumulated net flow of CO2, that is to underestimate the fixing of C. The model has accumulated the native savana at the measuring site (Salum of Agrosavia) is a net sink of C of ~3 t has-1 accumulated during a period of two years. The simulation indicates that the savana has been accumulated since the 20th century
