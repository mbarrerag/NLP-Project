# TECHNICAL MEMORY AND REPAIR CULTURE: A SIMONDONIAN PERSPECTIVE

Gilbert Simondon's reflections on technical reality, taken as a whole, provide important keys to thinking about the crises the planet is experiencing today.The idea that individual actions have a major impact on global phenomena is gaining more and more strength.In this sense, we want to dialogue with some of Simondon's approaches to technical memory and the culture of reparation from different perspectives.The objective is to point out that in addition to being hobbies in which you invest time and save money, repair activities facilitate exchange flows between people, even between generations.In the recovery of objects, in the understanding of their operating logic, not only a materialized fragment of the past is brought to the present, but also the inventive gestures crystallized in the object.Repair practices are practices of recovering living memory, the anamnesis of a time, the technical memory that at the time was present in people's inventive capacity.Non-theological spirituality, which in Simondon consists of the possibility of continuing to live in the memory of others once the body disappears, finds in the culture of reparation a path that can connect knowledge of the past with current knowledge, projecting it into the future.For the movements that promote these alternatives, ecology is much more specific in gestures that reflect a personal conscience than in an ideological crusade for global salvation
