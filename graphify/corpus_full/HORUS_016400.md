# INCIDENTAL ANEXIAL TUMOR IN ABDOMINAL CIRUGY

The possibility of finding a benign or malignant ovarian neoplasm, unexpectedly during an abdominal surgery, is a reality for all surgical specialists. While in many cases it will be a benign lesion, the risk of cancer should not be underestimated, as, for example, the intraoperative rupture of a malignant cystic lesion may re-stabilize a patient with stage IA ovarian cancer to HF, with all the consequences that this implies, such as the need to require adjuvant chemotherapy and have a worse prognosis. On the other hand, in premenopausal women, the risk and implications of single or bilateral oophorectomy should be discussed with the family or patient (ideally) even if this warrants leaving the operating room to obtain consent, or postpone the definitive procedure for a second time. Intraoperative consultation with a gynecologist, whenever possible, should be carried out in order to support the adopted behavior. Knowing the proper management of the anexial masses found incidentally during an abdominal surgery, is a priority for all general surgeons
