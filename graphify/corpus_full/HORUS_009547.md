# INTRODUCTION TO GENERAL CONCEPTS

In this space, it was clarified what a museum and a scientific collection is. These correspond to a set of natural and artificial objects, generally collected for the purpose of being studied; however, they are differentiated by the purpose given to them. In museums, the extension and non-formal teaching are prioritized, but research is also done, while in scientific collections research is prioritized. It was discussed that it is a specimen, which corresponds to an animal, plant, complete fossil or fragment thereof. It was emphasized that in museums of art, history and natural sciences are based on the principles of preventive conservation. It was also emphasized that for the storage of collections the analysis must be done from the type of specimen being preserved, which are simple (inert materials) and complex (mixture between inert elements and organic material); and not from the taxonomic group. Finally, the question of why the specimens and their associated information is needed was left for analysis, emphasizing in the reflection that biological collections are a library of past and present biodiversity
