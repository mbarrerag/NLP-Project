# Exploring in and out-of-equilibrium learning regimes of restricted Boltzmann machines.

Although artificial intelligence methods based on automated learning are considered one of the disruptive technologies of our times, the understanding of these tools lies well behind their practical success. Statistical physics of disordered systems enjoys a long history of studying problems of inference and learning using their own tools. Following this tradition, this final mastery work studied how the learning protocol affects patterns extracted by a Boltzmann Restricted Machine. In particular, machines were trained inside and outside the balance with samples of the Ising model in 1 and 2 dimensions to then, using a new deference method, extracting the matrix of couplings from the effective model learned in each case. This experiment allowed elucidating some consequences of training regimes in and out of balance. Additionally, the potential of the use of Boltzmann Restricted Machines for automatic removal of patterns for samples similar to those of the Ising model was explored, being the first step to address more complex problems
