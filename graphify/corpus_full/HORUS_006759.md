# From Adam Smith's Concept of Sympathy to Natsume Sōseki's Social Proposal

The beginning of the Meiji (1868) period marked the official beginning of the modernization process of Japan. At the same time, a feeling of uncertainty and identity disassociation arose in the Japanese population. Various were the causes that caused this feeling of uprooting: the possibility of absorption by another country, the rejection of traditional values, aesthetic changes and, no less importantly, the loss of a collective ethos. Natsume Sōseki is situated precisely at this historical moment and, without any doubt, describes through his conferences and novels not only the changes resulting from the modernization process, but also the problems of his time. One of the recurring themes in Sōseki is about the moral dimension of Japan and the possible degradation of Japanese society towards individualism.
