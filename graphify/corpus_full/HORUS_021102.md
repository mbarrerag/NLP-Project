# Everything solid fades on earth: approximations between art and archaeology.

In the Master's Degree in Visual and Plastic Arts, a group of professors and students have been making different approaches to the heritage collections of the University. This exploratory exercise developed during the second semester of the Master's degree has involved sensitive approaches that go through understanding the history of these collections, their places of permanence and conservation, their uses and forms of pervivence. As early results of these investigations, the students have incorporated into their research projects records and information about the objects and images that are kept there. This experimentation and reflection has been theoretically supported in the postulates of the historian Aby Warburg, specifically in the study of his Atlas Mnemosyne that continues through different ways the question for the survival of the images.The reflections of A Warbyburg have found echoes in updates that the art historian Georges Didi-Huberman has realized through the publications and exhibitions as Atlas: how to take the world to the hills? The dense collection of images that constitute the Atlas leads to rethinking forms of temporality and, constitutes, one of the most complex forms of thought that invite not only to interpret those images and exhibitions but also modern artists produced.
