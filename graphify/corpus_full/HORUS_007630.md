# Structure of the frugivorous butterfly community as an indicator of ecosystem restoration

This project addresses the problem of accelerated deterioration of natural ecosystems, especially deforestation of tropical forests. The project seeks to evaluate the effectiveness of ecological restoration in tropical forests using fruity butterflies as bioindicators. These butterflies are sensitive to environmental changes and their functional and phylogenetic diversity can provide valuable information on the structure and functioning of restored ecosystems. The general objective of the project is to evaluate the structure of the fruity butterfly community between a natural ecosystem, an area in natural regeneration and an area in active restoration, from different dimensions of diversity. The proposed methodology includes the study in the reserve El Centello, owned by the Botanical Garden Joaquín Antonio Uribe of Medellín, in the Madrid area, Antioquia. Van Someren-Rydon traps will be used for the collection of butterflies and will be carried out analysis of taxonomic diversity, functional and phylogenetic. It is a project that is already developing with more than 50% of completed field work, with low impact on the studied species and will be carried out according to the highest ethical and scientific standards.
