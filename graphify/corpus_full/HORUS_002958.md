# Variables that affect the viability of circular economy in the priority supply chain of the agricultural sector in the Shoes-Cesar Swamp

This master's research addresses the identification and evaluation of the crucial variables for the viability of the circular economy, with the agricultural supply chain of the Swamp of the Shoesa, Cesar Colombia. As part of the objectives is to contribute to the reduction of the loss and waste of food, among other purposes oriented to sustainability. The methodological approach combines primary and secondary analysis to identify both the conceptual barriers of the circular economy described in the literature and the structural challenges typical of the territory, derived from a territorial diagnosis. Based on the results of the diagnosis, strategies aimed at improving the supply chain, evaluating its relevance and viability through the Analytic Process in Network (ANP). This method allowed to identify and prioritize the interdependencies between crucial variables providing an integral framework for decision-making. The research highlights the coordination between local actors, the integration of the final consumer and the implementation of economic incentives as essential elements for the transition to circular practices in the region. The conclusions underline the importance of aligning the strategies with the principles of economic, social and environmental sustainability, thus promoting a development model adapted to the characteristics of small scale agriculture. The recommendations seek to offer actions for the gradual economy, the gradual development of the spas, the developed to improve the supply chain, assessing their relief and reliability through the Analytical Network Process (ANP). This method enabled the identification and prioritization of interdependents as crucial variables, providing a comprehensive framework for decision-making. The research highlights the importance of local actors’ coordination, consumer integration, and the implementation of economic incentives as essential elements for the transition towards circular practices in the region. The conclusions emphasise the need to encourage strategies with economic, social, and environmental sustainability principles, promoting a development model adapted to the characteristics of small-scale agriculture. The proposed recommendations offer similar to the progressive adaptation of circular development, the innovative model adapted to the characteristics of small-scale agriculture
