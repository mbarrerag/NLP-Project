# Constitutional control and transitional justice in Colombia. Judicial roles in negotiated peace processes

The article analyzes the roles of the Constitutional Court in the context of democratization promoted by the discourse of transitional justice in Colombia. This, in order to outline analytical inferences on national courts in peace processes negotiated within the constitutional order. Based on the comparative study of the roles of the Court through constitutional control in the 2005 Justice and Peace process and the process of construction and implementation of the 2016 Final Agreement, it follows that when there is a judicial deference with the executive as a historical constant: (a) to a greater degree of anticipation of the jurisprudence of the Courts by political actors there will be a more restricted and predictable judicial intervention; (b) when there is an institutional history of robust judicial independence, the legitimizing role of the Courts makes it more likely to increase their level of influence in the negotiated peace process or in its implementation; and (c) despite the restrictions of the political context, the Courts have some freedom to define their institutional position in transitions. These inferences may illustrate other cases or studies compared with similar variables
