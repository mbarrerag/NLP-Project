# GENDER STEREOTIPOSES AND THE OCCUPATIONAL ROLE STRESS: THE SITUATION IN DISEASE PROFESSIONALS

In the context of the interdisciplinary vision for the analysis of the health-work relationship, research recognizes an opportunity for the collective construction of knowledge that, from the different areas of training, contributes its understanding to the phenomena derived from the work processes and its impact on health conditions. In the constant search for new clarifications that in fact entail the orientation of knowledge towards innovation and development of proposals in specific areas of training, the lines of research articulated to the research group Health and Work, as an epistemic support of the curriculum conception of graduate programs in Health and Safety at Work, are structured. With this framework of reference, the analysis of gender stereotypes is assumed as a topic of research in the line of research on gender, work and health. The historical nature of the natural role of caregiver that has been attributed to nursing, because it is essentially exercised by women, reflects the connotation that is culturally assigned to it as a female profession despite the increasingly notorious participation of men who enter into the training programmes in the career.
