# Estimation of short-term water balances in the Cauca River Basin: an approach to the operation of soil water storage

This thesis presents a methodology of hydrological simulation that allows to describe at basin scale, the spatial temporal variability of change in water storage in soil (AAS).The methodology explores the predictive power of an aggregate hydrological model (MHA) and of the short term water balance (BHCP), in the reproduction of the spatial temporal variability of change in the AAS. The methodology was applied in 32 sub-basins of the Cauca river basin, and as a result the monthly intra- and inter-annual variability of change in the AAS in the period between 1990 and 2009. From the analysis of the seasonality of change in the AAS in the sub-basins, three patterns of intra-naual variability of change in the AAS were identified and three geographical sub-areas were defined in terms of variability patterns.In the basins of each sub-area, the AOS was determined that seasonality is controlled by the annual cycle of monthly precipitation, with a temporal lag varying between 10 and 60 days according to the sub-area.In the regional scale (sum of all sub-basings) the ASAS was reduced and the main seasonality. The methodology explores the predictive responsibility of an aggregate hydrological model (MMA) and the short-term water balance (BHCP), in the reproduction of the spatial and temporary variant of the change in soil water storage. Based in the analysis of the consistency of the change in the sub-basins, three patterns of the change in the environment in the environment in the environment between 1990 and 2009 is present.. To validate the results of the hydrological simulation methodology, the equivalent depth of water in the soil from Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment (GRACE) was used. With the comparison, it was determined that the MHA reproduces the long-term trends observed by GRACE in the sub-basen scale, and it reproduces the seasonality, the inter-annual monthly variation and the long-term trend in the regional scale. Ultimately, in this theses the influence of changes in the AAS on the hydrological dynamics of the sub-bases was quantified.
