# Modeling, design, construction and evaluation of an Indian fixed point cell as a thermometric pattern for calibration of contact thermometers.

A fixed point cell of closed Indian was designed, constructed and evaluated satisfactorily. A computational model was developed to study the solidification process of a pure metal, which allows to vary the geometric parameters of the cell. This model was validated with the experimental results obtained from the cell. Finally, an exercise of calibration was carried out with a platinum resistance thermometer in the H2O-In sub-range. Eleven solidification curves were performed and an average solidification temperature of 429.746493 K was obtained with an expanded uncertainty of 2.7 mK, for a coverage factor +2 with a maximum duration of the plateau of 8 h. These results are satisfactory since they are comparable with those of fixed point cells of Indian from other metrological institutions. The results obtained were validated from two methods: 1. The standardized error test, where the temperature of solidification of the constructed cell was concluded to be comparable with the fixed point cell of an external supplier that currently possesses the National Institute of Metrology (INM) 2.
