# Invisible Faces, Emerging Voices: An Approach to the History of the LGBTIQ+ Community in Medellín Between 1980-2000

During the 1980s Medellin was affected by drug trafficking violence and armed conflict.In this context, people with diverse sexual identities and orientations faced a double marginalisation: generalized violence and specific discrimination due to their sexual orientation and gender identity.The conservative attitudes contributed to maintaining the stigma and the invisibility of the LGBTIQ+ community in the department, since they were frequently subjected to violence and social exclusion, so added to the lack of legal recognition and protection that caused many to live their lives in hiding.However, the fact of decriminalization of homosexual relations in 1980 in the country can be considered as a starting point for future transformations. The 1990s brought with it some significant changes at the national level. The most notable, the promulgation of the new 1991 Constitution, can be considered as a turning point, because it explicitly advocates equality and non-discrimination, laying the foundations for future claims of rights by the LGBTIQ+ community. For its part, the movements and organizations that sought to create the specific forms of health of the community began to emerge. According to the above, this research also aims to explore visual and written sources, to conduct interviews with academics or activists who have addressed the problems, challenges and advances of this community, in order to be able to account for the above-mentioned transformations. In summary, the representation and participation of the LGBTIQ+ community in Medellín between 1980 and 2000, was characterized by profound inequalities and discrimination. During this period, LGBTIQ+ people faced an environment marked by violence and lack of recognition of their rights. The invisibility of this community and the lack of safe spaces and support exacerbated its marginalization. Although some progress was made towards the end of the 1990s, such as the creation of organizations and the start of marches of pride, media representation and access to basic rights remained limited. In this framework, the project asks: What was the representation and participation of LGBTIQ+ people, social movements and organizations in Medellín during 1980 and 2000?
