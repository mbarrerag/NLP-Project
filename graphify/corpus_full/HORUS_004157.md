# Family adenomatous polyposis in two brothers. Case report

Family adenomatous polyposis (FAP) is an autosomal dominant hereditary disease caused by a mutation in the APC gene (adenomatous polyposis coli). Its symptomatology begins early and the risk of developing colorectal cancer (adenocarcinoma) is almost 100% if not received timely treatment. As it is an uncommon disease with high morbidity and mortality, it is pertinent to do studies on this matter. Presentation of the cases. The case of a PAF recorded in two adolescent brothers that manifested with digestive haemorrhage. In both patients, colectomy was performed by laparoscopia and endorectal ileoanal descent plus ileostomy and the postoperative evolution was favorable and without complications. The two brothers continued in outpatient controls by the pediatric surgery service. PAF is a rare disease in the pediatric population. Colectomy is the treatment of choice, since this is the treatment of the symptoms and associated morbidity. Genetic evaluation is essential to make adequate follow-up in short, medium and long term decisions in patients with this disease
