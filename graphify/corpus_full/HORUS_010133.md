# OUTER WATERS: INDIGENOUS RESISTANCE TO MINERITY AND THEIR DOCUMENT VISIBILIZATION

Several indigenous cosmovisions in the Americas struggle for the survival of their cosmovisions and conceive the Earth as a Mother when threatened, among others, by mining projects that directly disrupt indigenous peoples and their territories. Assuming that every vision of the world is a construction, judging whether a world view is true or false is irrelevant. The important thing is to consider that indigenous cosmovisions have as priority the preservation of water is synonymous with life, and to end them means to eliminate ontological positions that safeguard this liquid.In this conference I use two documentaries.The first, "The River that was Stolen" (2006), led by Gonzalo Guillén, visualizes how mining impacts the Colombian Wayúu people; the second, "Huichols: the last guards of the peyote" (2014), led by Hernán Vilchez, shows how the Mexican people of Mexico resists mining. Analyze them, contextualizes, these peoples, and, secondly, they generate part of the audiovisual treatment of the videos to consider how they are visible and their indigenous struggles
