# The Liberal Concept in the Political Culture of New Granada (1810-1853)

In the course of the first half of the nineteenth century, the word liberal went from an adjective that collected a series of moral virtues to a fundamental sociopolitical concept that gradually was encompassing in its semantic field a whole set of practices, values and institutions: representative government, constitution, division of powers, respect for private property, individual rights, among others. Added to the above, during the mid-nineteenth period the term liberal would cease to be synonymous with a set of qualities and virtues (universal) appreciated by the neo-Grandian political community to become a partisan concept that refers to a specific political sector, with its own political agenda and principles. Our aim consisted of analysing this semantic transformation in the New Granada, from the monastic crisis to the promulgation of the constitution of 1853, which cemented the previous period of reformism in which the liberal party had a betagonical role in the administration of José Hilario López (1849-1853).
