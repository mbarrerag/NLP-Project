# Roots of peace: PAGMA, Environmental Peace and Ethnicity as the Mirror of the Final Peace Agreement.

The Group of Research Territorial Development, Peace and Posconflict (GIDETEPP) has focused in recent years on promoting reflections, dialogues and research that link the academy with institutions, social organizations and citizens around collective efforts to address socio-territorial conflicts that emerged after the signing of the Peace Agreement and during the pandemic, thus contributing to the formation of a critical opinion and to the strengthening of peace scenarios from different social perspectives. Seven years after the signing of the Peace Agreement, while there are significant advances in the implementation of the Peace Agreement, a series of challenges to be addressed and opportunities emerge to progress in the construction of peace in Colombia are highlighted. In this sense, GIDETEPP is proposing the organization of a conversation between academics, institutional actors and social leadership that explains the advances and challenges in these issues, especially the voice of leaders and social leaders in the territories that work on the basis of the sustainable development of the land. The group also emphasizes collaboration with academic communities such as the Comparative Constitutionalism Research Group (CC) through practical research that allows the two groups to unite their expertise that have addressed peace from different approaches. The academic production of the line _x0093_social and territorial conflicts following the Final Peace Agreement_x0094_ seeks to deepen the new socio-territorial conflicts in ethnically differentiated territories, emphasizing the importance of territorial diversity in the construction of peace. The products to be developed include a scientific article of dissemination, an academic forum or event and the strengthening of PAGMA means of dissemination that presents the results of the work in conjunction with the lines of research "Constitutionalism and Peace" and "Socio-territorial Conflicts after the Final Peace Agreement"
