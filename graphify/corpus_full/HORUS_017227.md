# Effect of phosphoric fertilization on re-mobilization of herbicide glyphosate in soils and risk associated with interaction with rice cultivation.

Glyfosorate is the herbicide of greatest use in agricultural areas in the world, is a non-selective herbicide, which acts in plants inhibiting the path of shikimic acid in plants. The molecule is strongly adsorbed to the exchange sites in the soil colloids, where it persists in a variable way, depending on the type of union that conforms to the adsorbate (the minerals and the organic matter of the soil), the environmental conditions and the possibilities of degradation of the herbicide. Several authors have shown that the process of adsorption of the herbicide in soils is reversible and strongly influenced by the increase in the anion load in the system, so that, in agricultural production conditions, the use of phosphatised fertilizers would favour the remobilisation of the herbicide. Marked in these premises was executed the present project that evaluated in laboratory conditions, the process of adsorption-disortion of glyphosorate in three agricultural soils (CS1, CS2 and CS3), the influence of the presence of monoamorbital phosphate (MAP) on the movement and the soil profile of the hebaceous. The adsorption process is found to be reversible and in most of the scenarios evaluated the addition of phosphate favours reversibility, reiterating the high affinity for phosphate in these soils and the similarity of the adsorption and desorption mechanisms of the two molecules. The infiltration tests in columns indicate that, in the evaluated conditions, glyphosate is strongly retained in the soil, only a fraction of this is moved through the soil profile, in the water flow and the addition of MAP in the system favours the movement. The direct radical exposure of herbicide solutions in the indicator plants indicates that the herbicide is in the plant and the arrival at the site of action, where it generates the temporal modification of the metabolism, causing alteration of the growth and development. The addition of MAP together with glyphosate in the soil indicates that this increases the response of the plant to the herbicide, compared to when it is added only glyphosate.
