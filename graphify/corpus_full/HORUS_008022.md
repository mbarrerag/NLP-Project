# Compulsory inclusion of trans people in Bogotá and Quibdó: experiences and situations

From an approach that dialogues with the trans epistemologies and the studies of the work, the present thesis shows how some trans people in Bogotá and Quibdó, who have been linked to companies and governmental entities, have built their work experiences. This research was carried out between February 2019 and September 2022 in the Colombian cities of Bogotá and Quibdó. As part of the methodological design, 17 interviews were conducted with trans people, including trans women and trans-male or trans-male persons. Additionally, 8 of them were accompanied by work. 16 interviews were also made with representatives of companies, governmental entities and consultants of inclusion, diversity and equity. The central idea of this research is the relationship that includes, the work and neoliberal practices with the way some trans people have involved in forced inclusion processes, category that emerges as a result of this thesis. The obligatory inclusion refers to coercive practices that combine organizational interests and trans-person needs to survive economically. In the forced inclusion, the companies and governmental entities do not give birth to the experiences of trans people, while these are subject to exercises of precarisation, exploitation and the ins, and the instance of trans. additionally, I conducted 16 interviews with representatives of companies, governance entities and inclusion, diversity, and equity consultants. The central idea of this research is that inclusion, work, and neoliberal practices in which trans people have performed is related to the mandate inclusion processes, a category that emerges as a result of thissis. Mandate inclusion refers to coordinate practices that combine organizational interests and the needs of trans people to survive economically. Companies and governance entities practice mandate inclusion by being aware of the work experience of trans people, while subjecting them to jobs to precariousness, exploitation, and objection of their identities.
