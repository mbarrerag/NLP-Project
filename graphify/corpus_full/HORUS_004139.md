# THE EVANGEL OF THE MONDED ROLE OF MIGUEL ANTONIO CARO: MONEY AND POWER IN THE DECIMONIC COLOMBIA.

This article investigates the monetary writings of Miguel Antonio Caro in the framework of the militarisation of the paper currency during the War of the Thousand Days (1899-1903). It argues that the key to understanding the monetary creed of economic liberalism is found, ironically, in the thought of the most reactionary intellectual of Colombia. This Latinist, the most recalcitrant defender of the paper currency of his time, understood better that none of his contemporaries that money is the central affective sign that links the providential horizon of the economy with the patriarchal violence of the State. Although it is often presented as an antimodern ideologue, the monetary dimensions of his writings reveal a very different image.
