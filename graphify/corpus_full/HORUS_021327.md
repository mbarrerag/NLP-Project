# Climate change and its effects on biodiversity and epidemiology: A stochastic study

One of the main problems facing humanity in recent years is the variation in temperature on the planet and its effects on it, according to the report on climate change and its effects on biodiversity in Latin America [ECLAC and European, 2017]. It is estimated that climate change will have direct effects on individual organisms, populations and ecosystems. As for individuals, it has been found that climate change could affect their development, their physiology and their behaviors during the phases of growth, reproduction and migration, according to [Prillaman, 2022]. In fact, climate change has exacerbated more than 200 infectious diseases and dozens of non-communicable conditions [Prillaman, 2022]. The main increases will occur in epidemics related to floods, such as cholera. The number of cases due to diseases, such as dengue, malaria and yellow fever, which are transmitted by mosquitoes, have a high probability of increasing over the next few years as a consequence of global warming (Shuman, 2010], [Eikenberry and Gummel 2018].
