# EXTRACTION AND CHARACTERIZATION OF THE COCOA POD HUSK OF PECTIN FROM (THEOBROMA CACAO L.) BY MEANS OF AN ULTRASOUND ASSISTED PROCESS

The production of agro-industrial waste is a necessity of the production chains to make them more sustainable and competitive. The processing of cocoa generates a significant amount of residues, among which the cacota represents between 70-80% by weight of the fruit. This by-product is rich in compounds of interest to the food industry, such as the pectin. Optimizing the process of extraction assisted by ultrasound of the pectin of the cocoa cacottate by means of an ultrasound-assisted process, and evaluating its chemical characteristics and technological functionality. It was used as a raw material cocoa (clon TCS01) from the Department of Nariño. The cacota residues were dried, ground and sifized, and the fractions passing through a mesh of 60, were subjected to ultrasound-assisted extraction (UAE), using citric acid at 2.5% as solvent. The solid to solvent ratio was applied a design of the response surface Box-Behnken to maximize the extraction of pectin according to the following process variables: Extract time (10-20 min), temperature (10-90 min), temperature (70-90° C) and 9° C) and ultrasound (AU) to the ultrasound (AUE At 54.3%, total carbohydrates 26.9% and YGalA 5.7%. The pectins obtained formed 60% sucrose gels at pH values of 2.5, 2.7 and 3.0, presenting gel rupture values 15.7, 12.5 and 18.8 N. The pectins obtained were characterized by being high methoxyle and with the ability to form sugar gels at low pH values (= 3). Impact: Taking advantage of high volumes of solid residues generated during the process of the benefit of cocoa, as an alternative to obtain value added products (pectins) for application in food that allows to focus productive processes on circular economy policies
