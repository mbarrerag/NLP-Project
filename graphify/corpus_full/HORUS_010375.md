# History of women and female agencies in Latin America in the 20th century

This dossier, entitled "History of women and women's agencies in Latin America in the twentieth century", brings together seven articles that seek to deepen the study of women's movements and platforms during the twentieth century. Various studies suggest that while European women fought for the right to vote, access to the institutions of political power and against gender discrimination, Latin American women mobilized more often in the face of capitalism, colonialism, dictatorships, land defense and access to resources by their communities. The texts that compose it give voice to dissident Catholic women; to members of armed groups; to victims resistant to the armed conflict who organize to demand truth, justice and reparation; to women who instrumentalize male elites' spaces in their favor to end up turning them into platforms for political activism; and to women proud of their knowledge and the legacy of their Afro-descendant ancestors, but racialized by a society that exoticizes and marginalizes them. In this dossier we would also like to draw attention to the geographical diversity of the authors, who belong to countries such as Mexico (two articles), Colombia (three articles from the Caribbean, the border with Venezuela and another from the center of the country); Spain (a text referring to Cuba) and Argentina (one article)
