# COVID-19 PANDEMIC, PUBLIC HEALTH AND OCCUPATIONAL THERAPY

Introduction / Justification: The COVID-19 pandemic is a clinical and social phenomenon that continues to disrupt and end lives worldwide. While many answers regarding the clinical aspects of the SARS-CoV-2 virus remains unknown, occupational therapy should examine the pandemic implications for its education, research and practice. Public health offers evidence on the disparities that affect our health, in other words, an understanding of people's circumstances and opportunities to live a healthy life, which is often disregarded in occupational therapy. This presentation aim is twofold: i) to call for a long-needed change of our profession to align with public health priorities and ii) to highlight that public health offers elements to occupational therapy to develop in these moments of crisis. Method / Approach: Two Occupational Therapy academics, from different countries, with doctoral training and experience in public health education, research and policy development discuss the potential changes in occupational therapy in times of Covid-19. Practice implications: We indicate that (i) occupational therapy needs to enhance and expands existing professional networks (internal and external to the professional); (ii) produce reliable documentation of experimental educational and practical strategies; (iii) use public health methods to produce data that guide work with collective groups, and finally (iv) apply the unique professional understanding on the personal-social-political-economic-religious entanglement of people's everyday life to feed back the discussion with public health professionals. The growing interest in occupational therapy on inequalities, justice, environmental issues will support the professional connecting with public health knowledge and practice
