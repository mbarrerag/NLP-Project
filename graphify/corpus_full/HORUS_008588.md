# VALIDATION OF PREPAREDNESS FOR CAREGIVING SCALE IN FAMILY CAREGIVERS OF PEOPLE WITH CNCD IN COLOMBIA

The Preparedness for Caregiving Scale (PCS) aims to assess the level of confidence that caregivers have in their own abilities to provide multidimensional care. It was designed in 1980, and in 2020, it was translated and validated in Spain. Studies have shown the relationship between caregiver burden and caregiving preparedness. In this sense, preparation for care is a predictor variable of burden of care. The objective of this study was to determine the relationship between preparation for care and burden in family caregivers of people with chronic noncommunicable disease in Bogotá, Colombia. Psychometric validation study of the PCS instrument in family caregivers of people with chronic illness in Bogotá, Colombia in 2022. A statistical analysis was performed, including KMO, Varimax, Chronbach's alpha, and Pearson's correlation coefficient for test-retest stability. Prior to construct validity, the sample adequacy criteria were met with a KMO index of 0.899 and a statistically significant Bartlett's test of sphericity (p<0.01). The structural analysis carried out using a Varimax orthogonal rotation with the principal component extraction method showed a one-dimensional structure with factor loadings greater than 0.8 and an explained variance of 66.45%. Regarding reliability, the instrument had a Cronbach's alpha of 0.925, which is adequate internal consistency. In addition, a test-retest showed correlations between moderate and strong in the items, which demonstrates adequate stability. The Preparedness for Caregiving Scale has construct validity as well as adequate internal consistency for its use in the Colombian context and among family caregivers of people with chronic non-communicable diseases. Future studies are recommended where caregiving preparedness is correlated with other predictor variables of the care burden. Additionally, it is necessary to measure the impact of preparation for care on the quality of life, both of the caregiver and of the person cared for.
