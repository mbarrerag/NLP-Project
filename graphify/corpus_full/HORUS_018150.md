# EXPLORING THE EFFECTS OF FONT SIZE AND LETTER SPACING ON JUDGMENTS OF LEARNING (JOLS)

In textbooks, changes in perceptual characteristics such as large font size or boldface often highlight important contents. Font size generates a metamemory illusion, that is, participants think that font size will affect memory, but it does not. However, past research has failed to show metamemory illusions for boldface with single words or word pairs. In two experiments, we tested whether boldface causes metamemory illusions with ecological materials: long sentences. In Experiment 1, we confirmed that most participants hold the belief that a word in boldface in a sentence will be better remembered than when in regular font. In Experiment 2, we presented long sentences with a target word in bold or regular font and requested estimations of future memory for that word (judgments of learning or JOL). We also measured memory for the target words. JOLs were higher for boldface than regular font, but boldface did not affect memory. Thus, our results show that with more ecological materials boldface causes metamemory illusions. This result suggests that boldface may not be the best tool to highlight contents in textbooks
