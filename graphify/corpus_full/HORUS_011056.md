# HYDROLOGICAL RESPONSE IN HEADWATER BASINS WITH LAND USE IN FORESTS PLANT IN THE INTERTROPICAL ANDES OF COLOMBIA

In the Central Andes Mountains of Colombia (4°50'' N, 75°35'' W, altitude 2,000 m), three Order 1 experimental basins were established: Natural Forest (BN), as a baseline;Pasture (PA), with livestock activity;and Planted Forest (PF) with Eucaliptus salignagandis in a second rotation after replacing pastures 20 years ago.Digital instruments were made available to measure precipitation and flows for 48 months.Comparisons were made in terms of (1) water balance, (2) hydrological response (Water Production (PH) and Runoff Coefficient (CE)), and (3) assessment of the hydrological regulation capacity based on the Flow Duration Curve (CDV).The results showed that in the coverage of Planted Forests, water consumption is 14% higher than the reference value in the Natural Forest;pasture coverage generates 4% more flows in relation to reference values.The PH and EC parameters confirm these hydrological response trends.The CDC showed that Planted Forests exert a very positive effect on attenuating maximum flows and maintaining base flows, which, although they do not match the condition presented by Natural Forests, are close to it, and notably distant from the generation dynamics in pasture conditions, which confirms a valuable effect of hydrological regulation when transforming areas used for pastures into forests planted for commercial purposes
