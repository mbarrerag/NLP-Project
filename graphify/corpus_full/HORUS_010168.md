# INTEGRATION OF A HYBRID LABORATORY FOR LEARING IN DIGITAL ELECTRONICS

This paper describes an educational experience in which a hybrid laboratory composed of two modalities was used: remote laboratory and hands-on (or face-to-face) laboratory, in a digital electronics subject oriented to learning a language of hardware description. A case study is presented describing the interaction of students with the hybrid laboratory over a period of 10 weeks, including the number of experiments carried out, the laboratory reports delivered and the days and hours of access to the remote laboratory. In addition, the answers to a question that students were asked at the end of the experience, in which they were asked to indicate their preferences on the characteristics of the hybrid laboratory. The results show that the flexibility and accessibility of the remote laboratory were positively assessed. The implications of these findings for the design of hybrid laboratories and the teaching of digital electronics are discussed
