The ocarro (Priodontes maximus) is a species of mammal large in condition of threat and with high requirements of food and habitat. In Colombia, it is distributed in the region of Amazonia and Orinoquia. Although in this last it is symmetrical with four other species of armadillos, which makes it the most important area for this group of mammals at national level. This region is faced with an accelerated change of terrestrial coverage that reduces the quality and quantity of the habitat available for the wildlife. However, the effect or adaptation of the species threatened to vast agroecosystems is unknown. Thus, a study was developed to know some aspects of ecology of P. maximus in areas with anthropic intervention of the Colombian Altillanura (departments of Meta and Vichada). Between 2021 and 2022 it was installed 69 stations of phototrampeo in five palm oil plantations (PPA) to determine the use of habitat. It was equipped 4 individuals with radio-transmitigations VHF. The results highlight the importance of riparian forests as a suitable habitat for the ocaeros. The strong association with this type of coverage was evidenced, where they build the majority of their burrows, which are the main refuges and breeding sites. Therefore, the presence of riparian forests and natural savannas increases the possibilities of the coexistence of the ocarrh in this type of landscape with palm oil crops
