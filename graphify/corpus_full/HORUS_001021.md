# Potential insecticide of essential oils from species of the genus Piper with high phenylpropanoid content for the control of Sitophilus zeamais and Tribolium castaneum

The conservation of cereals faces great challenges during storage, mainly due to pests of insects such as Sitophilus zeamais and Tribolium castaneum, which can cause product losses between 5 and 30%. For their control, the use of chemical insecticides has been extended; however, these present several limitations such as the generation of resistance in the treated populations, risks to human health and environmental persistence. In the search for safe alternatives for their control, the essential oils (AEs) stand out for their biological potential, their structural diversity and their low environmental persistence. The Piper genus is recognized at the scientific level for its insecticide properties and many of its species usually produce AE with an interesting chemical diversity between monoterpenoids and phenylpropanoids. The present study contributes to the characterization of the potential insecticide of Aes related to the genus Piper and its main constituents in the control of S. zeamais and T. casteneum.
