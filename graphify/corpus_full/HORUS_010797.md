# Traditional cuisine, an alternative for strengthening food sovereignty in the peasant population of the municipality of Monguí, Boyacá.

Traditional cuisine is understood as a system of knowledge and techniques that include techniques of food acquisition, transformation and consumption. In turn, it encompasses knowledge, food and culinary practices that forge the heritage and cultural identity of peoples. Food sovereignty is understood as the right of peoples to produce nutritious and culturally appropriate food, accessible and produced in a sustainable and ecological way, food sovereignty is a living and permanent process. The objective of this research was to analyze the knowledge and practices of traditional cuisine in function of strengthening the dynamics of food sovereignty, in the peasant population of the municipality of Mongui, Boyacá. This through a qualitative methodology with an ethnographic approach. Traditional cuisine is consolidated as a fundamental pillar for the construction and exercise of food sovereignty. The findings show that traditional cooking and food sovereignty maintain a close link that responds to dynamic land that comes alive with traditional farming experience and knowledge. In this relationship, traditional cooking is found to be a fundamental pillar for the construction and exercise of food sovereignty. The findings show that traditional cooking and food sovereignty maintains a strong link that responds to the traditional recipes, beliefs and traditional knowledge.
