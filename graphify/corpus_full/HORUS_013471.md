# School climate, student welfare and literacy: PISA 2018 based analysis

Readable literacy is essential for the success and development of students. Within the framework of educational psychology, there is evidence that the school climate and student welfare affect the development of literacy. This research seeks to identify the factors of school climate and student wellbeing that predict literacy reading in secondary school in Latin America using the data of PISA 2018. A multilayered perception model was built to generate innovative and robust hypotheses about the factors associated with literacy reading. These hypotheses were verified with a different set of data building an exploratory general model and models of mixed effects. This methodology was proposed in the framework of the replication crisis in education and development psychology. Regarding the school climate, the oriented climate, feedback and the discriminatory climate are associated with a lower reading performance (in the meantime, the adaptive education and the sense of belonging is associated with a greater literacy reading. Regarding student wellbeing, self-efficacy, motivation to dominate the tasks, the reader's enjoyment and the growth mentality predicts a greater reading capacity. These hypotheses were tested with a different dataset by building a Multivariate General Linear Model and Mixed Effects Models. This methodology was proposed in the framework of the replication crisis in research and developmental psychology. Regarding school climate, it was found that directly instructed, fedback and discriminatory climate are associated with lower reading achievement. Meanwhile, adaptive instruction and sense of belonging predicting higher reading literacy. Regarding student well-being, self-efficiency, motivation to master tasks, satisfaction of reading, and growth mind of forecasting better reading literacy. Meanwhile, life satisfaction is associated with lower learning literacy. These results result results decrease of reading, and utilization of better predicting better reading literacy.
