# Study of the accounting regulation for SMEs in Colombia: A look from the problems in the Latin American context

This paper focuses on the relevance of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in the economic growth of a country. Therefore, the objective of the work is to analyze the accounting regulatory alternatives that have been assumed, after several years, by countries such as Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Argentina and Mexico, and some basic aspects of Chile, Bolivia and Brazil in the face of the adoption processes of IFRS for SMEs. For this purpose, a qualitative methodology is used, which starts from the documentary review that focuses on accounting for the common and recurring problems of the convergence process. As a result of the respective content analysis of the documents found, the following categories of the problems found are established: effects of the convergence process on users of financial information and decision-making, asymmetries in the accounting treatment in IFRS for SMEs, costs in the generation of information, and some demo-graphic and particular aspects. Finally, the accounting regulation alternatives for SMEs that have been developed in some Latin American countries are collected. Thus, it was found that some countries, such as Colombia, adopted accounting regulation for SMEs. Others, such as Argentina, allow IFRS for SMEs on a voluntary basis. For its part, Mexico has local rules and Brazil opted for convergence
