# EFFECTIVENESS OF SCREENING WITH HPV-DNA IN WOMEN UNDER 30 YEARS OF AGE

To assess the safety and effectiveness of imple- menting primary HPV-DNA screening in women under 30 years of age. Systematic review and meta-analysis Method: A systematic literature search was conducted in the MEDLINE, Embase and CENTRAL databases. The search was expanded to include lecture summaries (RCOG, FIGO, ACOG, and FECOLSOG) and contact with experts. No language or publication date restrictions were applied. Controlled clinical trials recruiting women under 30years of age and comparing a primary HPV-DNA screening pro- gram with cytology or any other option were included. The protocol of this review was registered. Two authors work- ing independently reviewed the trials, extracted the data and assessed the risk of bias using the Cochrane RoB-2 tool. The GRADE approach was used to assess the quality of the evidence. Five clinical trials recruited populations under 30 years of age (n = 287,177 women) and compared primary HPV-DNA screening with vaginal cytology. Moderate- quality of evidence suggests that primary HPV-DNA screening reduce the incidence of invasive carcinoma (OR 0.19 95% CI 0.07'0.53) and NIC2+ (OR 0.67 95% CI 0.48¿ 0.92) during the second screening round. Primary HPV DNA-based primary screening probably increases the fre- quency of referral to colposcopy during the first round (OR 1.73 95% CI 1.01'2.96), with no significant differences at the end of the second screening round (OR 0.79 95% CI 0.56'1.12). Moderate-quality evidence suggests that primary HPV-DNA screening in women under 30years of age may probably reduce the incidence of invasive car- cinoma and NIC2+ during the second screening round. HPV-DNA screening probably increases the frequency of referral to colposcopy during the first round, with no sig- nificant differences at the end of follow-up. These results support the UK NHS and the American Cancer Society statement: cervical cancer screening should begin at age 25 with HPV-DNA
