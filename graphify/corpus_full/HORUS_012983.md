# Integration of statistical information and Earth observations for the calculation of indicators SDGs 11.3.1 and 11.7.1 in Colombia, using Random Forest classification techniques

This article presents the calculation of the indicators SDGs 11.3.1 and 11.7.1 in Colombia, integrating statistical and geospatial information as essential sources for achieving a robust and territorially disaggregated measurement. Based on the processes defined by UN-Habitat, a methodology with geospatial emphasis, supported in the processing of satellite images through the Random Forest supervised classification algorithm, is developed to obtain the metrics required in the calculation of the two indicators, such as the built areas, urban soil consumption, and open spaces, in conjunction with integrated statistical information analysis. SDG 11.3.1 during the period 2015-2020 was calculated for 63 defined cities, whose national value of 0.43 highlights that in the country land is used efficiently, while SDG 11.7.1 for 2018 was calculated in a representative sample of nine cities, resulting in 33.2% of the constructed areas being used nationally for open spaces for public use. These results make the country a regional benchmark in monitoring SDGs, highlighting the possibility of updating the results in the medium, thanks to automated cloud processings
