# PRIMARY TAMIZATION WITH ADN-VPH TEST IN WOMEN under 30: HEALTH TECHNOLOGY ASSESSMENT

Background From 2014 in Colombia, the primary screening was incorporated when comparing with the Human Papillomavirus (HPV) test from 30 to 65 years old, when the test is positive. triage with cytology is performed for remission to colposcopy. Currently, the advisability of starting the screening with HPV DNA test from 25 years old is discussed. Thus, the objective of this evaluation of healthcare technologies is to analyze the evidence available around safety, effectiveness, cost-effectiveness, values and preferences, ethical dilemmas and aspects related to the implementation for the Colombian context of the HNVID test as a strategy of cervical screening in women under 30 years old. Domains to evaluate Clinical effectiveness and safety 1. Cumulative rate of cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (NIC) grade 2 or more advanced after 2 rounds of screening. 2. Cumulsified rates of cervical cancer and post-selection. 3. Security: remission to colposcopy. Cost-effectiveness Cost-effectiveness Effectiveness effectiveness for Colombia. Other domains considered ethical aspects associated with cervical screening in women under 30 years old. First screening round (RR: 1.57; 95% CI: 1.20 to 2.04; certainty in low evidence), with a lower incidence of IAS2+ (RR: 0.67; 95% CI: 0.48 to 0.92; certainty in low evidence) and is associated with a lower frequency of invasive carcinoma at the end of follow-up (RR: 0.19; 95% CI: 0.07 to 0.53; certainty in high evidence). Economic results From the economic point of view, the alternative of DNA-PH and triage with cytology since 25 years may represent the most cost-effective alternative for Colombia (cost-effective incremental ratio $8,820,980 COP 2013). Other implications Two studies suggest that implementation barriers, attributable to circumstances of intermediation, public order and geographical, could be addressed by new technologies or screening strategies. It is important to consider alternatives of way of administration and delivery of services to solve some barriers of acceptability and access. Any cervical screening program should contemplate the ethical principles of screening, benefit, autonomy and equity.
