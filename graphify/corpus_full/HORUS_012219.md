# Merit in access to higher education: a case study on the pyramid of educational inequality in Colombia

The purpose of this work is to demonstrate the way in which access to public higher education in Colombia with its restricted access hides deep inequalities and centuries of social and economic exclusion. For this, the starting point is to present the egalitarian insufficiency of John Rawls's theory of justice, the problems of merit as a guiding principle in the formulation of social policies and the dilemma between equal opportunities and equal results, opting for equality of results as the place from which the fight against inequality is faced. Then, the trends in higher education policies in Colombia from 2011 are presented, highly influenced by the obsessive search for macroeconomic stability and microeconomic efficiency, where the underfunding of public higher education and the strengthening of education systems stand out. individual reward associated with merit. In a third moment, from the sample of 24 official higher education institutions, using descriptive statistics and linear regressions, the results of analyzing the determining factors outside the responsibility of the families are presented (sex, socioeconomic stratum and type of school of the student) that affect access to public higher education in Colombia, especially in institutions that offer high-quality university education, where exclusion mechanisms that reinforce the pyramid of educational inequality are evident. Finally, general guidelines are proposed for the guarantee of the right to higher education and the consolidation of an integrated and horizontally functional educational system
