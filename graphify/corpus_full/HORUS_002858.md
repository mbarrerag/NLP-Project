# SYNNESS OF ALTERNATIVE SOLVENTS (IONIC LICKS AND EUTECTIC SOLVENTS PROFOUND)

In this master's thesis were synthesized LIs and DES based on protonated caffeine with potential application in the desulphurization of model fuels. To this, the protonation of commercial caffeine was performed, converting it into a quaternary ammonium salt, precursor necessary for the synthesis of LIs and DES. This process was carried out by the reaction of caffeine with anhydrous hydrogen chloride in inert atmosphere, resulting in the formation of caffeine hydrochloride (CafCl) based on the methodology of Salami & Ezabadi [9]. The characterization of CafCl was performed by techniques such as thermogravimetry (TGA), FTIR and RMN, which allowed evidence the formation of the N-H bond in the ring of caffeine. Subsequently, once protonated caffeine, it was performed the synthesis of LIs using FeCl3 hexahydrate in two different molar relationships (1:2 and 1:3) with the aim of obtaining LIS with manageable viscosity. These LIS were characterized by two-protoned caffeine of the μg to confirm the formation of the techlorin and <a. The results are attributed to the use of salts with acidic hydrogens, such as CafCl, which suggests that the predominant mechanism is the formation of hydrogen bridges between LI/DES and CM. However, aromatic rings of sulfur are considered weak as acceptors of hydrogen bridges, which is reflected in low desulphurization rates. It is proposed to improve this aspect by applying more extraction stages in the process, recycling LI/DES, evaluating longer desulphurization times and considering other anions and HBDs in the synthesis of LIs and DES
