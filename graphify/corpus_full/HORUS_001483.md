# ENTRY SOCIAL RESPONSIBILITY AND THE LEGAL MANDATE: A SYSTEMATIC ANALYSIS OF LITERATTURE

The growing demand for ethical behaviour in companies by stakeholders has been answered through corporate social responsibility (CSR). CSR is normally exercised under hard (hard law) or soft law (soft law), which encourages social responsibility without the need for legislation. Recently, several countries have legislated on CSR, traditionally voluntary, although literature shows academic skepticism about the effectiveness of mandatory laws, due to contextual and axiological differences in the exercise of CSR. 148 scientific papers have been developed on the subject of the discussion whether CSR should be understood as a legal mandate or voluntary action. a relationship between mandatory non-financial reporting and legitimacy, control, corporate performance and innovation is evidenced. Likewise, it is argued in favour of maintaining the voluntary nature of CSR as a moral obligation. The main results show that companies must reconcile their interests with those of their stakeholders with an a oxiological basis beyond the legal mandate.
