# Design of prototype electromagnetic electroporation for control of pathogenic bacterial microorganisms in wastewater

This project aims to study the possibility of controlling the growth rate of pathogenic microorganisms in wastewater (such as E.Coli), using pulsed electromagnetic energy. To this end, an electromagnetic applicator fed with laboratory sources will be designed to produce electric field amplitudes between 1-10 kV/m and variable durations of at least 1 microsecond. The excitation of cells with these electrical field levels has proven to be useful in producing electroporation, thus dimming their reproduction. The applicator consists of a coplanar wave guide made with micro-tape technology where capillary tubes will be inserted to manage the cultures under test. By means of experiments in which the parameters of waveform vary, it is intended to determine the influence of these arousations on the reproduction/survivance of the microorganisms using tests carried out in a biochemistry laboratory. Finally, the feasibility and costs of this technique will be compared with existing techniques on the market
