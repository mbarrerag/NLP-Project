# Identification of receptors for a Plasmodium vivax merzoite protein in malaria host cells

Human malaria is caused mainly by Plasmodium vivax and Plasmodium falciparum, with the most widespread and lethal species being these. The study of P. vivax faces challenges due to its exclusive invasion of reticulocytes, which are the precursor cells of erythrocytes. The rapid maturation and low availability of reticulocytes have made it difficult to develop a continuous culture in vitro that allows to study the biology and receptor-ligand interactions of the parasite. In this context, the potential of the erythroid cell lines JK-1 and BEL-A2 was explored, with the possibility of achieving the potential of the functionally active cellular lines JK-1 and Pticulocyte alternatives, confirming its suitability by the successful invasion of the parasite. In addition, the liquid chromatography coupled to mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) was used to perform a quantitative comparison of the membrane proteins of these cellular lines, with the potential for the LGBC5 and the neurocytes. These findings enrich knowledge about the receptor-ligand interactions of P. vivax and reinforce the relevance of JK-1 and BEL-A2 cells as alternative cell models in their study. .
