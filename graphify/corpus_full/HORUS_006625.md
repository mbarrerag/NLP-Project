# Effect of a computer programming learning environment based on video game development on student motivation to learn

Due to the increasing application of computer programming in the areas of work and knowledge, more courses related to this field are being included in university careers. The complexity of the content of associated subjects requires new strategies and teaching methodologies that are comfortable for students and motivation. In this sense, the use and application of video games has become a promising alternative. GDBL (Game Development Based Learning) is a strategy that uses the construction or modification of video games as teaching technique. The main tool used by the GDBL is called GDF (Game Development Frameworks), and consists of a series of environments, libraries or development components that allow the development of such video games. This strategy is based on the theory of constructivist learning, which is based on the construction and refinement of mental models based on experience and sense of autonomy derived from the own motivation to learn. In this work, the model SRL (Self-regulated Learning) will be specifically addressed.
