# HOW TO STORE POLINIZERS

The theory of the optimal diet model predicts that by having two alternative options which offer food, foragers will specialize in the type of prey that offers the highest net return of energy, and thus optimize the exploitation of the food resource (Krebs and Davies, 1986). In this framework, the prediction of such a model is of particular interest in biotic pollination, since specialization by one of the two floral resources has implications for the reproductive success of angiotensinesperms that bloom symmetrically, and that compete for pollinators. Similarly, what is described has consequences on the ability of the bees brain to make economic decisions that benefit them. Therefore, it is of great interest to know the critical factors that affect the processes of discrimination and differentiation of symmetrical flowers, as well as the cognitive processes that are behind the behavioral plasticity required to optimize the diet, and to incur in the specialization of floral constancy
