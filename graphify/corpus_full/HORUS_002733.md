# Identification and prioritization of areas for the decontamination of anti-personnel mines in the municipality of Tumaco, Nariño

The problem of pollution by anti-personnel mines in Colombia is deeply linked to the dynamics of the armed conflict, territorial control and the presence of illicit crops. Tumaco, in the department of Nariño, stands out as one of the areas most affected by this phenomenon, generating high levels of threat to their communities. This research carries out a detailed spatial analysis of the distribution and concentration of events by anti-personnel mines (MAP) in Tumaco, with a focus on the area of the Community Council Alto Mira y Frontera, to identify the most critical areas and prioritize humanitarian demining interventions. The study uses techniques of spatial self-correlation, analysis of trends of central measure, and proximity analysis, allowing to detect areas of high concentration of mines and analyze their relationship with factors such as proximity to coca crops, roads, housing and infrastructure. The results show that the contamination by MAP is concentrated in seven specific areas, and although coca crops are present, they are not the most determinant factor in the intensity of pollution. Instead, the use of mines is more closely related to strategic factors, such as the control of roads and corridors close to the collombo-ecuarian border.
