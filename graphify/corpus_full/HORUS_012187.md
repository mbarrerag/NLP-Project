# EXTENSION STUDIES FOR THE STATIFICATION OF THE INSILTRY MOTHER CANCER. REVISION OF THERAPEUTIC EVIDENCE AND ADVANCE AT THE NATIONAL INSTITUTE OF CANCEROLOGY, BOGOTA - COLOMBIA

Breast cancer is the most frequently diagnosed cancer in women around the world. In 2020, we estimated 2.3 million new cases being the fifth cause of mortality with 13.6 deaths per 100,000 women. In Colombia the incidence is calculated at 48.3% per 100,000 women with an ascending mortality rate (ASR 13.1/100,000).The objectives of the staging of the cancer are to determine the extent of the disease, to define the treatment and prognosis of the disease.Historically, the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system for breast cancer has assigned the stage according to the size of the tumor (T), the presence of ganglionic involvement (N), and the presence or absence of metastases (M).The knowledge of the tumor biology has introduced changes in the staging manual in its eighth edition, incorporating information on some prognostic factors such as the histological grade, state of the estrogen receptors (RE) and progestogens (PR) in comparison with the status of HER2.The patients including these prognostic factors showed that the stages were modified in a decrease in 29.5% and a decrease in the percentage of the patient. In light of this distance involvement, the commonly used staging methods are bone scan (GO), chest x-ray (RxT) and liver ultrasound (UH). In the case of unclear findings, additional studies such as computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance (MRI) are performed. In patients with small tumors without ganglion disease, given the low probability of distant disease, no benefit has been documented in the performance of these tests; in fact, in addition to the unnecessary remedy, the results of false positives requiring additional confirmations delay treatment and generate anxiety in patients. Changes in clinical staging and breast cancer biology motivated the Functional Unit of Breast and Clinical Oncology of the National Institute of Cancerology to review the available scientific evidence to recommend the relevance of extension studies
