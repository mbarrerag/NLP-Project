# PHYSICOCHEMICAL AND TOXICOLOGICAL PROPERTIES OF MERCOSUR AND ITS COMPOUNDS IN RELATION TO ITS USE IN GOLD MINING

The control of mercury in the environment is complex and costly, due mainly to its high mobility and dilution in the natural environment, since it is present not only in the inorganic component of soils, but also in living organisms, which increases its mobility. Mercury is a highly reactive metal, and its chemical form changes depending on the conditions prevailing in the environment, and is particularly affected by the presence of organic agents. This paper initially presents a perspective of the serious toxicological and environmental effects resulting from the use and/or exposure to mercury, which gave rise to the Minamata Convention, whose treaty faces great difficulties in its implementation in Colombia. Based on this general framework, and taking into account that there are still many questions about the mobility of mercury in nature, it reviews the main physicochemical characteristics of mercury and its cycle in the natural environment, together with the main relationships of thermodynamic stability in the aqueous environment through Eh-pH diagrams.
