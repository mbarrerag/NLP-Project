# Preliminary evaluation of mechanical properties of recycled PET fiber reinforced concrete

This paper presents the findings of an experimental study on the mechanical properties (compressive strength, flexural strength, tensile strength, and modulus of elasticity) of concrete reinforced with recycled PET fibers. The main goal of the research was to investigate the effect of recycled PET fiber addition into concrete on some mechanical properties of the material. PET fibers with a length of 55 mm were mixed into concrete by different amounts from 0 kg to 6 kg per m3. After that, tests of workability, modulus of elasticity, compressive, flexural strength, and tensile strength were made.The results indicate that the inclusion of PET fibers reduces the workability of fresh concrete, as evidenced by decreased slump values. However, PET fibers contribute to a lower apparent density and reduced water absorption and void percentages in the hardened concrete. Compressive strength tests revealed an optimal PET fiber content of approx. 2 kg per m3, which enhanced strength, while higher fiber contents led to a decline in performance. Flexural and splitting tensile strength tests demonstrated significant improvements with the addition of PET fibers.Overall, the study confirms that recycled PET fibers can effectively reinforce concrete, improving its mechanical properties while offering an innovative solution to plastic waste management. These findings support the integration of recycled PET fibers in construction practices, promoting environmental sustainability and advancing the development of eco-friendly building materials
