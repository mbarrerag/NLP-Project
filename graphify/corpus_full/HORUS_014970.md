# COLOMBIAN PACIFIC: TERRITORIAL FORMATION

The Colombian Pacific coast is a territory of difference, as Arturo Escobar (2015) puts it, in which biotic and abiotic elements formed a unique region, in relation to population configuration and biodiversity, which cannot be dissociated.It is a territory or a ¿territory-region¿, as called by social movements and activists who, after years of struggles, achieved collective title to the territory, in a movement parallel to the construction of an identity (ESCOBAR, 2015). The concept of "territory-region" is conceived as a political construction for the defense of territories and sustainability, as it emerged and spread in association with social movements in the 1980s and 1990s, against a backdrop of socio-environmental conflicts and territorial disputes. In this conception, the trait of territoriality is present, which is the identity, immaterial and symbolic aggregation to the more traditional idea of ​​territory and which mentions the specificity of the place, with its particularities, which form territorialities (SAQUET, 2015). Given these delimitations, we will carry out a case study here seeking to analyze the construction of the Afro-Colombian territory, through a historical perspective contemplating the main events that occurred from the 16th century onwards, when the Colombian Pacific region began to be occupied by Africans and Europeans.The focus will be on events that occurred throughout, and mainly at the end of, the 20th century, with the rise of the concept of territory-region and the implementation of Law 70 of 1993. To this end, a bibliographical survey was carried out.
