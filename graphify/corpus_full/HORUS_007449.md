# Evaluation of transcriptional changes of immortalized human trophoblastic cells (BeWo) infected with Trypanosoma cruzi

The congenital transmission of Trypanosoma cruzi represents an important challenge for global health, derived from complex interactions between the para, mother, placenta and the fetus. Although the molecular mechanisms of T. cruzi passing through T. cruzi through the placenta are largely unknown, their interaction with trophoblastic cells is essential for infection. Research has revealed substantial changes in the gene expression of the placenta during infection, especially in routes related to the immune response. However, there is still a gap in knowledge about specific molecular interactions, particularly in relation to the discrete characterization unit (DTU) I of T. cruzi, which is prevalent in Colombia and Venezuela, but little explored in the context of congenital transmission.
