# PHYSICAL-CHEMICAL AND CHEMICAL PROPERTIES OF PAPAYA SUBJECTED TO DIFFERENT DRYING METHODS

Papaya (Carica papaya L.) has a great influence on food worldwide, and can be consumed in different ways, in addition to having a large presence in the market.Drying is a process for preserving biological products, which aims to reduce product moisture.Drying, if done incorrectly, can cause negatively on the quality of biological products, altering their color, as well as degrading important active compounds.In this work, the objective was to evaluate changes in some physical-chemical properties of papaya, such as water activity (Aw), color and carotenoid content, after convective drying (C), by refractance window (RW) and by freeze drying (L).As a result, Aw was reduced, being considered stable for non-proliferation of microorganisms.The color change (¿E*) was significantly different only for test L. The hue angle (h*) varied, in general, from reddish orange to yellowish orange, with L and C50 being the only ones statistically distinct from the initial sample. Regarding the carotenoid content, all tests were different from the initial sample, with L being the largest among all, and indicating a degradation of these compounds during drying for the other treatments.In this way, drying provided changes in the physicochemical properties of all tests, mainly in L, which proved to be distinct in all analyzes carried out, with relevant maintenance of bioactive compounds
