# COMPLEMENTARY SERVICE CAPACITY OPERATION BY ISLANDS-CAP 4.

The expansion of the industrial sector and population growth, combined with technological advances in recent decades, has led to the growth of electricity demand and the need to meet demand with more complex systems of generation, transport and distribution of electricity, and with technical, social and environmental challenges. On the one hand, there is an increasing difficulty in expanding and renewing the large power stations and current transmission networks, due to, among many other factors, the costs of construction and renovation, the problems associated with the acquisition of servitudes and the opposition of some populations to the construction of this infrastructure; on the other hand, it has been necessary to return to the beginnings of power systems and change the paradigm of centralized and remote generation from the consumption centres to a distributed generation of less installed and located capacity to the populations.On the other hand, in the last decade, the so-called traditional energy transition has begun to take force, which can be defined as the systemic change of an economy based on fossil energy, large scale and centralized operation, to a energy economy based on the technical footprint, and the economic impact of the last decade
