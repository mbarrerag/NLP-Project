# National Urban systems and connectivity. Analysis from internal citizens in Guatemala, Cuba and Colombia

Latin America is one of the most urbanized developing areas in the world. Today, with a globalized context where relations are increasingly needed, urban systems must also seek to consolidate different connected nodes that allow for expanding opportunities for the entire population. For this reason, the article seeks to analyze the role of intermediate cities in the organization of national urban systems in Colombia, Cuba and Guatemala. For this purpose, qualitative research is developed that uses quantitative tools such as spatial syntax and Social Network Analysis to identify those relationships. It is concluded that there are very different cases analyzed, where Guatemala shows the greatest concentration and perhaps a lesser consolidation of intermediate cities; Colombia also presents a high urban concentration around three main cities, but with intermediate cities that connect to them; and Cuba emerges as the case with a greater distribution of the urban system, with a similar presence throughout the territory of intermediate cities
