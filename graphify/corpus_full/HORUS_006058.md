# Effects of play on cortisol levels in children with ADHD in Bogotá and its relationship to occupational performance

The malpractice disorder by Attention Deficit and Hyperactivity (TDAH) represents a challenge in public health due to its impact on the school, work and social performance of boys and girls. It has been observed that alterations in the hypothalamus-hypophysis-adrenal axis (HHA), especially the production of cortisol, affect the regulation of alert states and sensory reactivity, contributing to the increase in the severity of central symptomatology. This generates limitations in the occupational participation in activities specific to children, especially in the game. Current treatments present limitations in terms of their effectiveness and access. Therefore, the intervention based on play is proposed as an alternative. This strategy, focused on activating the HHA axis through activities that stimulate the movement and the senses, could offer a solution to improve the occupational performance of children with ADHD.
