# Co-ordination compounds of divalent metals with amino acids and dicarboxylic acids: potential antibacterial activity

The coordination compounds with organic ligands of biological interest have shown antibacterial activity in view of a wide variety of genera including some bacteria resistant to conventional treatments. The amino acids are a very versatile group of molecules with two functional groups (amino and carboxyl), this favours interaction with metal allowing synthesis of compounds with high antibacterial potential. In this thesis they were synthesized by conventional methods composed of coordination with copper and nickel divalent metals, and were used as ligands the amino acids glycine, alanine and the dicarboxylic acids itaonic and oxal. The characterization of the synthesized compounds was performed by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA), infra-red spectroscopy by Fourier transform (FTIR), ultraviolet spectroscopy - visible (UV-vis) and anti-ffraction of X rays (DRX), showing that the compounds are octaedric, crystalline and stable at temperatures between 250 and 300°C.
