# PHYSIOLOGY OF CONSERVATION, PROGRESS AND PROSPECTS WITHIN HERPETHOLOGY

Amphibians and reptiles are vertebrates that due to their morphological, physiological and behavioral characteristics have managed to colonize several terrestrial and aquatic environments. Within the great richness of morpho-physiological adaptations we can refer accumulation of molecules against thermal stress, different systems of gaseous exchange, rapid growth in biomass (compared with endothers), enter. Despite this great diversity of structures to address environmental stresses, factors such as climate change, environmental pollution, as well as processes of fragmentation and loss of habitat have strongly affected the biodiversity of herpets. Disciplines of biology such as ecology, biogeography or genetics have supported the development of methodologies to address various environmental problems. Even in the last 20 years, a new area of biological research has been developed, called conservation physiology, which has supported the defense of biodiversity by analyzing how the functioning of several species has been affected by stressors of natural and anthropic origin.
