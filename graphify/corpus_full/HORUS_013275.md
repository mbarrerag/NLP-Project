# Methodology for the recognition of non-stationary seismic-volcanic patterns through adaptive learning techniques

Volcanic monitoring is an essential task in the context of prevention and risk management; in this sense, the vulcanological and seismic observatories fulfill a transcendental mission in the declaration of early warnings of volcanic eruption. And within this work, the correct classification of seismicity represents an indispensable input for the interpretation of the volcanic phenomenon and the characterization of eruption dynamics; for this reason, it is necessary that the classification is carried out in an agile and reliable manner. Through the seismicity correctly labeled, the analysis experts can characterize the processes that would be occurring inside a volcano, and identify precursors of a eruption. However, the correct discrimination of seismic events is often affected by the migration of seismic sources, alterations in the fluid dynamics, changes in the mechanisms of generation of cracks, among other situations, which can modify the probability distribution of seismic registers (changes of concept), and therefore, increase the non-station of these signals. To this end, a classification model was designed focusing on the area of adaptive learning and based on incremental learning (not yet explored in seismic data), which addresses the paradigm of change of the concept, so that some properties such as the continuous recurrence of acquired data, the multiclass nature of records, geological effects and generalisation restrictions in classification, are considered, exploited and eventually counterbalanced at the time of making automatic classification of earthquakes .
