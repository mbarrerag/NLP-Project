# SLEEP ARCHITECTURE CHARACTERIZATION IN THE MURINE MODEL OF DEMYELINATION BY CUPRIZONE

Background Multiple sclerosis (MS) is the most common chronic inflammatory disease of the central nervous system in young adults, clinically characterized by a relapsing-remitting course with progressive neurological deterioration and demyelinating lesions of the white matter. MS patients have a higher prevalence of sleep disorders compared to the general population, estimated to be between 25 to 50%. Sleep disorders may impact negatively que quality of life in MS patients and it has been associated with fatigue, the most common symptom of MS patients. Objectives To characterize the sleep architecture in the murine model of demyelination by cuprizone. Methods Male Wistar rats of 10 weeks were separated into control and experimental groups each with 8 animals. Daily cuprizone (2g/kg/día) was administered to the experimental group for 8 weeks, and polysomnographic recordings of the sleep-wake cycle of freely-moving rats of 10 weeks were examined for 72 hours at baseline pretreatment and post treatment with cuprizone. Additionally, the motor coordination and balance was evaluate using the beam balance test with beams measuring 12 mm and 6 mm width, at baseline and post treatment. After that animals were sacrificed and their brains were fixed and histology with sudan black for myelin was performed. Results Compared to baseline values, cuprizone treatment showed in the dark period a significant increase in the percentage of rapid eye movement sleep (REM) (4,46% to 7,49%, p=0.025) with a trend to an increased percentage of nonrapid eye movement (NREM) (31.56% to 41.33, p=0.09), in the light period there was a significant decrease in the duration of the REM sleep events in the light period (¿. Treated animals also showed a trend to and increased total sleep time (43.6% to 56.6), total REM sleep increased from 5.9 % to 8.9 %, NREM sleep increased from 37.7 % to 47.7 % with a concomitant reduction in total awake time (56,4% to 43,4%). Animals treated with cuprizone showed a worsening of the motor function with a significant decrease in the average performance score in the beam balance test (3,5 to 2,56, p=0.022). In addition, there was a significant decrease in the myelin staining in the corpus callosum and in the projections from the locus coeruleus and mesencephalic trigeminal nucleus (p < 0.001). Conclusions Cuprizone treatment resulted in alterations of the sleep architecture in the animals, possibly related with demyelination of projections from brain structures associated with the sleep/wake cycle
