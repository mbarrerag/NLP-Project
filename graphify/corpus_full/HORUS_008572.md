# THANATOPHOBIA, FEAR OF DEATH, AND COMFORT AGAINST PALLIATIVE CARE IN COLOMBIAN NURSING STUDENTS

Nursing training in palliative care is an area of growing development and interest given the global burden of chronic disease and with it the growing demand for long-term care. Studies conducted in Spanish nursing students showed a high prevalence of fear of death, where inverse correlations were found between fear of death with age and religious practice. A study conducted in Colombia showed low levels of knowledge, a high prevalence of fear of death, and low comfort when providing palliative care. This same study revealed that, after an educational intervention, comfort when providing palliative care was significantly improved. The purpose of this study was to describe and correlate the level of thanatophobia, fear of death, and comfort with palliative care in nursing students in Colombia. Quantitative analytical and cross-sectional study, with a sample of 66 Colombian nursing students. The Collett Lester Fear of Death, Thanatophobia and Comfort scales in palliative care were applied. Descriptive statistics and correlations between the variables of interest were processed. The sample consisted of 77.3% women, 21.2% men, and 1.5% non-binary. 90.9% of the students declared that they have low economic income. 69.7% reported having received some training in palliative care and 50% reported having had some experience in providing palliative care. 80.3% stated that they were very interested in the subject. The participants had an average age of 23.44 years. The fear of one's own death had an average of 19.37, the fear of the dying process itself of 24.68, the fear of the death of others of 23.56 and the fear of the dying process of others of 22.69; all the variables of fear of death have a maximum possible score of 28 points, so it can be seen that they are at high levels. Comfort in palliative care had an average of 33.93 points, which positions it at a medium level, while the thanatophobia scale had an average of 14.18 points, that is, an intermediate level. Direct correlations were observed between fear of one's own death and the dying process (0.461 p<0.05), fear of one's own death and the death of others (0.556 p<0.05), fear of one's own death and the process of dying of others (.422 p<0.05), in addition to an inverse correlation between fear of the process of dying of others and comfort with palliative care (-.341 p<0.05). More training is required in nursing students with the purpose of reducing the levels of fear of death and the process of dying, one's own and that of others, reducing the levels of thanatophobia and increasing comfort with the provision of palliative care
