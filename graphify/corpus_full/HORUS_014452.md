# Study of the formation of carbon nanostructures from biomass gasification tar.

During biomass gasification, tars are produced together with gases. These compounds can cause operational problems when condensed, decreasing the overall efficiency of the process. Many methods have been studied to reduce or eliminate these compounds, the most common being gasifier modifications, wet and dry cleaning methods, and thermal or catalytic cracking. The cracking of these molecules produces more gases, but few studies are interested in formed carbon deposits, which in some cases can lead to carbon nanostructures. This paper proposes a methodology that allows the use of these compounds for the formation of value added products in gasification or biomass pyrolysis systems. A fractionation was made to determine the size and type of molecules present in tar fractions. Also, the use of a catalyst supported in carbonisation that is the subproduct of these thermochemical processes of biomass, so that contact with volatiles or tarns forms carbon deposits in it. These new carbon structures can provide new possibilities for gasification and pyrolysis technologies that can translate into a technical process
