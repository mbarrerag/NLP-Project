# BOND STRENGTH RESISTANCE EVALUATION OF CAD/CAM INTRARADICULAR RETAINERS

To determine the behavior of the micro tensile bond strength of intraradicular retainer (IR) made with CAD/CAM technology. An in vitro quantitative and qualitative experimental study was carried out where 45 dentin discs were used from 18 single-rooted premolars extracted for orthodontic indication. The samples were divided into 3 experimental groups (n=15/group) according to the IR material: type III gold core, berglass post, and hybrid ceramic. Previously, the samples underwent endodontic treatment and the IR cementation was carried out with dual self-adhesive resin cement. Coronal, medium, and apical radicular discs were obtained (1 mm high). The push-out test was performed on the Shimadzu AG-SI universal testing machine, where an indenter was positioned in the load cell that coincided with the length of the IRs, and each disc was positioned in an attachment with holes that would allow optimal dislodgement of the RI without interference. Each one of the discs was analyzed and photographed in the stereomicroscope, and the type of failure was classi ed. ANOVA and post hoc test demonstrated that there were no signi cant di erences between the means of the 3 experimental groups after the push-out test (p=0.0825). For the qualitative analysis, a higher percentage of adhesive failure was obtained (49%), for cohesive failure there was 36% and for mixed failures 15%. Both gold and berglass had a higher failure rate than hybrid CAD/CAM ceramics, being 34% for the rst two and 32% for the latter. There is no statistically signi cant di erence in bond strength between the experimental groups of type III gold intraradicular retainers, berglass posts, and hybrid ceramic CAD/CAM retainers.
