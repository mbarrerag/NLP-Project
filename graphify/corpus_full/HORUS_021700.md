# Safe glycemia regulation considering parameter variations under the offset-free MPC with pulse inputs scheme

Type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) treatment with an insulin infusion pump can be represented as a control system with pulse inputs, in which a discontinuous control action has an amplitude with nonnegligible duration but remains null a significant part of the sampling time. Despite the vast development of control strategies for discrete systems and some others for impulsive ones, the generalization for pulse-controlled systems has not been widely studied. In this chapter, pulse-controlled systems are characterized and their uses with diabetes treatment is briefly explained. Afterwards, with the aim of providing safe glycemic control by considering parameter variations, an offset-free model predictive control (OF-MPC) approach for pulsatile systems is presented. Physiological variations in subjects with T1DM constantly change their insulin requirements, which, if not compensated, can lead to hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia episodes. In this regard, the OF-MPC strategy aims to compensate for the offset problem caused by a plant–model mismatch or constant disturbances by correcting the control input. The strategy consists of an augmented model with an integrating state, an estimator to capture the mismatch, and the inclusion of the estimated mismatch in the prediction model and the target calculation to achieve the objective. Finally, the OF-MPC formulation is enhanced with an insulin-on-board constraint to avoid insulin overdoses at mealtimes. The constraint is activated or released when trends towards hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia are detected, respectively. The developed methods are evaluated in 10 adult virtual patients of the UVA/Padova Simulator considering sensor noise and physiological variations
