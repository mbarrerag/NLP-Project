# CHAPTER 5 ANALYSIS OF TEXTURA PROFILE (TPA)

The quality of meat is an essential attribute that is recognized and approved by customers. Therefore, the identification of the biological factors that control the ternity of meat is important to improve that quality.The research carried out during the last decades has established that the collagen content within a muscle can partially explain the variation in the ternity of the meat cooked.Most studies have focused on the identification of the nature of the intramuscular connective tissue (tcim), in particular the quantity of covalent tissues and its association with the physiological age, nutrition and the rate of growth of the animal.The variation in the ternity has been attributed to structural changes in the muscle fibers that are produced due to the post-mortem proteolysis (pm) of the myofibres and cytoskeletal proteins; however, the studies on the texture of the meat have argued against this concept, and the extracellular matrix (mec) is to some extent the structure responsible for the process of meat tyrinization, due to its direct association with both the microfibres and the tissue with the previous. For foods such as meat, texture refers to ternity, which makes it the most important quality factor for the sensory appreciation of this food (Kramer and Szczesniak, 1973). The perception of a food has been described as a cyclical process that starts with anticipation through visual impulses that are complemented and favored by previous experiences. In addition to the structure of the food and its behavior when consumed, its attributes (mechanical, geometric and superficial) must be taken into account, as well as the speed of the movement of the jaw and tongue in the mouth, which are critical factors in the perception of texture; when an individual consumes a food and chews it must go beyond the initial breakage which, along with stimuli, will form part of the overall feeling of texture (Rosenthal, 2001). On the other hand, the texture is an attribute of multiple parameters, which is influenced by the structure of the food, either molecular, microscopic or macroscopic.
