# Perception of high school students of their own responsibility from the “Theory of Choice” with the use of “The Language of Internal Control”

This study examines the perception of the teaching of the Language of internal control, based on the Theory of William Glasser's choice, in the daily behavior and personal responsibility of the students of the Rochester College in their school activities. A quantitative approach was used through a Likert type survey with 30 statements, developed collaboratively by students, teachers and managers. The main objective was to explore how students perceive the influence of the Language of internal control on their ability to take responsibility for their actions, considering factors such as the time of exposure to theory and gender differences. The data were analyzed by descriptive statistics to identify patterns and trends in the perceptions. The results suggest that the integration of the Theory of choice into the curriculum promotes the autonomy and responsibility of the students, benefiting their academic and personal development. These findings provide a basis for improving pedagogical strategies and optimizing the use of the Language of internal control in the language, in the daily language and be participation. and, benefiting both their academic and personal development. These results provide a foundation for improving educational strategies and optimizing the use of Internal Control Language in the educational process
