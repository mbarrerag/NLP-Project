# From the stone prison to Mare Nostrum: the anti-Oqueño project to go out to the Urabá through Dabeiba and Pavaradocito, 1886-1950

Towards the end of the nineteenth century the Anti-Oquequesian elite initiated a regional project whose main objective was to ensure the exit of Antioquia to the Caribbean Sea through the Gulf of Urabá. The components of this project were, first, the construction of a road that from Medellin reached the West of the department, either to one of the rivers that taxed its waters to the river Atrato or directly to the Gulf. Second, it was sought to obtain an cession of 100,000 hectares of waste by the nation to the department in Dabiiba and Pavarandocito (hoy Mutatá), that is, to the westernmost end of the territory of Antioquia, in order to fix from there a point of entry to the Urabá from which the colonization and the settlement of the area would be promoted. The last element was the dissolution of the former indigenous reserve of Cañasgordas, and to establish the necessary regional missions, since colonial times it had become a kind of diqueno that prevented the passage of settlers towards the West of Anti-Aqueño. The idea was that their lands would be free for the market and their inhabitants as a useful hand of exploiting. of a road that, starting from Medellín, would raise the West of the department, each to one of the rivers that flowed into the Attra River or directly to the Gulf. Second, they sought to obtain a transfer of one hundred hundred hectares of vacation land by the nation to the department in Dabiiba and Pavarandocito (now days Mutatá). It was the westernmost extreme of the Antioquia territory. They wanted to set an entry point to Urabá to promote the colonization and settlement in the area.
