# Organic carbon reserves in mangroves of the Colombian Pacific and their relationship to the degree of anthropic intervention

The mangrove forests, despite occupying less than 1% of the total area of tropical forests, represent approximately 3% of carbon sequestration by the world's tropical forests. Mangrove forests capture and store organic carbon (CO), which gives them a fundamental role in mitigating climate change. However, they are subject to anthropic disturbances that negatively affect their structure and functioning. The aim of this study was to evaluate: (i) the relationship of CO with the modification of the plant structure by anthropic action, with the assumption that, to the lesser degree of disturbance, greater CO would be in forests. (ii) the relationship of CO in sediments with the depth of soil in forests with different degrees of anthropic intervention, where the hypothesis that preserved forests would have greater CO in the surface layers, and disturbed forests in the deeper layers of the organic ones. (iii) the CO from the coast to the interior in forests with different degrees of anthropic intervention, with the hypothesis of higher CO reserves in the forest with respect to the coastal line and (iv) the effect of the anthropogenic soil and the soil (iii) The analysis of the soil and the soil in the soil with different degrees of the soil. The results show that there is a general tendency for anthropotropo-tropical impacts to decrease CO reserves in mangrove forests, without forgetting that natural impacts such as coastal erosion pose a very significant threat to these reserves. The estimated organic carbon losses were between 33 and 58%, corresponding to 754,9 and 1.313,6 Mg CO2 -eq. ha-1, respectively. The impact on organic carbon reserves depends on the type and magnitude of anthropic disturbage. The forests with higher structural development are subject to increased pressure. The results obtained in this study serve as the basis for decisions in management programmes and for the elaboration of appropriate measures in the framework of the REDD+ schemes, which allows for the projecting of sustainable uses of the ecosystem (Text with the source).
