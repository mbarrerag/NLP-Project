# ON LEGAL MIGRATIONS 2.0: TRANSNATIONAL EXCHANGE OF CONSTITUTIONAL CONCEPTS IN THE HISTORY OF MODERN CONSTITUTIONALISM, WITH SPECIAL ATTENTION TO THE DISCUSSION OF SOCIAL CONSTITUTIONALISM OF GERMANY TO IHISPANIC AMERICA

This article aims to outline the science of constitutional law compared to overcoming the mechanistic model of legal transplantation, based on transnational perspectives of the diffusion and transculturation of constitutional concepts. The theoretical-methodological foundation is the Socio-cultural, Historical and Transnational School of Public Law and the Constitutional State, developed from 2006 by the author with his colleagues and team within the framework of the CC-Constitutionalism Comparative research group that is attached to the Faculty of Law, Political and Social Sciences of the National University of Colombia in Bogotá. Basically, it combines elements of the history of law, comparative law, political science and social and cultural historiography. After developing this conception, the article applies it to the subject of social constitutionalism, asking to what extent the respective norms of the Weimar Constitution of 1919 (Germany) inspired the constitutional reforms of the interbellum in Latin America including the Colombian one of 1936 and elsewhere.
