# Geochemical characterization and tectonic evolution of the Geneva Olympic Complex

The occurrence and types of of ofiolites in the Central mountain range of the Colombian Andes have not been clearly defined in terms of tectonic configuration and geochemical characteristics. Therefore, the study of the tectonic environment and subduction zones in the magmatic evolution of the oliolites becomes a significant topic. The Odiolytic Complex of Geneva (COG) is located in the western flank of the Central Cordillera in the Department of the Cauca Valley, southwest of Colombia, assigned to the Cretaceous Inferior value, as indicated by the contact of the COG with the Buga Batolith. The COG is formed by three main lithologies: amphibolith, gabre and ultramaphic rocks (pyroxine and peridotite), according to Ossa (2007). The rocks in the section of Puente Piedra, located to the northeast of the municipality of Geneva, are part of the Gabrotic cumulations within the physic sequence.
