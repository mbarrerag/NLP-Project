# Identification of genes associated with neurocomportative changes due to Zika virus infection in BALB/c mice

The Zika virus (ZIKV) is an arbovirus that causes the “Zika congenital syndrome”, associated with various neurodevelopment disorders (NDDs) where the microcephaly is one of the most severe manifestations. There are milder NDDs that can go unnoticed in neonates, such as autistic spectrum disorders, delays in neuropsychomotor development and language resulting in social and academic difficulties. The murine models of ZIKV infection reproduce motor and cognitive defects reported in humans which can be evaluated by behavioral devices for subsequent contrast with genetic expression profiles, obtained useful in the characterization of NDDs by ZIKV. This study focused on identifying genes associated with behavioral changes by Zika virus infection in BALB/c juvenile mice. It was inoculated subcutaneously with ZIKV (MH544701.2) mice with doses of 6.8x103 PFU at 1 postnatal day (DPN). The presence of the virus in the brain and corrodin was verified and quantified by the 10 and 30 days of the post- "Congenital Zika Syndrome," associated with various neurodevelopmental disorders (NDDs), where microcephaly is one of the most serious manifestations. Milder NDDs, including autism spectrum disorders and delays in neuropsychomotor and language development, may go unnotified in neonates, reducing in social and academic challenges. Murine models of ZIKV infection replicate motor and cognitive defects reported in humans, assessable through behabitual diseases for secondary comparison with gene expression profiles, valued in characterizing ZIKV induced virus with reduced profile. This was associated with a strong downward regulation of genes related to synapses and structural functions in axons, dendrites, and myelin sheaths. Overall, this data provides new insights into genes and potential molecular paths altered in a middle infection processes, suggesting candidate genes for future investigations
