# From the ox to the locomotive, energy, steel and metabolism in the Irac Valley (Boyaca - Colombia, 1954-1983)

The Colombian historiography and the economic development after the Second World War has concentrated on identifying industrial and business conditions, as well as the technological availability in national production, and how these have translated into development, growth of the coverage of services and the improvement of the quality of life in various regions of the country. However, less attention has been given to the appreciation of the historical-environmental impact that have had on the territories these human and industrial activities on a large scale. For the most part of its history, Colombia has been characterized by being a nation of agricultural and mining vocation, but during the 1950s-1970s the palmary effort to stimulate the domestic production of manufactured raw materials, fundamental for the infrastructure, the internal consumption and the consequent economic growth. Regarding the mining and the steel industry, two strategic sectors, the case allows for the productiveness of the Paz del Río, a factor that, from the extraction and transformation of iron, the coal and the limestone sought to supply steel to the nation. The present research seeks to understand what were the most significant social and ecological changes during the years of the boom of the production of steel (1954-1983). The first step is to ensure that the economic situation of the economy is not affected by the economic situation of the country. However, no studies have emerged that historically address the impact that these large-scale human and industrial activities have on the territories.
