# Systematic Review of Controlled Random Trials on Behavioral Interventions Directed at Alcohol and Cannabis Consumption in Young Adults

There is a wide range of behavioural interventions based on different theoretical approaches; some have shown some level of effectiveness in reducing cannabis and alcohol use rates, but few have been tested through a Random Controlled Trial (Eca) design. Searches were conducted in medline, Embase, apa psychnet and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CCS) of eca, published in English, which examined behavioural interventions for cannabis and alcohol use. 207 summaries were examined and 11 eca were identified with 4 211 young adults. Interventions with higher retention rates and statistically significant results were administered in online format and focused on reducing cannabis use. Only the combination of motivational interview and behavioral economy showed significant results after follow-up, with a high retention rate. The integration of behavioral interventions into public policies at institutional, local or national level could lead to more positive outcomes and to longer-term effectiveness of the implementation of these interventions could lead to improved evidence-based public consumption outcomes
