# PERFORMANCE AT REAL TIME FOR OXFORD SEQUENCE OF SARS-COV-2 IN THE VALLEY OF CAUCA, COLOMBIA

The genomic surveillance of the SARS-CoV-2 has been incorporated as an effective strategy to give rapid response to public health systems in order to carry out an early identification of emerging variants of the virus. In this sense, the protocols of sequencing by amplitude using portable technologies of Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) have achieved the accumulation to the date of more than 6.8 million genomes of SARS-CoV-2 according to the GISAID baseline. However, these protocols do not provide information of the expected performance of variables such as mapping coverage or minimum depth in steps prior to bioinformatics analysis. This is important to define the sequence times of samples and preferably reduce the costs of loss of reagents or cells (flowcell) in environments with limited resources such as Latin America. With the aim of standardizing and predicting the depth of the samples SARS-CoV-2, the most effective dependency of the SAC-2 was validated between the parameters of the threshold cycle (Ct) of the PCR for the N2 gene (CDC Protocol), dilution of the sample, number of readings, depth and sequence coverage, obtained from more than 800 samples samples of the SACS-S-SV-2.
