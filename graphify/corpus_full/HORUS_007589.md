# Microbiological characteristics associated with virulence of defective mutants in membrane conveyors CtpF and MmpL7 of Mycobacterium tuberculosis

Tuberculosis (TB) is an infectious disease mottagious caused by the bacillus alcohol resistant Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb). TB is a constant threat to public health due to its high incidence, the appearance of multi-pharmaceutical-resistant strains (MDR) and extremely-pharmaceutical-resistant (XDR) and co-infection with the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) and the limited effectiveness of the only vaccine approved against this disease, the BCG vaccine. The design of new tools for the control of TB requires a greater understanding of the molecular mechanisms used by Mtb to achieve a successful intracellular pathogen. In this sense, some studies have shown that certain transmembranal transporters have essential functions for the micro-organisms, including the cyto-solate to the cell wall, thus helping the construction of the complex wall (Mfct) The formation of biofilms, which will allow to recognize whether the absence of the encoding genes of these transporters affects elements relevant for the interaction of the tuberculose bacillus with its environment. In addition, the effect of the deletion of mmpL7 on the lipid content by thin layer chromatography, as well as the transcriptional behavior of some genes related to the transport and biosynthesis of PDIM, will be analyzed. Finally, the effect of the deletion of ctpF on the expression of the immunomodulatory antigen ESAT-6 of Mtb. All this will be useful in the future construction of a double mutant Mtb (MtbH37Rv::: {8710;ctpF}8710;mmpL7) with vaccine potential
