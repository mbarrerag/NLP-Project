# Pilot study of neurological manifestations in patients positive for COVID-19 and Long COVID, and their relationship with variants of the genes ACE2 (rs2074192 and rs879922) and APOE (rs429358 and rs7412) in a group of patients in Bogotá D.C.

The Pandemic of COVID-19, caused by the virus SARS-CoV-2, has brought with it not only an immediate challenge to the overall public health, but also a series of clinical manifestations of long term known as LONG COVID or post-acute sequelae of infection by SARS-CoV-2 (PASC).This multisystem syndrome is characterized by a wide range of persistent or emerging symptoms that extend beyond 4 weeks, has generated growing concern in both the medical community and in the general population.In addition, its appearance is independent of the initial severity of the infection, but it is observed in more severe cases and is still subdiagnostic.The understanding of the pathogenesis of LONG COVID is still incipient. Among the most debilitating symptoms are the neurological and psychiatric manifestations, such as loss of memory, cognitive impairment and mood disorders such as anxiety and depression. Recent studies have suggested similarities between the brain disorders observed with neurodegenerative pathologies. The relationship between genetics and manifestations of COVID-19 and LONG COVID is complex and no association was found between the studied NPSs and the symptoms of the disease. The persistent neurological and psychiatric symptoms suggest that multiple factors, both genetic and environmental, may be involved in the individual response to the SARS-CoV-2, highlighting the need for a more detailed exploration of the underlying biological mechanisms .
