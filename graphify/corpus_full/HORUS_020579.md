# Editorial. History and literature: Reading the past with eyes on the future

At present history and literature have, or pretend to have, clear and definite limits. When a historian carries out an investigation with historical characteristics, he knows, in advance, how he should carry it out and how he should present the results obtained. For his part, a literary writer also knows that the way he presents his literary products is qualitatively different from historical fiction. That is, historical and literary fictions are, par excellence, different. Historians, for the most part, would not accept that one of their own would write a historical novel as a result, for example, of a doctoral thesis in history. Meanwhile, a novelist will not pretend, when writing a historical novel, that it replaces the research carried out by a historian
