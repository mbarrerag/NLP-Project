# Gastroprotective effect of the butanolic fraction from Physalis peruviana fruits

Physalis peruviana L. berries have been used as an adjuvant treatment in different inflammatory diseases, but their pharmacological activity in gastritis has not been reported. To evaluate the gastroprotective effects of the butanol fraction of goldenberry fruits (BF-PP), first, it was obtained by ethanol percolation process from dried and crushed ripe fruits, and then it was tested as prophylactic in in vivo animal models (rats and mice) with gastric injury induced by oral administration of EtOH-50% and Indomethacin, and surgically by pyloric ligation. Gastric injury index (GII), mucus, secretions, and histopathological changes were evaluated, as well as oxidative stress biomarkers in gastric tissue homogenate. The BF-PP showed antioxidant activity in vitro (FRAP assay) and reduced significantly the GII, thiobarbituric acid reactive substances, and infiltration of inflammatory cells in gastric tissue. Based on the results, BF-PP at 100mg/kg in acute models of erosive gastritis presented gastroprotective properties, possibly related to antioxidant and antiinflammatory activities
