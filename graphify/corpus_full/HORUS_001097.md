# Dosimetric study in the treatment modality of total skin irradiation with electrons, based on the rotational technique at the National Cancer Institute

In 2023, the National Institute of Cancer (INC) implemented the total skin radiation technique based on the Stanford protocol to treat clinical pathologies of skin cancer, such as mycosis fungoid, Sézary syndrome and sarcomas of the Kaposi type. The main objective of the project was to conduct a dosimetric study to evaluate and optimize a protocol of total skin irradiation with electrons, using the rotational technique (RTSEI) as an alternative to the Stanford method. This rotational approach was specifically designed for the treatment of skin carcinomas as Micosis Fungoid. During the study, thermoluminescent dosimeters (TLD), radiochromic films and diode-type dosimetry systems were used, allowing a precise evaluation of the distribution of the dose. The results showed that the rotational technique offers significant advantages over the Stanford technique, including a greater homogeneity in the distribution of the dose and a considerable reduction in the total treatment time.
