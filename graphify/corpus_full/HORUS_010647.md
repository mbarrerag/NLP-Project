# AES-SUNDAY-780. GENOMIC ANALYSIS REVEALS THE GENETIC DETERMINANTS ASSOCIATED WITH ANTIBIOTIC RESISTANCE IN PANTOEA ANANATIS ISOLATED FROM PINTOMYIA EVANSI VECTOR OF LEISHMANIA IN COLOMBIA SOUTH AMERICA

Pantoea ananatis has been closely associated with the microbiome and different bioactivities in vector insects. This bacterium is considered ubiquitous as it is found in diverse and unusual ecological niches. The genome of the strain Pantoea ananatis 140-UN isolated from Pintomyia evansi, was sequenced using the MiSeq platform with paired reads of 300 bases. The SPAdes program was used for de novo assembly and the PGAP and RAST platforms for genome annotation. the results revealed a chromosome with 4,929,135 bp and a plasmid of 308,225 bp. The Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD) and ResFinder 3.2 server were used to search for antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes. Finally, nine genes related to AMR were identified, four of which showed an identity and coverage greater than 90%. The first, identified by homology, is related to resistance to antibiotics and biocides, including beta-lactams, which potentially explains the resistance of this strain to penicillin observed in previous studies. This information will be clue to understanding possible activities against pathogens, resistance against antibiotics as well as the specific organism-host relationship that occurs with the vector insect within the gastrointestinal compartment
