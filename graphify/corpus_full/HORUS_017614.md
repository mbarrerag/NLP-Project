# BIOMODIFICATION OF DEMINERALIZED DENTIN BY THE APPLICATION OF MYRICETIN

To evaluate the biochemical and mechanical changes induced by myricetin as dentin collagen cross-linker Methods: Slabs of medium coronal dentin were obtained from healthy human third molars. The slabs was conditioned with 10% phosphoric acid for 5h, and randomly distributed into test groups. Control groups: mineralized dentin (DM) and demineralized dentin (DD); and experimental groups: 100% ethanol (EtOH), 0.2% chlorhexidine (CHX), 1% glutaraldehyde (GA), 6.5% proanthocyanidin (PAC), and 600-μM myricetin (MYR). Dentin slabs were assessed for chemical interaction and cross-linking effect under Diode-Laser Raman Spectroscopy. To evaluate collagen solubilization, treated dentin specimens were exposed to 0.1% collagenase for 24h. The supernatant was collected and analyzed by hydroxyproline assay using a spectrophotometer at a wavelength of 540/560nm (n=3). Additionally, dentin sticks (1x1x8mm) were exposed to the test substances for 1h to evaluate flexural strength and elastic modulus. A three-point bending test was performed on the Shimadzu AG-SI Series universal testing machine at a cross-speed of 0.5 mm/min (n=15).Data were analyzed using ANOVA and Tukey's test (a=0.05). MYR significantly decreased the collagen solubility (10,47±2,82mg/mL) of DD (35,81±5,62mg/mL), similar to the results obtained with GA (8,84±4,60mg/mL) and PAC (12,47±4,57mg/mL). In contrast, significant differences were found with dentin treated with EtOH (31,37±4,33mg/mL) and CHX (30,74±4,43mg/mL), which did not have the ability to decrease collagen degradation. The cross-linking effect on MYR, PAC, and GA was corroborated by the increase of amide I and II bands and by the reduction in the intensity of amide III. MYR-treated dentin showed the highest flexural strength and demonstrated a viscoelastic behavior. MYR, GA and PAC showed a positive effect on the recovery of the elastic modulus of DD. (p=0.073). Myricetin positively promoted dentinal collagen crosslinking comparable to GA and PAC. These dentin biomodifiers induce biochemical and biomechanical changes in the substrate that can benefit the stabilization of the hybrid layer
