# The hidden plots of the derailleur: an aesthetic interpretation of the morals of Manuel Antonio Carreño's "Manual of urbanity and good ways"

The "Manual of urbanity and good ways" by Manuel Antonio Carreño is a work of the mid 19th century whose moral value contains aesthetic significances. When studying it, a field of research opens up that, dialoging mainly with the history of education and societies, sociology and philosophy, reveals that aesthetic dimension. Contextualizing the role of manuals in Latin American formation from the late 19th century and the beginning of the 20th century, its role in the hygiene of morals is underlined. In addition, a conceptual framework is established that allows to find intersections between morality and aesthetics, and the discourse of the Manual is analyzed and interpreted in a hermeneutic manner based on its structure, origins, objectives and place that it occupied in the search for renewal that were based on modern and illustrated imperatives. The reflections raised give prominence to the virtue of the recato and it is argued that, by linking with the prudence, modesty, decency and decorum, this is configured as an aesthetic of the appearances that allow to explore topics on the body, the senses and passions. The reflections result is a concept, and orientation, which, with the fundamental is a sense of the urse of the Manual is hermeneutically analyzed and interpreted based on its structure, origins, objects, and the place it occupied in the quest for renewal based on modern and enlightened imperatives. The resulting insights give prominence to the virtue of deaths, and it is argued that this virtue, in conjunction with proof, modesty, decrease, and decorum, is configured as an aesthetics of application that allows for the exploration of those related to the body, the senses, and the passions.
