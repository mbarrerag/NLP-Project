# Fractal analysis of hydrothermal gaps in both emeraldiferous belts: Mechanisms of gaping and evolution

The mechanisms that gave rise to the gap were determined that allowed the migration of the hydrothermal fluids generators of the Colombian emeralds in both esmeraldifer belts. From the development of a petrographic analysis, the different hydrothermal events that acted in these belts were observed. Subsequently, an analysis of Raman spectroscopy was made in the gaseous phase present in the fluid inclusions, to determine the bands that make up Fermi doublet in minerals of the different hydrothermal stages, with the intention of calculating an approach to the pressure to which the rock was subjected in those events. Finally, starting from a fractal analysis of the gaps present in the area, the mechanisms of gaping developed in both smeraldiferal belts along the hydrothermal history. Although both belts presented similar hydrothermal events, the eastern belt presented in general to the lower pressures for the hydrothermal events compared to the western ones. In addition, in this belt, the mechanisms of gaping show a corrosive event of greater intensity and with a less mechanical energy taken in the western region. Throughout hydrothermal history were determined. Although both Belts experienced similar hydrothermal events, the easter generally exhibited lower risks for hydrothermal events compared to the western Belt. Moreover, in the easter Belt, brewion mechanisms indicate a more intense corrosive event with lower mechanical energy than in the western Belt. Lastly, the presence of fluidization processes in the easter Belt (as well as in the western Belt) suggest the potential of class migration predominantly assisted by fluids, rather than migration driven by halokinesis
