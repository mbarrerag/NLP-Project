# Evaluation of the presence of anthropomorphic traits in logos of brands on impact on consumer confidence

This document focuses on the evaluation of the impact that anthropomorphic characteristics in brand logos have on consumer confidence. Based on a comprehensive review of literature and a solid theoretical framework, an experimental model is developed that postulates that the presence of anthropomorphic elements in logos not only influences the confidence in the brand, but also through the visual attention that these logos manage to capture. The results show that logos with anthropomorphic characteristics capture more effectively the consumer's attention, which is essential for the beginning of the cognitive process that culminates in the formation of perceptions of trust. Thus, the research concludes that the incorporation of anthropomorphic characteristics in the design of logos not only increases the visibility and recognition of the brand, but also strengthens the relationship of trust between the consumer and the brand. This finding has important practical implications for the design of branding strategies, suggesting that the anthropomorphic elements should be considered as a strategic tool in the construction of consumer confidence.
