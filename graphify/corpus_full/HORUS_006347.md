# Relationships between thermal tolerance and foliar traits: comparing ecological groups in high Andean forests

The world currently faces an accelerated increase in global temperatures due to climate change, which leads to an expected increase in the global average temperature and a higher frequency of heat waves. The thermal stress, when exceeding the tolerance thresholds of each species, has negative consequences on the growth, survival and reproduction of plants. Therefore, understanding the mechanisms underlying the thermal tolerance through the foliar traits is of vital importance. In this study, we examine the variation of the thermal tolerance between seventeen wood species of a high Andean forest and its relationship with anatomical, morphological and stomatic foliar traits. We find that the thickest foliar tissues contributed to the increase of T50 and Tcrit, providing an effective isolation against the environmental heat. We also observe that the foliar stomatic density increased T50, improving the overall thermoregulation of plants.
