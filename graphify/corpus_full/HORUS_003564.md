# Development of instrumental systems for the nutritional diagnosis of plants and soils in the field

The increasing global demand for food requires improving agricultural productivity and efficient use of fertilizers, which, when applied in excess, impact negatively the environment. To stimulate the local development of systems that support the rational use of fertilizers, this research presents two preliminary instrumental developments of open source for the nutritional diagnosis of crops. The first is a prototype of 3D printing coupled to the environmental light sensor of a smartphone, which was evaluated to estimate the chlorophyll content in oil palm leaves (Elais guineensis Jacq), potato (Solanum tuberosum; L.), coffee (Coffea arabica L.), cocoa (Theobroma caca L.) and grass kikuyo (Pennisetum clandestinum Hocst. ex Chiov.). This device presented a performance comparable to that of chlorophyll measurements with a higher concentration of cocoa samples, but with a higher degree of effectiveness (Theroma theatheatrician) and a higher degree of efficiency, used as a standard of comparison. The first is a 3D printing prototype jointed to a smartphone's ambient light sensor, which was evaluated to estimate the chlorophyll content in oil palm leaves, potato (Solanum tuberosum; L.), coffee (Coffea arabica L.), coconut (Theobroma cacao L.), and kikuyu grass (Pennisetum clanstinum Hocst. ex Chiov.).
