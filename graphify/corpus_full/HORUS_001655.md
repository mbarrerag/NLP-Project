# Single particle models

A very important criterion in the design of thermochemical reactors is to ensure that all the particles stay in the reactor long enough to achieve targeted conversion. The time required for the particle to achieve complete conversion depends on the rate of reaction as well as on mass and transfer phenomena. The effective reaction rate is controlled by the temperature and, in the case of gasification, the concentration of oxidizing agents on the particle’s surface. This chapter addresses the effect of particles on the effective reaction rate. Experimental and modeling strategies to estimate effective biomass particle reaction time are discussed. The simplified biomass particle models presented consider the biomass particles as a pseudo homogeneous “porous solid.” The models described use effective diffusivity and conductivity as parameters describing particle resistance to heat and mass transfer. Methods to calculate effective diffusivity and conductivity are discussed. The model of Bamford is presented as the simplest model to estimate pyrolysis time. A general methodology for developing single-particle models is discussed and applied to developing explicit formulations for biomass pyrolysis and char gasification. Although the total conversion time may be controlled by the behavior of the particle in some reaction systems, additional heat and mass transfer reactor resistance may control the effective conversion rate. This chapter focuses exclusively on the analysis of cases in which the total conversion time is controlled by the behavior of the particle
