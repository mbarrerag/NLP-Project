# Medellín. Continuities and breaks in the practices of living in the streets of the popular neighborhoods: between urban conflict and neighborhood improvement

The practices of living are fundamental expressions that human beings develop in their daily life, as a result of a specific habitat in which societies develop. In this context, geography, culture, history and various elements interact to give rise to a particular way of living. These practices are in constant transformation: some adapt to the changes experienced by the societies that generate them, while others arise or disappear according to the context. Show these variations is the main objective of this research. To this, a case study is developed in the self-generated neighborhoods of the northeastern areas of Medellín, where thousands of inhabitants decided to appropriate part of the urban soil in the second half of the twentieth century. In this process, the role of the street as a structural element of daily life in this type of neighborhoods, conceived as a space in constant construction and re-inflictment by its inhabitants. In addition, an analysis is carried out on the impact of two simultaneous processes inhabiting in these neighborhoods: the first is the strong increase of the violence that affected Medellín in the latter part of the twentieth century, with special intensity in the area of the north-east.
