# Construction of a finite element model for the study of thermomechanical behaviour of a joint during the Direct Control Rotative Friction welding process (RFWDD).

This paper describes and evaluates a numerical model developed for the simulation of thermomechanical behavior present during the direct control rotary friction welding process (RFWDD). At the thermal level, the model considers the phenomena of heat generation by friction and heat transfer over the bars involved in the process as transient over time. Additionally, a mechanical model was coupled that allows to study the plastic behavior of the joint by updating the flow stress of the material in time according to the evolution of the temperature profile. In addition, the mechanical model enriches the heat generation model and allows to consider the heat generated by deformation. To validate the model constructed three different process conditions were simulated and compared with experimental results reported in the literature. Validation processes correspond to a condition of RFWDD simile of low deformation, RFWDD simile of high deformation and RFWDD dissimile between copper and iron
