# BLOOD PARASITES FROM NON-AVIAN TETRAPODA CIRCULATING THROUGH A NEOTROPICAL COUNTRY

Blood parasites of wildlife have been studied in Colombia since the middle of the 20th century; however, those studies had focused mainly on birds. This megadiverse country has 857 species of herpetofauna and 543 species of mammals that harbor different parasite groups. Apicomplexa are the most commonly found parasites infecting herpetofauna with a prevalence of 26.3 %. In Amphibia there are frequent infections by Lankesterella 9.9%, Hepatozoon 2.8%, and Dactylosoma 2.3%, mainly in hosts belonging to Bufonidae and Leptodactylidae. Within reptiles, in turtles and tortoises, Haemogregarina has a prevalence of 31.2%, but infection with Hepatozoon 0.35% has been also seen; this last parasite accounts for 31.3% of infections in snakes and 10% in Crocodilia. Unlike other reptile taxa, lizards showed a high number of infections with Plasmodium of 20.3%. On the other hand, relatively incipient samplings of small mammals had shown Babesia 6.5% and Polychromophilus 1.6%. Non-Apicomplexa hemoparasites are also widely distributed. In the case of Trypanosoma and microfilaria, they are particularly frequent in amphibians (29.1%, 3.3%), but are also found in all the vertebrate taxa mentioned here. There is currently a need to move forward in the standardization of molecular markers not only for their use as barcodes, but also to have a stronger phylogenetic signal when exploring the relationships of those parasites. Study on vectors in the Neotropics is a line of research waiting to be explored, as well as the details of the ecological parameters that are directing this type of infections
