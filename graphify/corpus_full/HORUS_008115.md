# EVIDENCE OF SEXUAL PHEROMONE PRODUCTION BY FEMALES OF PRODIPLOSIS LONGIFILA (DIPTERA: CECIDOMYIIDAE) A KEY PEST OF TOMATO IN SOUTH AMERICA

The bud midge Prodiplosis longifila Gagné (Diptera: Cecidomyiidae) is a key pest of tomato crops. Larvae feed by sucking the juices from the epidermal tissues of leaf buds, and small fruits. We investigated if females release a sexual pheromone to attract males. Virgin females were individualized in plastic cups and observed for ovipositor extrusion every hour from 5:00 to 20:30. Groups of virgin females were introduced in plastic containers covered with a mesh lid (treatment); control containers did not contain females. All cups were covered with Vaseline®. A pair of control and treatment containers were set under the upper side of a mesh cage (2400 cm3) where virgin males were released. Groups of 10 or 20 females were set in the containers and 10 males were released in the cage. Twelve hours later the number of males sticked to the containers was registered. At field experiments on tomato crops groups of 19-32 virgin females were introduced in a plastic cup covered with a mesh lid and set in sticky Jackson traps (treatment), the control trap did not contain females. Jackson traps were set between 18:00-19:00 h and 12, 24 and 36 h later the number of attracted males was determined. Last abdominal segments of virgin females were cut during oviposition extrusion, frozen at -20 °C set in n-hexane and analyzed by GC-MS. Results showed females call males more frequently during scotophase. Males were significantly more attracted to containers with females than to empty ones at both lab and field conditions. Chemical profiles reveal presence of compounds with mass spectra like those reported for pheromones from other cedidomid species. Our results strongly indicate P. longifilia females release a sexual pheromone and reveal promissory strategies to monitor and control this pest
