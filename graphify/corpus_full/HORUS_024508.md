# PIODERMA GANGRENOUSO

The pioderma gangrenous (PG) is an uncommon and chronic neutrophilic dermatosis characterized by the appearance of one or more papules and/or painful nodules of rapid evolution forming an ulcer and usually affecting the lower extremities.1,2 In 1916 it was first described by Brocq. In 1930 Brusting et al. coined the term ‘pioderma gangrenous’ to ulcers of purulent appearance evidenced in 5 patients with history of ulcerative colitis. Initially it was considered a bacterial etiology by Streptococcus pyogenes and Staphylococcus aureus3; it was subsequently observed that there was a neutrophilic infiltrate but the lesions were aseptic and its nature was inflammatory with secondary overinfection.4. The PG is mainly associated with the inflammatory disease and autoinflammatory syndromes, its evolution is torpid and recalcitrant, with mortality of up to 30% without treatment
