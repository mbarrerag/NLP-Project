# From anima mundi: exegesis of Platonic cosmology and its influence on stoicism

According to tradition, Zenón de Citio would have studied in the former Academy before founding his own school. Based on the above, some authors, already from ancient times, have pointed out an important platonic influence on the doctrinal foundations of the stoic cosmology. However, understanding the Stoa as heir of the academic dogmas turns out, prima facie, unfounded, as is known the stoic rejected completely the theory of the Platonic Ideas and with it all reality separated from the cosmos. However, by examining closely the ontological systems developed in the former Academy, it can be confirmed that the Platonism was in effect determinant for the genesis of the stoa doctrine on the first principles. This influence is specifically found in the field of physics: the Time together with the later exegesis of this will prove key to understand the heritage of the Platonism in the stoicism. The World Soul, which makes its first appearance in the Time, which will be understood by the academics as an ‘intermediary’ substance, represents the link which most clearly evidence the philosophical legacy that Zend itself, the world of the time. is of the Stoic doctrine of first principles.
