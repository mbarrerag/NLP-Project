# HYDROGEN IN COLOMBIA: EVALUATING THE POTENTIAL OF AGROINDUSTRIA FOR SUSTAINABLE ENERGY TRANSITION

The idea of using hydrogen (H2) as an energy carrier goes back to the 70; however, factors such as the cost of raw materials, their environmental footprint and technological constraints have slowed down their introduction. Currently, more than 96% of H2 comes from non-renewable sources such as natural gas (49%), liquid hydrocarbons (29% and coal (18%).Although the use or take- advantage of H2 does not generate GHG, during its synthesis they can be emitted between 10 and 20 kgCO2-eqv/kg H2. The synthesis of H2 by electrolytic means, using renewable sources of electricity, leads to emisio-nes near 1 kgCO2-eqv/kg H2. Its environmental performance and the low predicted costs for electricity and electrolysis systems, will make it the dominant alternative for 2050; however, it should be noted that its technical profitability and environmental sustainability is linked to the degree of renewable electricity used, and to the degree of the economy, and to the degree of the economy, and to be considered as being the most viable. Similarly, the use of complex substrates such as cane vinasse (2-3 kmol H2/kg sugar) and urban solid waste shows the potential of various agro-industrial by-products for H2 synthesis with near or lower carbon footprints (-10 to 7 kgCO2-eqv/kg H2) than those reported by conventional processes without compromising food production or its cost. Finally, it should be noted that in our country the penetration of biogas production pro-cesses (CH4-CO2) has been evidenced, in addition to producing H2 and CH4, would allow for potentially adaptable fuel mixtures to the national market such as Hitane (H2: 10%, CH4: 50% and CO2: 40%) leading to cleaner and more efficient combustion at lower temperatures
