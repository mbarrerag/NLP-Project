# CIRCULATION, GOODS, AND CONSUMPTION OF CHINESE PRODUCTS IN THE NEW GRANADA IN THE 18TH CENTURY

It is widely known the significant role of global history in the most recent transformation of historiographical writing history. For these purposes, its most relevant well-known academics (Osterhammel, Gunder Frank, and Pomeranz), have introduced a renewed research field, that is keeping in suspense the conservative discourse logic focused on the dominant importance of northern Europe in the world economy until the 19th century. Without going any further, the historiographical trend of global studies has revealed the meaningful performance of Asiatic economies in the interchange, circulation, and consumption of goods until the first years of the 19th century. Thus, the Pacific and Indic oceans, before the Atlantic, appear as the sceneries of enormous commercial circuits, erased by the European historiographical speech. As it has been proved, colonial viceroyalties in America kept a dynamic consumption of Chinese goods, that throughout Manila, passing by Acapulco and finally arriving into the Callao for the next continental journey. From there, those goods developed an independent path, that lead them across the Pacific to Popayan (in the south-western side of New Granada); also employing the Atlantic, through Portobello, arriving in alternative ports in the Caribbean coast of New Granada, within a commercial logic covered many times by the colonial administration authorities, sometimes inside an underground commercial network. Certainly, historical colonial data such as inventories, testaments, and customs documents, has registered the consumption of Chinese goods inside the New Granada, although without the accuracy required. In any case, the existence of those products written down in the legal documents shows a dynamic market of Asiatic products, that offered an interesting historical landscape of paths, consumption, and representation, that shows a different socio-economic history in which Chinese goods took relevant importance. Even more, it is already proved that the consumption of Chinese goods in New Granada complemented the scarce production of European goods destined to the most popular sectors
