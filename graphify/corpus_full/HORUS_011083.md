# Mathematical model for capacity management installed in a water analysis laboratory.

Managing capacity is a process that is part of an organization’s strategic planning to define its long, medium or short-term goals. Proper use of available capacity allows companies to increase their efficiency and productivity. For service companies, it is not an easy task to estimate their capacity due to the variety of resources they must contemplate and manage, the restrictions to satisfy and the uncertainty in the demand for services. Therefore, this work focuses on a water quality laboratory, which currently does not quantify its available capacity, so it is not aware whether an adequate allocation of resources and the number of tests that could be provided in a planning horizon is being made. For the development of the model, a capacity characterization was carried out, the model is executed in scenarios and the results are contrasted by occupation indicators. The results of the proposed scenarios reveal that the laboratory has a high percentage of idle capacity, for which, through a DOFA analysis, strategies are proposed aimed at strengthening aspects that result in the laboratory having a greater demand for services and hence the use of its capacity increases
