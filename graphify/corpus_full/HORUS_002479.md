# PALAEOHYDROLOGY IN THE MID-MIOCENE PALEOSOLS OF LA VENTA, LA TATACOA-COLOMBIA: A MICROMORPHOLOGICAL APPROACH.

In La Tatacoa region, Colombia, micromorphological features of red-beds paleosols (~ 13.1-12.8 Ma) in the Baraya Member of the Villavieja Formation, suggest important changes in soil moisture dynamics associated with variations in the seasonality of the rain and the geomorphological position of the soil profiles. The fossil soils hosted in the overbanks of meandering rivers, encompass valuable information about the environmental conditions that prevailed in the Middle Miocene during the peak of the La Venta Fauna. This study aims to determine the humidity regime and the driving soil forming factors on the water balance of the two red-beds paleosol profiles of the Baraya Member. Pedogenetic properties such as stagnic matrix, bluish-gray to greenish-gray coloration in the soil, disortic nodules of Fe and Mn, mottling, secondary carbonates, coatings of Fe (hematite) and manganese oxides allowed us to identify redox processes and the alternation of wet and dry cycles. These typical soils of the overbank deposits, distal to the fluvial channels, constitute cumulative soils that were formed under a regional udic to ustic moisture regime. Features such as clays, cutans, slickensides, and vertic features allowed us to classify the paleosols as Alfisols in the Ferruginous red-bed, and Vertisols in the La Venta red-bed. The pedofeatures suggest that the main drivers of the redoximorphic processes were the surface water stagnic properties due to its geomorphological position, and the seasonality of the precipitation. The well-expressed pedofeatures are indicative of changes from a wetter sub-humid conditions in the Alfisols to a drier sub-humid conditions in the Vertisols, which interpretation is consistent with the fossil flora and fauna described at Monkey Beds of La Venta, Tatacoa-Colombia
