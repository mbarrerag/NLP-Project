# THREE DECADE OF DISFUSION OF FECUNITY WITHIN ONES FREE IN COLOMBIA: THE PROBLEMSABLE END OF THE SOCIOECONOMIC ENTERPRISE IN THE FAMILY TRAINING PROCESS

This article examines the patterns and levels of fertility according to the type of marital union and socioeconomic class between Colombian women. It focuses especially on narrowing some of the gaps that previously distinguished fertility behavior in the different socio-economic classes. Using Demographic and Health Survey (DHS) data for 1995, 2005 and 2015, we analyze the changes in the context in which reproduction occurs that reflect changes in values that guide family formation, supported by legal frameworks. There was a widespread increase in free unions in all social groups over the decades. It also shows that free unions became more involved in the Global Fertility Rate (GFR) than in marriages, being responsible for 57.5 % of Colombia’s overall fertility, that is, 39.4 percentage points higher than the participation of married women in the TGF in the most recent year. Apparently, there are even more children born out of unions of any kind than within marriage
