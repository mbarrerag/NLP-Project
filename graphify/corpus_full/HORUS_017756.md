# Semi-supervised deep learning for ocular image classification

The loss of vision and blindness as a complication of diabetes could be prevented with early diagnosis, frequent detection tests, and appropriate timely treatment. Unfortunately, access to an expert ophthalmologist is limited and is not readily available. This is why automated detection systems could improve access to specialized care by reducing time, cost, and effort for detection. Deep learning methods became popular for the detection of eye diseases in eye-back images due to their good results. However, deep learning methods need a large amount of tagged images to learn, with manual labelling of medical images being an expensive and slow process requiring few medical experts in the retina. As a result, the number of annotated images available is limited. With this thesis work a semi-supervised method is proposed that takes advantage of the unlabeled images in addition to the images tagged to train a model that detects diabetic retinopathy through self-supervised learning followed by a fine-supervised adjustment and destilled image. This method was evaluated in the dataset of Mesictor 0
