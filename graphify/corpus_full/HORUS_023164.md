# Global salt iodization policy: challenges

Objective To identify the challenges that the implementation of the global salt iodization policy entails for human consumption. Methods A review of the literature published between 2000 and 2021 in PubMed was carried out, with the terms MeSH iodine, public policy, sodium chloride and the complementary terms iodine/deficiency, excess. 141 articles were obtained and 50 were revised applying as a criterion of inclusion the approach to the implementation of the iodine policy. Results Seven challenges were identified: sustainability of the policy, eliminating the gap between the exploitation and the marketing of salt, preventing the indiscriminate fortification of food, promoting education on the intake of iodized salt, controlling the excessive intake of iodine, balancing optimal iodine intake with the reduction of sodium chloride consumption and producing sufficient official information about the policy. Conclusion Universal salt iodization is a policy that requires greater sensitivity to the local situations of each country to effectively mitigate the public health problem of deficiency disorders and excess in iodine intake around the world
