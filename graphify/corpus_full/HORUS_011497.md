# THE REPRESENTATION OF NEGRAD ETHNICITY IN THE CASANARE, THE ORIENTAL PLANES, COLOMBIA

Before the 1970s, in the department of Casanare located in the macroregion of the Eastern Plains of Colombia, there was no broad presence of population identified as black. As in the rest of the region, most people have self-characterized as white-mestizo and plain, a term that implies the status of cowry workers. The advent of activities such as oil exploitation and agro-industry around the African palm or oil palm, resulted in a pole of attraction for people of black ethnic origin from the Pacific and Caribbean coasts of Colombia. Today, there are several generations of Afro-Colombian women born and raised in the region. This article presents the partial results of an investigation that arises from the invitations of the Afro-descendant community of the municipalities of Villanueva and Monterrey of the aforementioned department to accompany it in some cultural and gastronomic encounters of the Afro-Colombianity.
