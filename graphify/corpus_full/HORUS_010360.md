# Water deficit stress mitigation in fodder maize by using PGPB

The global scarcity of available water for irrigation represents one of the main threats to agriculture and food security. It is expected that the situation will be aggravated by periods of drought more recurrent due to climate change. Strengthen the tolerance of crops to poor water conditions and improve their efficiency in the use of the water resource. It is expected that the situation becomes more harmful to the current challenges. The PGPB (Plant Growth-Promoting Bacteria) are presented as a viable alternative to reduce the deleterious effects of drought. This study was aimed at evaluating the potential of ten strains of PGPB of the genera Pseudomonas spp., Bacillus spp. and Lysinibacillus sp. belonging to the collections of the Institute of Biotechnology of the National University (IBUN) and the Colombian Corporation of Agricultural Research (AGROSAVIA) in the mitigation of the water deficit in maize plants. For this, tests were carried out in greenhouse (Bogota) and mesh house (Cesar) using individual inoculums. The plants were grown in irrigation conditions for 20 days and then were evaluated for the hydro deficit (in the 11 days). Tolerating osmotic stress induced by the presence of Polyethylene glycol (PEG) 6000 in the growth medium and possessed multiple promotion traits, such as the ability to synthesize indol and exopolysaccharide compounds (EPS), solubilize and mineralize phosphorus, and establish in roots even under stress conditions.These attributes could be related to the benefits observed in plants, by increasing the bioavailability of nutrients, maintaining moisture in the rizosphere and stimulating hormone growth. It was concluded that the strains of Pseudomonas PSL80 and PSL63, and Bacillus XT14, have potential to mitigate stress by water deficiency in maize plants. .
