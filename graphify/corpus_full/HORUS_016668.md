# Spatial distribution of organic carbon stock of soil in landscapes occupied by sugar cane (Saccharum officinarum) for panela

The soil, second global carbon reservoir (C), is important in the regulation of the biogeochemical cycle of this element. However, the balance of this cycle is disturbed by anthropogenic activities that decrease the content of C in the soil, increasing its concentrations in the atmosphere. The strategies of soil conservation adopted from the agricultural sector arise as a proposal to mitigate these changes, since they allow it to recover its powers as regulator and deposit of C. The sugar cane cultivation (Saccharum officinalum) for panel production has a high potential in carbon capture and its stability in the soil. Therefore, the objective of research was to evaluate the spatial distribution of the stock of Organic Carbon of the Soil (COS) in landscapes occupied by sugar cane crops for panela. For this, the agricultural landscapes of paneling cane were characterized as the model, the analysis of the COS and its distribution in the municipalities of Quebradegra and Nocaima by means of geospatial information. The Latin hypercube method was used to define a sample of 13 farms with variable geoforms and representative managements where the COS was determined by the information and its distribution in a depth of 50 cm. Soil conservation strategies adopted in the agricultural sector emerges as a proposal to mitigate these changes, since they allow soil to recover its potential factor and deposit of C.
