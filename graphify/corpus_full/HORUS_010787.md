# Creation of a protocol for the construction of survival curves on the SiHa cell line using a clinical linear accelerator.

In the late 19th century, shortly after the invention of X-rays, it was discovered that ionizing radiation could be used as an oncological treatment. During the next century, a series of experiments were carried out in vivo and in vitro, which sought to explain the properties of radiation-cell interaction, creating the field of radiobiology. From these researches, the linear-quadramatic model was developed, which serves to quantify the lethal and sub-lethal damage of radiation in the cell, comparing the dose received with the cell survival fraction. This is known as a cell survival curve and is very useful for comparing different characteristics of a treatment in radiotherapy (such as fractionation, quality of radiation, among others). In this final master's work, it is sought to create a protocol for the construction of cell survival curves using 6-m V radiation beams with a linear accelerator to facilitate the conduct of radiobologic experiments in the future
