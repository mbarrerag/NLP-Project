# GENETIC VECTOR EDITION Measured by REMOT CONTROL

The editing of genes mediated by Cas9 is a powerful tool to address research questions in arthropod disease vectors. Traditional approaches are based on the administration of the ribonucleoprotein complex (RNP) Cas9/RNA-guide to arthropod eggs by microinjection. However, microinjection is difficult, limited to a small number of species and is inefficient even in optimized taxons. In this work we present the development of the technology called Receptor-Mediated Ovary Transduction of Cargo (ReMOT Control) or is it possible to transduce load to the receptor-mediated ovaries (Control ReMOT) to specifically administer Cas9 RNP to the germinable line of the insect by injection into adult females. For this purpose, the P2C peptide was identified which mediated the P2C peptide and produced the P9 RNP protein transduction from the P9 PDPN hemolympha to the developing germline, resulting in the generation of gene-inheritable mutations in the offspring. First, the P2C peptide was identified by means of the P2C. This protein is taken directly from hemolymph by non-specified endocytosis. Since endocytosis uptake in the germ line is conserved in oviparous animals, this technology and other derived from it have been implemented in different species of arthropod disease vectors including mosquitoes, ticks or triatomins. Once optimized for multiple species, ReMOT Control could drastically change the research landscape in molecular entomology, allowing for easy and flexible genetic manipulation of a wide variety of arthropod vectors and non-model species
