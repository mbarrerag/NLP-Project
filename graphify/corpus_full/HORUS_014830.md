# LAND GRABBING OR LAND ACCOMPARATION: THE CASE OF CHINA IN LATIN AMERICA

According to the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO), land grabbing is currently one of the world's leading causes of hunger, and is also presented without consideration of the social and environmental impacts it involves. Food security is a major concern for China and not a few States of the twenty-first century, which may be leading to a process of agricultural relocation. Regions of the developing world such as Latin America are a breeding ground for the proliferation of land grabbing. Part one of this article provides a characterization of the phenomenon, and then explains the origin and process of evolution of land grabbing in the world, contextualizing the Chinese case - the reasons and dimensions of the process - to finally address land grabbing in Latin America. As a main conclusion, it is stated that in the absence of comprehensive agricultural policies designed for the long term in the region, the phenomenon will continue its evolution associated with all kinds of practices that have as common denominator a total neglect of sustainable land use, which places the land on the land grubbing as one of the direct threats of climate change
