# TENDING TOWARDS AURICULARY AND CORNUAL LONGITUDS SEgestED IN THE COLOMBIAN BOWIN

The study of the bilateral asymmetries has an evident value at the time of analyzing adaptive characters. One of these types of asymmetry is the directional, in which, systematically, at the population level, a character presents higher values on one side than on the other. The directional asymmetry is usually explained by adaptive reasons. At the level of domestic races, this type of studies has not been very common and, generally, the existing studies have referred to behavioral lateralities in chewing and in the locomotive function. The criollo Sanmartinero cattle is derived from the foothills of the Meta department, in the Colombian Orinoquia; it was formed by the natural selection from the cattle populations introduced by the Spaniards in the fifteenth century. The aim of this work was to study the possible asymmetries in bilateral characters, as are the horn and the ear, in the Sanmartinero bovine. For this, a total of 28 animals (16 males and 12 females) of a range of age of 2 months to 9 years, of which was obtained a total of 4 linear differences between two structures, and long ones were studied. The authors would recommend studies of this type in future morphometric investigations in domestic animals, especially in Sanmartinero cattle
