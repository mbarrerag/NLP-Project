# MACHINE RESPECT TO THE

The process of identifying minerals in thin sections and extracting statistical parameters from them (e.g., points counting, degree of packaging, grain orientation, porosity) is a lengthy work and requires qualified personnel to be carried out; recent advances in deep learning and already cemented algorithms of image processing and artificial vision (computer vision), are and will be more supportive for this work in petrography. This poster seeks to show some of the common techniques used in analysis of images applied to thin sections (e.g. binarization, border segmentation, edge detection, detection of guidelines and ellipses) in addition to showing others in the state of art, which make predominant use of clustering algorithms and artificial neural networks (e.g. learning transfer, border detection via neural networks, semantic segmentation, algorithms of classification, object detection and iterative clustering). This seeks to show the importance of these new techniques as additional techniques to the repertoire of geological analysis and open geographics; to encourage their open-sourced mode of analysis and geotechnical improvement
