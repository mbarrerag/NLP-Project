# CLASS MODEL OF HELIUM-LIKE ION ATOMS Analysis of a regular behavior in the ground state energy of the helium-like ion atom series

When the Bohr model was previously applied to predict the ground-state energy of two-electron atomic systems, negative excess values of the real ones were found, particularly in the lighter species: negative hydrogen ion and helium. Inspired by the popular game of thunderbolts and based on a regular behavior revealed by experimental data, a new extended interpretation of Bohr's model is proposed. According to it, the electrons rotate at the same frequency while maintaining a rigid relative angular position to each other, at the moment when an electronic resonance is expected to occur between symmetrical positions in the atomic structure. As a result, a Z-independent angular separation of 106° is obtained for helium-like ions, with an energy discrepancy of less than 0.04% in the Z interval ≤ 12. The simplicity of the emerging atomic image encourages to use the model as a teaching aid in Moderna physics, chemistry-physics and other university courses of science and engineering
