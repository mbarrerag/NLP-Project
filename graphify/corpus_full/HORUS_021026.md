# Mediation around the plural senses of peace in dialogue with indigenous communities and survivors of armed conflict

This proposal starts from the hypothesis that peace, as other socio-political fundamental concepts of our political culture, are the scene of a social dispute in which diverse and antagonistic visions are faced. Peace, therefore, cannot be thought of as a single concept, a state of harmony to which comes after the war. We propose instead to think of peace as processes of historically and culturally located mediation, which require transformation practices and which ensure the recognition and inclusion of the parts (Dietrich Museum, 2012; centre of thought pluralizing peace, 2022). Explore diverse concepts of peace, beyond the hegemonic ones, discovers new ways to build coexistence and democratic inclusion. Mediation proposes a multi-disciplinary work that combines conceptual history, intercultural research and the critical and collaborative museum work (Simon 2010). Since 2019 we have developed intercultural dialogues with five indigenous plants: Ginel Dokoe, Saber Murui, Iris Aguilar, teacher Wayuu, Jairo Palchucán, artist Camëntá, Pastora Tarapués, parterás and Mauricia de las. The first is to propose the construction of civic laboratories for the development of pedagogies of coexistence and recognition. It develops mediation activities with the public for two purposes: on the one hand, so that visitors can critically examine their own convictions of peace and, on the other hand, to take advantage of other perspectives relations in the construction of coexistence and inclusion. Mediation is supported by the exhibition Sanaduria but is projected further, through modules that can be implemented in different civic and pedagogical scenarios. It also builds on the experience of the Inter-Sedes Chair pluralized feelings of peace, offered in 2021-I. Through an intercultural dialogue on practices and epistemologies associated with the use of various plants, it answers the following questions: how to communicate, effectively, the historical and culturally specific forms of conflict-resolution construction among the indigenous peoples Wayuu, nasa, grass, camëntsá and muui society and the communities represented by ASOVISNA will give the most critical contribution of the concept of peace (what are the most important epitemology)
