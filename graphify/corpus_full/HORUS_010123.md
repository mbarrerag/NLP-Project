# PLANT ECO-EVOLUTIONARY MODELLING FOR ADAPTATION TO EXTREME ENVIRONMENTS

Plant susceptibility to extreme abiotic stresses such as drought, heat and flooding is increasing due to climate change. Consequently, new analytical strategies are urgently required for mining sources of adaptation, and pyramid them into plant populations. Here we offer modelling analytical tools capable to predict plant adaptive responses to extreme environments. First, we describe how climate data meets ecophysiology and evolutionary modelling in order to forecast current and historical in situ stresses. Second, we encourage coupling these climate-based eco- physiological inferences with genomics via genome-environment associations, as proxy to model standing natural polygenic adaptation already contained within current plant populations. Third, we discuss genomic-enabled predictive modelling alternatives to optimise the introgression and assisted gene flow (AGF) of polygenic adaptive variation into future populations. Finally, we prospect alternative models that could boost de novo adaptive variation, such as in silico genomic selection, speed breeding, and genomic-coupled assisted migration. Throughout this work, the scientific community will be able to identify the need for more robust high-resolution ecological data, combined with (i) explicit eco-physiological indices targeting concrete abiotic stresses, and (ii) empirical summary statistics of the genomic diversity, divergence and selection signatures within plant gene-pools. Only then, ecophysiological-based models in an evolutionary context would meet genomic-enabled predictions of the adaptive potential, securing plant biodiversity in the face of extreme climate
