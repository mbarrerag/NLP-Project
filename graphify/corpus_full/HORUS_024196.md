# Expression of indirect aggressiveness mediated by the development of language in children and adolescents with Complex Trauma: Comparison with a control group

Children with complex trauma have experienced chronic abuse by their primary care figures, showing negative impacts on various aspects of development, including a lower development of language skills, and an increase in the level of aggressiveness exhibited in interaction. The aim of this research was to measure the effects of complex trauma on language production capacity and indirect aggressiveness in 84 participants between the ages of 5 to 14 years, through an instrument called game of secret messages. It was hoped that children with complex trauma would show higher levels of indirect aggressiveness, as well as a lower linguistic development than their peers without complex trauma. Meanwhile, in the variables of linguistic production, no differences were found in productivity, density or diversity of the lexicon. Through a regression analysis, the variables showed differences in levels of indirect aggressiveness between the two groups, showing higher levels of ingressivity were found in the target group. game. Children with complex trauma were specified to show higher levels of indirect aggression as well as how to improve their peer without complexity trauma. A quantitative-experimental design was carried out out with a target group with complex trauma (n = 45) and a control group (n = 39) in the city of Bogotá. Statistically significant differences were found in the levels of indirect aggression between the target group and the control group, showing higher levels in the complex trauma group.
