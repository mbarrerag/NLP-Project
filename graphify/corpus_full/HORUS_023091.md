# COST-UTILITY OF TIOTROPIUM FOR CHILDREN WITH SEVERE ASTHMA IN PATIENTS AGED 1¿5 YEARS

Severe asthma is a disease that affects more than 300 million people worldwide.1 At least 24% of the patients with asthma are classified as severe asthma requiring high doses of inhaled corticosteroids-long- acting beta2-agonist (ICS-LABA) or oral corticosteroids (OCS).2 New pharmacological alternatives have been developed for patients with severe asthma, among them the addition of long-acting muscarinic antagonists (LAMA), as tiotropium, to the current treatment with ICS-LABA. 3 Tiotropium is delivered using a slow and soft-mist, non-breath- actuated, propellant-free inhaler (Respimat Soft Mist Inhaler). The inhaler device delivers the medication dose to the airways independent of the patient's variable inspiratory flow. Tiotropium showed the potential to reduce asthma exacerbation risk in children aged 1'5 years with persistent asthmatic symptoms without differences in the frequency of adverse events respect to current treatment in clinical trials and real-life studies.4,5 Concerns are generated, as to whether if the extra benefit offered by tiotropium outweighs the additional cost compared to therapy with only ICS-LABA in patients aged 1'5 years. This question is even more relevant in developing countries with an increasing prevalence of asthma and constrained health care
