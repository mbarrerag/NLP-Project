# MICROBIOLOLOGICAL DETECTION OF PHITOPATOGEN HONGS FROM SAMPLING OF CACCOA OWN FROM A GRANTEE PRODUCING THE BELL PEOPLE'S MUNICIPE,

Cocoa is a tropical fruit of great importance in the food industry and the Colombian economy. However, many crops are discarded because they do not meet the quality standards necessary for its subsequent marketing. One of the causes of low yield is the presence of phytopathogenic fungi, since they infect the cocoa plant causing symptoms such as wilting, inhibition of growth, chlorosis, necrosis and spots on the leaves, decreasing production. In recent years, the farmers of Pueblo Bello-Cesar have been renewing and expanding the cocoa crops, turning it into their main economic activity. The early and precise diagnosis of phytopathogenic fungi becomes indispensable to take early measures that avoid their propagation and greater economic losses. Considering the above, the aim of this preliminary study was to identify phytopathogenic fungi present in cocoa plantations in a cocoa farm of cocoa production Pto-Carás, using microbiological insulation techniques such as fungal isolation in the medium of PDA, morphological analysis of colonies and visualization of microscopic structures.
