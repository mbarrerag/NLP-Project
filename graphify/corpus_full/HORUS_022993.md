# BIOCARBONES OBTAINED FROM AGRO-INDUSTRIAL WASTE: AN ENVIRONMENTAL ALTERNATIVE FOR SWEET AMENDMENT

5 biocarbons obtained, by slow pyrolysis, from palm rachis (BRP), palm cousk (BCP), coffee pulp (BC), rose stems (BTR) and tree pruning residues (BP) were studied to determine their potential use as soil amendment. The materials were determined elementary analysis, apparent and real density, porosity, cation exchange capacity (CIC), pH and electrical conductivity (EC). Finally, the results were validated with an agronomic test in maters for the production of lettuce (Lactuca sativa). BP showed the highest carbon content (71.3 per cent). BRP and BC presented a high CIC (~43 chol (+) kg-1) suggesting great potential for amending low fertility soils. All biocarbons except BCP are strongly alkaline and suitable for the amendment of acid soils.
