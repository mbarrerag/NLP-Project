# Orange spread “Valencia” (Citrus x synensis Osbeck) by mini grafts

The demand for citrus plants (Citrus spp) for crops has increased worldwide due to the needs for new plantings, replacement of sick trees in established crops, and strict regulations for the propagation of plants. In order to evaluate faster and lower cost propagation alternatives for citrus, orange plants “Valencia” were propagated using two mini graft methods (Hindible and T-inverted). The patterns were obtained from mandarin “cleopatra” and the yolks were isolated from 1 year old grafted plants. For the cleft grafts, the buds were inserted at 5-7 cm height into the patterns or decapitated porttainjertos and were covered with Epppendorf® tubes. For grafts in T-inverted, the yolks were inserted at 5-7 cm height below the pattern’s bark. The unions were fixed with Parafilm. The grafts were kept in a mesh house (59% with two daily irrigations (2 min each). The grafts could be distributed with such a completely random design.
