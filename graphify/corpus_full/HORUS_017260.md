# Location of faults in rural electricity distribution systems in high atmospheric electrical activity areas.

Given the need to reduce the times for location of faults in energy distribution networks, this work addresses the study of an efficient technique for locating a three-phase or one-phase failure that occurs on a main feeder. This technique is based on the analysis of different components of the frequency spectrum extracted from the voltage signals measured at a single point of the feeder. When a failure occurs, traveling waves are produced that spread between the location of the feeder and the substation, and another part along the main feeder, branch of the network and back to the feeder failure. By identifying the frequency components incorporated in the travel waves, it is possible to determine the section and approximate distance of the failure. The method applied presents an ambiguity in the location of the faults for those occurring in the branch of the feeder and the result of the failure indirectly, with the result of the failure of the main feeder, with the aim of having a high frequency spectras produced by these faults
