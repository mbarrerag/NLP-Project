# Impact of a sequenced physical activity programme on executive functions in children aged 3 to 4.

Early childhood has been considered a critical period in brain development due to the maturation of structures and circuits destined for the acquisition of behaviors and skills as pillars that marked an important trajectory in later life. This development has been marked by opportunities that seek to optimize brain functions, where physical activity (AF) has shown a positive influence on executive functions (FE) specifically in: inhibitory control (CI), working memory (MT) and cognitive flexibility (FC) and playing the context an important role in the formation and learning processes.A longitudinal study was designed with 33 subjects between 3-4 years, which were randomly divided into three groups: a) intervention of AF program plus cognitive challenges, b) intervention of cognitive challenges, c) control, was performed prior sociodemographic characterization, the levels of AF were measured by means of acelerometry and the condition of the FE with psychological tests before and after intervention which had a duration of 12 weeks.The evaluated FEs significantly improved and there was a more marked trend for the experimental groups compared to the control, besides there are strong correlations with the variables of educational level of the FCEs and the functionals
