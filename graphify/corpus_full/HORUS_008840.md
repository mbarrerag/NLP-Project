# PHONOTIPIC PLASTICITY OF CTMAX AND EFFECT OF CULENTATION TEST AS CLIMATE CHANGE SCENE IN ADULTS OF DENDROOPSOPHYS MOLITOR (ANURA: HYLIDAE) IN COLOMBIA

Phenotypic plasticity is the modification in the expression of a trait depending on environmental conditions. An example is Dendropsophus grinder, an endemic frog in Colombia, which has phenotypic plasticity in relation to its body size and size of its eggs. It inhabits various altitudes, including paramo habitats, is closely related to bodies of water. For ectoterms, the maximum critical temperature (ctmax) is the average temperature at which the individual loses his locomotive capacity. This value is fundamental to model the niche and evaluate the vulnerability to climate change. We conducted a study to analyze if intraspecific plasticity exists in the ctmax of D. molator in a population of the Cundiboyacense altiplano (2654 msm). We selected 9 male frogs, which were exposed to some warming rates: 0.25, 0.5 and 1 °C per minute, at the same time the order of exposure was altered for each group of 3 frogs.
