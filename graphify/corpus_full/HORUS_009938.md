# THE PROBLEM OF THE KINGDOM

The problem of the 8 queens is to find all possible ways in which 8 queens can be placed on a chess board without being eaten among them. This problem was proposed by a German chess player named Max Bezzel in 1848, and caught the attention of many mathematicians among them Carl Friedrich Gauss, who worked on the solution of this problem as in their generalization to a board of n × n squares. The idea of the solution that we are going to present, uses tools of commutative algebra and computational algebra. We will assign a variable to each square of the board to then consider the polynomial ring in these variables. In this ring we will build a monomial ideal in such a way that the cognition of our ring by this ideal, has the property that the dimension as vector space of its homogeneous component of grade 8, is precisely the number of configurations of eight queens on the chess board that are not eaten between them
