# Identification of candidate genes, associated with the absence of intermuscular bones in white Cachama (Piaractus brachypomus) originating in the Amazon region and the Orinoquia

This research is proposed with the aim of identifying candidate genes potentially responsible for the presence or absence of intermuscular bones (HI) in characiform teleosteos fish belonging to the species Cachama blanca (Piaractus brachypomus).The presence of intermuscular bones in these fish makes it difficult to market and consume, causing potentially lethal complications if they are ingested.In addition, these HI hinder the processing of fillets, affecting the conservation, so that the commercial value of the fishery products is reduced.Some authors have described the total absence of HI in this species or the presentation of them in the right or left hemibody only.To address this problem, it is proposed to candidize genes associated with the presence or absence of intermuscular bones in Cachama blanca, so that once this characteristic of absence of HI, it is possible to identify those parents who segregate the responsible alleles or with additive value for this characteristic, and thus generate an added value to the quality of the fillet of that species, by implementing a genetic improvement program based on the present purpose of DNA markers assisted by those farms with plant with a plan with a predefined
