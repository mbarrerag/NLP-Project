# ANTIPACITIST HORIZON IN THE CARE SYSTEMS

This is chapter 1 of the book which seeks to integrate an anticapacitist horizon to rethink care regimes. It is for this reason that it proposes a series of ways in which the ideology of capacitism is rooted with respect to discalibrated populations, Spoonies populations and age-old populations as active ways of naming, making visible, conceptualizing and accounting for care, knowing that these demand the redefinition of anticapacitist positions in the proposed debate along its pages. In any case, before the question about who are the people involved in care and why these people are also forced to settle to the established care regimes, a multidimensional response arises with a common visible component, although very often not tangible: the body of certain people or persons with certain bodies, whose experiences make them dependent, vulnerable and fragile by reasons of age (infancy and third age), or for reasons of health and capacity (people in situations of chronic illness and persons with disabilities). They are bodies with socially and institutionally devalued functions, bodies thought of bodies based on a dichotomies and fragile by reasons of age (infanance and third age), or third age), or by reasons of the people, or by reasons of health and capacity
