# The Colombian government's impact on the elaboration of Plan Colombia (1998-2000): a study on asymmetrical relations.

This research is based on the premise that in the elaboration of Plan Colombia there were two versions: the first presented by President Andrés Pastrana to President Bill Clinton in 1998 (Plan Colombia I) whose priority was to achieve peace with FARC-EP and as a consequence of it, to redirect the anti-drug policy, that is, first peace, then drug trafficking; and the second, signed by President Clinton in 2000 (Plan Colombia II), prioritized the fight against drug trafficking to take away resources from the guerrilla and strengthen the Army to establish a correlation of forces more favorable for the State, that is to say, first drug trafficking, then peace. The analysis focuses on the role that the Colombian government played in the transit of Plan Colombia I to Plan Colombia II. Much has been published on Plan Colombia and the vision predominates that it was a unilateral imposition of U.S. to Colombia, following the generalized notion of the asymmetric relations of the big and powerful country, putting the lower and weaker country under control to favor its hegemonic interests.
