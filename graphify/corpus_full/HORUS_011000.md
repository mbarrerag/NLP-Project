# The Wayúu people's case: the approach to the right to water in Colombia from the standard of international soft law.

Water is present in the environment and in most living beings and its consumption is fundamental to the life of human beings, so access to water is essential for the development of all human rights. To guarantee this right, conventions have been made and treaties promulgated at the global level by various international human rights organizations. Since hard law has not addressed water as an autonomous right, but has been recognized as a primary element for dignified life and related to human health, however, soft law international has provided fundamental elements for the defence and enforceability of this right. The indigenous wayuu community has historically seen as the right to water (and many more rights) has been violated in its territory, as it is geographically isolated from large urban centres, causing difficulties in access and state abandonment; in this situation, the Constitutional Court of Colombia has issued multiple sentences in order to restore the rights of the community, however, the problems do not show a structural improvement
