# STUDY OF URBANISTRY AND HYGIENIST ARCHITECTURE AROUND THE DISEASE OF LEBRARY.

The illness of leprosy or Hansen's disease1 generated important changes in society, as well as in architecture and urban planning, evolving as a result of the progress of scientific studies on disease and allowing the development of forms of employment and types that integrated concepts of hygiene, ventilation, treatment and isolation; in those places referred to as lazzaretti2. The study of these hospitals in the world, in particular Agua de Dios, Colombia, represents an opportunity to understand how architecture and urban planning contribute to health and mitigation processes in the context of a health emergency associated with the idea of wealth of knowledge to assume a fundamental role for the valorization and conservation of this reality and to investigate better living conditions for communities and their territories. The integration of various disciplines, including medicine, architecture, urbanism, bioclimatics, and sociology, among others; in the light of the graphic documentation, history and evolution, have proved to be fundamental in generating proposals relevant for the occupation of the territory
