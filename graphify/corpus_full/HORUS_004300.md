# PARTICIPATION OF THE REAL OBJECT IN THE MODELATION PROCESS AND FINAL MODEL EFFECTS.

Mathematical modeling involves knowing the functioning of the real process that is being modeled to capture the characteristics that the final model must reproduce. However, the actual process can be very complex due to energy exchanges, physical connections between components, mixtures of substances and in general complex interactions between parts of the process. Therefore, when modeled, it is necessary to make sometimes arbitrary partitions in order to simplify the model and obtain balance equations with some interpretable sense. The purpose of this work is then to show how these partitions are performed and explain two examples, one simple and another more or less complex, in order to contribute to the discussion of this topic. In the first, the model of the DC Motor, in which the electrical part is separated from the mechanical part to obtain the energy balances. In addition, it has a magnetic separation of axes to avoid working with mutual inductances between the rotor and the stator. This property is used to model other more complicated electrical machines.
