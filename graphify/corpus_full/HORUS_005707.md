# Environmental Incidences of Vertical Gardens: Case Study Building Mayor of Medellín

One of the main challenges facing contemporary cities is to improve the thermal insulation of buildings to reduce energy demand. In this context, vertical gardens, as a solution based on nature, have emerged as a sustainable strategy within the construction industry. The incorporation of vertical gardens into the architectural design requires detailed planning that includes essential aspects, such as the selection of suitable plant species, the design of efficient irrigation systems and adequate maintenance. Likewise, it is essential to have policies and regulations that promote the adoption of these sustainable solutions in the construction industry. A relevant example of the benefits of vertical gardens is the study carried out in the eastern façade of the Distrital Administrative Centre (CAD) in Colombia, which has an area of 1 875 m2 of garden. The results demonstrated a clear impact on the reduction of extreme heat and the improvement of thermal insulation, which results in the emergence of thermal comfort and greater energy efficiency. In addition, the results of the research conclude that, despite the costs associated with the consumption of water and sewerage, the installation of a vertical garden is financially viable and provides significant net savings, which improves the building and its energy efficiency in the long term. in the construction industry.
