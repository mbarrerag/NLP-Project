# Community-Based Research and Higher Education for Peace: A Case Study in Colombia

Community-based research is an umbrella term that refers to different types of participatory research processes that promote community–university collaboration towards the creation of knowledge to foster inclusion and social justice in society. This chapter presents an unpublished case study of how a university in Colombia collaborated with communities to support the implementation of the peace agreement signed by the Colombian government and the Revolutionary Armed Forces—People’s Army. This case concerns the second stage of an institutional project aimed at introducing higher education as an alternative path to young people of affected communities in isolated territories, who are in danger of being recruited by illegal armed groups. The analysis of the case is informed by the Latin American intellectual tradition of critical pedagogy inspired by Brazilian pedagogue, Paulo Freire, as well as by the participatory action research approach proposed by Colombian sociologist, Orlando Fals Borda. I explain a methodology that emerged during this community–university collaboration and four pedagogical principles that inspired the academics in the case study to find ways to co-construct knowledge with high schoolers and other community members. A conclusion sums up the significance of the learning and its implications for ethical research with communities
