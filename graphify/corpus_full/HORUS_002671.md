# REACTIVE OXYGEN SPECIES PHOTOGENERATED FROM FRUITS-DERIVED CARBON DOTS AND VISIBLE LIGHT

Nitrogen-doped carbon dots (N-CDs) were prepared from three kinds of exotic tropical fruits, such as lulo (Solanum quitoense), Cape gooseberry (Physalis peruviana), and passion fruit (Passiflora edulis). Three samples of N-CDs were obtained with ethylenediamine and the respective juice fruits at 150 °C. Optical, morphological, and structural properties were characterized by X-ray Diffraction (XRD), Electronic Transmission Microscopy (TEM), Infrared (FT-IR) and Raman Spectroscopies, Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) absorption and fluorescence Spectroscopies. Nanoparticles with semi-spherical shapes and sizes ranging between 2.0 and 2.3 nm were obtained. N-CDs exhibited high capacity to absorb and emit in the visible region. Functional groups such as carboxylic acid, hydroxyl, and amine groups were identified on N-CDs surface. Samples were tested in photocatalytic probes to determine the main Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) under visible light irradiation. In this context, hydroxyl radicals (¿OH) were determined by the luminol model reaction, while superoxide radical anions (O2¿-) were studied from the model reaction using 10, 10¿-dimethyl-9.9'-biacridinium nitrate and by 2, 2¿-chloride as substrate. The results evidence N-CDs' abilities to produce (¿OH and O2¿-) under visible light irradiation. The morphological, cristalline and structural properties of the nanoparticles were correlated with the capacity to generate ROS. N-CDs obtained by ecological methods show potential to be used as a photocatalyst to degrade contamination molecules
