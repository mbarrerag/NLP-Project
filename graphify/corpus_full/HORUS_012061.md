# Identification of an enzyme involved in the metabolism of NAD+ in Leishmania braziliensis, phosphoribosil transferase nicotinete (LbNAPRT).

Leishmaniasis is a disease caused by the protozoar parasite of the genus Leishmania that, in countries such as Afghanistan, Argentina, Colombia, Brazil, Iran, Syria, Ethiopia, Sudan of the north, Costa Rica and Peru, is caused by its protozozoa paratozoal parathythyemia, 75 % of the prevalence index [1]. The World Health Organization (WHO) reported a total of 1.5 million new cases in 102 countries including Colombia. Currently, the medicines used for the treatment of this parasitemia as pentavalent antimonials cause various side effects, so it is necessary to identify new effective pharmacological targets [2]. This way the energy metabolism could be considered relevant in the search for possible pharmacological targets, since it is essential for the maintenance of the cellular integrity of the protozoan [3]. In this context, the production of the nicotin and labor-traspheric acid (NAPRT) is a protein of interest (NA) and the 5-parti-in-intracellular DNA, because it determines the levels of the levels of DNA + intra-in-intra-intra-the 6xHis. In addition, western blot and immunofluorescence trials were conducted to determine the intracellular location of the protein, which presents a cytoplasmic location in the promastigating stage of the parasite. The results obtained constitute an important step in the metabolism of NAD, since it was possible to functionally identify the phosphoribosyl transferase nicotine of Leishmanian braziliensis, essential for the synthesis of NAD+ precursors, as the first NAPRT in protozoan of importance in public health
