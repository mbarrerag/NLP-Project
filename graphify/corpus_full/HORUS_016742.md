# Effect of growth regulators on the mitigation of thermal stress in rice (Oryza sativa)

The increase in global temperatures that has been presented over the last decades is affecting the different physiological and biochemical processes of plants.Many of the crops have decreased their yields, including rice, which has shown decreases in their yield between 6% and 7%. It has been observed that the exogenous application of different growth regulators (phytohormones), particularly the use of auxins (AUX), pan-elastics (GA), cytokinins (CK), and brasinosteroids (BR) have shown potential to mitigate the effects of different types of abiotic and biotic stress in different cultivated species, but in the case of rice under combined thermal stress, the aim of this work was to assess the effect of the exogenous application of different growth regulators on rice plants exposed to a high temperature condition during daytime and night. In this context, four experiments were developed, the first was carried out under greenhouse conditions and growth chambers which had the purpose of knowing which of the evaluated growth regulators managed to mitigate the negative effects of the combined thermal stress and the previous variable physiological conditions. Intracellular carbon, CRA, photosynthetic pigment content (CFC and carotenoids), chlorophyll α fluorescence variables, NPQT and MDA increase; (ii) The use of growth regulators mitigated the effects of combined thermal stress on rice plants.
