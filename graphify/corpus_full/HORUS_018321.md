# How to understand the relationship between science and democracy today?

In the 1970s the Sociology of Scientific Knowledge was characterized as a novel commitment in the Anglo-Saxon world to study science in a naturalistic way. Its perspective was defined from the beginning as contrary to the normative version of the school of Sociology of Science. But the critique of that enveloped vision of science and the desire to build strong programs to understand technoscience initially had the effect of asking to one side explicit questions for the normative aspects of science and its relationship with democracy. However, the preference of Social Studies of Science Technology (ESCT) for studying techno-scientific controversies, for stimulating the participation of publics in the discussion of science and technology agendas and for understanding the complex agency of experts and lay publics in the discussion of risk issues, kept alive in the field the interest to understand the co-production of technosciences, naturcultures and social orders.
