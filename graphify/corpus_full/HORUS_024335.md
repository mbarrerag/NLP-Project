# Economic theory and the behavioral perspective model: an experimental approach to the effect of income and prices on consumer choice

This work starts from the formulation of the behavioral perspective model and builds an analysis of the theoretical versions in economy on the consumer's behavior. In particular, the neoclassical and postkeynesian version is opposed in relation to the effect of price and income on the buyer's behavior and the neoclassical and behavioral explanations on the intertemporal choice. To examine the theoretical debate, an exercise was carried out to simulate a purchasing task with the following characteristics: homogeneous products and information on price and product quantities. The purchasing environment was closed and executed during six periods in which the programs of reason and income available to the participants were modified. A panel of 214 consumers was built for a repertoire of 46 products. In the processing of information it was found that the behavioral and postkey versions of the consumer's particular comparison theory explain the patterns of choice observed and that only in circumstances in which the price is the most outgoing attribute, it is possible to infer a response to it.
