# CATALYTIC CONVERSION OF COFFEE WASTE INTO FURFURAL OVER FE/SIO2 CATALYSTS

In Colombia, for every million 60-kg bags of coffee that are exported, some 55,500 tons of waste are produced, which can be used in the production of value-added platform molecules such as furfural. Furfural is a biobased molecule and can be implemented as a selective solvent, fungicide, and nematicide. On the other hand, furfural is a chemical platform used in the pharmaceutical, plastic, cosmetic and energy industries to obtain furfuryl alcohol, levulinic acid, 5-hydroxymethylfurfural, among others. In the present investigation, furfural was obtained by dehydration of pentoses such as xylose present in biomass through heterogeneous catalysis. The investigation was developed through a methodology by stages, stage 1 corresponded to the collection and preparation of the sample for its compositional analysis where it was determined that the biomass contains 85.7 % of humidity, 14.4 % of total solids and 7.60 % ash. Stage 2 corresponded to the development of hydrothermal pretreatments where the biomass was subjected to temperature and pressure conditions to more easily access the structural and non-structural sugars. Stage 3 corresponded to the evaluation of obtaining furfural biomass using Fe/SiO2 catalysts. Thus, the analyzes were carried out with xylose at 170 °C, 120 min and 0.2 g of catalyst charge. Subsequently, the production of furfural the liquid fractions obtained in stage 2 was evaluated. The reactions were carried out in a discontinuous reactor with an inert atmosphere designed for the investigation, equipped with a type J thermocouple, TC4 temperature controller, resistance and control Panel. Furfural production was evaluated using a SHIMADZU Prominence-i LC 2030 chromatographic system, with a UV and RID20 detector to determine conversion, yield and selectivity. Finally, catalyst stability tests were carried out through the development of three catalytic cycles and furfural formation tests without catalyst. The latter showed a furfural yield of 21.8%, associated with high reaction temperature
