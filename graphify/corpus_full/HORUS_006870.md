# Overcoming urban monotony in VIS projects, variety strategies for urban landscape diversification: Counterproposed through scenarios, partial plan Las Vegas de Comfandi in Cali

The urban monotony, a common characteristic in the urban residential structure, is affecting the perception of the urban landscape and poses a challenge in the development of social housing projects in the country. These projects tend to focus on technical, normative and economic aspects during the urban and architectural design process, resulting in standardized and repetitive urban forms. The present Final Master's Work (TFM) focuses on analyzing the impact of urban monotony on the Las Vegas de Compandi Partial Plan, south of Santiago de Cali. The objectives of the study include: i. Defining urban monotony and its origins. ii. Quantifying the urban variety and monotony in Las Vegas de Comfandi. iii. Presenting three urban design alternatives to mitigate the negative impact of the urban monotonous environment. iv. Suggest design strategies to anticipate and correct the negative impact on social housing environments. This work also promotes an integral perspective of urban design in the urban environments of social housing, going beyond the technical and economic efficiency to create environments with urban quality and diverse landscapes that improve the experience of its inhabitants. In conclusion, the TFM, the focus is on: i. This work focuses on raising awareness and promoting self-criticism among the real estate and construction developers who have been building our cities for more than two decades through partial plans and macroprojects. It aims to question the way in which residential environments are designed in order to reform the design strategy for the future. In this sense, this TFM suggests a design strategy that becomes aware of the problem of urban monotony and proposes a process that allows mitigating its negative effect on social housing projects .
